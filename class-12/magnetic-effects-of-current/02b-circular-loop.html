Magnetic Field due to a Circular Loop

The prototype of a magnetic dipole — a current loop in closed form.

Setup and Geometry

A circular loop of radius \( R \) carries current \( I \). Place the loop in the \( xy \)-plane, centred at the origin. We seek the magnetic field at a point \( P \) on the axis of the loop, at a distance \( x \) from its centre.

A current element \( Id\vec{l} \) at the rim of the loop is tangential (in the \( xy \)-plane). The displacement vector \( \vec{r} \) from the element to \( P \) lies in the plane containing the element and the \( x \)-axis.

Field on the Axis

The magnitude of \( d\vec{B} \) from any single element is:

\[ dB = \frac{\mu_0 I\, dl}{4\pi\, (R^2 + x^2)} \]

Due to the circular symmetry of the loop, the transverse (radial) components of \( d\vec{B} \) from diametrically opposite elements cancel. Only the axial (\( x \)-direction) components survive. Each element contributes an axial component:

\[ dB_x = dB \cdot \sin\alpha = \frac{\mu_0 I\, dl}{4\pi(R^2+x^2)} \cdot \frac{R}{\sqrt{R^2+x^2}} \]

Integrating over the full circle (\( \oint dl = 2\pi R \)):

\[ \boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}} \]

The field is directed along the axis, in the direction determined by the right-hand rule: curl the right hand's fingers in the direction of current flow; the thumb points in the direction of \( \vec{B} \).

This is one of the most important results in magnetostatics. It appears in NMR machines, Helmholtz coils (which produce nearly uniform fields), and in all discussions of magnetic dipoles.

Special Case: Field at the Centre

At the centre of the loop, \( x = 0 \):

\[ B_{\text{centre}} = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2 R^3} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R} \] \[ \boxed{B_{\text{centre}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}} \]

This is the simplest result in this chapter. The field at the centre is uniform in direction (perpendicular to the plane of the loop) and depends only on the current and radius.

Special Case: Far Field (\( x \gg R \))

When the field point is far from the loop (\( x \gg R \)), we neglect \( R^2 \) under the radical:

\[ B \approx \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2x^3} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{2m}{x^3} \]

where \( m = I\pi R^2 \) is the magnetic moment of the loop. This is the far-field of a magnetic dipole — falling off as \( 1/x^3 \), identical in form to the far-field of an electric dipole along its axis.

Arc of a Circle

For an arc of angular extent \( \theta \) (in radians) of a circular loop of radius \( R \), the field at the centre is:

\[ B_{\text{arc}} = \frac{\mu_0 I \theta}{4\pi R} \]

For a full circle \( \theta = 2\pi \), this reduces correctly to \( \mu_0 I / (2R) \). For a semicircle, \( \theta = \pi \), giving \( B = \mu_0 I / (4R) \).
