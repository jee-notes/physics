Magnetic Field due to a Solenoid

Constructing nearly uniform magnetic fields through wound coils.

The Solenoid — A Stack of Loops

A solenoid is a long helical coil of wire wound uniformly. It can be thought of as a stack of \( N \) closely packed circular loops, each of radius \( R \). If the total length is \( L \) and the number of turns per unit length is \( n = N/L \), then each infinitesimal slice \( dx \) of the solenoid contains \( n\,dx \) turns.

The field on the axis from a thin slice at position \( x \) (treating it as a single loop) is:

\[ dB = \frac{\mu_0 (n\,dx) I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}} \]

Integrating over the full length (with angular substitution \( x = R\cot\phi \)) yields the general result for a finite solenoid. For a point at the centre of a very long solenoid, the result simplifies dramatically.

Field Inside an Ideal (Infinite) Solenoid

For an infinitely long solenoid with \( n \) turns per unit length carrying current \( I \), the magnetic field inside is:

\[ \boxed{B = \mu_0 n I} \]

Key features of this result:

  • The field is uniform throughout the interior — independent of position.
  • The field is directed along the solenoid axis.
  • The field outside an ideal infinite solenoid is exactly zero.
  • The direction is given by the right-hand rule: curl the fingers in the direction of current winding; the thumb indicates \( \vec{B} \).
This result is most cleanly derived using Ampere's Circuital Law, where the rectangular Amperian loop (shown in the diagram, length \(L\)) immediately gives \( BL = \mu_0 (nL) I \) inside and \( B = 0 \) outside. The Biot-Savart integration confirms this limiting result.

Field at the Ends of a Finite Solenoid

At either end (open face) of a finite solenoid, the field is exactly half that at the centre:

\[ B_{\text{end}} = \frac{\mu_0 n I}{2} \]

This is because the integration covers only half the angular range (\( 0^\circ \) to \( 90^\circ \)) compared to the interior (\( 0^\circ \) to \( 180^\circ \)).
