Relation Between Magnetic Moment and Angular Momentum

Magnetic moment of rotating charge distributions.

Rotating Charged Ring

Consider a ring of radius \(R\) carrying total charge \(q\) rotating with angular velocity \( \omega \).

The rotating charge constitutes a current.

\[ I = \frac{q}{T} \]

Since the time period of rotation is

\[ T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} \]

the equivalent current becomes

\[ I = \frac{q\omega}{2\pi} \]

Magnetic Dipole Moment of the Ring

The magnetic dipole moment of a current loop is

\[ \mu = I A \]

For a circular ring,

\[ A = \pi R^2 \]

Substituting for the current gives

\[ \mu = \frac{q\omega}{2\pi} \times \pi R^2 \] \[ \mu = \frac{q\omega R^2}{2} \]

Angular Momentum of the Rotating Ring

If the ring has mass \(m\), its angular momentum is

\[ L = m \omega R^2 \]

Relation Between Magnetic Moment and Angular Momentum

Dividing the magnetic moment by the angular momentum gives

\[ \frac{\mu}{L} = \frac{q}{2m} \]

Thus

\[ \mu = \frac{q}{2m} L \]

This relation shows that whenever charge is associated with rotational motion, the system automatically acquires a magnetic dipole moment.

Physical Significance

  • Explains magnetism of orbiting electrons in atoms.
  • Connects magnetic properties with angular momentum.
  • This ratio \(q/2m\) is known as the gyromagnetic ratio.
