Magnetic Field due to an Infinite Current Sheet

Integrating infinite straight wires to find the field of a continuous sheet.

Setup and Geometry

Consider an infinite flat sheet lying in the YZ-plane carrying a uniform surface current in the +Z-direction (out of the page in our 2D cross-section). We wish to find the magnetic field at a point P located at a perpendicular distance \( d \) from the sheet along the X-axis.

Let the surface current density (current per unit width) be \( K \) (Amperes per meter). The sheet can be viewed as an infinite array of parallel, infinitely long straight wires running along the Z-axis.

Consider an infinitesimal strip of width \( dy \) located at coordinate \( y \). This strip acts like an infinite thin straight wire carrying a current:

\[ dI = K \, dy \]

The distance from this strip to the point P (which is at coordinate \((d, 0, 0)\)) is:

\[ r = \sqrt{d^2 + y^2} \]

Integration Using Biot-Savart Law

The magnitude of the magnetic field \( dB \) produced by this single infinite geometric "wire" strip at point \(P\) is given by the exact formula for an infinite straight wire:

\[ dB = \frac{\mu_0 dI}{2\pi r} = \frac{\mu_0 K \, dy}{2\pi \sqrt{d^2 + y^2}} \]

From the right hand rule, the vector \( d\vec{B} \) is perpendicular to both the current direction (+Z) and the position vector \( \vec{r} \). This means \( d\vec{B} \) lies in the XY-plane. Looking at the geometry, \( d\vec{B} \) has two components:

  • An X-component \( dB_x = dB \sin\theta \) (pointing parallel to the X-axis)
  • A Y-component \( dB_y = dB \cos\theta \) (pointing parallel to the Y-axis)

where \( \theta \) is the angle between the X-axis and \( \vec{r} \). From the geometry, \( \cos\theta = \frac{d}{r} \) and \( \sin\theta = \frac{|y|}{r} \).

Crucially, for every current strip located at a positive coordinate \(+y\), there exists a symmetrically located strip at \(-y\). While their Y-components add up perfectly, their X-components point in opposite directions (one inward towards the X-axis, one outward) and sum exactly to zero.

Thus, the net magnetic field has only a Y-component:

\[ B = \int dB_y = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dB \cos\theta \] \[ B = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\mu_0 K \, dy}{2\pi \sqrt{d^2 + y^2}} \left(\frac{d}{\sqrt{d^2+y^2}}\right) \] \[ B = \frac{\mu_0 K d}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dy}{d^2 + y^2} \]

This is a standard trigonometric integral. It evaluates to:

\[ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dy}{d^2 + y^2} = \left[ \frac{1}{d} \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{d}\right) \right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} = \frac{1}{d} \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right) \right) = \frac{\pi}{d} \]

Substituting this result back into our expression for \(B\):

\[ B = \frac{\mu_0 K d}{2\pi} \left( \frac{\pi}{d} \right) \] \[ \boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 K}{2}} \]

Key Insights

  • The magnetic field produced by an infinite uniform sheet of current is entirely independent of the distance \(d\) from the sheet. It is perfectly uniform in the entire space on either side of the sheet.
  • The field direction is always parallel to the plane of the sheet and perpendicular to the current. As confirmed exactly by the right hand rule, it points in the +Y direction for \(x > 0\) and -Y direction for \(x < 0\).
  • This result \( B = \frac{\mu_0 K}{2} \) is the magnetic counterpart to the electric field \( E = \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0} \) produced by an infinite sheet of charge. They share identical mathematical distance independence.
  • While derived here with integration, this result can also be found swiftly using Ampère's Circuital Law. By drawing a rectangular Amperian loop intersecting the sheet, the symmetry demands \( \oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = 2BL = \mu_0(KL) \), immediately giving the same \( B = \mu_0 K / 2 \).
