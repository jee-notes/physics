Magnetic Dipole Moment of Coils in Different Planes

Vector nature of magnetic dipole moment and superposition of multiple loops.

Magnetic Dipole Moment is a Vector

The magnetic dipole moment of a current loop is defined as

\[ \vec{\mu} = N I \vec{A} \]

The direction of \( \vec{A} \) is perpendicular to the plane of the loop, determined using the right-hand rule.

Because the dipole moment is a vector, the total magnetic dipole moment of a system of loops is the vector sum of the individual moments.

Two Coils in the Same Plane

If two coils lie in the same plane and carry currents in the same direction, their dipole moments are parallel.

\[ \vec{\mu}_{total} = \vec{\mu}_1 + \vec{\mu}_2 \]

The magnitude simply becomes

\[ \mu_{total} = \mu_1 + \mu_2 \]

If currents flow in opposite directions, the dipole moments oppose each other.

Two Coils in Perpendicular Planes

If two coils lie in mutually perpendicular planes, their dipole moments are also perpendicular vectors.

For example:

  • Loop 1 in the XY plane → \( \vec{\mu}_1 \parallel \hat{k} \)
  • Loop 2 in the YZ plane → \( \vec{\mu}_2 \parallel \hat{i} \)

The resultant dipole moment is given by vector addition.

\[ \mu = \sqrt{\mu_1^2 + \mu_2^2} \]

Many JEE problems use perpendicular loops so that the resultant dipole moment can be obtained using the Pythagorean relation between vector components.
