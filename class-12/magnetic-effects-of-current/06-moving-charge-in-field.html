Motion of a Charged Particle in a Magnetic Field

Circular orbits, helical paths, and the machinery of particle accelerators.

Circular Motion in a Uniform \( \vec{B} \)

When a charged particle of mass \( m \), charge \( q \), and speed \( v \) enters a region of uniform magnetic field \( \vec{B} \) with its velocity perpendicular to \( \vec{B} \), the magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity. This is exactly the condition for uniform circular motion.

The magnetic force provides the centripetal force:

\[ qvB = \frac{mv^2}{r} \]

Solving for the radius of the circular orbit (the cyclotron radius or Larmor radius):

\[ \boxed{r = \frac{mv}{qB}} \]
A heavier particle orbits in a larger circle. A faster particle also orbits in a larger circle. But for a given speed, a stronger field curves the orbit more tightly. This forms the basis of mass spectrometry.

Cyclotron Frequency and Time Period

The time period of the circular orbit is:

\[ T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{qB} \]

The cyclotron frequency (angular frequency) is:

\[ \omega_c = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{qB}{m} \]

And the cyclotron frequency in Hz:

\[ \nu_c = \frac{qB}{2\pi m} \]
The cyclotron frequency \( \omega_c = qB/m \) is independent of the particle's speed. Faster particles orbit in larger circles but complete the circle in exactly the same time. This remarkable fact is exploited in the cyclotron particle accelerator.

Helical Motion

If the velocity of the particle has a component along \( \vec{B} \) (let's call it \( v_\parallel \)) in addition to the perpendicular component \( v_\perp \), the motion is helical:

  • The perpendicular component \( v_\perp \) drives circular motion in the plane perpendicular to \( \vec{B} \), with radius \( r = mv_\perp / (qB) \).
  • The parallel component \( v_\parallel \) is unaffected by the magnetic force (since \( \vec{v}_\parallel \times \vec{B} = 0 \)).
  • The result is a helix — the particle spirals along the direction of \( \vec{B} \).

The pitch of the helix (distance advanced along \( \vec{B} \) per revolution) is:

\[ p = v_\parallel \cdot T = \frac{2\pi m v_\parallel}{qB} \]

Helical motion explains why charged particles in Earth's magnetic field are trapped and spiral along field lines — this is the origin of the Van Allen radiation belts and auroras.

The Velocity Selector

A velocity selector uses crossed electric and magnetic fields to select particles of a specific speed. Set up \( \vec{E} \) and \( \vec{B} \) perpendicular to each other and to the particle beam.

For a particle to travel in a straight line, the electric force must balance the magnetic force:

\[ qE = qvB \implies \boxed{v = \frac{E}{B}} \]

Only particles with exactly this speed pass through undeflected. Faster or slower particles are deflected and blocked by a slit. This device is used in the front end of mass spectrometers.

The Cyclotron

The cyclotron is a particle accelerator that exploits the fact that \( \omega_c = qB/m \) is independent of speed. It consists of two D-shaped metallic chambers (dees) placed in a uniform magnetic field, separated by a gap across which an alternating electric field is applied.

  • A particle enters the gap, is accelerated by the electric field, and then curves in a semicircle inside the dee.
  • After exactly half a revolution, it returns to the gap — at which point the electric field has reversed sign, and it is accelerated again.
  • Because the period of revolution is constant (independent of speed), the AC frequency can be locked to \( \nu_c = qB/(2\pi m) \) — the cyclotron resonance condition.
  • The particle spirals outward in ever-larger semicircles, gaining energy at each gap crossing.

The maximum kinetic energy achieved when the particle exits at radius \( R \) is:

\[ KE_{\max} = \frac{q^2 B^2 R^2}{2m} \]
The cyclotron fails at relativistic speeds. As the particle approaches the speed of light, its relativistic mass increases, making \( \omega_c = qB/m \) smaller — the particle falls out of phase with the alternating field. This is corrected by the synchrocyclotron (which varies the frequency) and the synchrotron (which varies both field and frequency).
