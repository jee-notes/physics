Torque and Potential Energy of a Magnetic Dipole

Interaction of a current loop with an external magnetic field.

Magnetic Dipole in an External Field

A current carrying loop behaves like a magnetic dipole with magnetic dipole moment

\[ \vec{\mu} = N I \vec{A} \]

where \(N\) is the number of turns, \(I\) is the current in the loop, and \(A\) is the area vector perpendicular to the plane of the loop.

When this dipole is placed in a magnetic field \( \vec{B} \), forces act on different segments of the loop. Although these forces may cancel translationally, they produce a net torque that tends to rotate the loop.

Torque on a Current Loop

The torque experienced by a magnetic dipole in a uniform magnetic field is given by

\[ \vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu} \times \vec{B} \]

The magnitude of the torque is

\[ \tau = \mu B \sin\theta \]

where \( \theta \) is the angle between the dipole moment and the magnetic field.

This torque tends to align the dipole moment with the magnetic field direction.

A uniform magnetic field cannot produce a net force on a current loop, but it can produce a torque. The loop therefore rotates but does not translate.

Potential Energy of a Magnetic Dipole

When the dipole rotates in a magnetic field, work is done against the magnetic torque. This leads to potential energy associated with the orientation of the dipole.

The potential energy of a magnetic dipole in a magnetic field is

\[ U = -\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{B} \]

or

\[ U = -\mu B \cos\theta \]

Energy Interpretation

  • Minimum energy occurs when \( \vec{\mu} \) is aligned with \( \vec{B} \).
  • Maximum energy occurs when the dipole is anti-parallel to the field.
  • The system naturally tends toward the minimum energy configuration.

The magnetic dipole behaves very similarly to an electric dipole placed in an electric field. In both cases, the field tends to rotate the dipole to align with itself.
