Magnetic Field due to a Solenoid & Toroid

Constructing nearly uniform magnetic fields through wound coils.

The Solenoid — A Stack of Loops

A solenoid is a long helical coil of wire wound uniformly. It can be thought of as a stack of \( N \) closely packed circular loops, each of radius \( R \). If the total length is \( L \) and the number of turns per unit length is \( n = N/L \), then each infinitesimal slice \( dx \) of the solenoid contains \( n\,dx \) turns.

The field on the axis from a thin slice at position \( x \) (treating it as a single loop) is:

\[ dB = \frac{\mu_0 (n\,dx) I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}} \]

Integrating over the full length (with angular substitution \( x = R\cot\phi \)) yields the general result for a finite solenoid. For a point at the centre of a very long solenoid, the result simplifies dramatically.

Field Inside an Ideal (Infinite) Solenoid

For an infinitely long solenoid with \( n \) turns per unit length carrying current \( I \), the magnetic field inside is:

\[ \boxed{B = \mu_0 n I} \]

Key features of this result:

  • The field is uniform throughout the interior — independent of position.
  • The field is directed along the solenoid axis.
  • The field outside an ideal infinite solenoid is exactly zero.
  • The direction is given by the right-hand rule: curl the fingers in the direction of current winding; the thumb indicates \( \vec{B} \).
This result is most cleanly derived using Ampere's Circuital Law (see Section 3), where the rectangular Amperian loop immediately gives \( B = \mu_0 n I \) inside and \( B = 0 \) outside. The Biot-Savart integration confirms this limiting result.

Field at the Ends of a Finite Solenoid

At either end (open face) of a finite solenoid, the field is exactly half that at the centre:

\[ B_{\text{end}} = \frac{\mu_0 n I}{2} \]

This is because the integration covers only half the angular range (\( 0° \) to \( 90° \)) compared to the interior (\( 0° \) to \( 180° \)).

The Toroid

A toroid is a solenoid bent into a closed ring (a doughnut shape). If the mean radius of the toroid is \( r \), it has \( N \) total turns, and carries current \( I \), the magnetic field inside the toroid is found using Ampere's Law with a circular Amperian loop of radius \( r \):

\[ B \cdot 2\pi r = \mu_0 N I \] \[ \boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r} = \mu_0 n I} \]

where \( n = N/(2\pi r) \) is the number of turns per unit length along the mean circumference.

The magnetic field of a toroid is:

  • Confined entirely inside the toroid — it is zero both in the central hole and outside the ring.
  • Not perfectly uniform — it is slightly stronger near the inner edge (smaller \( r \)) and weaker near the outer edge (larger \( r \)).
  • Becomes uniform in the idealization of a tightly wound toroid where the cross-sectional radius \( \ll r \).

Comparison: Solenoid vs Toroid

Property Solenoid Toroid
Shape Straight cylinder Closed ring (torus)
Field inside \( \mu_0 nI \) (uniform) \( \mu_0 nI \) (nearly uniform)
Field outside ~Zero (infinite case) Exactly zero
Field at ends \( \mu_0 nI / 2 \) No ends (closed)
Application Electromagnets, inductors Transformers, inductors with zero leakage
