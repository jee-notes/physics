Magnetic Field due to a Straight Wire

Integrating the Biot-Savart Law along a linear current source.

Setup and Geometry

Consider a thin straight wire carrying current \( I \). We wish to find the magnetic field at a point \( P \) located at a perpendicular distance \( d \) from the wire.

Place the wire along the \( x \)-axis. The point \( P \) lies in the \( xy \)-plane at position \( (0,\, d,\, 0) \). A current element at position \( (x, 0, 0) \) is \( d\vec{l} = dx\,\hat{x} \).

The displacement vector from the element to \( P \) is:

\[ \vec{r} = -x\,\hat{x} + d\,\hat{y}, \quad r = \sqrt{x^2 + d^2} \]

The angle \( \phi \) is defined from the perpendicular drawn from \( P \) to the wire: as the element scans from one end to the other, \( \phi \) varies from \( \phi_1 \) to \( \phi_2 \).

Applying the Biot-Savart Law

The cross product gives \( d\vec{l} \times \hat{r} = d\vec{l} \times \vec{r}/r \), which points in the \( +\hat{z} \) direction (out of the \( xy \)-plane) for all elements — by symmetry, there is no cancellation and all contributions add up in the same direction.

The scalar magnitude of \( d\vec{B} \) from element at angle \( \phi \) is:

\[ dB = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \frac{\sin\theta}{r^2}\, dl \]

Substituting \( x = d\tan\phi \), \( dx = d\sec^2\phi\, d\phi \), and \( r = d/\cos\phi = d\sec\phi \):

\[ dB = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}\cos\phi\, d\phi \]

Integrating from \( \phi_1 \) to \( \phi_2 \):

\[ \boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}(\sin\phi_2 - \sin\phi_1)} \]
The angles \( \phi_1 \) and \( \phi_2 \) are measured from the perpendicular at \( P \) to the two ends of the wire. For a point beyond one end of a semi-infinite wire, one angle is \( 0° \) and the other is \( 90° \).

Special Case: Infinite Straight Wire

For an infinite wire, both ends extend to infinity: \( \phi_1 = -90° \) and \( \phi_2 = +90° \). Substituting:

\[ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}(\sin 90° - \sin(-90°)) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}(1+1) \] \[ \boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi d}} \]

The field lines of an infinite straight wire are concentric circles around the wire. The direction is given by the right-hand rule: curl the right hand's fingers around the wire in the direction of current; the thumb points in the direction of the field.

Special Case: Semi-Infinite Wire

For a wire extending from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = +\infty \), with \( P \) at perpendicular distance \( d \) from the near end:

\[ \phi_1 = 0°, \quad \phi_2 = 90° \implies B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d} \]

This result is exactly half the field of an infinite wire at the same distance — which makes intuitive sense from superposition.

Behaviour of the Field

  • The field is stronger closer to the wire: \( B \propto 1/d \).
  • The field circles the wire — it is not radial like an electric field, but tangential.
  • Two parallel wires carrying currents in the same direction attract; in opposite directions, they repel.
