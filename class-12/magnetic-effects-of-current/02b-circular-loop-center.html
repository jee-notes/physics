Magnetic Field due to a Circular Loop

The prototype of a magnetic dipole — a current loop in closed form.

Setup and Geometry

Consider a circular loop of radius \(R\) carrying a steady current \(I\). The loop lies in the XY-plane and is centred at the origin. Our goal is to determine the magnetic field produced by this current at the center of the loop, denoted by point O.

Every point on the loop is at the same distance \(R\) from the center. This symmetry is the key reason the calculation becomes very simple.

For the purpose of this derivation, the current is taken to flow in the anticlockwise direction when viewed from above the plane of the loop.

Applying the Biot–Savart Law

According to the Biot–Savart law, a small current element \(I\,d\vec{l}\) produces a magnetic field contribution

\[ d\vec B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I\, d\vec l \times \hat r}{r^2} \]

The differential current element \(d\vec l\) lies along the tangent to the circular loop. Since the loop lies in the XY-plane, this tangent direction also lies in the same plane.

The observation point O is at the center of the circle. The position vector from any current element on the loop to the center therefore has magnitude

\[ r = R \]

and points radially inward toward the center.

Thus both the vectors \(d\vec l\) and \(\vec r\) lie in the XY-plane. Their cross product \(d\vec l \times \hat r\) must therefore point perpendicular to this plane — along the \(+\hat{k}\) direction (out of the page).

An important consequence follows from this symmetry: every current element on the circular loop produces a magnetic field at the center pointing in exactly the same direction.

Therefore none of the contributions cancel each other. Instead, all the infinitesimal magnetic field contributions add constructively along the same direction.

Because the direction is already determined by symmetry, we can work only with the magnitudes of the contributions while keeping the direction understood.

The magnitude of the magnetic field contribution from an element \(dl\) is therefore

\[ dB = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R^2}\, dl \]

Notice that the distance \(R\) is constant for every point on the loop. Therefore the only variable in this expression is the length element \(dl\).

Magnetic Field due to the Full Circular Loop

For a complete circular loop, the element \(dl\) runs along the entire circumference of the circle.

\[ \int dl = 2\pi R \]

Substituting into the Biot–Savart expression gives

\[ B = \int \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R^2} dl = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R^2}(2\pi R) \] \[ \boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}} \]

The magnetic field at the center of a circular loop therefore depends only on the current and the radius of the loop.

Magnetic Field due to a Circular Arc

The same reasoning can be used to determine the magnetic field at the center due to a circular arc.

If the arc subtends an angle \( \theta \) at the center, then its length is

\[ L = R\theta \]

Integrating the Biot–Savart expression over this arc length gives

\[ B = \int \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R^2} dl = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R^2}(R\theta) \] \[ \boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R}\theta} \]

Thus the magnetic field produced at the center by a circular arc is directly proportional to the angle subtended by the arc.
