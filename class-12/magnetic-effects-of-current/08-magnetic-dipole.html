Magnetic Dipole and Current Loop

The current loop as the fundamental unit of magnetic matter.

The Magnetic Dipole Moment

A planar current loop is the most fundamental magnetic object. It is the magnetic analogue of the electric dipole. We characterise it by its magnetic dipole moment \( \vec{m} \):

\[ \vec{m} = I A \hat{n} \]

where \( I \) is the current, \( A \) is the area of the loop, and \( \hat{n} \) is the unit normal to the plane of the loop. The direction of \( \hat{n} \) is given by the right-hand rule: curl your right hand's fingers in the direction of current flow; your thumb points in the direction of \( \vec{m} \).

The SI unit of magnetic moment is A·m².

A bar magnet, a compass needle, a proton, and an electron all behave as magnetic dipoles in appropriate limits. The concept of magnetic dipole moment unifies these apparently different phenomena.

Torque on a Dipole in a Uniform Field

When a magnetic dipole \( \vec{m} \) is placed in a uniform magnetic field \( \vec{B} \), it experiences a torque:

\[ \vec{\tau} = \vec{m} \times \vec{B} \]

The magnitude is:

\[ \tau = mB\sin\theta \]

where \( \theta \) is the angle between \( \vec{m} \) and \( \vec{B} \).

  • \( \theta = 0° \): \( \tau = 0 \) — stable equilibrium (dipole aligned with field).
  • \( \theta = 90° \): \( \tau = mB \) — maximum torque.
  • \( \theta = 180° \): \( \tau = 0 \) — unstable equilibrium (dipole anti-aligned with field).

The torque always acts to align the dipole with the external field — this is why compass needles point north.

Potential Energy of a Dipole in a Field

The potential energy of a magnetic dipole in a uniform external field is:

\[ U = -\vec{m} \cdot \vec{B} = -mB\cos\theta \]
  • \( \theta = 0° \): \( U = -mB \) — minimum energy (stable equilibrium).
  • \( \theta = 90° \): \( U = 0 \) — reference position.
  • \( \theta = 180° \): \( U = +mB \) — maximum energy (unstable equilibrium).

The work done in rotating the dipole from angle \( \theta_1 \) to \( \theta_2 \) against the torque:

\[ W = \Delta U = mB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2) \]
The formula \( U = -\vec{m} \cdot \vec{B} \) is identical in form to the electric dipole result \( U = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E} \). This is one of many deep structural analogies between electrostatics and magnetostatics.

Force on a Dipole in a Non-Uniform Field

A magnetic dipole in a uniform field experiences zero net force (only torque). In a non-uniform field, however, there is a net force. For a dipole aligned along the \( z \)-axis in a field varying along \( z \):

\[ F_z = m \frac{dB}{dz} \]

This force pulls the dipole toward stronger field regions if \( \vec{m} \) is aligned with \( \vec{B} \) (paramagnetic behavior), and pushes it toward weaker field regions if anti-aligned (diamagnetic behavior). This underlies the Stern-Gerlach experiment and magnetic separation techniques.

Analogy with Electric Dipole

The electric dipole \( \vec{p} \) and the magnetic dipole \( \vec{m} \) show a profound mathematical parallel. This analogy is structural and deep — both arise from the multipole expansion of a field:

Property Electric Dipole \( \vec{p} \) Magnetic Dipole \( \vec{m} \)
Definition \( \vec{p} = q\vec{d} \) \( \vec{m} = IA\hat{n} \)
Torque \( \vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E} \) \( \vec{\tau} = \vec{m} \times \vec{B} \)
Potential Energy \( U = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E} \) \( U = -\vec{m} \cdot \vec{B} \)
Axial far field \( \propto p/r^3 \) \( \propto m/r^3 \)
Isolated monopole? Yes (point charge) No (no magnetic monopole observed)
