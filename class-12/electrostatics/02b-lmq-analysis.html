LMQ Analysis

Charged Particle Dynamics under Electrostatic, Gravitational and Constraint Forces

What is LMQ Analysis?

LMQ analysis refers to a broad class of particle dynamics problems in which a charged particle simultaneously experiences three types of forces:

  • Electrostatic force
  • Gravitational force
  • Constraint force (such as tension or normal reaction)

The electrostatic force is obtained from Coulomb's law, while the resulting equilibrium or motion is analysed using the methods of mechanics. Thus, LMQ analysis forms the natural bridge between mechanics and electrostatics.

LMQ analysis is not a single problem. It is a mechanics framework for analysing charged particles under electrostatic, gravitational and constraint forces.

Why "LMQ"?

The abbreviation LMQ refers to the idealized physical model used in this class of problems:

  • L — A light, inextensible string of length \(l\).
  • M — A point mass \(m\).
  • Q — A point charge \(q\).

The body is modelled simultaneously as a point mass carrying a point charge and suspended by a light string. The charge determines the electrostatic force, the mass determines the gravitational force, while the string provides the geometric constraint and the tension force.

LMQ combines three standard idealizations of mechanics and electrostatics into a single model: a light string, a point mass and a point charge.

Standard Configuration

The most common LMQ setup consists of two small conducting spheres of masses \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), carrying charges \(q_1\) and \(q_2\), suspended from a common support by light strings of length \(l\).

Depending on the magnitudes and signs of the charges, the spheres either attract or repel each other until mechanical equilibrium is established. The equilibrium configuration is determined by the combined effects of electrostatic force, gravity and tension.

Although this is the standard configuration, the same methods extend naturally to other constrained charged-particle systems such as charged pendulums, beads on smooth surfaces and other particle dynamics problems.

Parameters

A typical LMQ problem may involve variation of one or more of the following quantities:

  • Masses \(m_1,\;m_2\)
  • Charges \(q_1,\;q_2\)
  • String length \(l\)
  • Gravitational acceleration \(g\)
  • Equilibrium angle
  • Separation between the charged particles
  • External electric field (advanced cases)

The objective is to determine either the equilibrium configuration or the subsequent motion of the system as these parameters are varied.

Mechanics Revisited

LMQ analysis revisits many techniques already developed in mechanics. The only new ingredient is the electrostatic force obtained from Coulomb's law. Once this force is incorporated into the free-body diagram, the remaining analysis follows the familiar principles of mechanics.

  • Free-body diagrams
  • Vector resolution
  • Static equilibrium
  • Effective gravity
  • Simple harmonic motion
  • Circular motion
  • Work-energy methods
Mechanics is independent of the origin of the force. Whether the force is gravitational, elastic or electrostatic, the motion is analysed using the same Newtonian principles. Coulomb's law simply introduces a new force into the mechanics toolkit.
