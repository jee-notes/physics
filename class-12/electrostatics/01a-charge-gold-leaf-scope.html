Gold Leaf Electroscope

Device to measure presence of charge

Gold Leaf Electroscope - Physical Setup

A Gold Leaf Electroscope (GLE) is a sensitive instrument used to detect the presence of electric charge. It consists of a conducting assembly enclosed inside a transparent glass container to protect it from air currents and moisture.

At the top is a metallic knob, which acts as the point where a charged body is brought in contact with the instrument.

The knob is connected to a conducting metal rod that passes through an insulating sheath or cover.

At the lower end of the rod are attached two extremely thin gold leaves.

The insulating stopper electrically isolates the conducting assembly from the outer case, preventing charge from leaking away.

The delicate gold leaves are free to move and serve as the indicator of the instrument. Any redistribution or transfer of charge to the conducting assembly eventually appears as a change in the separation of these leaves.

The conducting knob, metal rod, and gold leaves together form a single conducting body.
Any charge transferred to or redistributed within the electroscope spreads over this entire conducting assembly.

Working of the Gold Leaf Electroscope

The Gold Leaf Electroscope operates on the principles of charge redistribution and electrostatic repulsion. The separation between the two gold leaves indicates the presence of charge on the conducting assembly.

1. Charging by Contact

When a charged conductor touches the metallic knob, charge is transferred to the conducting assembly of the electroscope. The charge spreads over the knob, rod and both gold leaves.

Since both leaves acquire charges of the same polarity, they repel each other and diverge.

2. Identifying the Nature of Charge

Suppose the electroscope is first given a known positive charge so that its leaves are already separated.

  • If an unknown charged body is brought into contact and the leaves diverge further, the unknown body is positively charged.
  • If the leaves move closer together, the unknown body is negatively charged because it partially neutralizes the existing positive charge.
A neutral conductor can also reduce the separation of the leaves.
When it touches the electroscope, charge redistributes between the two conductors, reducing the charge remaining on the electroscope.
Therefore, a decrease in leaf separation does not necessarily imply that the unknown body is negatively charged.

3. Detection by Induction (Without Contact)

Physical contact is not necessary for the electroscope to respond. When a charged body is merely brought near the metallic knob, its electric field redistributes the free electrons within the conducting assembly.

This redistribution causes both gold leaves to acquire charges of the same polarity, making them repel each other. Consequently, the leaves diverge even though no charge has been transferred to the electroscope.

In induction, the electroscope remains electrically neutral as a whole.
Only the distribution of charge changes.

Even a neutral conductor can cause separation reduction through induction.
