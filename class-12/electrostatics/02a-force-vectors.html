ElectroMechanics

Coulomb's law forms the bridge between mechanics and electromagnetism. It tells us how to calculate the electrostatic force acting between charged bodies. Once this force is known, the motion or equilibrium of the bodies is analysed using the laws of mechanics.

In other words, Coulomb's law supplies the force, while mechanics determines the resulting motion.

For students who have already studied mechanics, this chapter is therefore a natural extension of force analysis.

The same techniques of free-body diagrams, vector addition, equilibrium and Newton's laws are now applied to electrostatic forces.

Mechanics is independent of the origin of the force.
Whether the force is gravitational, elastic, frictional, Normal or electrostatic, the subsequent analysis is carried out using the same principles of mechanics.

Vector Regular Polygon

Many electrostatics problems involve several charges arranged symmetrically. The electrostatic force on a charge is obtained by vector addition of the individual Coulomb forces.

A particularly useful result is that if equal vectors are arranged as the outward spokes (or equally spaced radii) of a regular polygon, then their vector sum is zero.

This symmetry greatly simplifies the analysis of force on charges placed at the vertices of regular polygons.

Whenever identical charges are placed symmetrically, always look for symmetry before beginning vector addition.
In many cases, the resultant force can be obtained immediately without resolving individual components.
