Resonance and Quality Factor

The frequency at which the RLC circuit draws maximum current — and how sharp that peak is.

Resonance in Series RLC

The impedance of a series RLC circuit is:

\[ Z = \sqrt{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2} \]

\( Z \) is minimum when the reactive term is zero:

\[ \omega_0 L = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C} \implies \boxed{\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}} \]

At this resonant frequency \( \omega_0 \):

  • Impedance \( Z = R \) (minimum, purely resistive).
  • Current \( I_0 = V_0/R \) (maximum).
  • Phase difference \( \phi = 0 \) — voltage and current are in phase.
  • \( V_L = V_C \) individually, but they cancel each other in the circuit.
At resonance, \( V_L \) and \( V_C \) can each be much larger than the source voltage \( V_0 \) — the capacitor and inductor exchange energy between themselves at \( \omega_0 \), and the source only needs to replenish the resistive losses. This "voltage magnification" is exploited in radio receivers to select a station.

Voltage Magnification at Resonance

At resonance with \( I_0 = V_0/R \):

\[ V_L = I_0 X_L = \frac{V_0}{R}\cdot\omega_0 L, \qquad V_C = I_0 X_C = \frac{V_0}{R}\cdot\frac{1}{\omega_0 C} \]

Both equal \( V_0 \cdot (X_L/R) = V_0 Q \), where \( Q \) is the quality factor. So:

\[ V_L = V_C = Q \cdot V_0 \quad \text{(at resonance)} \]

Quality Factor (Q-factor)

The quality factor \( Q \) measures the sharpness of resonance — how selective the circuit is around \( \omega_0 \):

\[ Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = \frac{1}{\omega_0 CR} = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} \]

\( Q \) also equals the voltage magnification at resonance (as derived above). A high-\( Q \) circuit has:

  • A sharp, narrow resonance peak (selective — useful for radio tuning).
  • Large voltages across \( L \) and \( C \) at resonance.
  • Small energy loss per cycle relative to stored energy.

Conversely, a low-\( Q \) circuit has a broad, flat resonance — less selective but more robust.

Bandwidth

The bandwidth \( \Delta\omega \) is the range of frequencies over which the current is at least \( 1/\sqrt{2} \) of its maximum (resonant) value — equivalently, the power is at least half the maximum. This is called the half-power bandwidth (or 3 dB bandwidth):

\[ \Delta\omega = \omega_2 - \omega_1 = \frac{R}{L} \]

where \( \omega_1 \) and \( \omega_2 \) are the two half-power angular frequencies. The relationship between \( Q \) and bandwidth is:

\[ Q = \frac{\omega_0}{\Delta\omega} \]

This confirms a high-\( Q \) circuit is narrow-band (selective); low-\( Q \) is wide-band.

JEE Key Points

  • Resonant frequency: \( f_0 = \dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} \) (Hz); \( \omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC} \) (rad/s).
  • At resonance: \( Z = R \), \( I_{\max} = V_0/R \), \( \phi = 0 \).
  • Quality factor: \( Q = \omega_0 L/R = 1/(\omega_0 CR) = \sqrt{L/C}/R \).
  • Voltage magnification: \( V_L = V_C = Q\cdot V_{\text{source}} \) at resonance.
  • Bandwidth: \( \Delta\omega = R/L = \omega_0/Q \).
  • Increasing \( R \) lowers \( Q \), broadens resonance, reduces peak current.
