Alternating Current

Sinusoidal voltages, impedance, resonance — and how power travels across continents.

Conceptual Overview

Alternating current (AC) is the form in which virtually all electrical power is generated and distributed. Unlike direct current, the voltage and current in an AC system vary sinusoidally with time — oscillating between positive and negative values at a fixed frequency (50 Hz in India).

The reason AC dominates power transmission is the transformer — a device that can step voltage up or down almost without loss, using the principle of electromagnetic induction. High voltage, low current transmission losses are minimal; local step-down makes it safe for homes.

The AC Source

An AC generator produces a sinusoidal EMF. The instantaneous voltage is:

\[ v(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t + \phi) \]

where \( V_0 \) is the peak voltage, \( \omega = 2\pi f \) is the angular frequency, and \( \phi \) is the initial phase. The same sinusoidal form applies to the current \( i(t) = I_0\sin(\omega t + \phi') \), but with (in general) a different phase \( \phi' \) — the phase difference \( \phi - \phi' \) depends on the circuit elements.

Impedance — The AC Analogue of Resistance

In a DC circuit, Ohm's Law relates voltage, current, and resistance: \( V = IR \). In an AC circuit, resistors, inductors, and capacitors all oppose current, but with important differences. The generalised opposition is called impedance \( Z \):

\[ V_0 = I_0 Z, \quad Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} \]

where \( X_L = \omega L \) is the inductive reactance and \( X_C = 1/(\omega C) \) is the capacitive reactance. Impedance depends on frequency — making AC circuits fundamentally frequency-dependent in a way DC circuits are not.

Resonance

In a series RLC circuit, there exists a special frequency \( \omega_0 \) at which the inductive and capacitive reactances exactly cancel:

\[ \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \]

At this resonant frequency, the impedance is purely resistive (\( Z = R \)), the current is maximum, and the phase difference between voltage and current is zero. Resonance is the basis of radio tuning, signal filters, and oscillators.
