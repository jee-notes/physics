Pure R, L, C in AC Circuits

How each circuit element individually responds to AC — resistance, reactance, and phase.

Pure Resistive Circuit (AC through R)

For an AC source \( v = V_0\sin(\omega t) \) connected to a pure resistor \( R \), Ohm's law applies at every instant:

\[ i = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{V_0}{R}\sin(\omega t) = I_0\sin(\omega t) \]

Peak current: \( I_0 = V_0/R \). Phase difference: \( \Delta\phi = 0 \) — voltage and current are in phase.

The resistor dissipates power continuously. Average power: \( P = \frac{1}{2}I_0 V_0 = I_{\text{rms}}V_{\text{rms}} \).

A resistor is the same in DC and AC — it has no reactance and introduces no phase shift. It is the reference element against which inductors and capacitors are compared.

Pure Inductive Circuit (AC through L)

For a pure inductor \( L \), the voltage across it is \( v_L = L\,di/dt \). With \( v = V_0\sin(\omega t) \):

\[ L\frac{di}{dt} = V_0\sin(\omega t) \implies i = -\frac{V_0}{\omega L}\cos(\omega t) = \frac{V_0}{\omega L}\sin\left(\omega t - \frac{\pi}{2}\right) \] \[ I_0 = \frac{V_0}{\omega L} = \frac{V_0}{X_L}, \quad X_L = \omega L \]

The current lags the voltage by 90°. The opposition \( X_L = \omega L \) is called inductive reactance (unit: ohm).

  • \( X_L \propto \omega \) — at higher frequency, inductor opposes more (harder for current to change rapidly).
  • At DC (\( \omega = 0 \)): \( X_L = 0 \) — the inductor is a short circuit.
  • As \( \omega \to \infty \): \( X_L \to \infty \) — the inductor is an open circuit.
An inductor passes DC freely but blocks high-frequency AC — the classic low-pass filter element.

Pure Capacitive Circuit (AC through C)

For a pure capacitor \( C \), the charge \( q = Cv \), so \( i = dq/dt = C\,dv/dt \). With \( v = V_0\sin(\omega t) \):

\[ i = C\omega V_0\cos(\omega t) = \omega C V_0\sin\left(\omega t + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) \] \[ I_0 = \frac{V_0}{1/(\omega C)} = \frac{V_0}{X_C}, \quad X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C} \]

The current leads the voltage by 90°. The opposition \( X_C = 1/(\omega C) \) is called capacitive reactance (unit: ohm).

  • \( X_C \propto 1/\omega \) — at higher frequency, capacitor opposes less (charges and discharges faster).
  • At DC (\( \omega = 0 \)): \( X_C \to \infty \) — the capacitor is an open circuit (blocks DC).
  • As \( \omega \to \infty \): \( X_C \to 0 \) — the capacitor is a short circuit.
A capacitor blocks DC but passes high-frequency AC — the classic high-pass filter element. It is complementary to the inductor.

Comparison of R, L, C in AC

Resistor \( R \) Resistance \( R \) In phase None
