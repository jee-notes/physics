Series RLC Circuit

Combining resistance, inductance, and capacitance — impedance, phase angle, and resonance.

Setup

A series RLC circuit has a resistor \( R \), inductor \( L \), and capacitor \( C \) connected in series with an AC source \( v = V_0\sin(\omega t) \). The same current \( i \) flows through all three elements.

The instantaneous voltages across each element are:

\[ v_R = iR, \quad v_L = L\frac{di}{dt}, \quad v_C = \frac{1}{C}\int i\,dt \]

Their sum equals the source voltage. Since \( v_R \), \( v_L \), and \( v_C \) are not in phase with each other, we cannot simply add their magnitudes — we must add them as phasors.

Phasor Analysis

Taking current \( i = I_0\sin(\omega t) \) as reference phasor:

  • \( V_R = I_0 R \) — in phase with current.
  • \( V_L = I_0 X_L = I_0\omega L \) — leads current by \( 90° \).
  • \( V_C = I_0 X_C = I_0/(\omega C) \) — lags current by \( 90° \).

Since \( V_L \) and \( V_C \) are in opposite directions, the net reactive phasor has magnitude \( |V_L - V_C| \). The resultant source voltage phasor:

\[ V_0 = \sqrt{V_R^2 + (V_L - V_C)^2} = \sqrt{(I_0 R)^2 + (I_0 X_L - I_0 X_C)^2} \] \[ \boxed{V_0 = I_0 Z, \quad Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}} \]

Impedance and Phase Angle

The impedance \( Z \) (in ohms) is the effective AC resistance of the series combination:

\[ Z = \sqrt{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2} \]

The phase angle \( \phi \) is the angle by which the voltage leads the current:

\[ \tan\phi = \frac{X_L - X_C}{R} = \frac{\omega L - 1/(\omega C)}{R} \]
  • \( \phi > 0 \): \( X_L > X_C \) — circuit is inductive; voltage leads current.
  • \( \phi < 0 \): \( X_C> X_L \) — circuit is capacitive; voltage lags current.
  • \( \phi = 0 \): \( X_L = X_C \) — circuit is resistive; resonance.
The impedance triangle has \( R \) as the base, \( (X_L - X_C) \) as the perpendicular, and \( Z \) as the hypotenuse — exactly like a right-angle triangle with \( \phi \) as the angle at the base.

Instantaneous Current

The current in the series RLC circuit driven by \( v = V_0\sin(\omega t) \) is:

\[ i(t) = \frac{V_0}{Z}\sin(\omega t - \phi) = I_0\sin(\omega t - \phi) \]

where \( I_0 = V_0/Z \) and \( \phi = \arctan\!\left(\frac{X_L - X_C}{R}\right) \). For an inductive circuit (\( \phi > 0 \)), the current lags behind the voltage.
