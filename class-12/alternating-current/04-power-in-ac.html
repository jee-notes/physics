Power in AC Circuits

Instantaneous, average, and reactive power — and the crucial role of power factor.

Instantaneous Power

If voltage and current are \( v = V_0\sin(\omega t) \) and \( i = I_0\sin(\omega t - \phi) \), the instantaneous power is:

\[ p(t) = v\cdot i = V_0 I_0\sin(\omega t)\sin(\omega t - \phi) \]

Using the product-to-sum identity:

\[ p(t) = \frac{V_0 I_0}{2}[\cos\phi - \cos(2\omega t - \phi)] \]

This oscillates at twice the supply frequency. The second term averages to zero over a full cycle.

Average Power

The average of the oscillating term over a cycle is zero. The average power is:

\[ P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{V_0 I_0}{2}\cos\phi = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi \] \[ \boxed{P = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi} \]

where \( \phi \) is the phase angle between voltage and current.

The factor \( \cos\phi \) is called the power factor. It is dimensionless and lies between 0 and 1. Only the resistive part of the circuit dissipates energy — purely reactive elements (ideal L and C) consume zero average power.

Power Factor

The power factor \( \cos\phi \) indicates what fraction of the apparent power is actually consumed:

\[ \cos\phi = \frac{R}{Z} \]
  • Pure resistance (\( \phi = 0 \)): \( \cos\phi = 1 \) — all apparent power is consumed.
  • Pure inductance or capacitance (\( \phi = \pm 90° \)): \( \cos\phi = 0 \) — zero average power dissipated.
  • Series RLC at resonance (\( \phi = 0 \)): \( \cos\phi = 1 \) — maximum power, purely resistive.

For a general series RLC circuit, since \( Z\cos\phi = R \):

\[ P = I_{\text{rms}}^2 R = I_{\text{rms}}^2 Z\cos\phi = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi \]

All three forms are equivalent and useful depending on what is given.

Apparent, Active, and Reactive Power

Type Symbol Formula Unit Meaning
Apparent power \( S \) \( V_{\text{rms}}I_{\text{rms}} \) VA Total power supplied by source
Active (real) power \( P \) \( S\cos\phi \) W Actually consumed (heat, work)
Reactive power \( Q \) \( S\sin\phi \) VAR Exchanged with L and C (not consumed)

These form a right-angle power triangle: \( S^2 = P^2 + Q^2 \).

Wattless Current

The total current \( I_{\text{rms}} \) can be split into two components:

\[ I_{\text{rms}} = \underbrace{I_{\text{rms}}\cos\phi}_{\text{active (power) component}} + \underbrace{I_{\text{rms}}\sin\phi}_{\text{reactive (wattless) component}} \]

The wattless component \( I_{\text{rms}}\sin\phi \) is 90° out of phase with the voltage — it carries no average power. For a purely inductive or capacitive load, the entire current is wattless.
