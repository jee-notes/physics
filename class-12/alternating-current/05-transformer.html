Transformers and AC Transmission

Stepping voltage up and down — and why high voltage is essential for long-distance power transmission.

Principle of the Transformer

A transformer is a device that transfers AC electrical energy between two circuits at different voltage (and current) levels, using mutual induction. It consists of:

  • Primary coil: \( N_p \) turns, connected to the AC input voltage.
  • Secondary coil: \( N_s \) turns, connected to the load.
  • Soft iron core: Channels the magnetic flux between the coils.

The changing current in the primary creates a changing magnetic flux in the core. By Faraday's Law, this induces an EMF in the secondary.

Ideal Transformer Equations

For an ideal transformer (no flux leakage, no resistance losses):

The same flux \( \Phi \) links both coils. The EMFs are:

\[ \mathcal{E}_p = N_p\frac{d\Phi}{dt}, \qquad \mathcal{E}_s = N_s\frac{d\Phi}{dt} \] \[ \frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} = n \quad \text{(turns ratio)} \]

By conservation of energy (input power = output power for an ideal transformer):

\[ V_p I_p = V_s I_s \implies \frac{I_s}{I_p} = \frac{V_p}{V_s} = \frac{N_p}{N_s} \] \[ \boxed{\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}, \qquad \frac{I_s}{I_p} = \frac{N_p}{N_s}} \]
  • Step-up transformer: \( N_s > N_p \) — output voltage higher, output current lower.
  • Step-down transformer: \( N_s < N_p \) — output voltage lower, output current higher.
A transformer does not create energy — it trades voltage for current (or vice versa) while keeping power constant. The ratio \( V_p I_p = V_s I_s \) is the statement of power conservation across the transformer.

Energy Losses in Real Transformers

Real transformers have losses that reduce efficiency below 100%:

  • Eddy current losses (iron loss): Circulating currents induced in the core. Reduced by using a laminated soft iron core (thin sheets insulated from each other).
  • Hysteresis loss: Energy dissipated in repeatedly magnetising and demagnetising the core with alternating current. Minimised by using soft iron (low hysteresis area in B-H loop).
  • Copper loss (resistive loss): \( I^2R \) heating in the primary and secondary windings. Reduced by using thick copper wire.
  • Flux leakage: Some flux does not link both coils. Minimised by winding the coils on the same limb and using a closed-core geometry.

Efficiency of a transformer: \( \eta = P_s/P_p \times 100\% \). Power transformers typically achieve 95–99% efficiency.

Why Use High Voltage for Transmission?

Electrical power is transmitted from generating stations to consumers over long distances through transmission lines. These lines have a resistance \( R_{\text{line}} \). The power dissipated in the line is:

\[ P_{\text{loss}} = I^2 R_{\text{line}} \]

Since power transmitted \( P = VI \), for the same power:

\[ I = \frac{P}{V} \]

At higher voltage, the current is lower, and the line loss \( P_{\text{loss}} = I^2 R_{\text{line}} = P^2 R_{\text{line}} / V^2 \) is drastically reduced.

Doubling the transmission voltage reduces line losses by a factor of 4! India transmits power at 400 kV or higher — step-up transformers at the generating station take 11 kV to 400 kV, and step-down transformers at substations reduce it for local use.
