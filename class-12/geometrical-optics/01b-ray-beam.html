Ray vs Beam

Beam is system of Rays satisfying geometric constraint.

Beam as a System of Rays

A beam is a system of rays that satisfy a common geometrical constraint.

Thus, not every collection of rays forms a beam. The rays must be related by a consistent geometric relation such as parallelism or intersection at a point.

For a system of exactly two rays:

  • Two non-parallel coplanar rays may not always form a beam, even if they will always intersect (possibly upon extension). This is because, concurrency as well as direction \( \to \) both are critical for constraint.
  • Two parallel rays form a beam only if they propagate in the same direction.
  • Two non-coplanar rays (skew lines in three dimensions) do not form a beam, as no single geometric constraint can be imposed on them.

For systems of more than two rays, all rays must satisfy the same geometric constraint for the collection to qualify as a beam.

Geometric Constraint

The geometric constraint is that a beam can only be one of the following three types:

  • Parallel Beam: Rays are parallel to each other and propagate in the same direction.
  • Converging Beam: Rays are directed toward a single point (concurrent), possibly upon extension.
  • Diverging Beam: Rays are directed away from a single point (concurrent), possibly upon extension.

This classification is based on the angular relationship between the rays. For a beam, this angular structure must remain consistent across the entire system.

Rays are described as converging or diverging based on their geometric behavior.
A beam, being a system of rays, can be associated with a point of origin or termination as a geometric construct.
A converging beam is associated with a point of termination, while a diverging beam is associated with a point of origin.

Examples of Ray–Beam Terminology

Example 1:

Two non-parallel coplanar rays form a beam only when their directions are consistent with convergence or divergence.

Solution

Ray AA′ and Ray BB′ form a converging beam. Even though they do not physically meet, they can be extended to intersect at point O.

At point O, the converging beam terminates. Beyond O, Ray OA″ and Ray OB″ form a new diverging beam originating from O.

Thus, the point of convergence of one beam becomes the point of origin of the next beam.

Question: Can Ray AA′ and Ray OB″ together form a beam?

Answer: No. These two rays do not constitute a beam even if they meet at O. Its because the direction sense with respect to point of intersection is different.
