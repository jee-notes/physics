Image Regions & Graphical Analysis

A systematic study of object-image movement and graphical \( u \) vs \( v \) relation .

Object–Image Regions

For a concave mirror (\(f \lt 0\)), the principal axis is divided into critical zones by the Focus (\(F\)) and the Center of Curvature (\(C\)). Understanding these regions allows for an immediate qualitative assessment of the image.

It is considered useful to perform zonal analysis. Each zone/region corresponds to a qualitatively different ray behavior.

  1. At infinity: Incident rays are parallel.
  2. Between infinity and center of curvature (C): Rays are converging toward the mirror.
  3. At center of curvature (C): Rays are symmetrically incident; normal passes through C.
  4. Between C and focus (F): Rays are strongly converging.
  5. At focus (F): Reflected rays become parallel.
  6. Between F and pole (P): Rays are diverging after reflection.
  7. At pole (P): Object is extremely close; paraxial approximation breaks down.

Each of these regions leads to a distinct image formation scenario, which can be systematically analyzed using either ray diagrams or the mirror formula.

The "Speed" Observation: Note that as the object moves from \( -\infty \) to \( -F \), the image moves from \( -F \) to \( -\infty \). The image "meets" the object at \( -C \). Between \( -C \) and \( -F \), the image moves faster than the object.

The focus (F) is the critical boundary:
  • For object beyond F → reflected rays converge → real image
  • For object between F and P → reflected rays diverge → virtual image

In a convex mirror, reflected rays always diverge. Hence, all images are virtual and erect, regardless of object position.

Graphical Representation: \( \frac{1}{v} \) vs \( \frac{1}{u} \)

The mirror formula is given by: \[ \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \] By rearranging this into the slope-intercept form \( y = mx + c \), where \( y = \frac{1}{v} \) and \( x = \frac{1}{u} \): \[ \frac{1}{v} = -1\left(\frac{1}{u}\right) + \frac{1}{f} \]

  • Slope: Always \( -1 \) (at a \( 135^\circ \) angle to the x-axis).
  • Intercepts: Both intercepts are equal.
For convex mirror, both intercepts are equal to \(+\frac{1}{f}\).
For concave mirror, both intercepts are equal to \(-\frac{1}{f}\).

Physical Interpretation of the Graph

The graph maps input ray behavior (\(1/u\)) to output ray behavior (\(1/v\)). The quadrant determines whether the object and image are real or virtual.

Important Limitation:
The transformation \( u \to \frac{1}{u} \) compresses infinity into a single point:
  • \( u \to -\infty \Rightarrow \frac{1}{u} \to 0^- \)
  • \( u \to +\infty \Rightarrow \frac{1}{u} \to 0^+ \)
Thus, physically distinct cases (real object at \(-\infty\) and virtual object at \(+\infty\)) appear at the same location on the graph.
