Impact of Reflection on Vectors

Vector Reflection

Reflection can be understood as a transformation of a vector across a surface.

Any vector can be decomposed into two components:

  • Parallel (tangential) component: Along the surface
  • Perpendicular (normal) component: Along the normal to the surface

During reflection:

  • The parallel component remains unchanged
  • The normal component reverses direction
Reflection reverses the normal component of a vector while preserving the tangential component.

If \(\vec{i}\) is the incident vector and \(\hat{n}\) is the unit normal, then:

\[ \vec{r} = \vec{i} - 2(\vec{i} \cdot \hat{n})\hat{n} \]

2D Example

Let the incident vector be: \[ \vec{i} = a\hat{i} + b\hat{j} \] and mirror along the X-axis

As it is given that mirror is along X axis - normal vector is either \(\hat{j}\) or \(-\hat{j}\). But as the incident ray is moving upwards (assuming 'b' > 0), normal has to be along \(-\hat{j}\). Therefore, \( \vec{n} = -\hat{j}\).

We learn how to solve this geometrically. Component of \(\vec{i}\) along tangent plane = \(a\hat{i}\). Component of \(\vec{i}\) along normal = \(b\hat{j}\)

We use geometric transormation logic. Component of \(\vec{r}\) along tangent plane = Component of \(\vec{i}\) along tangent plane = \(a\hat{i}\).

Component of \(\vec{r}\) along normal = - Component of \(\vec{i}\) along normal = \(-b\hat{j}\).

\[ \vec{r} = a\hat{i} - b\hat{j} \]

Thus, the x-component remains unchanged while the y-component reverses.

3D Example

Let the incident vector be: \[ \vec{i} = a\hat{i} + b\hat{j} + c\hat{k} \] and mirror along the XY-plane.

As it is given that mirror is along XY plane - normal vector is either \(\hat{k}\) or \(-\hat{k}\). But as the incident ray is moving outwards (assuming 'c' > 0), normal has to be along \(-\hat{k}\). Therefore, \( \vec{n} = -\hat{k}\).

We learn how to solve this geometrically. Component of \(\vec{i}\) along tangent plane = \(a\hat{i} + b\hat{j}\). Component of \(\vec{i}\) along normal = \(c\hat{k}\)

We use geometric transormation logic. Component of \(\vec{r}\) along tangent plane = Component of \(\vec{i}\) along tangent plane = \(a\hat{i} + b\hat{j}\).

Component of \(\vec{r}\) along normal = - Component of \(\vec{i}\) along normal = \(-c\hat{k}\).

\[ \vec{r} = a\hat{i} + b\hat{j} - c\hat{k} \]

Thus, the z-component reverses while x and y components remain unchanged.

In 2D - consecutive reflection from 2 mutually perpendicular mirrors would reverse the direction of incident ray vector.
In 3D - consecutive reflection from 3 mutually perpendicular mirrors would reverse the direction of incident ray vector.
In 3D - the mirror arrangement is similar to corner of a cube and is called as corner reflector.
