Mirror Velocity

Object Image Velocity Relation for Spherical Mirrors.

Velocity of Image in Spherical Mirrors

If an object moves along the principal axis of a spherical mirror with velocity \(v_o = \frac{du}{dt}\), the image will also move with velocity \(v_i = \frac{dv}{dt}\). We can find the relationship by differentiating the mirror formula with respect to time.

Starting with the mirror formula: \[ \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \]

Assuming the mirror is stationary (\(f\) is constant, and pole is fixed), differentiating with respect to time \(t\): \[ -\frac{1}{v^2}\frac{dv}{dt} - \frac{1}{u^2}\frac{du}{dt} = 0 \] \[ \frac{dv}{dt} = -\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^2 \frac{du}{dt} \]

Velocity Relation (Along Principal Axis)

\[ \vec{v}_i = -m^2 \vec{v}_o \]

Where:

  • \(\vec{v}_i\) = Velocity of image relative to mirror.
  • \(\vec{v}_o\) = Velocity of object relative to mirror.
  • \(m = -\frac{v}{u}\) = Transverse magnification.
Because the square of any real number (\(m^2\)) is always positive, the negative sign strictly means that the velocity of the image is always opposite to the velocity of the object relative to the mirror when moving along the principal axis. They always move in opposing directions.

Velocity Perpendicular to the Principal Axis

If the object is moving perpendicular to the principal axis, its \(u\) coordinate remains constant, which means \(v\) and \(m\) remain constant. Since \( h_i = m \cdot h_o \), differentiating with respect to time gives: \[ \frac{dh_i}{dt} = m \frac{dh_o}{dt} \] \[ \Rightarrow v_{i,\perp} = m \cdot v_{o,\perp} \] Here, the magnification acts as a simple scale factor for vertical velocity components.
