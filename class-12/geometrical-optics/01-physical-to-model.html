Model of Light

Modeling light as rays to analyze reflection, refraction, and image formation.

Sources of Light

In geometrical optics, light is treated as originating from a source. Sources are classified based on how they emit or reflect light:

  • Self-Luminous: Objects that generate their own light (e.g., Sun, stars, electric bulbs, red-hot iron). Their color depends on the physical mechanism of emission, such as temperature or atomic transitions.
  • Non-Luminous: Objects that do not emit light but become visible by reflecting light from a self-luminous source (e.g., Moon, planets, this page).

The Mechanism of Color

For non-luminous objects, perceived color arises due to selective absorption. An object appears a certain color because it reflects specific wavelengths of light while absorbing others.
Example: Chlorophyll appears green because it reflects green light and absorbs red and blue.

For self-luminous objects, color depends on the emission mechanism.
Example: A red-hot iron emits due to temperature (thermal radiation), while a sodium vapor lamp emits characteristic yellow light due to atomic transitions.

In modeling, sources are idealized as point sources (dimensionless) or extended sources (a system of point sources).
Ray diagrams typically begin by considering light originating from a point on the source.

Rays and Beams

A Ray is a straight line representing the direction of propagation of light energy.

A Beam is a system of rays.

  • Parallel Beam: Rays are parallel to each other. Corresponds to light arriving from a very distant source.
  • Converging Beam: Rays meet at a specific point.
  • Diverging Beam: Rays spread out from a point.

This classification is geometric and depends on the angular spread of the rays in the beam.

Objects and Images

In geometrical optics, whether an object or image is real or virtual depends entirely on the behavior of the incident and emergent rays.

Type Incident Rays (Approaching Optical Element) Emergent Rays (Leaving Optical Element)
Real Diverging from a point Converging to a point
Virtual Converging toward a point Diverging from a point

Key Definitions:

  • Object: The point of actual or apparent intersection of incident rays.
  • Image: The point of actual or apparent intersection of emergent rays.
Rays will actually meet at a point if incident rays diverge and emergent rays converge.
If incident rays converge or emerging rays diverge, then we need to backtrace the rays to make them meet.
The backtracing needs to be done with reference to the optical element.

Ray diagram

A ray diagram is an optical element or a system of optical elements. Rays are incident or emergent with respect to an optical element. For a system of optical elements, emerging rays from one optical element can be incident rays for other. In this case image of one optical element becomes object for next optical element.

Often students incorrectly memorize that "object is to the left" or "images form to the right". Its the behavior of rays that determines everything. For any optical element (in ray diagram), the object is defined by the intersection of incident rays, and the image is defined by the intersection of emergent rays.

Convex Lens L followed by plane mirror M. Light rays from source O fall on convex lens, converge and fall on mirror M.

Solution

For Lens (L):

  • Rays from O are diverging and incident on the lens.
  • Hence, O is a real object for the lens.
  • After refraction, rays are converging .
  • Hence, I₁ is real image for the lens.
  • Even if extended forward, its a real image for Lens as rays are converging after refraction. It doesnt matter where the screen is.
A real image is defined by converging emergent rays, not by whether the rays are physically allowed to meet.

For Mirror (M):

  • Rays incident on the mirror are converging toward I₁.
  • Hence, I₁ is a virtual object for the mirror.
  • After reflection, rays converge to I₂.
  • Hence, I₂ is a real image formed by the mirror.
Our eyes are designed as an optical instrument to see real objects for the human eye.
Hence, even if a plane mirror makes virtual image of real objects, we can see the image as it is real object for human eye.
