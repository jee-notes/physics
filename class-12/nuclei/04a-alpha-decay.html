Alpha Decay

Emission of helium nuclei

Alpha Decay Process

Alpha decay is the process in which an unstable heavy nucleus emits an alpha particle (a helium nucleus, \(_2^4\text{He}\)) to transition into a more stable state. This decay is typical for very heavy nuclei (e.g., Uranium, Thorium) where the strong nuclear force cannot overcome the electrostatic repulsion of the many protons.

When a nucleus undergoes alpha decay, its mass number \(A\) decreases by 4, and its atomic number \(Z\) decreases by 2. The general reaction is written as:

\[ _{Z}^{A}\text{X} \longrightarrow _{Z-2}^{A-4}\text{Y} + _{2}^{4}\text{He} \]

Where \(X\) is the parent nucleus and \(Y\) is the daughter nucleus.

Example:

Decay of Uranium-238 to Thorium-234:

\[ _{92}^{238}\text{U} \longrightarrow _{90}^{234}\text{Th} + _{2}^{4}\text{He} \]

Q-value of Alpha Decay

The \(Q\)-value, or disintegration energy, of a nuclear reaction is the difference in rest mass energy between the initial and final states. It represents the total kinetic energy shared by the products after the decay.

For the alpha decay \(X \rightarrow Y + \alpha\), the \(Q\)-value is calculated as:

\[ Q = [m_X - m_Y - m_\alpha] c^2 \]

Where:

  • \(m_X\) = Mass of the parent nucleus
  • \(m_Y\) = Mass of the daughter nucleus
  • \(m_\alpha\) = Mass of the alpha particle

For alpha decay to occur spontaneously, the \(Q\)-value must be positive (\(Q > 0\)). This implies that the total mass of the products is strictly less than the mass of the parent nucleus. The "missing mass" becomes the kinetic energy of the products.

Kinematics of Alpha Decay

When an alpha particle is emitted from a parent nucleus at rest, conservation of linear momentum requires that the alpha particle and the daughter nucleus move in opposite directions.

Let \(v_\alpha\) and \(v_Y\) be the velocities, and \(K_\alpha\) and \(K_Y\) be the kinetic energies of the alpha particle and daughter nucleus, respectively.

Conservation of Momentum:

\[ 0 = m_Y v_Y - m_\alpha v_\alpha \implies p_Y = p_\alpha \]

Since \(K = p^2 / 2m\), and \(p\) is the same for both:

\[ \frac{K_\alpha}{K_Y} = \frac{m_Y}{m_\alpha} \]

The total kinetic energy is the \(Q\)-value:

\[ Q = K_\alpha + K_Y \]

Solving these equations for the kinetic energy of the alpha particle \(K_\alpha\):

\[ K_\alpha = \frac{m_Y}{m_Y + m_\alpha} Q \approx \frac{A - 4}{A} Q \]

Since \(m_Y \gg m_\alpha\), the alpha particle carries away the vast majority of the released energy (\(Q\)-value). The daughter nucleus gets a small fraction as recoil energy.
