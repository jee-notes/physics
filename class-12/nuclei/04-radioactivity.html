Radioactivity

The spontaneous decay of unstable nuclei

Discovery and Nature of Radioactivity

Radioactivity was accidentally discovered by Henri Becquerel in 1896 while working with uranium salts. Later, Marie and Pierre Curie discovered other radioactive elements like polonium and radium. Radioactivity is a nuclear phenomenon; it is the process by which an unstable atomic nucleus loses energy by spontaneously emitting radiation.

Radioactive decay is completely spontaneous and unaffected by external physical conditions such as temperature, pressure, or chemical combination. It depends solely on the instability of the nucleus itself, typically arising when there is an unfavorable ratio of neutrons to protons.

Properties of Alpha, Beta, and Gamma Rays

When a radioactive substance is placed in a magnetic or electric field, the emitted radiation splits into three distinct components, named alpha (\(\alpha\)), beta (\(\beta\)), and gamma (\(\gamma\)) rays.

Alpha (\(\alpha\)) Rays

  • Nature: They are fast-moving helium nuclei (\(_2^4\text{He}\)), containing 2 protons and 2 neutrons.
  • Charge and Mass: Charge is \(+2e\) and mass is roughly \(4\) u.
  • Penetrating Power: Very low; can be stopped by a piece of paper or a few centimeters of air.
  • Ionizing Power: Very high, roughly 100 times that of \(\beta\)-rays and 10,000 times that of \(\gamma\)-rays, due to their large mass and charge.

Beta (\(\beta\)) Rays

  • Nature: They are fast-moving electrons (\(\beta^-\)) or positrons (\(\beta^+\)) emitted from the nucleus.
  • Charge and Mass: Charge is \(-e\) (or \(+e\)) and mass is equal to the mass of an electron.
  • Penetrating Power: Moderate; can pass through paper but are stopped by a few millimeters of aluminum.
  • Ionizing Power: Moderate, roughly \(1/100^{th}\) of \(\alpha\)-rays.

Gamma (\(\gamma\)) Rays

  • Nature: They are high-energy electromagnetic waves (photons) of very short wavelength.
  • Charge and Mass: They are electrically neutral and have zero rest mass.
  • Penetrating Power: Extremely high; requires several centimeters of lead or meters of concrete to stop them.
  • Ionizing Power: Very low.
