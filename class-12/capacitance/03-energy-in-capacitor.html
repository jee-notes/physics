Energy Stored in a Capacitor

Work done to charge a capacitor is stored as potential energy in the electric field.

Work Done in Charging

When we charge a capacitor, we move charge from one plate to the other against the growing electric field. At any instant when charge \( q \) has already been transferred, the potential difference between the plates is \( v = q/C \). The work needed to transfer an additional element of charge \( dq \) is:

\[ dW = v\, dq = \frac{q}{C}\, dq \]

Integrating from \( q = 0 \) to \( q = Q \):

\[ W = \int_0^Q \frac{q}{C}\, dq = \frac{1}{C} \cdot \frac{Q^2}{2} \] \[ \boxed{U = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2}QV} \]

All three forms are equivalent — use whichever suits the given information.

The factor of \( 1/2 \) appears because the average voltage during charging is \( V_{\text{avg}} = V/2 \). The first charge element is moved against zero potential; the last against full potential \( V \). So \( U = Q \cdot (V/2) \), not \( Q \cdot V \).

Energy Density of the Electric Field

For a parallel plate capacitor (\( C = \varepsilon_0 A/d \), \( V = Ed \)), the stored energy is:

\[ U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} \cdot (Ed)^2 = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 \cdot (Ad) \]

Since \( Ad \) is the volume of the capacitor:

\[ u = \frac{U}{Ad} = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 \]

This is the energy density of the electric field — the energy stored per unit volume. It applies universally, not just inside capacitors.

The energy is not stored in the charges — it is stored in the electric field. This is not merely a bookkeeping convention; the field carries the energy even in free space, far from any charges.

Energy at Constant Charge vs. Constant Voltage

Two important scenarios arise when the geometry of a capacitor is changed (e.g., plates are pulled apart):

Isolated Capacitor (constant Q)

If the capacitor is isolated (not connected to a battery), \( Q \) is fixed. Using \( U = Q^2/(2C) \):

  • Increasing \( d \) decreases \( C \), so \( U \) increases.
  • The extra energy comes from the work done by the external agent pulling the plates apart (against the attractive force).

Capacitor in a Circuit (constant V)

If the capacitor remains connected to a battery, \( V \) is fixed. Using \( U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 \):

  • Increasing \( d \) decreases \( C \), so \( U \) decreases.
  • Charge returns to the battery; the total work done by the external agent is negative (the system does work on the agent).

Energy When Dielectric is Inserted

When a dielectric of constant \( K \) is inserted into a capacitor:

  • Isolated (constant Q): \( C \) increases by \( K \), so \( U = Q^2/(2C) \) decreases by factor \( K \). The electric field and voltage both decrease. Energy is released — it goes into the dielectric (polarisation).
  • Battery-connected (constant V): \( C \) increases by \( K \), so \( U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 \) increases by factor \( K \). Extra charge flows from the battery — the battery does more work than the energy stored (the other half goes into the dielectric and heat).
