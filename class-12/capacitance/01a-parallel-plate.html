Parallel Plate Capacitor

The most important capacitor geometry — and the derivation of its capacitance.

Setup

A parallel plate capacitor consists of two large, flat conducting plates of area \( A \), separated by a distance \( d \) (with \( d \ll \sqrt{A} \) so edge effects are negligible). One plate carries charge \( +Q \) and the other \( -Q \).

The surface charge density on each plate is:

\[ \sigma = \frac{Q}{A} \]

Electric Field Between the Plates

Using Gauss's Law with a pillbox surface straddling one plate, the field due to a single conducting plate with surface charge density \( \sigma \) is \( E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0 \). Two plates of opposite charge reinforce in the gap and cancel outside.

The field between the plates is uniform and directed from positive to negative plate:

\[ E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A} \]

The field outside the capacitor is zero (fields from the two plates exactly cancel).

The field is strictly uniform inside a parallel plate capacitor (in the ideal case). This makes it ideal for theoretical analysis and for practical devices requiring a well-controlled field.

Potential Difference and Capacitance

The potential difference between the plates is:

\[ V = E \cdot d = \frac{Qd}{\varepsilon_0 A} \]

Therefore the capacitance is:

\[ C = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{Q}{\displaystyle\frac{Qd}{\varepsilon_0 A}} \] \[ \boxed{C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}} \]

Key observations:

  • \( C \) increases with plate area \( A \) — more plate area allows more charge at the same voltage.
  • \( C \) decreases with separation \( d \) — a wider gap means a lower field for the same charge, so more charge is needed to reach a given \( V \).
  • \( C \) is independent of \( Q \) and \( V \) — it is a purely geometric property.

Effect of a Conducting Slab Between the Plates

If a conducting slab of thickness \( t \) (where \( t < d \)) is inserted between the plates, the field inside the conductor is zero, and the effective separation is reduced to \( (d - t) \):

\[ C' = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - t} \]

The capacitance increases — the conducting slab effectively shortens the gap. In particular, if the slab fills the gap completely (\( t = d \)), the capacitance becomes infinite (the two plates are directly connected — not really a capacitor anymore).

Force Between the Plates

The field acting on one plate is only due to the other plate, which has field \( E/2 = \sigma/(2\varepsilon_0) \). The electrostatic force on plate of area \( A \) with charge \( Q \) is:

\[ F = Q \cdot \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q^2}{2\varepsilon_0 A} = \frac{\sigma^2 A}{2\varepsilon_0} \]

This is an attractive force (the plates attract each other). It can also be derived as the negative gradient of stored energy with respect to separation:

\[ F = -\frac{dU}{dd}\bigg|_Q = -\frac{d}{dd}\left(\frac{Q^2 d}{2\varepsilon_0 A}\right) = -\frac{Q^2}{2\varepsilon_0 A} \]

The negative sign confirms attraction.
