Maxwell's Equations

The four equations that unify electricity and magnetism.

Maxwell's Four Equations

Maxwell synthesised all of classical electromagnetism into four elegant equations (integral form):

1. Gauss's Law for Electricity

\[ \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0} \]

Electric field lines originate from positive charges and terminate at negative charges.

The total electric flux through any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by \( \varepsilon_0 \).

2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism

\[ \oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0 \]

Magnetic field lines form closed loops — there are no magnetic monopoles.

The net magnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero.

3. Faraday's Law of Induction

\[ \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{l} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} \]

A changing magnetic flux induces an electric field (even in empty space).

This is the basis of electromagnetic induction and the electric generator.

4. Ampere's Law (Modified by Maxwell)

\[ \oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0\left(I + \varepsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}\right) \]

A current or a changing electric flux produces a magnetic field.

Maxwell's addition of the \(\varepsilon_0\,d\Phi_E/dt \) term (displacement current) was the key that made the equations consistent and predicted EM waves.

Equations 3 and 4 taken together say: a changing \( \vec{B} \) creates \( \vec{E} \), and a changing \( \vec{E} \) creates \( \vec{B} \).
This mutual generation is what sustains a self-propagating electromagnetic wave.

Symmetry of Maxwell's Equations

Equation Physical Meaning
Gauss (E) Charges are sources of \( \vec{E} \)
Gauss (B) No magnetic monopoles; \( \vec{B} \) lines always close
Faraday Changing \( \vec{B} \) → induced \( \vec{E} \)
Ampere–Maxwell Current or changing \( \vec{E} \) → \( \vec{B} \)

The asymmetry between equations 1 and 2 (charges exist, monopoles don't) and between 3 and 4 (Faraday has no current source term on the right) reflects the observed asymmetry of nature.

If magnetic monopoles existed, all four equations would be perfectly symmetric.
