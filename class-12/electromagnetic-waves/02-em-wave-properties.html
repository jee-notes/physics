Nature and Properties of EM Waves

What electromagnetic waves are — how they travel, carry energy, and exert pressure.

Structure of an EM Wave

An electromagnetic wave consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields, perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation. For a plane wave travelling in the \( +x \) direction:

\[ \vec{E} = E_0\sin(kx - \omega t)\,\hat{j} \] \[ \vec{B} = B_0\sin(kx - \omega t)\,\hat{k} \]

where \( k = 2\pi/\lambda \) is the wave number and \( \omega = 2\pi f \) is the angular frequency. The three directions \( \hat{i} \) (propagation), \( \hat{j} \) (\( \vec{E} \)), and \( \hat{k} \) (\( \vec{B} \)) are mutually perpendicular, forming a right-handed coordinate system.

The direction of propagation of an EM wave is along \( \vec{E} \times \vec{B} \). This cross product also defines the direction of energy flow — captured mathematically by the Poynting vector.

Key Properties

1. Transverse Nature

EM waves are transverse waves — the oscillating fields are perpendicular to the direction of travel. Unlike sound (longitudinal), EM waves can be polarised.

2. Speed in Free Space

\[ c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}} = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s} \]

All EM waves travel at the same speed \( c \) in vacuum, regardless of frequency. In a medium with refractive index \( n \):

\[ v = \frac{c}{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}} \]

3. Ratio of E and B Amplitudes

From Maxwell's equations, the amplitudes are related by:

\[ \frac{E_0}{B_0} = c \quad \Rightarrow \quad E_0 = cB_0 \]

At every instant, \( E/B = c \). The electric and magnetic fields are always in phase (they peak and zero simultaneously).

4. No Medium Required

EM waves are self-sustaining oscillations of field — they travel through vacuum. This distinguished them from all previously known waves (mechanical waves require a medium). Speed in vacuum is maximum and equals \( c \).

Energy Carried by EM Waves

The energy densities stored in the electric and magnetic fields of an EM wave are:

\[ u_E = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2, \qquad u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} \]

Since \( E = cB \) and \( c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} \):

\[ u_E = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 c^2 B^2 = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} = u_B \]

The electric and magnetic fields carry equal amounts of energy. The total energy density:

\[ u = u_E + u_B = \varepsilon_0 E^2 = \frac{B^2}{\mu_0} \]

Intensity and Poynting Vector

The Poynting vector \( \vec{S} \) represents the energy flux — energy per unit time per unit area flowing in the direction of the wave:

\[ \vec{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\vec{E} \times \vec{B}) \]

Its magnitude gives the instantaneous intensity. The average intensity (over a complete cycle) is:

\[ I = \langle S \rangle = \frac{E_0 B_0}{2\mu_0} = \frac{E_0^2}{2\mu_0 c} = \frac{c\varepsilon_0 E_0^2}{2} = \frac{c B_0^2}{2\mu_0} \]

In terms of RMS values \( E_{\text{rms}} = E_0/\sqrt{2} \):

\[ I = c\varepsilon_0 E_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{c B_{\text{rms}}^2}{\mu_0} \]

Radiation Pressure and Momentum

EM waves carry momentum, and when absorbed by a surface, they exert a radiation pressure. For a wave of intensity \( I \):

\[ P_{\text{rad}} = \frac{I}{c} \quad (\text{perfectly absorbing surface}) \] \[ P_{\text{rad}} = \frac{2I}{c} \quad (\text{perfectly reflecting surface}) \]

The momentum of an EM wave with energy \( U \) is:

\[ p = \frac{U}{c} \]
Radiation pressure is tiny under normal conditions (sunlight exerts about \( 4.7 \times 10^{-6} \) Pa), but it is measurable and has been experimentally confirmed. It is significant in stellar astrophysics — radiation pressure can balance the gravitational collapse of very massive stars.

Summary of Key Relations

Quantity Expression
Speed in vacuum \( c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} = 3\times10^8 \) m/s
Amplitude ratio \( E_0/B_0 = c \)
Equal energy densities \( u_E = u_B \)
Average intensity \( I = c\varepsilon_0 E_{\text{rms}}^2 \)
Radiation pressure (absorbed) \( P = I/c \)
Momentum of wave \( p = U/c \)
