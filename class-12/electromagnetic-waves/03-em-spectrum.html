The Electromagnetic Spectrum

A complete family — from radio waves kilometres long to gamma rays a trillion times smaller than a millimetre.

Overview

All electromagnetic waves travel at \( c = 3\times10^8 \) m/s in vacuum and obey:

\[ c = f\lambda \]

They differ only in frequency (and hence wavelength). The electromagnetic spectrum is the ordered arrangement of all EM waves by frequency (or wavelength). Despite their vastly different properties and uses, they all share the same fundamental wave nature.

Radio Waves

Wavelength: \( >0.1 \) m  |  Frequency: \( <3\times10^9 \) Hz

Source: Accelerating electrons in antennas (oscillating LC circuits driving antenna).

Properties and Uses:

  • AM (amplitude modulation) radio: 530 kHz – 1.7 MHz. Reflects off the ionosphere — long-range communication.
  • FM radio: 88–108 MHz. Travels in straight lines (line of sight) — better audio quality.
  • Microwaves: 300 MHz – 300 GHz (wavelength 1 mm – 30 cm). Used in radar, satellite communications, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and microwave ovens (2.45 GHz resonates water molecules to heat food).
  • TV signals: 54 MHz – 890 MHz.

Infrared (IR) Waves

Wavelength: \( 700 \) nm – \( 1 \) mm  |  Frequency: \( 3\times10^{11} \) – \( 4\times10^{14} \) Hz

Source: All warm objects emit IR radiation (thermal radiation). Hotter objects emit more and at shorter wavelengths.

Uses: Remote controls, night-vision devices, heat lamps, infrared spectroscopy, thermal imaging cameras, optical fibre communication (near-IR).

The human body at 37 °C radiates primarily in the infrared range (peak near 9–10 µm). Thermal cameras detect this radiation to produce images based on temperature differences.

Visible Light

Wavelength: \( 400 \) nm – \( 700 \) nm  |  Frequency: \( 4\times10^{14} \) – \( 7.5\times10^{14} \) Hz

Source: Electron transitions in atoms; incandescent objects at \( >3000 \) K.

The narrow band of EM radiation visible to the human eye:

  • Violet: \(\approx 380\)–450 nm (highest frequency in visible)
  • Blue: \(\approx 450\)–495 nm
  • Green: \(\approx 495\)–570 nm
  • Yellow: \(\approx 570\)–590 nm
  • Orange: \(\approx 590\)–620 nm
  • Red: \(\approx 620\)–700 nm (lowest frequency in visible)

Ultraviolet (UV) Waves

Wavelength: \( 10 \) nm – \( 400 \) nm  |  Frequency: \( 7.5\times10^{14} \) – \( 3\times10^{16} \) Hz

Source: Very hot objects (surface of the Sun at \(\sim 5800\) K), sparks, mercury lamps.

Uses and Effects: Sterilisation (kills bacteria and viruses), fluorescence, UV spectroscopy. Causes sunburn; the ozone layer absorbs most solar UV before it reaches Earth's surface. High-energy UV can break molecular bonds.

X-Rays

Wavelength: \( 0.01 \) nm – \( 10 \) nm  |  Frequency: \( 3\times10^{16} \) – \( 3\times10^{19} \) Hz

Source: Rapid deceleration of high-energy electrons hitting a metal target (bremsstrahlung); electron transitions in inner shells of heavy atoms.

Uses: Medical imaging (X-ray photographs, CT scans — bones absorb X-rays strongly), crystallography (X-ray diffraction reveals crystal structure), airport security scanners.

Gamma (\( \gamma \)) Rays

Wavelength: \( <0.01 \) nm  |  Frequency: \( >3\times10^{19} \) Hz

Source: Nuclear transitions — emitted from radioactive nuclei during decay; also from cosmic processes (neutron stars, black holes, gamma-ray bursts).

Uses: Killing cancer cells (radiotherapy), food irradiation for sterilisation, industrial radiography, PET scans (positron annihilation produces 511 keV gamma pairs).

Properties: Highest energy, most penetrating EM radiation. Ionises atoms — biologically damaging. Requires lead or thick concrete for shielding.

The EM Spectrum — Summary Table

Type Wavelength Frequency (Hz) Key Use
Radio / Microwave \( >1 \) mm \( <3\times10^{11} \) Communication, radar, ovens
Infrared 700 nm – 1 mm \( 3\times10^{11} \)–\( 4\times10^{14} \) Thermal imaging, remote controls
Visible 400–700 nm \( 4\text{–}7.5\times10^{14} \) Vision, photography
Ultraviolet 10–400 nm \( 7.5\times10^{14} \)–\( 3\times10^{16} \) Sterilisation, fluorescence
X-Rays 0.01–10 nm \( 3\times10^{16} \)–\( 3\times10^{19} \) Medical imaging, crystallography

Note: Boundaries between types are not sharp — the spectrum is continuous. Classification is based on source and typical application, not strict frequency cutoffs.
