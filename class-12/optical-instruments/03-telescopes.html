Telescopes

Bridging cosmic distances by gathering light and increasing visual angle.

Introduction to Telescopes

Unlike microscopes, which magnify tiny nearby objects, telescopes are designed to observe large, very distant objects like planets, stars, and galaxies. The objects are already large, but because of their vast distance, they subtend a very small visual angle at the eye, appearing tiny.

A telescope's primary job is to gather as much light as possible from these distant objects and form an image that subtends a larger visual angle at the eye than the object itself does.

In a microscope, the objective lens has a short focal length. In a telescope, the objective must have a very large focal length and aperture to gather maximum light and provide high angular magnification.

Types of Telescopes

Telescopes are broadly classified into two categories based on their objective element:

  • Refracting Telescopes: Use a combination of lenses (refraction) to form the image. The most common type is the Astronomical Telescope.
  • Reflecting Telescopes: Use a large curved mirror (reflection) as the primary objective to gather light and form the image. Examples include Newtonian and Cassegrain telescopes.
