Astronomical Telescope

The standard refracting design for celestial observation.

Refracting Type Telescope

An astronomical telescope consists of two convex lenses mounted coaxially in a tube:

  • Objective Lens: Has a large focal length (\( f_o \)) and a large aperture. Its large aperture allows it to gather sufficient light from faint astronomical objects. It forms a real, inverted, and diminished image of the distant object at its focal plane.
  • Eyepiece: Has a short focal length (\( f_e \)) and a small aperture. It is positioned such that the intermediate image formed by the objective falls within its focal length, acting as a simple microscope to magnify the intermediate image.
The final image is inverted with respect to the original object. Since astronomical bodies like stars and planets are mostly spherical, an inverted image does not affect observations.

Magnifying Power in Normal Adjustment

Magnifying power (\( m \)) is the ratio of the angle subtended by the image at the eye (\( \beta \)) to the angle subtended by the object at the unaided eye (\( \alpha \)).

In Normal Adjustment, the final image is formed at infinity. This happens when the intermediate image formed by the objective lies exactly at the focal point of the eyepiece. Thus, the distance between the two lenses is \( f_o + f_e \).

\[ m = \frac{\beta}{\alpha} = \frac{f_o}{f_e} \] To achieve high magnification, the objective should have a very large focal length and the eyepiece should have a very short focal length.

Note: If the final image is formed at the least distance of distinct vision (\( D \)), the magnifying power is slightly higher: \( m = \frac{f_o}{f_e} \left(1 + \frac{f_e}{D}\right) \).

Length of Telescope Tube

The length of the telescope tube (\( L \)) is the distance between the objective lens and the eyepiece.

For normal adjustment (final image at infinity):
\[ L = f_o + f_e \]
For final image at least distance of distinct vision (\( D \)):
\[ L = f_o + u_e \] where \( u_e \) is the object distance for the eyepiece, calculated from \( \frac{1}{-D} - \frac{1}{-u_e} = \frac{1}{f_e} \).
