The Human Eye

The biological optical instrument.

Structure and Function of the Eye

The human eye is remarkably similar to a photographic camera. It consists of a converging lens system that forms a real, inverted image on a light-sensitive screen called the retina. Light enters the eye through a transparent curved membrane called the cornea. Behind the cornea is a dark muscular diaphragm called the iris, which controls the size of the pupil, thereby regulating the amount of light entering the eye.

The eye lens is a crystalline, fibrous, jelly-like material. Unlike a glass lens, the focal length of the eye lens is not fixed. It is controlled by the ciliary muscles.

Although the image formed on the retina is inverted, our brain processes these signals and interprets the image as erect.

Convex converging lens with fixed image distance on retina screen and variable focal length

For the human eye, the image distance (\( v \)) is strictly fixed. It is the distance between the eye lens and the retina, approximately \( 2.5 \text{ cm} \).

The thin lens formula is given by: \[ \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} \] Where:

  • \( f \) is the focal length of the eye lens.
  • \( v \) is the image distance (fixed).
  • \( u \) is the object distance (variable).

Since \( v \) is constant, to see objects at different distances (\( u \)), the focal length \( f \) must change. When looking at distant objects (\( u \to -\infty \)), the ciliary muscles are relaxed, the lens is thin, and the focal length is maximum (\( f_{\text{max}} \approx v \)). When looking at nearby objects, the ciliary muscles contract, the lens becomes thicker (more convex), and its focal length decreases.

Accommodation and Near/Far Points

The ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length to see objects at different distances clearly is called accommodation.

Near Point (Least Distance of Distinct Vision, \( D \)): The minimum distance at which an object can be seen most distinctly without strain. For a normal adult eye, \( D \approx 25 \text{ cm} \).

Far Point: The maximum distance up to which the normal eye can see objects clearly. For a normal eye, the far point is infinity.

The difference between the maximum and minimum focal lengths represents the power of accommodation. As a person ages, the ciliary muscles often weaken and the crystalline lens loses some flexibility, reducing this power.

Defects of Vision and Corrections

When the eye loses its power of accommodation or when the shape of the eyeball changes, it cannot form sharp images on the retina. These conditions are known as defects of vision. They are corrected using external lenses.
