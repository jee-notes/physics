Compound Microscope

Achieving high magnification through a two-lens system.

Ray Diagram and Construction

To achieve much higher magnification than a simple microscope, a compound microscope uses two convex lenses:

  • Objective Lens: The lens facing the object. It has a very short focal length (\( f_o \)) and a small aperture. It forms a real, inverted, and magnified intermediate image of the object.
  • Eyepiece (Ocular) Lens: The lens close to the eye. It has a slightly larger focal length (\( f_e \)) and larger aperture than the objective. It acts as a simple microscope, taking the intermediate image as its object and forming a highly magnified, virtual final image.
The final image in a compound microscope is inverted with respect to the original object. For biological observations, this inversion is usually acceptable.

Magnifying Power Calculations

The total magnifying power \( M \) is the product of the linear magnification of the objective (\( m_o \)) and the angular magnification of the eyepiece (\( m_e \)): \[ M = m_o \times m_e \] The intermediate image formed by the objective is at distance \( v_o \), and the object is at \( u_o \). Thus, \( m_o = \frac{v_o}{|u_o|} \). If the intermediate image is formed close to the eyepiece, \( v_o \) is approximately equal to the tube length \( L \) (the distance between the focal point of the objective and the focal point of the eyepiece). Since \( u_o \approx f_o \), we can write \( m_o \approx \frac{L}{f_o} \).

Case 1: Final Image at Least Distance of Distinct Vision (\( v_e = -D \))

\[ M = \left(\frac{v_o}{u_o}\right) \left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right) \approx \left(\frac{L}{f_o}\right) \left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right) \]

Case 2: Final Image at Infinity (Normal Adjustment)

\[ M = \left(\frac{v_o}{u_o}\right) \left(\frac{D}{f_e}\right) \approx \left(\frac{L}{f_o}\right) \left(\frac{D}{f_e}\right) \]

Resolving Power of a Microscope

The resolving power of a microscope is its ability to separate the images of two closely spaced point objects. It is the reciprocal of the minimum limit of resolution (\( \Delta d \)).

\[ \text{Resolving Power} = \frac{1}{\Delta d} = \frac{2 n \sin \theta}{\lambda} \] Where:
  • \( \lambda \) is the wavelength of light used.
  • \( n \) is the refractive index of the medium between the object and the objective lens.
  • \( \theta \) is the half-angle of the cone of light entering the objective from the object.
The term \( n \sin \theta \) is called the Numerical Aperture (NA) of the objective lens.
To increase the resolving power, we can use a medium of higher refractive index (like oil immersion), use light of shorter wavelength (like blue light or UV), or increase the aperture of the objective.
