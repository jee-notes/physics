Compound Microscope

Magnification through a two-lens system.

Compound Microscope

A compound microscope uses more than one convex lens arranged such that their magnifications compound each other to produce much higher magnifying power than a simple microscope.

The standard compound microscope consists of two coaxial converging lenses:

  • Objective Lens: The lens facing the object. It has a very short focal length (\(f_o\)) and a small aperture. It forms a real, inverted, and magnified intermediate image of the object.
  • Eyepiece (Ocular) Lens: The lens close to the eye. It has a slightly larger focal length (\(f_e\)) and larger aperture than the objective. It acts as a simple microscope, taking the intermediate image as its object and forming a highly magnified, virtual final image.
The final image in a compound microscope is inverted with respect to the original object.
For biological observations, this inversion is usually acceptable.

The objective lens may itself be a system of convex lenses. Its combined function as an optical system is to produce a real, inverted, and magnified intermediate image of the object. Hence, we evaluate the linear magnification (\(m_o\)) for the objective lens.

The eyepiece then functions as a simple microscope to magnify this intermediate image. Hence, we evaluate the angular magnification (\(m_e\)) for the eyepiece.

Magnifying Power

The total magnifying power \(M\) of the compound microscope system is the product of the linear magnification of the objective (\(m_o\)) and the angular magnification of the eyepiece (\(m_e\)):

\[ \boxed{M = m_o \times m_e} \]

The object is placed at distance \(u_o\) in front of the objective. To form a real, inverted, and magnified intermediate image at distance \(v_o\), the object distance must satisfy \(f_o \lt |u_o| \lt 2f_o\).

This real intermediate image (of height \(h_i'\)) is formed within the focal length \(f_e\) of the eyepiece, serving as the object for the eyepiece. The final virtual image seen by the observer's eye is formed by the eyepiece.

The linear magnification of the objective lens \(m_o\) in magnitude is given by:

\[ m_o = \frac{|v_o|}{|u_o|} = \frac{|h_i'|}{|h_o|} \]

Optical Tube Length (\(L\)):

To describe the magnifying power of compound microscope, a new parameter that bridges both lenses is introduced - called as the optical tube length \(L\)

It is defined as the distance between the focus of the objective (\(F_o'\)) and the focus of the eyepiece (\(F_e\)). These 2 focii are the ones that are between the 2 lenses and inside the casing.

\[ \boxed{L = \text{distance between } F_o' \text{ and } F_e} \] If \(L_{\text{separation}}\) is the physical distance between the two lenses, then: \[ L_{\text{separation}} = f_o + L + f_e \implies \boxed{L = L_{\text{separation}} - f_o - f_e} \]

Optical tube length is used to bridge the image distance of the intermediate image formed by objective. We describe \(v_o\) for the 2 boundary cases of magnification.

  • In Normal Adjustment: The objective forms the intermediate image exactly at \(F_e\). Therefore, the image distance from the objective is precisely \(v_o = f_o + L\), which gives \(v_o - f_o = L\) and \(m_o = \frac{L}{f_o}\) .
  • In Near-Point Adjustment: The intermediate image is formed inside \(F_e\) (at \(|u_e| \lt f_e\)). Thus, \(v_o \neq f_o + L\), and the exact magnification requires \(m_o = \frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\) .

Case 1: Final Image at Infinity (Normal Adjustment / Relaxed Eye)

This is the standard operating state of the microscope for fatigue-free observation.

The objective forms the real, inverted intermediate image exactly at the principal focus of the eyepiece (\(|u_e| = f_e\)). The eyepiece then projects the final virtual image to infinity (\(v_e \to -\infty\)).

The angular magnification of the eyepiece in normal adjustment is:

\[ m_e = \frac{D}{f_e} \]

For the objective, the intermediate image distance is \(v_o = f_o + L\). By similar triangles on the ray diagram passing through the focus \(F_o'\):

\[ m_o = \frac{|h_i'|}{|h_o|} = \frac{v_o - f_o}{f_o} = \frac{L}{f_o} \]

Multiplying \(m_o\) and \(m_e\), the magnifying power in normal adjustment is:

\[ \boxed{M_{\text{relaxed}} = m_o \times m_e = \left(\frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\right)\left(\frac{D}{f_e}\right) = \left(\frac{L}{f_o}\right)\left(\frac{D}{f_e}\right)} \]

The separation between the two lenses in normal adjustment is:

\[ \boxed{L_{\text{tube}} = v_o + f_e = f_o + L + f_e} \]

Case 2: Final Image at Least Distance of Distinct Vision (Near Point / Stressed Eye)

To achieve maximum magnification, the objective forms the intermediate image inside the focal length of the eyepiece (\(|u_e| \lt f_e\)) such that the eyepiece forms the final virtual image at the near point (\(v_e = -D = -25\text{ cm}\)).

The angular magnification of the eyepiece in near-point adjustment is:

\[ m_e = 1 + \frac{D}{f_e} \]

Using the exact linear magnification of the objective \(m_o = \frac{|v_o|}{|u_o|}\), the total magnifying power is:

\[ \boxed{M_{\max} = \left(\frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\right)\left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right)} \]

When the focal lengths \(f_o\) and \(f_e\) are very small compared to the tube length (so that \(|u_o| \approx f_o\) and \(v_o \approx L\)), this is commonly approximated as:

\[ \boxed{M_{\max} \approx \left(\frac{L}{f_o}\right)\left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right)} \]

The separation between the two lenses in near-point adjustment is:

\[ \boxed{L_{\text{tube}} = v_o + |u_e| = v_o + \frac{D f_e}{D + f_e}} \]
