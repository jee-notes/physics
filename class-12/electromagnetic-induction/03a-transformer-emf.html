Transformer EMF

EMF generated by time-varying magnetic field.

Flux Linked with Coil

In transformer EMF problems, determining the flux linked with the coil is often the main conceptual trap. Students frequently assume that flux is always calculated using the full area of the loop. However, this is not always correct.

Magnetic flux through a loop depends on the region where the magnetic field actually exists. If the magnetic field occupies only a part of the loop area, then only that portion contributes to the flux.

Therefore, the flux must always be calculated over the area common to both the loop and the magnetic field region.

1. Areas are Different

Question

A fixed square loop of side \(5\) units has vertices at \((0,0), (0,5), (5,5), (5,0)\). A magnetic field exists perpendicular to the plane of the loop and is given by

\[ \vec B = 2t^2 \,\hat{k} \]

Find the induced EMF in the loop for the following two cases:

  • Case 1: The magnetic field exists throughout the entire region of the loop.
  • Case 2: The magnetic field exists only inside a smaller square region with vertices \((0,0), (0,1), (1,1), (1,0)\).

Solution

Magnetic flux through the loop is

\[ \Phi = \int \vec B \cdot d\vec A \]

Since the field is perpendicular to the loop,

\[ \Phi = BA \]

The magnetic field varies with time:

\[ B = 2t^2 \]

Therefore the flux becomes

\[ \Phi = (2t^2)A \]

The induced EMF is

\[ \mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} \]

Case 1: Field covers entire loop

Area of the loop:

\[ A = 5 \times 5 = 25 \]

Flux:

\[ \Phi = 2t^2 \times 25 = 50t^2 \]

EMF:

\[ \mathcal{E} = -\frac{d}{dt}(50t^2) = -100t \]

Case 2: Field exists only in small region

Area of magnetic field region:

\[ A = 1 \times 1 = 1 \]

Flux:

\[ \Phi = 2t^2 \times 1 = 2t^2 \]

EMF:

\[ \mathcal{E} = -\frac{d}{dt}(2t^2) = -4t \]

Magnetic flux depends on the region where the magnetic field actually exists, not necessarily on the total area of the loop. If the magnetic field occupies only part of the loop, only that portion contributes to the flux.

This principle is true for all cases of electromagnetic induction, but it appears most clearly in transformer EMF problems, where the loop remains stationary and only the magnetic field varies with time.

2. Spiral Coil

Question

A plane spiral coil having \(N\) turns wound tightly next to each other is placed in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the spiral. The outer radius of the spiral is \(a\).

The magnetic field varies with time as

\[ B = B_0\sin(\omega t) \]

where \(B_0\) and \(\omega\) are constants.

Find the amplitude of the induced EMF in the coil.

Solution

Each turn of the spiral coil acts like an independent loop. When the magnetic field changes with time, an EMF is induced in every turn according to Faraday's law.

Since the turns of the spiral are connected in series, the EMFs induced in individual turns add together. Therefore, the total EMF in the coil can be obtained by summing the EMF induced in each turn.

For a single turn loop of radius \(r\), the magnetic flux through that loop is

\[ \Phi = B \cdot (\pi r^2) \]

The EMF induced in that loop is therefore

\[ dE = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} \]

Since only the magnetic field varies with time,

\[ dE = -(\pi r^2)\frac{dB}{dt} \]

If the spiral has \(N\) turns distributed uniformly from radius \(0\) to \(a\), then the number of turns in a small radial thickness \(dr\) is

\[ dN = \frac{N}{a}dr \]

The EMF contributed by these turns is

\[ dE = -(\pi r^2)\frac{dB}{dt}\, dN \] \[ dE = -(\pi r^2)\frac{dB}{dt}\frac{N}{a}dr \]

The total EMF of the spiral is obtained by summing (integrating) the EMFs of all turns from \(r=0\) to \(r=a\):

\[ E = \int dE \] \[ E = -\frac{dB}{dt}\frac{N\pi}{a}\int_0^a r^2 dr \] \[ E = -\frac{dB}{dt}\frac{N\pi}{a}\left(\frac{a^3}{3}\right) \] \[ E = -\frac{N\pi a^2}{3}\frac{dB}{dt} \]

The magnetic field varies as

\[ B = B_0 \sin(\omega t) \]

Thus

\[ \frac{dB}{dt} = B_0\omega \cos(\omega t) \]

Substituting,

\[ E = -\frac{N\pi a^2}{3}B_0\omega \cos(\omega t) \]

Hence the amplitude of the induced EMF is

\[ \boxed{E_0 = \frac{N\pi a^2 B_0\omega}{3}} \]
Turn Linkage and Series EMF
A coil with \(N\) turns behaves like \(N\) individual loops connected in series. Each turn experiences its own induced EMF according to Faraday's Law. Since the turns are connected in series, the EMFs produced in each turn add together. Therefore, \[ \mathcal{E}_{total} = \sum \mathcal{E}_i \] If every turn links the same magnetic flux \( \Phi_B \), then each turn produces the same EMF \[ \mathcal{E}_i = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} \] and the total EMF becomes \[ \mathcal{E} = -N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} \] Thus the factor \(N\) in Faraday's Law arises simply because the EMFs of the individual turns add in series.
