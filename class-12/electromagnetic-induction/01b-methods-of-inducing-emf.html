Methods of Inducing EMF

Changing \( B \), changing \( A \), or changing \( \theta \) — all produce EMF.

Overview

The magnetic flux through a loop is \( \Phi_B = BA\cos\theta \). An EMF is induced whenever this product changes. There are exactly three independent ways to change it, each corresponding to a distinct class of devices:

Method What Changes Device
Change \( B \) Magnetic field intensity Transformer (primary coil)
Change \( A \) Area enclosed by circuit Motional EMF (sliding rod)
Change \( \theta \) Angle between \( \vec{B} \) and \( \hat{n} \) AC Generator

Method 1 — Changing Magnetic Field

A stationary loop in a time-varying field \( B(t) \), with area \( A \) and orientation fixed (\( \theta = 0 \)):

\[ \Phi_B = B(t) \cdot A \implies \mathcal{E} = -NA\frac{dB}{dt} \]

This is the transformer EMF. The primary coil of a transformer carries an AC current, creating a time-varying \( B \). The secondary coil, linked by the same core, has this changing \( B \) through it — inducing an EMF proportional to the number of turns:

\[ \frac{\mathcal{E}_s}{\mathcal{E}_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} \]
In a transformer, no conductor moves. The induced EMF is purely due to a changing \( B \). This is the non-motional EMF, described by the Maxwell equation \( \nabla \times \vec{E} = -\partial\vec{B}/\partial t \) — an electric field created in space by a changing magnetic field.

Method 2 — Changing Area (Motional EMF)

A rod moving on rails in a steady field — the circuit area grows at rate \( dA/dt = lv \):

\[ \mathcal{E} = -B\frac{dA}{dt} = -Blv \]

General form for any moving conductor element \( d\vec{l} \) moving with velocity \( \vec{v} \) in field \( \vec{B} \):

\[ d\mathcal{E} = (\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot d\vec{l} \]

Total motional EMF for the entire conductor:

\[ \mathcal{E} = \int_{\text{conductor}} (\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot d\vec{l} \]

Method 3 — Rotating Coil (AC Generator)

A rectangular coil of \( N \) turns and area \( A \) rotates with angular velocity \( \omega \) in a uniform field \( B \):

\[ \theta = \omega t \implies \Phi_B = NBA\cos(\omega t) \] \[ \mathcal{E} = -\frac{d(N\Phi_B)}{dt} = NBA\omega\sin(\omega t) \]

This is sinusoidal — an alternating EMF. The peak value is:

\[ \mathcal{E}_0 = NBA\omega \]
  • EMF is maximum when the coil plane is parallel to \( \vec{B} \) (\( \theta = 90° \)) — flux is zero but changing fastest.
  • EMF is zero when the coil plane is perpendicular to \( \vec{B} \) (\( \theta = 0° \)) — flux is maximum but not changing.
The AC generator converts mechanical energy (rotation) into electrical energy. The frequency of the AC output equals the frequency of rotation: \( f = \omega/(2\pi) \). For India's grid, \( f = 50 \) Hz.
