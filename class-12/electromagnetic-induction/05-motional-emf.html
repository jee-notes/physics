Motional EMF

Conductor cuts magnetic field lines like a sword cutting grass.

Underlying Mechanism

Faraday's Law unifies two different mechanisms for induced EMF:

  • Motional EMF (due to motion of charges in magnetic field)
  • Induced Electric Field (due to changing magnetic field)
In motional EMF, the magnetic field can be constant — it is the motion of the conductor that causes flux change.

Physical Origin (Lorentz Force)

When a conductor moves through a magnetic field, free charges inside it experience the Lorentz force:

\[ \vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \]

This force pushes positive and negative charges in opposite directions, creating a potential difference across the conductor.

There is no electric field initially. The EMF arises purely due to magnetic force on moving charges.

Expression for Motional EMF

For a straight conductor of length \( \ell \) moving with velocity \( v \) perpendicular to a magnetic field \( B \):

\[ \mathcal{E} = B \ell v \]

This is obtained by summing the magnetic force acting on charges along the length of the rod.

Direction of EMF

Direction is given by:

  • Right-hand rule for \( \vec{v} \times \vec{B} \)
  • Or Fleming’s right-hand rule

Sliding Rod on Rails

Question

A conducting rod of length \( \ell \) slides on two parallel conducting rails with constant velocity \( v \). The entire arrangement is placed in a uniform magnetic field \( B \) directed into the page.

The rails are connected by a resistor \( R \). Find the current in the circuit.

Solution

Charges in the rod experience magnetic force:

\[ F = qvB \]

This creates a potential difference across the rod:

\[ \mathcal{E} = B \ell v \]

This EMF drives current in the circuit:

\[ I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} = \frac{B \ell v}{R} \]
Energy Perspective

The magnetic field does no work on charges.

External agent pulling the rod does work → converted into electrical energy → dissipated as heat in resistor.

Key Differences

Motional EMF vs Induced Electric Field

• Motional EMF → due to Lorentz force \( q\vec{v} \times \vec{B} \) • Induced field → due to \( \frac{dB}{dt} \)

• Motional EMF → requires motion of conductor • Induced field → exists even in empty space

• Motional EMF → local force on charges • Induced field → global circulating field
