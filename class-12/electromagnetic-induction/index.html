Electromagnetic Induction

A changing magnetic field creates an electric field — the principle behind every generator and transformer.

Conceptual Overview

Oersted showed that electricity produces magnetism. Faraday spent years searching for the reverse — can magnetism produce electricity? In 1831 he found the answer: a changing magnetic field produces an electric field. The key word is changing. A static magnetic field, no matter how strong, produces no current. Only flux in motion — or changing — does.

This discovery unlocked the entire modern world of electrical power. Every generator, every transformer, every induction motor operates on a single law: Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction.

Faraday's Law — The Central Idea

The induced EMF in any closed loop is equal to the negative rate of change of the magnetic flux through the loop:

\[ \mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} \]

The negative sign — captured in Lenz's Law — tells us the direction of the induced current: it always opposes the change in flux that caused it. This is the electromagnetic manifestation of energy conservation.

The magnetic flux \( \Phi_B \) through a surface bounded by the loop is:

\[ \Phi_B = \int \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = BA\cos\theta \]

Flux can change in three ways — \( B \) can change, \( A \) can change, or \( \theta \) can change — and each produces an EMF.

Self and Mutual Inductance

Any circuit carrying a changing current produces a changing magnetic flux through itself — inducing an EMF in itself. This self-opposition is characterised by the self-inductance \( L \):

\[ \mathcal{E}_{\text{self}} = -L\frac{dI}{dt} \]

When two circuits are nearby, a changing current in one induces an EMF in the other — characterised by the mutual inductance \( M \):

\[ \mathcal{E}_2 = -M\frac{dI_1}{dt} \]

Transformers operate entirely on mutual inductance, converting power between voltage levels with near-perfect efficiency.

Energy in the Magnetic Field

An inductor carrying current \( I \) stores energy in the magnetic field throughout its volume. The energy stored is:

\[ U = \frac{1}{2}LI^2 \]

Just as a capacitor stores energy in the electric field with density \( \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 \), an inductor stores energy in the magnetic field with density:

\[ u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} \]
