Magnetic Flux and Induced EMF

What exactly is flux and its dependency on Area vector orientation.

Magnetic Flux

Let there be a region in space containing Magnetic Field \( \vec{B}(\vec{r}) \). The magnetic field at position \( \vec{r}=\vec{r}_1 \) is \( \vec{B}(\vec{r}_1) \). To define flux at position \( \vec{r}=\vec{r}_1 \), we consider a small surface element \( d\vec{A} \) located at the point \( \vec{r}=\vec{r}_1 \). The magnetic flux \( d\phi \) through this infinitesimal point area is a measure of how much of the magnetic field passes through that surface element. It is defined as

\[ d\phi = \vec{B}(\vec{r}_1)\cdot d\vec{A} \]

The above expression gives the flux through a differential area element at a point. To determine the flux through a finite surface \( S \), we sum the contributions from all such infinitesimal elements over the surface. This leads to the surface integral

\[ \Phi_B = \int_S \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{A} \]

For the special case of a uniform magnetic field \( \vec{B} \) making an angle \( \theta \) with the normal \( \hat n \) to a flat surface of area \( A \), the flux becomes

\[ \Phi_B = BA\cos\theta \]

The SI unit of magnetic flux is the Weber (Wb):

\[ 1\,\text{Wb} = 1\,\text{T·m}^2 \]
  • \( \theta = 0^\circ \): \( \Phi_B = BA \) — maximum flux, when the field is perpendicular to the surface.
  • \( \theta = 90^\circ \): \( \Phi_B = 0 \) — the field is parallel to the surface, so no field lines pass through it.
Flux is a scalar quantity. A positive flux means the predominant direction of \( \vec B \) is along the chosen surface normal; a negative flux means it is opposite to it. The sign therefore depends on the orientation chosen for the surface normal.

Flux Linkage

For a coil consisting of \( N \) turns, each turn experiences the same magnetic flux. The total flux linkage \( \Lambda \) is therefore the sum of the flux through all the turns:

\[ \Lambda = N\Phi_B \]
