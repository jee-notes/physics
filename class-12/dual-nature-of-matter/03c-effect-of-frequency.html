Effect of Frequency

The crucial role of the light's color and the discovery of the threshold.

Threshold Frequency and Stopping Potential

In this phase of the experiment, we vary the frequency of the incident light while keeping its intensity rigidly constant. We observe how the stopping potential (the cut-off potential) reacts to different frequencies.

It is found that as the frequency of incident light is increased, the stopping potential becomes more negative. This demonstrates that a higher frequency of incident light ejects photoelectrons with higher kinetic energy, thus requiring a stronger retarding potential to stop them.

Threshold Frequency (\( \nu_0 \))

Definition: For a given photosensitive material, there exists a certain minimum, cut-off frequency of incident radiation below which no photoelectric emission occurs whatsoever, regardless of intensity. This is the threshold frequency.

Linear Relation between \( V_0 \) and Frequency

If we plot a graph of the stopping potential \( V_0 \) against the frequency \( \nu \) of incident light, we obtain a straight line.

This straight line proves that the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons varies linearly with the frequency of incident light. Importantly, the line does not pass through the origin. It intersects the frequency axis at exactly the threshold frequency \( \nu_0 \). Different metals will produce parallel straight lines on this graph, shifted to different threshold frequencies depending on their work functions.

Independence of Intensity

The core experimental takeaway summarizing both potential and frequency observations is stark:

The threshold frequency and the maximum kinetic energy of emitted photoelectrons are strictly determined by the color (frequency) of the light and the material properties. The intensity of the light has absolutely no effect on these parameters.

According to classic Maxwellian wave theory, simply waiting longer or using a brighter lamp (higher intensity) should eventually give the electrons enough energy to leap clear of the metal, regardless of the light's frequency. The existence of a sharp threshold frequency proved catastrophic for the wave model's applicability to this phenomenon.
