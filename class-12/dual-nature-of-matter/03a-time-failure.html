Time Lag in Wave Model.

The Collapse of Classical Wave Theory

Maxwell’s wave theory, while perfect for Interference and Diffraction, could not explain the "point-to-point" energy transfer seen here.

1. The Intensity Paradox
  • Wave Prediction: High intensity = high amplitude = more energy. Electrons should come out with more Kinetic Energy.
  • Experimental Reality: Intensity only increases the number of electrons; their individual Kinetic Energy stays the same.
2. The Frequency Paradox
  • Wave Prediction: Energy is continuous. Even low-frequency light should eventually "boil" off an electron if given enough time.
  • Experimental Reality: Below a specific Threshold Frequency (\(\nu_0\)), no emission occurs, even if the light is blindingly bright.
3. The Time-Lag Paradox (The "Soaking" Problem)
  • Wave Prediction: A wave spreads energy over the entire surface. A single electron would need to "soak up" energy for minutes or hours to reach the Work Function.
  • Experimental Reality: Emission is instantaneous (\(\lt 10^{-9}\) s). The energy is delivered in a single, localized hit.

Example: Classical Wave Theory and Photoelectric Time Delay

Problem:

A point source of monochromatic light of power \(1.0\,\text{mW}\) is placed at a distance of \(5.0\,\text{m}\) from a metal surface. Light falls perpendicularly on the surface. Assume wave theory of light to hold and that all the light falling on a circular area of radius \(1.0\times10^{-9}\,\text{m}\) (which is a few times the diameter of an atom) is absorbed by a single electron on the surface. Calculate the time required by the electron to receive sufficient energy to come out of the metal if the work function of the metal is \(2.0\,\text{eV}\).

Solution:

The energy radiated by the light source per second is its power:

\[ P=1.0\,\text{mW}=1.0\times10^{-3}\,\text{J\,s}^{-1} \]

Assuming that the source radiates uniformly in all directions, its total solid angle is \(4\pi\) steradians. The solid angle subtended by the circular area at the source is:

\[ d\Omega=\frac{dA}{r^2} \] \[ =\frac{\pi(1.0\times10^{-9})^2}{(5.0)^2} \] \[ =\frac{\pi}{25}\times10^{-18}\,\text{sr} \]

The fraction of the total energy radiated by the source that reaches this circular area is:

\[ \frac{d\Omega}{4\pi} =\frac{1}{100}\times10^{-18} =10^{-20} \]

Therefore, the energy reaching the circular area per second is:

\[ P_{\text{received}} =\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}P \] \[ =10^{-20}\times1.0\times10^{-3} \] \[ =1.0\times10^{-23}\,\text{J\,s}^{-1} \]

According to the classical wave picture, this energy is continuously supplied to the electron. The electron must accumulate an energy equal to the work function before it can escape.

The energy required to overcome the surface barrier is:

\[ W=\Phi=2.0\,\text{eV} \] \[ =2.0\times1.6\times10^{-19}\,\text{J} =3.2\times10^{-19}\,\text{J} \]

Hence, the time required to accumulate this energy is:

\[ t=\frac{W}{P_{\text{received}}} \] \[ =\frac{3.2\times10^{-19}}{1.0\times10^{-23}} \] \[ =3.2\times10^4\,\text{s} \]

Converting into hours:

\[ \boxed{t\approx 8.9\,\text{hours}} \]
Conclusion:

According to the classical wave description, the electron would require approximately \(8.9\) hours of continuous irradiation to accumulate sufficient energy to escape from the metal.

However, experimentally, photoelectrons emerge almost immediately after suitable light falls on the metal. There is no such prolonged accumulation of energy.

This reveals a fundamental difficulty with the classical wave description of energy transfer to individual electrons.

Wave model is Soaking model

To understand how the observations of photo electric effect contradict the wave model, we need to first understand what we expect from wave model.

Students think that the fact that Metal has characteristic work function \( \Phi \) is a contradiction to wave model of light.
This is clearly wrong way of thinking.
Metal having a characteristic work function is the property of metal and its internal design.
It does not indicate anything directly about nature of light.
Nature of light is determined by studying interaction of light with metal and knowing about characteristics of metal that are independent of nature of light.

The three failures frequency, intensity and time.

Soaking model

The soaking model of wave nature creates below expectations:

  1. There should be no threshold frequency : Wave model predicts continuous incidence of energy. And so, light of any frequency but sufficient intensity and in sufficient time should ultimately eject electrons.
  2. There should be time lag depending upon incident intensity : Wave model resembles heating a pot of water on a gas burner. Water has fixed boiling point and heat capacity similar to metal having fixed work function. If we heat water on a larger burner, we will take less time to boil.
  3. Stopping potential should depend on intensity : Stopping Potential depends sharply on frequency only. This is again a big blow because a continuous energy distribution is expected depending on intensity and time of incidence.
Basically, wave model predicts: Non- Impulsive Energy and Momentum Transfer.

The "Soaking" Analogy

In the classical wave model, light energy is distributed continuously across the wavefront. Imagine a wave washing over a beach; the energy is spread over every grain of sand. For an electron to escape, it must "soak up" this distributed energy until it accumulates enough to overcome the Work Function (\(\Phi\)).

The Classical Prediction:
  • Continuous Accumulation: Energy transfer is a slow, additive process.
  • Intensity Dependency: A brighter light (larger amplitude) should reduce the "soaking time."
  • Frequency Irrelevance: Even low-frequency light should eventually work if you wait long enough.

The Mathematical Contradiction

Let's perform a "Back-of-the-Envelope" calculation to see how long a wave would take to eject an electron.

Sample Calculation:
Suppose we shine a dim light of intensity \(I = 10^{-10} \text{ W/m}^2\) on a metal with \(\Phi = 2 \text{ eV}\).
1. Assume the target area of an electron is roughly the size of an atom (\(r \approx 10^{-10} \text{ m}\)).
2. Area \(A \approx \pi r^2 \approx 3 \times 10^{-20} \text{ m}^2\).
3. Energy absorbed per second \(P = I \times A = 3 \times 10^{-30} \text{ J/s}\).
4. Energy required (\(\Phi\)) \(\approx 3.2 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}\).

Time Required: \[ t = \frac{\text{Energy required}}{\text{Energy per second}} \approx 10^8 \text{ seconds} \approx \mathbf{3 \text{ years!}} \]

The Reality: In experiments, the emission is instantaneous (\( \lt 10^{-9} \text{ s} \)). The wave model isn't just slightly off; it is off by a factor of trillions.

Impulsive vs. Non-Impulsive Transfer

The fundamental difference lies in how energy is delivered:

Model Mechanism Analogy
Wave (Soaking) Non-Impulsive: Energy spreads over the surface. A gentle rain filling up a bucket.
Particle (Quantum) Impulsive: Energy is delivered in a single "gulp." A heavy brick falling into a bucket.

Quantized is fancy word for discrete

The difference between quantized and non-quantized is same as difference between the phenomenon of photo electric effect and metallic luster.

Both are phenomenon involving surface electrons and physical changes for the metal. They do not change the metal chemically.

There is a sea of electrons in the metal body and light falling on the metal surface is like rain falling on the surface.

Luster is a bulk (continuous) phenomenon. The entire sea responds in unision when the rain drops are not of sufficient energy. The electrons absorb energy of any wavelength and emit it instantaneously. Everything seems continuous.

Photo Electric Effect is a discrete (quantized) phenomenon. As individual drops have sufficient energy, the interaction is one-to-one - particle-to-particle. We are already aware about particle nature of matter, but this interaction zooms the incident radiation as well. It represents particle matter interacting with particle of radiation (light).

Bulk Response vs. Individual Interaction

The distinction between Metallic Luster and the Photoelectric Effect is a matter of "Scale of Interaction."

Luster (The Sea): When a wave hits the metal, the "sea of electrons" moves in collective oscillation (plasmons). This is a Non-Impulsive bulk reaction where the wave nature of light is perfectly sufficient to explain the reflection.

Photoelectric Effect (The Drop): The moment a single electron is ejected, the "Bulk" model fails. We are no longer looking at the sea; we are looking at a 1-on-1 collision.
