Effect of Intensity

How the brightness of incident light changes the photoelectric rate.

Variation of Photocurrent with Intensity

In the experimental setup, we keep the frequency \( \nu \) of the incident light strictly constant (above the threshold frequency) and maintain the accelerating potential on the collector plate A constant and sufficiently high.

Under these fixed conditions, the intensity of the incident light is varied. It is observed experimentally that the photoelectric current increases linearly with the intensity of incident light.

Graphical Representation

If a graph is plotted between the photoelectric current (on the y-axis) and the intensity of incident light (on the x-axis), the result is a straight line passing through the origin.

Since the photoelectric current correlates directly to the number of electrons emitted per second, the straight-line graph proves that the rate of emission of photoelectrons is directly proportional to the intensity of the incident light.

Physical Interpretation

From a quantum perspective, finding this strict proportionality is crucial. Light intensity, in the photon model, represents the number of photons crossing a unit area per unit time, while the energy of each individual photon remains constant (since frequency is held constant).

If you double the intensity, you double the number of photons striking the metal surface per second. Assuming one photon interacts with one electron, twice as many photons will eject twice as many photoelectrons. This neatly explains the linear relationship.
