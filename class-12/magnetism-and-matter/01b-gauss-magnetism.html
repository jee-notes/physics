Gauss's Law for Magnetism

Why magnetic monopoles do not exist — and what this means for the field.

Statement of the Law

Gauss's Law for Magnetism states that the total magnetic flux \( \Phi_B \) through any closed surface is always zero:

\[ \oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0 \]

This is one of Maxwell's four fundamental equations of electromagnetism. It holds universally — for any closed surface, any magnetic field configuration, at any point in space.

What Does It Mean?

Recall Gauss's Law for electricity:

\[ \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0} \]

The right-hand side is non-zero whenever charge is enclosed. For magnetism, the right-hand side is always zero — meaning no magnetic "charge" (monopole) exists inside any closed surface.

Mathematically, Gauss's Law for magnetism states that \( \nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0 \) everywhere. This means \( \vec{B} \) is a solenoidal (divergence-free) field — it has no sources or sinks. Every field line that enters a closed region must also exit it.

No Magnetic Monopoles

If a magnetic monopole existed — an isolated north pole or isolated south pole — it would act as a source (or sink) of magnetic field lines, giving a non-zero surface integral. Since we always observe \( \oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0 \) experimentally, we conclude that isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist in nature.

This is the deep physical content of the law. Every magnet must have both a north and south pole. Magnetic field lines form closed loops — they do not begin or end on any source. They emerge from one pole and re-enter at the other, continuing through the interior of the magnet.

Contrast with Gauss's Law for Electricity

Feature Electric (Gauss) Magnetic (Gauss)
Law \( \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0 \) \( \oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0 \)
Monopoles exist? Yes — point charges No — not observed
Field line topology Start at \( + \), end at \( - \) Always closed loops
Differential form \( \nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \rho/\varepsilon_0 \) \( \nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0 \)

Implication for Bar Magnet Field Lines

Draw a closed surface (Gaussian surface) that encloses only the north pole of a bar magnet. The field lines emerge from the north pole through the surface — but they must return through the same surface via a path that curves around outside the magnet and re-enters at the south pole end of the magnet.

In addition, field lines pass through the interior of the magnet from south to north. Counting all flux through any closed surface — those exiting through the equatorial regions cancel with those entering — the net flux is always zero.

The continuity of magnetic field lines through the interior of the magnet (from S to N) is why the field inside a bar magnet points from its south pole to its north pole, while outside the magnet it points from north to south — yet the lines are continuous closed loops.
