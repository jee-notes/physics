The Bar Magnet and Magnetic Dipole

The macroscopic magnet as a perfect magnetic dipole.

What is a Magnetic Dipole?

A magnetic dipole is the simplest possible magnetic system — one with a north pole and a south pole. It is the magnetic analogue of the electric dipole. Unlike the electric dipole, however, the two poles of a magnetic dipole cannot be separated: every magnet always has both a north and a south pole.

Cut a bar magnet in half, and each half becomes a complete magnet with its own north and south pole. This is the experimental basis of Gauss's Law for Magnetism — there are no magnetic monopoles.

A current loop and a bar magnet are equivalent descriptions of the same physical object at the macroscopic level. Any bar magnet can be modelled as an equivalent current loop with the same magnetic dipole moment.

Magnetic Dipole Moment of a Bar Magnet

For a bar magnet of pole strength \( m \) and magnetic length \( 2l \) (the distance between the poles), the magnetic dipole moment \( \vec{M} \) is:

\[ \vec{M} = m \cdot (2l)\,\hat{l} \]

where \( \hat{l} \) points from the south pole to the north pole. The magnitude is:

\[ M = m \cdot 2l \quad \text{(SI unit: A·m}^2\text{)} \]

The magnetic length \( 2l \) is slightly less than the geometric length of the magnet because the poles are located slightly inward from the ends.

Torque and Potential Energy in a Uniform Field

When a bar magnet (dipole moment \( \vec{M} \)) is placed in a uniform external magnetic field \( \vec{B} \), it experiences a torque:

\[ \vec{\tau} = \vec{M} \times \vec{B}, \quad \tau = MB\sin\theta \]

where \( \theta \) is the angle between \( \vec{M} \) and \( \vec{B} \). The torque tends to align the magnet along the field direction.

The potential energy of the dipole in the field is:

\[ U = -\vec{M} \cdot \vec{B} = -MB\cos\theta \]
  • \( \theta = 0° \): \( U = -MB \) — stable equilibrium (magnet aligned with field).
  • \( \theta = 180° \): \( U = +MB \) — unstable equilibrium.

Work done in rotating from \( \theta_1 \) to \( \theta_2 \):

\[ W = MB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2) \]
