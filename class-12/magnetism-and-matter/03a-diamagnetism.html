Diamagnetism

The universal but often overshadowed magnetic response — repulsion.

What is Diamagnetism?

Diamagnetism is a property of all materials — it is a universal quantum mechanical effect. However, in paramagnetic and ferromagnetic materials, it is completely overwhelmed by stronger magnetic responses and goes unnoticed.

A diamagnetic material placed in an external magnetic field develops a magnetisation opposing the applied field. As a result, it is feebly repelled out of a magnetic field toward regions of lower field strength.

Diamagnetism is the closest magnetic analogue of Lenz's Law. Just as an induced current opposes the change in flux, the orbital motion of electrons adjusts to oppose the applied field. The effect is always present but is masked by stronger effects in other materials.

Physical Origin

In a diamagnetic material, all electrons are paired. The atoms have zero net magnetic dipole moment in the ground state — there is no permanent dipole to align with an external field.

When an external field \( \vec{B}_0 \) is applied, it induces a change in the orbital angular momentum of the electrons via Faraday's law of induction at the atomic scale. The induced change produces a tiny magnetic moment opposing the applied field in every atom. This is the Lenz's law interpretation at the atomic level.

The key point: since all atoms contribute in the same direction (opposing), the bulk effect is a net negative magnetisation.

Characteristic Properties

  • Magnetic susceptibility \( \chi_m \) is small and negative: typically \( -10^{-5} \) to \( -10^{-9} \).
  • Relative permeability \( \mu_r = 1 + \chi_m \) is slightly less than 1.
  • The material is weakly repelled from stronger regions of a non-uniform field.
  • \( \chi_m \) is independent of temperature — since the effect is purely due to orbital electron response, not thermal alignment.
  • The induced magnetisation is proportional to the applied field, so the effect disappears when the external field is removed.

Examples

  • Bismuth (Bi) — the strongest known diamagnetic element.
  • Copper (Cu), Gold (Au), Silver (Ag)
  • Water (H\(_2\)O) — living organisms are diamagnetic, which enables MRI.
  • Superconductors — exhibit perfect diamagnetism (\( \chi_m = -1 \), \( \mu_r = 0 \)): they completely expel magnetic flux from their interior. This is the Meissner effect and allows magnetic levitation.
A superconductor is the extreme limit of diamagnetism. Its susceptibility is exactly \( -1 \), meaning the field inside is exactly zero. The surface currents induced perfectly cancel every external field line — this is why a magnet floats above a superconductor.
