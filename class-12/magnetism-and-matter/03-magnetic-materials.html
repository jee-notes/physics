Magnetic Properties of Materials

Why every material responds differently to a magnetic field.

Magnetisation

When a material is placed in an external magnetic field, it develops a net magnetic dipole moment per unit volume called the magnetisation \( \vec{M} \):

\[ \vec{M} = \frac{\text{net magnetic moment}}{\text{volume}} \quad \text{(unit: A/m)} \]

The total magnetic field inside a material has contributions from the external field and from the magnetised material itself. The auxiliary field \( \vec{H} \) (magnetic intensity) is defined to separate these:

\[ \vec{B} = \mu_0(\vec{H} + \vec{M}) \]

Magnetic Susceptibility and Permeability

For linear magnetic materials, the magnetisation is proportional to \( \vec{H} \):

\[ \vec{M} = \chi_m \vec{H} \]

where \( \chi_m \) is the dimensionless magnetic susceptibility. Substituting:

\[ \vec{B} = \mu_0(1 + \chi_m)\vec{H} = \mu_r \mu_0 \vec{H} = \mu \vec{H} \]

where \( \mu_r = 1 + \chi_m \) is the relative permeability and \( \mu = \mu_r \mu_0 \) is the absolute permeability of the material.

For vacuum: \( \chi_m = 0 \), \( \mu_r = 1 \), and \( \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{H} \). The material parameters \( \chi_m \) and \( \mu_r \) tell us how much the material amplifies or reduces the magnetic field compared to vacuum.

Three Classes of Magnetic Materials

The sign and magnitude of \( \chi_m \) determine the magnetic character of a material:

Property Diamagnetic Paramagnetic Ferromagnetic
\( \chi_m \) Small, negative \( (-10^{-6} \text{ to } -10^{-5}) \) Small, positive \( (10^{-6} \text{ to } 10^{-3}) \) Large, positive \( (10^3 \text{ to } 10^5) \)
\( \mu_r \) Slightly less than 1 Slightly greater than 1 Very much greater than 1
Behavior in field Weakly repelled Weakly attracted Strongly attracted
Temperature effect Independent Curie's Law applies Loses property above Curie point
Examples Bi, Cu, water, gold Al, Na, liquid O\(_2\) Fe, Ni, Co, Alnico
