Magnetic Field of a Dipole

The axial and equatorial field lines of a bar magnet.

Setup

Consider a bar magnet of magnetic moment \( M \) and half-length \( l \). The two poles are at \( \pm l \) from the centre. We find the magnetic field at two special positions:

  • Axial point \( P_1 \): a point on the axis of the magnet at distance \( r \) from its centre.
  • Equatorial point \( P_2 \): a point on the perpendicular bisector (equatorial plane) at distance \( r \) from the centre.

Both results are derived by treating the magnet as two isolated poles of strength \( +m \) and \( -m \) and vectorially adding their fields.

Field at an Axial Point

At point \( P_1 \) on the axis at distance \( r \) (with \( r \gg l \)):

The field from the nearer \( N \) pole and the farther \( S \) pole both act along the axis, but in opposite directions. The net field points from \( S \) to \( N \) — the same direction as \( \vec{M} \):

\[ \vec{B}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{2Mr}{(r^2 - l^2)^2} \]

In the short-magnet approximation (\( r \gg l \)), \( (r^2 - l^2)^2 \approx r^4 \):

\[ \boxed{B_{\text{axial}} \approx \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{2M}{r^3}} \]

The axial field is in the same direction as the magnetic moment \( \vec{M} \) (from S-pole to N-pole inside the magnet, or from south to north externally along the axis).

Compare with the electric dipole: \( E_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2p}{r^3} \). The magnetic and electric dipole formulas are structurally identical — only the constants differ.

Field at an Equatorial Point

At point \( P_2 \) on the equatorial plane at distance \( r \):

The components of the two pole fields along the perpendicular bisector cancel by symmetry. The components along the axis of the magnet add up. The net field points from \( N \) to \( S \) — opposite to \( \vec{M} \):

\[ \vec{B}_{\text{equatorial}} = -\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{M}{(r^2 + l^2)^{3/2}} \]

In the short-magnet approximation (\( r \gg l \)):

\[ \boxed{B_{\text{equatorial}} \approx \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{M}{r^3}} \]

Direction: antiparallel to \( \vec{M} \), i.e., from north to south along the external equatorial line.

Key Comparison

Property Axial Field Equatorial Field
Magnitude (short magnet) \( \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{2M}{r^3} \) \( \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{M}{r^3} \)
Ratio \( B_{\text{axial}} = 2\, B_{\text{equatorial}} \) at the same \( r \)
Direction vs \( \vec{M} \) Same (parallel) Opposite (antiparallel)
Distance dependence \( 1/r^3 \) \( 1/r^3 \)
