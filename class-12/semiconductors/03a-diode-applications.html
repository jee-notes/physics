Diode Applications

Rectification, power supply conversions, and waveform filtering circuits.

Diode as a Half-Wave Rectifier

Rectification is the process of converting alternating current (AC) into unidirectional direct current (DC). Since a p-n junction diode conducts current only in forward bias and blocks it in reverse bias, it acts as an ideal electronic valve for rectification.

In a half-wave rectifier, an AC supply is connected to the primary of a step-down transformer. The secondary is connected in series with a single diode \( D \) and a load resistor \( R_L \).

  • Positive Half Cycle: The diode is forward biased, conducts, and a current flows through \( R_L \), developing an output voltage across it.
  • Negative Half Cycle: The diode is reverse biased, does not conduct, and the output voltage is zero.

Half-Wave Rectifier Parameters

  • Output Frequency: \( f_{\text{out}} = f_{\text{in}} \) (e.g., for \( 50 \text{ Hz} \) AC input, output pulse frequency is \( 50 \text{ Hz} \)).
  • Maximum Theoretical Efficiency (\( \eta_{\text{max}} \)): \[ \eta = \frac{P_{\text{dc}}}{P_{\text{ac}}} = \frac{0.406 R_L}{r_f + R_L} \implies \eta_{\text{max}} = 40.6\% \]
  • Peak Inverse Voltage (PIV): The maximum reverse voltage the diode must withstand: \( \text{PIV} = V_m \).

Diode as a Full-Wave Rectifier (Center-Tapped and Bridge)

A full-wave rectifier rectifies both the positive and negative half-cycles of the AC input, providing continuous unidirectional pulsating DC.

1. Center-Tapped Transformer Rectifier

Uses a center-tapped secondary winding and two diodes (\( D_1 \) and \( D_2 \)):

  • During the positive half-cycle, \( D_1 \) is forward biased (conducts) and \( D_2 \) is reverse biased.
  • During the negative half-cycle, \( D_2 \) is forward biased (conducts) and \( D_1 \) is reverse biased.
  • In both half cycles, current flows through the load resistor \( R_L \) in the exact same direction.
  • \( \text{PIV} = 2V_m \).

2. Bridge Rectifier

Uses four diodes (\( D_1, D_2, D_3, D_4 \)) in a bridge network without requiring a bulky center-tapped transformer. Diodes conduct in diagonal pairs: \( (D_1, D_3) \) during positive half cycle and \( (D_2, D_4) \) during negative half cycle.

  • \( \text{PIV} = V_m \).

Full-Wave Rectifier Parameters

  • Output Frequency: \( f_{\text{out}} = 2 f_{\text{in}} \) (e.g., for \( 50 \text{ Hz} \) input, output ripple frequency is \( 100 \text{ Hz} \)).
  • Maximum Theoretical Efficiency (\( \eta_{\text{max}} \)): \[ \eta = \frac{P_{\text{dc}}}{P_{\text{ac}}} = \frac{0.812 R_L}{r_f + R_L} \implies \eta_{\text{max}} = 81.2\% \]

Filter Circuits and Ripple Factor

The output of a rectifier is a unidirectional pulsating DC voltage containing both a steady DC component and an undesirable fluctuating AC component called ripple.

Ripple Factor (\( r \))

Defined as the ratio of the RMS value of the AC ripple component (\( V_{\text{ac}} \)) to the DC component value (\( V_{\text{dc}} \)): \[ r = \frac{V_{\text{ac}}}{V_{\text{dc}}} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{V_{\text{dc}}}\right)^2 - 1} \]
  • For Half-Wave Rectifier: \( r = 1.21 \) (Ripple is larger than DC component).
  • For Full-Wave Rectifier: \( r = 0.482 \).

Filter Types

  1. Shunt Capacitor Filter: A large capacitor \( C \) is connected in parallel across the load resistor \( R_L \).
    • During conduction, the capacitor charges to peak voltage \( V_m \).
    • When the rectifier voltage drops below \( V_m \), the diode turns off and the capacitor discharges slowly through \( R_L \), maintaining a nearly smooth output voltage.
    • Capacitor impedance \( X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi f C} \) offers an easy path to high-frequency AC ripple to ground while forcing DC through \( R_L \).
  2. Series Inductor Filter: An inductor \( L \) in series with \( R_L \). Inductive reactance \( X_L = 2\pi f L \) blocks AC ripple while allowing DC with zero reactance.
