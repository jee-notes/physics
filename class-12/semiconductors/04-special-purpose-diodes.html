Special Purpose p-n Junction Diodes

Voltage regulation, optical detection, light generation, and solar energy harvesting.

Zener Diode and Breakdown Mechanisms (Zener vs Avalanche Breakdown)

A Zener Diode is a specially fabricated, heavily doped silicon p-n junction diode designed to operate continuously and safely in the reverse breakdown region without damage.

Comparison of Breakdown Mechanisms

Parameter Zener Breakdown Avalanche Breakdown
Doping Level Very heavily doped (\( \sim 1:10^4 \)) Lightly to moderately doped
Depletion Width Extremely thin (\( < 10^{-7} \text{ m} \)) Relatively wide (\( > 10^{-6} \text{ m} \))
Breakdown Voltage Low voltage (\( V_z < 6 \text{ V} \)) Higher voltage (\( V_z > 6 \text{ V} \))
Physical Mechanism High electric field (\( > 10^7 \text{ V/m} \)) directly pulls valence electrons out of covalent bonds (field emission). Thermally generated minority carriers accelerate, gain kinetic energy, and break bonds via collisions (impact ionization / carrier multiplication).
Temperature Coeff. Negative (\( V_z \) decreases with \( T \)) Positive (\( V_z \) increases with \( T \))

Zener Diode as a Voltage Regulator (Design and Calculations)

The sharp breakdown characteristic of a Zener diode makes it ideal for maintaining a constant DC output voltage across a load, despite fluctuations in input unregulated DC voltage or changes in load current.

Circuit Configuration: The Zener diode is connected in reverse bias across the load resistance \( R_L \), in series with a current-limiting resistor \( R_s \).

Governing Circuit Equations

\[ I = I_z + I_L \] \[ V_{\text{in}} = I R_s + V_z \] \[ V_{\text{out}} = V_z = I_L R_L \] \[ R_s = \frac{V_{\text{in}} - V_z}{I_z + I_L} \]

Example: Calculating Series Resistance \( R_s \)

A \( 10 \text{ V} \) Zener diode is used to provide a regulated voltage to a load needing \( I_L = 20 \text{ mA} \) from an unregulated DC source fluctuating between \( 14 \text{ V} \) and \( 20 \text{ V} \). If the minimum Zener current is \( I_{z,\text{min}} = 5 \text{ mA} \), determine the suitable value of \( R_s \).

Solution:
Regulation must be maintained at minimum input voltage (\( V_{\text{in,min}} = 14 \text{ V} \)), where Zener current is at its minimum: \[ I_{\text{min}} = I_{z,\text{min}} + I_L = 5 \text{ mA} + 20 \text{ mA} = 25 \text{ mA} \] \[ R_s = \frac{V_{\text{in,min}} - V_z}{I_{\text{min}}} = \frac{14 \text{ V} - 10 \text{ V}}{25 \times 10^{-3} \text{ A}} = \frac{4}{0.025} = 160 \ \Omega \]

Optoelectronic Junction Devices: Photodiode, LED, and Solar Cell

1. Photodiode

A reverse-biased p-n junction diode with a transparent window. When photons with energy \( h\nu \ge E_g \) strike the depletion layer, electron-hole pairs are optically generated. The internal field sweeps electrons to n-side and holes to p-side, increasing the reverse current.

Why operate Photodiodes in Reverse Bias?
In forward bias, the majority carrier density is already huge (\( \sim 10^{22} \)), so a light-induced carrier increase \( \Delta n \) creates a tiny fractional change (\( \Delta n / n_{\text{maj}} \ll 1 \)). In reverse bias, the dark current is governed by minority carriers (\( \sim 10^9 \)), so the same \( \Delta n \) creates a massive fractional change (\( \Delta n / n_{\text{min}} \gg 1 \)), making optical detection highly sensitive.

2. Light Emitting Diode (LED)

A heavily doped, forward-biased p-n junction made of direct bandgap compound semiconductors (like \( \text{GaAs}_{1-x}\text{P}_x \)).

  • Under forward bias, minority carrier injection occurs.
  • Electrons and holes recombine radiatively across the bandgap, releasing photons: \[ \lambda \approx \frac{hc}{E_g} \]
  • To emit visible light (\( 400 - 700 \text{ nm} \)), the bandgap must satisfy \( E_g \ge 1.8 \text{ eV} \). Silicon (\( E_g = 1.1 \text{ eV} \)) and Germanium (\( E_g = 0.7 \text{ eV} \)) are indirect bandgap semiconductors where recombination releases phonons (heat), not light.

3. Solar Cell

An unbiased p-n junction designed to convert photon energy directly into electrical energy via the Photovoltaic Effect in three sequential steps:

  1. Generation: Absorption of photons (\( h\nu > E_g \)) creates electron-hole pairs near the junction.
  2. Separation: The built-in electric field sweeps photo-electrons to the n-layer and photo-holes to the p-layer.
  3. Collection: Charges accumulate on the front and back metal contacts, developing an Open Circuit Voltage (\( V_{\text{oc}} \)) and supplying a Short Circuit Current (\( I_{\text{sc}} \)).
The V-I characteristic of a solar cell lies in the 4th quadrant because the device delivers power rather than consuming it.
