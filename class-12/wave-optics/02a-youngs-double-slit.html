Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE)

The definitive proof of the wave nature of light.

Experimental Setup (YDSE)

In 1801, Thomas Young experimentally demonstrated interference. A monochromatic source of light illuminates a narrow slit \( S \). The cylindrical wavefronts from \( S \) fall on two very closely spaced narrow parallel slits \( S_1 \) and \( S_2 \).

According to Huygens' principle, \( S_1 \) and \( S_2 \) act as coherent sources because they derive light from the same primary wavefront. The waves from \( S_1 \) and \( S_2 \) superimpose and produce an interference pattern on a screen placed at a distance \( D \). The pattern consists of alternate bright and dark bands called fringes.

Path Difference and Phase Difference

Let the distance between the two slits be \( d \) and the distance to the screen be \( D \). Let \( P \) be a point on the screen at a distance \( y \) from the central point \( O \).

The waves from \( S_1 \) and \( S_2 \) travel different distances to reach \( P \). The path difference \( \Delta x \) is: \[ \Delta x = S_2P - S_1P \] By geometry, for \( d \ll D \) and \( y \ll D \), we can approximate the path difference as:

\[ \Delta x \approx d \sin \theta \approx d \tan \theta = d \left(\frac{y}{D}\right) \] Where \( \theta \) is the angular position of point \( P \).

The corresponding phase difference is \( \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \frac{y d}{D} \).

Fringe Width Calculation

Using the conditions for constructive and destructive interference, we can find the positions of bright and dark fringes.

Position of Bright Fringes (Maxima)

For a bright fringe, \( \Delta x = n\lambda \). \[ \frac{y_n d}{D} = n\lambda \implies y_n = \frac{n\lambda D}{d} \quad (n = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \dots) \] The central bright fringe (Central Maximum) occurs at \( n=0 \) (\( y_0 = 0 \)).

Position of Dark Fringes (Minima)

For a dark fringe, \( \Delta x = (n - \frac{1}{2})\lambda \). (Using \( n = 1, 2, \dots \) for the 1st, 2nd dark fringe). \[ \frac{y_n d}{D} = \left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right)\lambda \implies y_n = \left(n - \frac{1}{2}\right) \frac{\lambda D}{d} \]

Fringe Width (\( \beta \) or \( w \))

The distance between two consecutive bright (or dark) fringes is called the fringe width. \[ \beta = y_{n+1} - y_n = \frac{(n+1)\lambda D}{d} - \frac{n\lambda D}{d} \]

\[ \beta = \frac{\lambda D}{d} \] This formula shows that the fringe width is directly proportional to wavelength \( \lambda \) and screen distance \( D \), and inversely proportional to the slit separation \( d \).
In YDSE, the width of all bright and dark fringes is exactly the same, provided the slits are identical.

Intensity Distribution

If both slits have the same width, the amplitudes are equal (\( a_1 = a_2 = a \)) and the intensities are equal (\( I_1 = I_2 = I_0 \)). The resultant intensity at any point is: \[ I = 2I_0 + 2I_0 \cos \phi = 2I_0 (1 + \cos \phi) = 4I_0 \cos^2\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right) \] Since \( I_{\text{max}} = 4I_0 \):

\[ I = I_{\text{max}} \cos^2\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right) \] The intensity vs phase difference graph is a \( \cos^2 \) curve, fluctuating perfectly between \( I_{\text{max}} \) and \( 0 \). The average intensity on the screen is \( 2I_0 \), which proves that interference does not create or destroy energy; it only redistributes it, obeying the law of conservation of energy.
