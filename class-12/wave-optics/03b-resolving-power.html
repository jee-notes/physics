Resolving Power

The diffraction limit of optical instruments.

Rayleigh Criterion

Due to diffraction, the image of a point object formed by any optical instrument with a circular aperture (like a telescope or microscope lens) is not a perfect point but a bright central circular disc (Airy disc) surrounded by faint concentric dark and bright rings.

When two point objects are very close together, their diffraction patterns overlap. The Rayleigh Criterion for Resolution states that two point sources are just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one source coincides with the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other source.

If the angular separation is less than the Rayleigh limit, the two objects appear as one unresolved blob.

Resolving Power of Microscopes and Telescopes

Resolving power is defined as the reciprocal of the smallest distance or angle by which two objects can be separated and still be seen as distinct.

Resolving Power of a Telescope

For a telescope, we measure the limit of resolution as an angle \( \Delta \theta \). A circular aperture of diameter \( D \) forms a first minimum at an angle \( \Delta \theta = \frac{1.22 \lambda}{D} \).

\[ \text{Resolving Power} = \frac{1}{\Delta \theta} = \frac{D}{1.22 \lambda} \] To increase the resolving power, telescopes are built with very large diameter objective mirrors or lenses.

Resolving Power of a Microscope

For a microscope, we measure the limit of resolution as a linear distance \( \Delta d \) between two close objects on a slide. \[ \Delta d = \frac{1.22 \lambda}{2n \sin \theta} \] where \( n \sin \theta \) is the Numerical Aperture (NA) of the objective.

\[ \text{Resolving Power} = \frac{1}{\Delta d} = \frac{2n \sin \theta}{1.22 \lambda} \] To resolve finer cellular structures, scientists use oil immersion lenses (high \( n \)) and electron microscopes (extremely small \( \lambda \)).
