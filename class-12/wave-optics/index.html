Wave Optics

Unraveling the true nature of light through interference, diffraction, and polarization.

Conceptual Overview

For a long time, the rectilinear propagation of light (its tendency to travel in straight lines) convinced physicists, notably Sir Isaac Newton, that light consisted of a stream of particles (corpuscles). However, certain phenomena like interference and diffraction could not be explained by a particle model. In the 17th century, Christiaan Huygens proposed the wave theory of light, which successfully explained reflection and refraction but initially lacked experimental backing for its more radical predictions.

It wasn't until Thomas Young's Double Slit Experiment in 1801 that the wave nature of light was decisively proven. Later, James Clerk Maxwell proved that light is an electromagnetic wave, fundamentally integrating optics with electromagnetism.

Wave optics deals with the phenomena that depend on the wave nature of light, primarily occurring when light interacts with objects whose dimensions are comparable to its wavelength (\( \lambda \approx 400 - 700 \text{ nm} \)).
