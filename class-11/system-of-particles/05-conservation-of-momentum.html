Conservation of Linear Momentum

If no net external force acts on a system, its total momentum remains constant — in magnitude and direction.

Statement

If the net external force on a system of particles is zero, the total linear momentum of the system is conserved:

\[\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = 0 \implies \vec{P} = \text{constant}\] \[\sum m_i \vec{v}_{i,\text{initial}} = \sum m_i \vec{v}_{i,\text{final}}\]

This is a direct consequence of Newton's second law applied to the system: \(\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d\vec{P}}{dt}\). If \(\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = 0\), then \(\frac{d\vec{P}}{dt} = 0\), so \(\vec{P}\) does not change.

The conservation of momentum is more fundamental than any individual Newton's law. It holds even in domains where Newton's laws fail — in relativistic mechanics and in quantum mechanics. It is ultimately a consequence of the translational symmetry of empty space.

Direction-Wise Conservation

Momentum is a vector, so conservation applies independently in each direction:

  • If \(F_{\text{ext},x} = 0\), then \(P_x = \text{const}\) — even if \(P_y\) and \(P_z\) change.
  • This selective conservation is the key tool in problems involving projectile explosions, collisions on inclined planes, and interactions in the presence of gravity.

Applications

1. Recoil of a Gun

A gun of mass \(M\) fires a bullet of mass \(m\) at speed \(v\). Before firing, total momentum is zero. After firing:

\[0 = mv + MV \implies V = -\frac{m}{M}v\]

The gun recoils backward with speed \(\frac{m}{M}v\). The heavier the gun, the smaller the recoil speed.

2. Explosion of a Stationary Object

A bomb at rest explodes into fragments. Total initial momentum is zero, so:

\[\sum m_i \vec{v}_i = 0\]

If it splits into two fragments, they move in opposite directions with momenta of equal magnitude. If into three or more, the vector sum of all fragment momenta must be zero.

3. Man on a Boat

A man of mass \(m\) walks a distance \(d\) on a boat of mass \(M\) on frictionless water. The boat moves in the opposite direction by:

\[\Delta x_{\text{boat}} = \frac{m \cdot d}{m + M}\]

The COM of the man–boat system remains stationary throughout (no external horizontal force).

Approximate Conservation During Collisions

In a collision, external forces (like gravity) are still present, but they are negligible compared to the impulsive internal forces during the very short collision duration \(\Delta t\). The impulse from gravity is \(mg\Delta t \approx 0\) when \(\Delta t\) is tiny, while the impulse from the collision force \(F_{\text{collision}} \cdot \Delta t\) is finite.

Therefore, we can treat the system as isolated during the collision and apply momentum conservation:

\[\vec{P}_{\text{just before}} = \vec{P}_{\text{just after}}\]
This "impulse approximation" is valid only during the collision interval. Before and after the collision, the particles follow ordinary dynamics under external forces. The conservation law bridges the two regimes.
