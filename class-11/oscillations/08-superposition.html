Superposition of SHMs

Adding two or more harmonic motions — the phasor method, beats, and Lissajous patterns.

Principle of Superposition

When two (or more) simple harmonic motions act on a particle simultaneously, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements:

\[x = x_1 + x_2\]

For two SHMs along the same direction:

\[x_1 = A_1\sin(\omega t + \phi_1), \qquad x_2 = A_2\sin(\omega t + \phi_2)\]

Same Frequency — Phasor Method

When two SHMs have the same frequency, their sum is also SHM of the same frequency:

\[x = A\sin(\omega t + \phi)\]

The resultant amplitude \(A\) and phase \(\phi\) are found by treating each SHM as a rotating vector (phasor) and performing vector addition:

\[A = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1 A_2\cos(\phi_2 - \phi_1)}\] \[\tan\phi = \frac{A_1\sin\phi_1 + A_2\sin\phi_2}{A_1\cos\phi_1 + A_2\cos\phi_2}\]

Special cases:

  • In phase (\(\Delta\phi = 0\)): \(A = A_1 + A_2\) (constructive)
  • Opposite phase (\(\Delta\phi = \pi\)): \(A = |A_1 - A_2|\) (destructive)
  • Quadrature (\(\Delta\phi = \pi/2\)): \(A = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2}\)
The phasor method converts trigonometric addition into simple vector addition in 2D. This is far faster than expanding and simplifying trig identities, especially for three or more SHMs.

Different Frequencies — Beats

When two SHMs of nearly equal frequencies \(\omega_1\) and \(\omega_2\) (with equal amplitudes \(A\)) are superposed:

\[x = x_1 + x_2 = 2A\cos\left(\frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{2}t\right)\sin\left(\frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2}t\right)\]

This describes an oscillation at the average frequency \(\frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2}\) with an amplitude that slowly varies at the difference frequency \(\frac{|\omega_1 - \omega_2|}{2}\).

The beat frequency (rate of amplitude maxima) is:

\[f_{\text{beat}} = |f_1 - f_2|\]

Perpendicular SHMs — Lissajous Figures

When two SHMs act in perpendicular directions:

\[x = A\sin(\omega_1 t), \qquad y = B\sin(\omega_2 t + \delta)\]

the particle traces a two-dimensional path called a Lissajous figure.

  • If \(\omega_1 = \omega_2\) and \(\delta = 0\): straight line
  • If \(\omega_1 = \omega_2\) and \(\delta = \pi/2\): ellipse (circle when \(A = B\))
  • If \(\omega_1/\omega_2 =\) simple ratio: closed curves (figure-8, etc.)
  • If \(\omega_1/\omega_2\) is irrational: the path never closes

Superposition of Many SHMs

For \(N\) SHMs of the same frequency with amplitudes \(A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_N\) and phases \(\phi_1, \phi_2, \ldots, \phi_N\), the phasor method extends naturally:

\[A_x = \sum_{i=1}^{N} A_i\cos\phi_i, \qquad A_y = \sum_{i=1}^{N} A_i\sin\phi_i\] \[A = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2}, \qquad \tan\phi = \frac{A_y}{A_x}\]
