Kinematics of SHM

Displacement, velocity, and acceleration — three sinusoidal functions locked in a permanent phase relationship.

Displacement

\[x(t) = A\sin(\omega t + \phi)\]

The displacement oscillates between \(-A\) and \(+A\). The argument of the sine — \((\omega t + \phi)\) — is called the phase of the motion.

Velocity

Differentiating displacement:

\[v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = A\omega\cos(\omega t + \phi)\]

In terms of displacement:

\[v = \pm\omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}\]
  • Maximum at equilibrium (\(x = 0\)): \(v_{\max} = A\omega\)
  • Zero at extremes (\(x = \pm A\))

Velocity leads displacement by a phase of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) (90°).

Acceleration

Differentiating velocity:

\[a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt} = -A\omega^2\sin(\omega t + \phi) = -\omega^2 x\]
  • Maximum at extremes (\(x = \pm A\)): \(a_{\max} = A\omega^2\)
  • Zero at equilibrium (\(x = 0\))

Acceleration leads displacement by a phase of \(\pi\) (180°) — it is always opposite in direction to displacement.

The phase relationships are: velocity is 90° ahead of displacement, acceleration is 180° ahead (i.e., opposite). When displacement is maximum, velocity is zero and acceleration is maximum (pointing back toward equilibrium). When displacement is zero, velocity is maximum and acceleration is zero.

Phase and Initial Conditions

The phase constant \(\phi\) is determined by the initial conditions \(x(0) = x_0\) and \(v(0) = v_0\):

\[x_0 = A\sin\phi, \qquad v_0 = A\omega\cos\phi\] \[\tan\phi = \frac{\omega x_0}{v_0}, \qquad A = \sqrt{x_0^2 + \left(\frac{v_0}{\omega}\right)^2}\]

Common initial conditions:

  • Released from extreme (\(x_0 = A, v_0 = 0\)): \(\phi = \frac{\pi}{2}\), so \(x = A\cos\omega t\).
  • Passing through mean position moving right (\(x_0 = 0, v_0 > 0\)): \(\phi = 0\), so \(x = A\sin\omega t\).
  • Passing through mean position moving left (\(x_0 = 0, v_0 < 0\)): \(\phi=\pi\).

Graphs of SHM

The \(x\text{–}t\), \(v\text{–}t\), and \(a\text{–}t\) graphs are all sinusoidal with the same period \(T\), but shifted by quarter periods:

  • \(x\text{–}t\): sine wave, amplitude \(A\)
  • \(v\text{–}t\): cosine wave (shifted left by \(T/4\)), amplitude \(A\omega\)
  • \(a\text{–}t\): negative sine wave (shifted left by \(T/2\)), amplitude \(A\omega^2\)

The \(v\text{–}x\) graph is an ellipse: \(\frac{v^2}{(A\omega)^2} + \frac{x^2}{A^2} = 1\).

The \(a\text{–}x\) graph is a straight line through the origin with slope \(-\omega^2\).
