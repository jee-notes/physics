Binary Star Systems & Two-Body Dynamics

Mechanics of mutually interacting gravitational systems, center of mass orbits, and the reduced mass formulation.

The Two-Body Problem Under Gravitational Interaction

In elementary planetary mechanics, we usually assume the central body (like the Sun or Earth) is fixed rigidly in space because its mass is vastly larger than the satellite's mass (\( M \gg m \)).

However, when two celestial bodies have comparable masses \( m_1 \) and \( m_2 \) (such as binary star pairs, the Pluto-Charon system, or the Earth-Moon system), neither star is stationary. Both stars revolve continuously in circular or elliptical orbits around their common Center of Mass (COM) under their mutual gravitational attraction.

Motion About Common Center of Mass

Let two stars of masses \( m_1 \) and \( m_2 \) be separated by a constant mutual distance \( d = r_1 + r_2 \).

By definition of the Center of Mass (chosen as the origin):

\[ m_1 r_1 = m_2 r_2 \implies r_2 = \frac{m_1}{m_2} r_1 \]

Using \( r_1 + r_2 = d \):

\[ r_1 + \frac{m_1}{m_2}r_1 = d \implies r_1 \left(\frac{m_1 + m_2}{m_2}\right) = d \]

Orbital Radii of the Two Stars

\[ r_1 = \left( \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \right) d, \quad r_2 = \left( \frac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \right) d \]
  • The heavier star moves in a smaller circle closer to the center of mass (\( r \propto 1/m \)).
  • The center of mass always lies on the line segment connecting the two stars.
  • Both stars are always on diametrically opposite sides of the center of mass at all instants, ensuring they have the exact same angular velocity \( \omega \) and the exact same orbital time period \( T \).

Orbital Radii, Speeds, and Common Time Period of Binary Stars

The mutual gravitational attraction between the two stars provides the necessary centripetal force for each star to maintain its circular orbit of radius \( r_1 \) and \( r_2 \) about the COM:

\[ F_{\text{grav}} = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{d^2} \]

Writing the equation of circular motion for star 1:

\[ G \frac{m_1 m_2}{d^2} = m_1 \omega^2 r_1 = m_1 \omega^2 \left( \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2} d \right) \]

Canceling \( m_1 m_2 \):

\[ \frac{G}{d^2} = \frac{\omega^2 d}{m_1 + m_2} \implies \omega^2 = \frac{G(m_1 + m_2)}{d^3} \]

Common Angular Velocity (\( \omega \)) and Time Period (\( T \))

\[ \omega = \sqrt{\frac{G(m_1 + m_2)}{d^3}} \] \[ T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{d^3}{G(m_1 + m_2)}} \]

Orbital Speeds

\[ v_1 = \omega r_1 = \frac{m_2}{\sqrt{m_1 + m_2}} \sqrt{\frac{G}{d}} \] \[ v_2 = \omega r_2 = \frac{m_1}{\sqrt{m_1 + m_2}} \sqrt{\frac{G}{d}} \] \[ \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{m_2}{m_1} \]
Notice that Kepler's Third Law generalizes seamlessly to binary systems: \[ T^2 = \left[ \frac{4\pi^2}{G(m_1 + m_2)} \right] d^3 \] When \( m_1 = M \) and \( m_2 = m \ll M \), this simplifies directly to the classic single-body Kepler's law: \( T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM} d^3 \).

Reduced Mass Concept in Gravitational Dynamics

The two-body problem can be elegantly transformed into an equivalent single-body problem by defining the reduced mass \( \mu \):

Reduced Mass Formulation

\[ \mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \quad \left( \frac{1}{\mu} = \frac{1}{m_1} + \frac{1}{m_2} \right) \]

In the relative coordinate frame, the relative position vector \( \vec{r} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1 \) (where \( |\vec{r}| = d \)) satisfies the single-body equation of motion:

\[ \mu \frac{d^2\vec{r}}{dt^2} = -G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}\hat{r} = -\frac{G (m_1 + m_2) \mu}{r^2}\hat{r} \]

Total Mechanical Energy in Terms of Reduced Mass

\[ E = K_{\text{total}} + U = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_2^2 - \frac{G m_1 m_2}{d} = \frac{1}{2}\mu (\omega d)^2 - \frac{G m_1 m_2}{d} \] \[ E = -\frac{G m_1 m_2}{2d} \]

Example: Symmetrical Two-Body Problem

Two identical stars of mass \( M \) each revolve in a circular orbit under their mutual gravitational attraction. If the distance between them is \( 2R \), find the speed of each star and the period of revolution.

Solution:

1. The distance between the stars is \( d = 2R \). The radius of the circular orbit of each star about the COM is \( r = R \).

2. Centripetal force equation for one star:

\[ \frac{G M^2}{(2R)^2} = \frac{M v^2}{R} \implies \frac{G M^2}{4R^2} = \frac{M v^2}{R} \] \[ v = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}} \]

3. Time period of revolution:

\[ T = \frac{2\pi R}{v} = \frac{2\pi R}{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}}} = 4\pi \sqrt{\frac{R^3}{GM}} \]
