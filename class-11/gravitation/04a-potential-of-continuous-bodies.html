Gravitational Potential of Continuous Bodies

Integration techniques and exact potential profiles for spherical shells, solid spheres, rings, and discs.

Gravitational Potential of Uniform Spherical Shell

Consider a thin spherical shell of mass \( M \) and radius \( R \). The gravitational potential at any distance \( r \) from its center is derived using \( V(r) = -\int_\infty^r I(r') dr' \):

1. Outside the Shell (\( r \ge R \))

\[ V_{\text{out}}(r) = -\int_\infty^r \left(\frac{GM}{{r'}^2}\right) dr' = -\frac{GM}{r} \]

2. On the Surface (\( r = R \))

\[ V_{\text{surface}} = -\frac{GM}{R} \]

3. Inside the Shell (\( r < R \))

Since \( I_{\text{in}} = 0 \), \( \frac{dV}{dr} = 0 \), meaning the potential inside a hollow shell is completely constant and equals its surface value:

\[ V_{\text{in}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{R} = \text{Constant} \]
No work is done in moving any test mass from one point inside a hollow spherical shell to another point inside it, because the potential is uniform throughout the interior cavity.

Gravitational Potential of Uniform Solid Sphere

Consider a solid sphere of uniform mass \( M \) and radius \( R \).

1. Outside the Sphere (\( r \ge R \))

\[ V_{\text{out}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{r} \]

2. On the Surface (\( r = R \))

\[ V_{\text{surface}} = -\frac{GM}{R} \]

3. Inside the Sphere (\( r \le R \))

Integrating the piecewise field from infinity to an internal radius \( r \):

\[ V_{\text{in}}(r) = V(R) - \int_R^r I_{\text{in}}(r') dr' = -\frac{GM}{R} - \int_R^r \frac{GM}{R^3} r' dr' \] \[ V_{\text{in}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{R} - \frac{GM}{2R^3} (r^2 - R^2) = -\frac{GM}{2R^3} \left[ 2R^2 + r^2 - R^2 \right] \] \[ V_{\text{in}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{2R^3} (3R^2 - r^2) \]

At the Center of the Sphere (\( r = 0 \)):

\[ V_{\text{center}} = -\frac{3GM}{2R} = 1.5 \, V_{\text{surface}} \]
Deepest Potential Well: The gravitational potential reaches its minimum (most negative) value at the very center of the sphere: \( V_{\text{center}} = \frac{3}{2} V_{\text{surface}} \).

Gravitational Potential Along the Axis of a Uniform Ring and Disc

1. Axis of a Uniform Ring of Mass \( M \) and Radius \( R \)

Every element \( dm \) of the ring is at the same distance \( s = \sqrt{R^2 + x^2} \) from an axial point \( P \) at distance \( x \) from the center. Since potential is a scalar:

\[ V_{\text{ring}}(x) = - \int \frac{G dm}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}} = -\frac{G}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}} \int dm = -\frac{GM}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}} \]

At the center (\( x = 0 \)): \( V_{\text{center}} = -\frac{GM}{R} \).

2. Axis of a Uniform Disc of Mass \( M \), Radius \( R \), and Surface Density \( \sigma = \frac{M}{\pi R^2} \)

Summing the potential contributions of concentric elemental rings of radius \( y \) and area \( 2\pi y dy \):

\[ V_{\text{disc}}(x) = -\int_0^R \frac{G (\sigma 2\pi y dy)}{\sqrt{y^2 + x^2}} = -2\pi G \sigma \left[ \sqrt{y^2 + x^2} \right]_0^R \] \[ V_{\text{disc}}(x) = -2\pi G \sigma \left( \sqrt{R^2 + x^2} - x \right) = -\frac{2GM}{R^2} \left( \sqrt{R^2 + x^2} - x \right) \]

At the center of the disc (\( x = 0 \)): \( V_{\text{center}} = -2\pi G \sigma R = -\frac{2GM}{R} \).

Example: Projection from Center of Earth

A particle is projected from the center of the Earth through a smooth diametrical tunnel with initial velocity \( v_0 \). What minimum velocity \( v_0 \) is required for the particle to reach infinity?

Solution:

By conservation of mechanical energy between the center (\( r=0 \)) and infinity (\( r=\infty \)):

\[ E_i = E_f \implies \frac{1}{2}m v_0^2 + m V_{\text{center}} = 0 + 0 \] \[ \frac{1}{2}m v_0^2 + m\left(-\frac{3GM}{2R}\right) = 0 \implies v_0^2 = \frac{3GM}{R} \] \[ v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{3GM}{R}} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{1.5} \, v_e \approx 1.225 \, v_e \]

The speed required from the center is \( \sqrt{1.5} \) times the surface escape speed.
