Escape Velocity

The threshold projection speed required for a projectile to overcome planetary gravitational binding.

Concept of Escape Velocity and Bound Systems

When a projectile of mass \( m \) is launched from a celestial body of mass \( M \) and radius \( R \), its total mechanical energy \( E = K + U \) dictates the nature of its trajectory:

  • \( E < 0 \) (Bound State): The projectile does not have sufficient energy to reach infinity. It follows an elliptical or parabolic sub-orbital trajectory and falls back or remains in orbit.
  • \( E = 0 \) (Parabolic Trajectory): The projectile just reaches infinity with zero residual kinetic energy (\( v_\infty = 0 \)). This is the critical threshold of escape.
  • \( E > 0 \) (Hyperbolic Trajectory): The projectile escapes to infinity and retains a non-zero residual speed \( v_\infty > 0 \).

Definition of Escape Velocity (\( v_e \))

Escape velocity is the minimum speed with which a body must be projected from a planet so that it completely overcomes the gravitational attraction of the planet and reaches infinity.

Derivation of Escape Velocity from Earth's Surface

Let a particle of mass \( m \) be projected with initial speed \( v_e \) from the surface of Earth (mass \( M \), radius \( R \)). Neglecting atmospheric drag, total mechanical energy is conserved:

\[ E_{\text{surface}} = K_i + U_i = \frac{1}{2}m v_e^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} \]

At infinity (\( r \to \infty \)), the potential energy \( U_\infty = 0 \). For minimum escape speed, the particle just reaches infinity with \( K_\infty = 0 \):

\[ E_\infty = K_\infty + U_\infty = 0 + 0 = 0 \]

Equating initial and final mechanical energy (\( E_{\text{surface}} = E_\infty \)):

\[ \frac{1}{2}m v_e^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} = 0 \implies \frac{1}{2}m v_e^2 = \frac{GMm}{R} \]

Escape Speed Formula

\[ v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2gR} \]

In terms of mean density \( \rho \) of the planet (\( M = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \rho \)):

\[ v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2G\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \rho\right)}{R}} = R \sqrt{\frac{8\pi G \rho}{3}} \]

Numerical Value for Earth

Taking \( g = 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2 \) and \( R = 6.4 \times 10^6 \, \text{m} \):

\[ v_e = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 6.4 \times 10^6} \approx 11.2 \times 10^3 \, \text{m/s} = 11.2 \, \text{km/s} \]

Escape Velocity from a Given Height or Depth

1. Escape Velocity from an Altitude \( h \) Above Surface

At radial distance \( r = R + h \):

\[ \frac{1}{2}m v_e'^2 - \frac{GMm}{R+h} = 0 \implies v_e' = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R+h}} = v_e \sqrt{\frac{R}{R+h}} \]

2. Escape Velocity from a Depth \( d \) Below Surface

Using the internal potential \( V_{\text{in}} = -\frac{GM}{2R^3}(3R^2 - r^2) \) with \( r = R - d \):

\[ \frac{1}{2}m v_e''^2 + m V_{\text{in}}(R-d) = 0 \implies v_e'' = \sqrt{-2 V_{\text{in}}(R-d)} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R^3}\left[ 3R^2 - (R-d)^2 \right]} \]

From the exact center of Earth (\( d = R \)):

\[ v_{e, \text{center}} = \sqrt{\frac{3GM}{R}} = \sqrt{1.5} \, v_e \approx 1.225 \, v_e \approx 13.7 \, \text{km/s} \]

Independence of Escape Velocity on Projection Angle and Mass

Key Invariances of Escape Velocity

  • Independent of Mass of Projectile: \( v_e \) is identical for a single hydrogen atom, a pebble, or a 100-ton spacecraft.
  • Independent of Angle of Projection: Because energy is a scalar quantity and gravity is conservative, a projectile launched at any angle \( \theta \) with the horizontal (provided it does not collide with the planetary surface) will successfully escape to infinity if launched with \( v \ge v_e \).
  • Residual Speed at Infinity (\( v_\infty \)): If launched with speed \( v > v_e \): \[ \frac{1}{2}m v^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} = \frac{1}{2}m v_\infty^2 \implies v_\infty = \sqrt{v^2 - v_e^2} \]

Black Holes and Schwarzschild Radius Concept

If a celestial body of mass \( M \) is compressed so intensely that its escape velocity equals or exceeds the speed of light (\( v_e \ge c \)), not even light can escape its gravitational field. Such an object is known as a Black Hole.

Setting \( v_e = c \) in the escape velocity equation:

\[ c = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R_s}} \implies R_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2} \]

Schwarzschild Radius (\( R_s \))

\[ R_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2} \]

The boundary at radius \( R_s \) is called the event horizon. For the Earth (\( M \approx 6 \times 10^{24} \, \text{kg} \)), the Schwarzschild radius is:

\[ R_s = \frac{2(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(6 \times 10^{24})}{(3 \times 10^8)^2} \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{m} \approx 9 \, \text{mm} \]

If Earth were crushed into a sphere smaller than a marble of radius 9 mm, it would become a black hole.
