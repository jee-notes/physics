Gravitational Potential & Potential Energy

Scalar energy formulation of gravitational interactions, potential functions, and gradient relationships.

Gravitational Potential Energy (U) of Point Masses

Because the gravitational force is strictly conservative, the work done by gravitational forces in moving a mass between two points depends solely on the initial and final positions, and is completely independent of the path taken.

The gravitational potential energy \( U \) of a two-particle system with masses \( M \) and \( m \) separated by distance \( r \) is defined as the external work done in assembling the system from an initial infinite separation (where \( U(\infty) = 0 \) is chosen as the standard zero reference):

\[ U(r) = - \int_{\infty}^r \vec{F}_g \cdot d\vec{r}' = -\int_{\infty}^r \left(-\frac{GMm}{{r'}^2}\right) dr' = GMm \left[ -\frac{1}{r'} \right]_\infty^r \]

Potential Energy Formula

\[ U(r) = -\frac{GMm}{r} \]
  • Scalar Quantity: Can be positive, zero, or negative.
  • SI Unit: Joule (\(\text{J}\)).
  • Dimensions: \([U] = [M^1 L^2 T^{-2}]\).
  • Significance of Negative Sign: A negative potential energy signifies a bound system. Positive work must be supplied from an external agent to separate the masses to infinity.

Concept and Definition of Gravitational Potential (V)

The gravitational potential \( V \) at any point in a gravitational field is defined as the work done by an external agent per unit mass in bringing a small test mass from infinity to that point slowly (without changing kinetic energy):

\[ V(\vec{r}) = \frac{U(\vec{r})}{m} = -\int_{\infty}^{\vec{r}} \vec{I} \cdot d\vec{r}' \]

Potential Due to an Isolated Point Mass \( M \)

\[ V(r) = -\frac{GM}{r} \]
  • Nature: Scalar field.
  • SI Units: \(\text{J/kg}\) or \(\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2\).
  • Dimensions: \([V] = [M^0 L^2 T^{-2}]\).
  • Superposition: For multiple point masses, potentials add algebraically: \[ V_{\text{net}} = \sum_{i=1}^N V_i = -G \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{M_i}{r_i} \]

Relation Between Gravitational Field and Potential (g = -grad V)

The relationship between the vector field intensity \( \vec{I} \) and the scalar potential \( V \) is given by the negative spatial gradient:

Vector Gradient Relationship

\[ \vec{I} = -\nabla V = -\left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\hat{i} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial y}\hat{j} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\hat{k} \right) \]

For spherically symmetric (radial) fields:

\[ I(r) = -\frac{dV}{dr} \implies \Delta V = V(r_2) - V(r_1) = -\int_{r_1}^{r_2} I(r) dr \]
Directional Takeaways:
  1. The gravitational field vector \( \vec{I} \) always points in the direction of the steepest decrease of gravitational potential \( V \).
  2. The slope of the \( V \text{ vs } r \) graph equals the negative of the gravitational field intensity: \( \text{Slope} = -I \).

Work Done in Moving Mass in a Gravitational Field

When a particle of mass \( m \) is displaced from point \( A \) (potential \( V_A \)) to point \( B \) (potential \( V_B \)):

  • Work done by internal gravitational force: \[ W_{\text{grav}} = -\Delta U = -(U_B - U_A) = -m(V_B - V_A) \]
  • Work done by external agent (moving without acceleration): \[ W_{\text{ext}} = \Delta U = U_B - U_A = m(V_B - V_A) \]

Work Done in Raising a Mass to Height \( h \) Above Earth's Surface

Find the minimum work done by an external agent to lift a mass \( m \) from the Earth's surface (\( r_1 = R \)) to an altitude \( h \) (\( r_2 = R+h \)).

Solution:

\[ W_{\text{ext}} = U_f - U_i = \left(-\frac{GMm}{R+h}\right) - \left(-\frac{GMm}{R}\right) = GMm \left( \frac{1}{R} - \frac{1}{R+h} \right) \] \[ W_{\text{ext}} = GMm \left( \frac{h}{R(R+h)} \right) = \frac{GM}{R^2} m \frac{h}{1 + \frac{h}{R}} = \frac{mgh}{1 + \frac{h}{R}} \]

Special Case (\( h \ll R \)): As \( \frac{h}{R} \to 0 \), \( W_{\text{ext}} \approx mgh \), recovering the familiar constant-gravity expression.
