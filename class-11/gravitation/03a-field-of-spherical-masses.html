Gravitational Field of Spherical Masses

Rigorous derivations and field distributions for uniform thin spherical shells and uniform solid spheres.

Field Due to Uniform Thin Spherical Shell (Internal, Surface, External)

Consider a thin spherical shell of total mass \( M \), uniform surface mass density \( \sigma = \frac{M}{4\pi R^2} \), and radius \( R \). Let \( r \) be the distance from the center of the shell to the field point \( P \).

1. Outside the Shell (\( r > R \))

By Newton's first shell theorem, the external field behaves exactly as if the entire mass \( M \) were concentrated at the center:

\[ I_{\text{out}} = \frac{GM}{r^2} \quad (\text{radially inward}) \]

2. On the Surface (\( r = R \))

\[ I_{\text{surface}} = \frac{GM}{R^2} \]

3. Inside the Shell (\( r < R \))

By Newton's second shell theorem, symmetrical cancellation of opposing solid angle elements ensures that the net gravitational field inside a hollow spherical shell is identically zero everywhere:

\[ I_{\text{in}} = 0 \]
Gravitational Shielding Fallacy: While \( I_{\text{in}} = 0 \) for the shell's own mass, a hollow spherical shell does not shield an inside object from the gravitational field of an external external mass! Gravity cannot be screened.

Field Due to Uniform Solid Sphere (Internal, Surface, External)

Consider a solid sphere of uniform mass density \( \rho = \frac{M}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3} \), total mass \( M \), and radius \( R \).

1. Outside the Sphere (\( r \ge R \))

The entire sphere acts as a point mass \( M \) located at its center:

\[ I_{\text{out}} = \frac{GM}{r^2} \]

2. On the Surface (\( r = R \))

\[ I_{\text{surface}} = \frac{GM}{R^2} = \frac{4}{3}\pi G \rho R \]

3. Inside the Sphere (\( r < R \))

Construct a concentric Gaussian sphere of radius \( r < R \). The shell outside radius \( r \) contributes zero field. Only the enclosed mass \( M_{\text{enc}} \) contributes:

\[ M_{\text{enc}} = \rho \left(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\right) = M \frac{r^3}{R^3} \] \[ I_{\text{in}} = \frac{G M_{\text{enc}}}{r^2} = \frac{G \left(M \frac{r^3}{R^3}\right)}{r^2} = \frac{GM}{R^3} r = \frac{4}{3}\pi G \rho r \]
SHM of a Particle Dropped in a Tunnel Through Earth:

Inside the solid Earth, the restoring force on a particle of mass \( m \) at distance \( r \) from the center is:

\[ F(r) = -m I_{\text{in}} = -\left(\frac{GMm}{R^3}\right) r = -k r \]

Since \( F \propto -r \), the motion is Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) with time period:

\[ T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{R^3}{GM}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{R}{g}} \approx 84.6 \, \text{minutes} \]

This period is completely independent of the mass \( m \) of the particle and the orientation or length of the chord/tunnel!

Non-Uniform Density Sphere (Advanced JEE Case)

If the density varies with distance as \( \rho(r) = C r^n \), the enclosed mass is:

\[ M(r) = \int_0^r \rho(r') 4\pi {r'}^2 dr' = 4\pi C \int_0^r {r'}^{n+2} dr' = \frac{4\pi C}{n+3} r^{n+3} \]

The internal field then scales as:

\[ I_{\text{in}}(r) = \frac{G M(r)}{r^2} = \frac{4\pi G C}{n+3} r^{n+1} \]

For a constant internal field \( I = \text{const} \), we must have \( n+1 = 0 \implies n = -1 \implies \rho(r) \propto \frac{1}{r} \).
