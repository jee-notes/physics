Conical Pendulum

Horizontal circular motion.

Concept

A conical pendulum is a special case of circular motion dynamics. We study it separately because its basic configuration involves circular motion in a horizontal plane.

Horizontal and vertical circular motions are categorized based on how gravity impacts the circular motion.

In horizontal circular motion, the gravitational height of the particle remains constant.

Consequently, its gravitational potential energy does not change, and gravity does no work. Gravity just plays the role of balancing the forces vertically to sustain planar horizontal motion.

We will study more about regular simple pendulum in the later SHM chapter.

For now, the setup of a conical pendulum comprises of a particle (bob) suspended from a string and made to perform circular motion in the horizontal plane.

The string tilts away from the vertical as the bob revolves.

The tension in the string has a vertical component that balances gravity and a horizontal component that provides the required centripetal force.

The string traces out the surface of a cone, which gives rise to the name conical pendulum.

In steady motion, the speed, radius of the circular path and angle made by the string with the vertical remain constant with time.

This approach of force-resolution is also used in banked circular roads, which we will study later.

The Conical Pendulum

Consider a bob of mass \( m \) tied to a string of length \( L \), whirling in a horizontal circle of radius \( R \) with speed \( v \) .

The string sweeps out a cone, making an angle \( \theta \) with the vertical. Let \( T \) be the tension in the string.

We resolve the tension \( T \) into two orthogonal components:

  1. A vertical component \( T \cos \theta \) which balances the gravitational force (weight).
  2. A horizontal component \( T \sin \theta \) which points toward the center of the circle and provides the required centripetal force.

We use the fact that as the motion is planar, the net of forces perpendicular to the plane cancel each other.

\[ T \cos \theta = mg \]

The component \( T \sin \theta\) is in the plane of the circle and there is no other force. So we can say that it provides centripetal force.

\[ T \sin \theta = \frac{mv^2}{R} \]

Dividing the second equation by the first equation eliminates the tension \( T \):

\[ \tan \theta = \frac{v^2}{Rg} \]

From this, we can determine the necessary speed \( v \) for a given angle \( \theta \):

\[ v = \sqrt{Rg \tan \theta} \]

Above is basic equation of conical pendulum. In this equation, \(g\) is constant. So, the 3 variables \(v\) , \(R\) and \(\theta\) get related.

However, in this case, the radius \(R\) too is related to the angle \(\theta\) through string length \( L \) which is constant by geometric relation (\( R = L \sin \theta \)). And so, we can treat \( v \) too only as a function of \(\theta\) .

\[ v = \sqrt{Lg \tan \theta \sin \theta} \]
The geometrical constraint in a conical pendulum is different from that in a banked circular track.
In a conical pendulum, the string length is fixed, so the radius depends on the angle: \[ R=L\sin\theta \] In an idealised banked circular track, the banking angle remains fixed while the radius can vary.
Consequently, the required speed changes with radius.
This distinction will be useful when we study banked roads and velodromes.
Velodromes are funnel like structures used by cyclists.

Time Period

In SHM, we study that pendulum is a special machine that works under gravity and provides us motion whose time period is important.

In conical pendulum, we have to find the time period \( P \) of one complete revolution (\( P = 2\pi R / v \)):

\[ P = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L \cos \theta}{g}} \]
We use different variable for time period as (\(P\) for Period) because (\(T\) for Tension).

This relation is important because the period \(P\) depends on height \(H\) alone \(L \cos \theta = H\).

\[ P = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{H}{g}} \]

Two different pendulums at same height from suspension point will have same time period even if their length of strings and speed are different.
