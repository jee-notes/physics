Centripetal and Tangential Acceleration

The two components of acceleration in a non-linear path.

1. Centripetal Acceleration (\(a_c\))

Also known as radial acceleration, it is always directed toward the center of the circle. It is responsible for changing the direction of the velocity vector.

\[ a_c = \frac{v^2}{R} = R\omega^2 \]
Centripetal acceleration is present in every circular motion, even if the speed is constant (Uniform Circular Motion).

2. Tangential Acceleration (\(a_t\))

This component acts along the tangent to the path and is responsible for changing the magnitude (speed) of the velocity.

\[ a_t = \frac{dv}{dt} = R\alpha \]

3. Net Acceleration (\(a_{net}\))

Since \(a_c\) and \(a_t\) are perpendicular to each other:

\[ a_{net} = \sqrt{a_c^2 + a_t^2} \]

In Uniform Circular Motion (UCM), \(a_t = 0\), so \(a_{net} = a_c\).
