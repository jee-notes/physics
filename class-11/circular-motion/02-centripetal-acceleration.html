Centripetal and Tangential Acceleration

The two orthogonal components of acceleration in a non-linear path.

1. Centripetal Acceleration (\(a_c\))

Centripetal acceleration (also known as radial or normal acceleration) is the component of acceleration directed toward the center of the circular path. Its primary role is to continuously change the direction of the velocity vector, keeping the particle on a curved trajectory.

\[ a_c = \frac{v^2}{R} = R\omega^2 = v\omega \]
  • \( v \): Instantaneous linear speed.
  • \( \omega \): Instantaneous angular velocity.
  • \( R \): Radius of the circular path.
SI Unit: meter per second squared (\(\text{m/s}^2\))
Dimensional Formula: \([M^0 L^1 T^{-2}]\)

In vector notation, since centripetal acceleration points towards the center (opposite to the radial position vector \( \vec{r} \)), it is written as:

\[ \vec{a}_c = -\omega^2 \vec{r} \]
Centripetal acceleration is an absolute requirement for circular motion. It is present in every circular motion, even in Uniform Circular Motion (UCM) where the speed is strictly constant. Without \(a_c\), the object would simply fly off in a straight line tangent to the circle.

2. Tangential Acceleration (\(a_t\))

Tangential acceleration is the component of acceleration that acts along the tangent to the circular path. Its sole physical purpose is to change the magnitude (speed) of the velocity vector.

\[ a_t = \frac{dv}{dt} = R\alpha \]
  • \( \frac{dv}{dt} \): Rate of change of instantaneous speed.
  • \( \alpha \): Angular acceleration.
SI Unit: meter per second squared (\(\text{m/s}^2\))
Dimensional Formula: \([M^0 L^1 T^{-2}]\)

If the tangential acceleration is positive, the particle speeds up. If it is negative, the particle slows down.

In Uniform Circular Motion (UCM), the speed \(v\) is constant, meaning \( \frac{dv}{dt} = 0 \). Therefore, tangential acceleration \( a_t = 0 \). Tangential acceleration only exists in Non-Uniform Circular Motion.

3. Net Acceleration (\(a_{net}\))

In a general circular motion, the particle experiences both radial and tangential changes. Since the radial vector and the tangent vector are always perpendicular (\(90^\circ\)) to each other, the net acceleration \( \vec{a}_{net} \) is the vector sum of these two mutually orthogonal components.

\[ \vec{a}_{net} = \vec{a}_c + \vec{a}_t \]

The magnitude of the total net acceleration is calculated using the Pythagorean theorem:

\[ a_{net} = \sqrt{a_c^2 + a_t^2} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{v^2}{R}\right)^2 + (R\alpha)^2} \]

Let \( \phi \) be the angle that the net acceleration vector makes with the radial direction (towards the center). The direction is given by:

\[ \tan \phi = \frac{a_t}{a_c} \]

Summary of Motion Types

  • Uniform Circular Motion (UCM): \( a_t = 0, \, a_c \neq 0 \implies a_{net} = a_c \)
  • Non-Uniform Circular Motion: \( a_t \neq 0, \, a_c \neq 0 \implies a_{net} = \sqrt{a_c^2 + a_t^2} \)
  • Linear Motion: \( a_t \neq 0, \, a_c = 0 \) (since \(R \to \infty\))
