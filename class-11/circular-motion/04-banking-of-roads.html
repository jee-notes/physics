Banking of Roads and Level Turns

How geometry helps vehicles navigate curves safely.

1. Negotiating a Curve on a Level Road

On a flat, level road, the only force providing the necessary centripetal force is static friction between the tires and the road.

\[ f_s = \frac{mv^2}{R} \leq \mu_s N \] \[ \implies v_{max} = \sqrt{\mu_s Rg} \]
If the speed exceeds this value, the required centripetal force will be greater than the maximum available friction, and the car will skid outwards.

2. Why Bank Roads?

Banking means raising the outer edge of the road relative to the inner edge. This allows a component of the Normal Reaction force to contribute to the centripetal force, reducing reliance on friction.

3. The Ideal Banking Angle

In the absence of friction (or to minimize tire wear), we can find an "optimum speed" (\(v_o\)) where the horizontal component of the normal force provides exactly the required centripetal force.

\[ N \cos \theta = mg \] \[ N \sin \theta = \frac{mv^2}{R} \] \[ \implies \tan \theta = \frac{v^2}{Rg} \]
