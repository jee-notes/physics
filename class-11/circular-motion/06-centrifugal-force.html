Centrifugal Force & Rotating Frames

The pseudo-force that emerges in accelerated reference systems.

1. What is Centrifugal Force?

Centrifugal force is a pseudo-force (or inertial force) that appears to act on all objects when viewed from a rotating reference frame. It acts radially outward from the axis of rotation.

\[ F_{cf} = m \omega^2 R \]

2. Real vs. Pseudo

  • Centripetal Force: A real force (tension, gravity, etc.) seen in an inertial frame. It is the CAUSE of the acceleration.
  • Centrifugal Force: An apparent force seen in a non-inertial (rotating) frame. It is used to explain why an object doesn't fly toward the center despite the centripetal pull.

3. Applications

Rotating frames are useful for analyzing complex systems like:

  • Centrifuges: Used to separate substances of different densities (like blood cells from plasma).
  • Washing Machine Dryers: Spinning drums that "flick" water out of clothes.
  • Artificial Gravity: Conceptual space stations that rotate to simulate gravitational pull on the outer hull.
To a person inside a rotating spaceship, the "floor" is the outer wall of the station, and the "centrifugal force" feels exactly like gravity pulling them down.
