Gas Mixtures & Dalton's Law

Partial pressure distributions, molar averages, volumetric fractions, and moist gas hygrometric corrections.

Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures

Formulated by John Dalton (1801):

Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures: The total pressure exerted by a mixture of non-reacting ideal gases enclosed in a common volume \( V \) at temperature \( T \) is equal to the sum of the partial pressures that each individual gas would exert if it occupied the entire container volume alone at the same temperature: \[ P_{\text{total}} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots + P_k = \sum_{i=1}^k P_i \]

Applying the Ideal Gas Law to each individual component:

\[ P_i = \frac{n_i R T}{V} \]

Summing all components:

\[ P_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{n_i R T}{V} = \left( \sum_{i=1}^k n_i \right) \frac{R T}{V} = \frac{n_{\text{total}} R T}{V} \]
Dalton's law is strictly valid only for chemically non-reacting ideal gases (e.g., \( \text{N}_2 + \text{O}_2 \), \( \text{He} + \text{Ar} \)). If gases react chemically (such as \( \text{NH}_3 + \text{HCl} \to \text{NH}_4\text{Cl(s)} \) or \( 2\text{NO} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{NO}_2 \)), the total number of moles changes and Dalton's Law cannot be applied directly without stoichiometric adjustment.

Amagat's Law of Partial Volumes

Complementary to Dalton's Law:

Amagat-Leduc Law: The total volume \( V_{\text{total}} \) of a mixture of non-reacting ideal gases maintained at constant total pressure \( P \) and temperature \( T \) is equal to the sum of the partial volumes that each constituent gas would occupy individually at that same \( P \) and \( T \): \[ V_{\text{total}} = V_1 + V_2 + \dots + V_k = \sum_{i=1}^k V_i \]

where the Partial Volume (\( V_i \)) of component \( i \) is:

\[ V_i = \frac{n_i R T}{P_{\text{total}}} \]

Mole Fraction and Pressure Fraction

The Mole Fraction (\( x_i \)) of gas \( i \) in a mixture containing \( n_{\text{total}} = \sum n_j \) total moles is defined as:

\[ x_i = \frac{n_i}{n_{\text{total}}} = \frac{n_i}{\sum n_j} \quad \left(\text{such that } \sum x_i = 1\right) \]

Dividing the partial pressure \( P_i \) by the total pressure \( P_{\text{total}} \):

\[ \frac{P_i}{P_{\text{total}}} = \frac{\left(\frac{n_i R T}{V}\right)}{\left(\frac{n_{\text{total}} R T}{V}\right)} = \frac{n_i}{n_{\text{total}}} = x_i \]
\[ P_i = x_i P_{\text{total}} \]

Similarly, for partial volumes:

\[ \frac{V_i}{V_{\text{total}}} = x_i \implies V_i = x_i V_{\text{total}} \]
For ideal gas mixtures: \[ \text{Mole Fraction } (x_i) = \text{Pressure Fraction } \left(\frac{P_i}{P_{\text{total}}}\right) = \text{Volume Fraction } \left(\frac{V_i}{V_{\text{total}}}\right) \]

Equivalent Molar Mass and Density of Gas Mixtures

1. Apparent / Equivalent Molar Mass (\( M_{\text{mix}} \))

The equivalent molar mass of a gas mixture is defined as the total mass of the mixture divided by the total number of moles:

\[ M_{\text{mix}} = \frac{m_{\text{total}}}{n_{\text{total}}} = \frac{\sum m_i}{\sum n_i} = \frac{\sum n_i M_i}{\sum n_i} = \sum_{i=1}^k x_i M_i \]

2. Mass Density of a Gas Mixture

Applying the ideal gas equation of state to the mixture:

\[ \rho_{\text{mix}} = \frac{P_{\text{total}} M_{\text{mix}}}{R T} \]

Example: Apparent Molar Mass of Atmospheric Air

Atmospheric dry air is composed of approximately \( 80\% \, \text{N}_2 \) (\( M_1 = 28\text{ g/mol} \)) and \( 20\% \, \text{O}_2 \) (\( M_2 = 32\text{ g/mol} \)) by mole. Find the apparent molar mass and density of air at STP (\( 1\text{ atm}, 273\text{ K} \)).

Solution:

\[ M_{\text{air}} = x_1 M_1 + x_2 M_2 = (0.80)(28) + (0.20)(32) = 22.4 + 6.4 = 28.8\text{ g/mol} = 0.0288\text{ kg/mol} \] \[ \rho_{\text{air}} = \frac{P M}{R T} = \frac{(1.013 \times 10^5)(0.0288)}{8.314 \times 273} \approx 1.284\text{ kg/m}^3 \]

Moist Gas and Aqueous Tension Corrections

When an insoluble gas is collected in the laboratory by downward displacement of water, the collected gas bubble becomes saturated with water vapor.

The observed total pressure of the moist gas is the sum of the dry gas partial pressure and the saturated water vapor pressure (Aqueous Tension \( p_v \)):

\[ P_{\text{moist}} = P_{\text{dry gas}} + p_v \iff P_{\text{dry gas}} = P_{\text{moist}} - p_v \]

Relative Humidity (\(\text{RH}\)):

In unsaturated atmospheric conditions, the Relative Humidity is defined as:

\[ \text{RH} = \frac{\text{Partial pressure of water vapor present in air}}{\text{Saturated vapor pressure of water at the same temperature } (p_v)} \times 100\% \]
