Capillary Tubes & Mercury Pellets

Trapped air column dynamics, orientation-dependent hydrostatic equilibria, tilting angles, and non-inertial frame kinetics.

Trapped Air Column with Mercury Pellet

A uniform bore capillary tube closed at one end traps a fixed mass of dry air beneath a liquid mercury pellet (thread) of length \( h \). Because the temperature is kept constant and the tube cross-sectional area \( A \) is uniform:

\[ \text{Air Volume: } V = A \cdot l \implies P \cdot l = \text{constant} \quad (\text{by Boyle's Law}) \]

where \( l \) is the length of the enclosed air column and \( P \) is the pressure of the trapped air.

Vertical, Horizontal, and Inverted Orientations

Let \( P_0 \) be the atmospheric pressure expressed in equivalent height of mercury column (e.g., \( P_0 = 76\text{ cm of Hg} \)):

1. Tube Placed Horizontally:

The mercury pellet experiences equal atmospheric pressure on its open surface and gas pressure on its inner surface:

\[ P_h = P_0 \implies P_0 \cdot l_0 = C \]

2. Tube Placed Vertically with Open End Upwards:

The trapped air supports both the atmospheric pressure and the weight of the mercury thread:

\[ P_v = P_0 + h \implies (P_0 + h) l_1 = P_0 l_0 \]

3. Tube Placed Vertically with Open End Downwards (Inverted):

The upward atmospheric pressure supports both the trapped air pressure and the downward weight of mercury:

\[ P_{\text{inv}} = P_0 - h \implies (P_0 - h) l_2 = P_0 l_0 \]
Comparing the three states: \[ (P_0 + h) l_1 = P_0 l_0 = (P_0 - h) l_2 \implies l_1 < l_0 < l_2 \] From this relation, the atmospheric pressure \( P_0 \) can be directly calculated: \[ P_0 = h \left( \frac{l_2 + l_1}{l_2 - l_1} \right) \]

Tilting of Capillary Tubes at an Angle

When the capillary tube is inclined at an angle \( \theta \) with the horizontal:

The effective vertical hydrostatic head contributed by the mercury thread is \( h_{\text{eff}} = h \sin\theta \).

  • Open End Pointing Upwards at Angle \( \theta \) to horizontal:
    \[ P = P_0 + h \sin\theta \]
  • Open End Pointing Downwards at Angle \( \theta \) to horizontal:
    \[ P = P_0 - h \sin\theta \]
  • If angle \( \alpha \) is measured with the vertical: \( h_{\text{eff}} = h \cos\alpha \).

Accelerating and Rotating Capillary Systems

1. Linearly Accelerating Vertical Capillary Tube

If the tube is in an elevator accelerating vertically with acceleration \( a \):

  • Accelerating Upward (\( g_{\text{eff}} = g + a \)): \[ P = P_0 + h\left(1 + \frac{a}{g}\right) \quad (\text{Open up}) \]
  • Accelerating Downward (\( g_{\text{eff}} = g - a \)): \[ P = P_0 + h\left(1 - \frac{a}{g}\right) \quad (\text{Open up}) \]
  • Free Fall (\( a = g \)): \( P = P_0 \) (Weightlessness).

2. Horizontally Accelerating Capillary Tube

If a horizontal tube accelerates along its axis with horizontal acceleration \( a_x \):

\[ P_{\text{gas}} = P_0 + \rho a_x h \]

3. Rotating Horizontal Capillary Tube

A horizontal tube is rotated about a vertical axis through its closed end with angular velocity \( \omega \). A mercury pellet of length \( h \) is located between radii \( r_1 \) and \( r_2 = r_1 + h \):

\[ dP = \rho \omega^2 r \, dr \implies \Delta P_{\text{centrifugal}} = \int_{r_1}^{r_2} \rho \omega^2 r \, dr = \frac{1}{2}\rho \omega^2 (r_2^2 - r_1^2) \]
\[ P_{\text{trapped gas}} = P_0 - \frac{1}{2}\rho \omega^2 (r_2^2 - r_1^2) \]
