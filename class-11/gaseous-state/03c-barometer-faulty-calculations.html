Faulty Barometer Calculations

Torricellian vacuum imperfections, trapped air column corrections, and aqueous vapor tension.

Torricellian Vacuum and Ideal Barometer

An ideal Torricellian mercury barometer consists of a long glass tube of uniform bore (length \( \approx 1\text{ m} \)) filled completely with pure, dry mercury and inverted into a mercury cistern.

In an ideal barometer:

  • The space above the mercury column is a pure Torricellian Vacuum (\( P_{\text{top}} = 0 \), neglecting negligible mercury vapor pressure \( \approx 0.0012\text{ mmHg} \) at room temperature).
  • The vertical height of the mercury column \( H_0 \) directly indicates true atmospheric pressure: \[ P_{\text{atm}} = H_0 \quad (\text{in cm or mm of Hg}) \]

Faulty Barometer with Trapped Air Column

If a small air bubble or moisture accidentally leaks into the space above the mercury column, the barometer becomes faulty.

Let:

  • \( L \): Total height of the closed top of the barometer tube above the cistern mercury level.
  • \( h \): Observed height of the mercury column (\( h < H_0 \)).
  • \( l = L - h \): Length of the trapped air column in the tube of cross-sectional area \( A \).
  • \( P_{\text{air}} \): Pressure exerted downward by the trapped air.

Balancing pressures at the level of the open mercury surface:

\[ P_{\text{atm}} = h + P_{\text{air}} \iff P_{\text{air}} = P_{\text{atm}} - h \]

Since the mass of trapped air and temperature remain constant, the trapped air obeys Boyle's Law:

\[ P_{\text{air}} \cdot V_{\text{air}} = \text{constant} \iff (P_{\text{atm}} - h)(L - h)A = \text{constant} \] \[ (P_{\text{atm}1} - h_1)(L - h_1) = (P_{\text{atm}2} - h_2)(L - h_2) \]

Aqueous Tension and Vapor Pressure Corrections

When a drop of water is introduced into the barometer tube, it vaporizes into the vacuum space until saturated water vapor is formed.

The pressure exerted by saturated water vapor at a given temperature is called Aqueous Tension (\( p_v \)):

\[ P_{\text{atm}} = h_{\text{obs}} + P_{\text{dry air}} + p_v \]
Aqueous tension \( p_v \) depends solely on temperature and is completely independent of the volume of the space above the mercury column, provided liquid water droplets remain present to maintain vapor saturation.

True Atmospheric Pressure Determination

Example: Finding True Pressure from a Faulty Barometer

The height of mercury in a faulty barometer is \( 70\text{ cm} \) when the true atmospheric pressure is \( 76\text{ cm of Hg} \). The length of the air column above mercury is \( 10\text{ cm} \). What will be the true atmospheric pressure when this barometer reads \( 68\text{ cm} \)? (Assume constant temperature).

Solution:

Case 1:

  • Observed reading \( h_1 = 70\text{ cm} \)
  • Length of air column \( l_1 = 10\text{ cm} \)
  • Total tube height above cistern: \( L = h_1 + l_1 = 70 + 10 = 80\text{ cm} \)
  • True atmospheric pressure \( P_1 = 76\text{ cm of Hg} \)
  • Pressure of trapped air: \( P_{\text{air},1} = P_1 - h_1 = 76 - 70 = 6\text{ cm of Hg} \)

Case 2:

  • Observed reading \( h_2 = 68\text{ cm} \)
  • Length of air column: \( l_2 = L - h_2 = 80 - 68 = 12\text{ cm} \)
  • Let \( P_2 \) be the unknown true atmospheric pressure.
  • Pressure of trapped air: \( P_{\text{air},2} = P_2 - h_2 = P_2 - 68 \)

Applying Boyle's Law on the trapped air column (\( P_{\text{air},1} l_1 = P_{\text{air},2} l_2 \)):

\[ 6 \times 10 = (P_2 - 68) \times 12 \] \[ 60 = 12(P_2 - 68) \implies P_2 - 68 = 5 \implies P_2 = 73\text{ cm of Hg} \]

The true atmospheric pressure is \( 73\text{ cm of Hg} \).
