Ideal Gas Equation & Mechanical Systems

Equation of state derivation, universal gas constants, gas density relations, and variable-mass open vessels.

Derivation of the Ideal Gas Equation of State

The behavior of an ideal gas across all arbitrary thermodynamic states is unified by combining the three fundamental empirical laws:

  1. According to Boyle's Law: \( V \propto \frac{1}{P} \) (at constant \( T, n \))
  2. According to Charles' Law: \( V \propto T \) (at constant \( P, n \))
  3. According to Avogadro's Law: \( V \propto n \) (at constant \( P, T \))

Combining these three simultaneous proportionalities:

\[ V \propto \frac{n T}{P} \implies V = R \frac{n T}{P} \]
\[ PV = nRT \]

where \( R \) is the Universal Gas Constant.

Alternative Microscopic and Mass Formulations:

  • In terms of Total Number of Molecules (\( N \)): \[ PV = \left(\frac{N}{N_A}\right) R T = N \left(\frac{R}{N_A}\right) T = N k_B T \]
  • In terms of Total Mass (\( m \)) and Molar Mass (\( M \)): \[ PV = \left(\frac{m}{M}\right) R T \]

Universal Gas Constant and Boltzmann Constant

1. Universal Gas Constant (\( R \))

\( R \) is a universal physical constant representing the molar work done by a gas during isobaric thermal expansion per Kelvin:

\[ R = \frac{PV}{nT} = \frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Mole} \cdot \text{Temperature}} \]

Dimensional Formula: \([M^1 L^2 T^{-2} K^{-1} \text{mol}^{-1}]\)

System of Units Value of \( R \) Practical Application Context
SI Units \( 8.31446 \, \text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} \) (\(\approx \frac{25}{3}\)) Thermodynamic work, internal energy, speed of sound, KTG.
Litre-Atmosphere \( 0.08206 \, \text{L}\cdot\text{atm}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} \) (\(\approx \frac{1}{12}\)) Direct volume-pressure calculations in laboratory chemistry.
Calorie \( \approx 1.987 \, \text{cal}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} \approx 2 \, \text{cal}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} \) Calorimetry and thermal capacity problems.
CGS Units \( 8.314 \times 10^7 \, \text{erg}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} \) CGS dynamical calculations.

2. Boltzmann Constant (\( k_B \))

The gas constant per individual molecule:

\[ k_B = \frac{R}{N_A} \approx \frac{8.314}{6.022 \times 10^{23}} \approx 1.3806 \times 10^{-23} \, \text{J}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} \]

Dimensional Formula: \([M^1 L^2 T^{-2} K^{-1}]\)

Gas Density and Molar Mass Relationships

From the mass form of the ideal gas law:

\[ PV = \frac{m}{M} R T \implies P M = \left(\frac{m}{V}\right) R T \]

Since mass density is \( \rho = \frac{m}{V} \):

\[ \rho = \frac{P M}{R T} \iff \frac{P}{\rho T} = \frac{R}{M} = \text{constant for a given gas} \]
Physical Implications of the Density Formula:
  1. Pressure Dependence: At constant temperature, \( \rho \propto P \). Compressing a gas increases its density linearly.
  2. Temperature Dependence: At constant pressure, \( \rho \propto \frac{1}{T} \). Heating a gas causes thermal expansion, decreasing density (driving buoyant convection).
  3. Molar Mass Dependence: Under identical \( P \) and \( T \), \( \rho \propto M \). Heavier gases are denser and settle downward.

Characteristic Gas Constant

In engineering thermodynamics, the Characteristic Gas Constant (\( r \) or \( R_{\text{specific}} \)) is defined per unit mass rather than per mole:

\[ r = \frac{R}{M} \]

SI Unit: \(\text{J}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}\)  |  Dimensional Formula: \([L^2 T^{-2} K^{-1}]\)

The ideal gas equation in terms of specific volume \( v = \frac{V}{m} = \frac{1}{\rho} \) becomes:

\[ P v = r T \]

For atmospheric air (\( M_{\text{air}} \approx 28.97\text{ g/mol} = 0.02897\text{ kg/mol} \)):

\[ r_{\text{air}} = \frac{8.314}{0.02897} \approx 287 \, \text{J}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} \]

Equation of State for Open Systems and Changing Mass

When a gas container is unsealed (open flask) or develops a leak, the mass/moles of gas inside the vessel change over time.

Heating of an Open Vessel:

Consider a rigid flask of fixed volume \( V \) open to the atmosphere. Because the flask is open, the interior pressure is clamped to atmospheric pressure (\( P = P_0 = \text{constant} \)).

When heated from initial temperature \( T_1 \) to final temperature \( T_2 \):

\[ P_0 V = n_1 R T_1 = n_2 R T_2 \implies n_1 T_1 = n_2 T_2 \]
\[ \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{T_1}{T_2} \]
  • Fraction of Gas Remaining in Vessel: \( f_{\text{remain}} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{T_1}{T_2} \)
  • Fraction of Gas Expelled / Escaped:
    \[ f_{\text{expelled}} = \frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1} = 1 - \frac{T_1}{T_2} = \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_2} \]

Example: Air Expelled on Heating an Open Vessel

An open glass bulb contains air at \( 27^\circ\text{C} \). To what temperature must it be heated so that one-third (\( 1/3 \)) of the air present initially is expelled into the atmosphere?

Solution:

Initial temperature \( T_1 = 27 + 273 = 300\text{ K} \).
Fraction of air expelled:

\[ f_{\text{expelled}} = \frac{1}{3} \implies f_{\text{remain}} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3} \]

Applying \( n_1 T_1 = n_2 T_2 \):

\[ T_2 = T_1 \left( \frac{n_1}{n_2} \right) = 300 \left( \frac{3}{2} \right) = 450\text{ K} \]

In Celsius:

\[ t_2 = 450 - 273 = 177^\circ\text{C} \]
