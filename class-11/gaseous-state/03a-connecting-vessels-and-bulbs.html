Connected Vessels & Bulbs

Mass conservation principles, stopcock mixing dynamics, temperature differential baths, and partition equilibria.

Two-Bulb System with Stopcock

A classical paradigm in JEE thermal physics involves two rigid vessels of volumes \( V_1 \) and \( V_2 \) containing ideal gases at initial states \( (P_1, V_1, T_1) \) and \( (P_2, V_2, T_2) \), connected by a narrow tube of negligible volume with a valve (stopcock).

The total initial moles of gas in the combined closed system is conserved:

\[ n_{\text{total}} = n_1 + n_2 = \frac{P_1 V_1}{R T_1} + \frac{P_2 V_2}{R T_2} \]

When the stopcock is opened, gas flows between the vessels until a uniform final equilibrium pressure \( P_f \) is established throughout the entire combined volume \( V_{\text{total}} = V_1 + V_2 \).

Common Equilibrium Pressure and Temperature

Case 1: Isothermal Mixing (Constant Temperature \( T \))

If both bulbs are maintained at the same constant temperature \( T \) throughout the process:

\[ n_{\text{total}} = \frac{P_f (V_1 + V_2)}{R T} = \frac{P_1 V_1}{R T} + \frac{P_2 V_2}{R T} \]
\[ P_f = \frac{P_1 V_1 + P_2 V_2}{V_1 + V_2} \]

Case 2: Thermally Insulated (Adiabatic) Mixing

If the bulbs are rigid and completely thermally insulated from the surroundings (\( W = 0, Q = 0 \)): By the First Law of Thermodynamics, the total internal energy is conserved:

\[ \Delta U = 0 \implies U_{\text{final}} = U_1 + U_2 \] \[ (n_1 + n_2) C_V T_f = n_1 C_{V1} T_1 + n_2 C_{V2} T_2 \]

For the same gas (\( C_{V1} = C_{V2} = C_V \)):

\[ T_f = \frac{n_1 T_1 + n_2 T_2}{n_1 + n_2} = \frac{P_1 V_1 + P_2 V_2}{\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} + \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}} \]

The final common equilibrium pressure is then:

\[ P_f = \frac{(n_1 + n_2) R T_f}{V_1 + V_2} = \frac{P_1 V_1 + P_2 V_2}{V_1 + V_2} \]
Remarkably, for adiabatic mixing of the same ideal gas, the final equilibrium pressure \( P_f = \frac{P_1 V_1 + P_2 V_2}{V_1 + V_2} \) takes the exact same mathematical form as isothermal mixing, because \( P \propto U/V \) for an ideal gas!

Connecting Tubes at Different Constant Temperatures

Consider two bulbs of volumes \( V_1 \) and \( V_2 \) initially containing \( n_0 \) total moles of gas at initial pressure \( P_0 \) and uniform initial temperature \( T_0 \). The valve is kept open, but bulb 1 is placed in a thermal bath at temperature \( T_1 \) and bulb 2 is placed in a thermal bath at temperature \( T_2 \).

Because the connecting tube remains open, the pressure in both vessels must equalize to a common final steady-state value \( P \):

\[ P_1 = P_2 = P \]

By conservation of total moles:

\[ n_1 + n_2 = n_{\text{total}} \] \[ \frac{P V_1}{R T_1} + \frac{P V_2}{R T_2} = \frac{P_0 (V_1 + V_2)}{R T_0} \]
\[ P = P_0 (V_1 + V_2) \left[ \frac{1}{T_0 \left( \frac{V_1}{T_1} + \frac{V_2}{T_2} \right)} \right] = \frac{n_{\text{total}} R}{\frac{V_1}{T_1} + \frac{V_2}{T_2}} \]

Example: Equal Bulbs in Hot and Cold Baths

Two identical glass bulbs of volume \( V \) are connected by a narrow tube and filled with gas at \( 0^\circ\text{C} \) and \( 76\text{ cm of Hg} \). One bulb is immersed in melting ice (\( 0^\circ\text{C} \)) and the other in steam at \( 100^\circ\text{C} \). Find the resulting common pressure.

Solution:

Given \( V_1 = V_2 = V \), \( P_0 = 76\text{ cm Hg} \), \( T_0 = 273\text{ K} \), \( T_1 = 273\text{ K} \), \( T_2 = 373\text{ K} \).

\[ \frac{P V}{R(273)} + \frac{P V}{R(373)} = \frac{P_0 (2V)}{R(273)} \] \[ P \left[ \frac{1}{273} + \frac{1}{373} \right] = \frac{2 P_0}{273} \] \[ P \left[ \frac{373 + 273}{273 \times 373} \right] = \frac{2 \times 76}{273} \] \[ P \left[ \frac{646}{373} \right] = 152 \implies P = \frac{152 \times 373}{646} \approx 87.77\text{ cm of Hg} \]

Multiple Bulb Systems and Partition Walls

For a container divided into \( k \) chambers by movable frictionless pistons:

  1. Mechanical Equilibrium: All internal movable pistons must experience equal net force, requiring uniform pressure across all compartments: \( P_1 = P_2 = \dots = P_k \).
  2. Diathermic Partitions: Temperatures equalize across conductive walls: \( T_1 = T_2 = \dots = T_k \).
  3. Adiabatic Partitions: Each compartment undergoes an independent reversible adiabatic process (\( P V^\gamma = \text{constant} \)) until mechanical force balance is achieved.
