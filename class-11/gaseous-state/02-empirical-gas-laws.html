Historical Development of Gas Laws

The formulation of thermal physics began with controlled experimental observations of gases. By holding two of the four state variables (\( P, V, T, n \)) constant, early investigators isolated the fundamental relationships linking the remaining two variables. These empirical generalizations laid the foundation for classical thermodynamics.

Boyle's Law (Isothermal Processes)

Formulated by Robert Boyle in 1662:

Boyle's Law: For a fixed mass (\( n = \text{constant} \)) of an ideal gas maintained at constant temperature (\( T = \text{constant} \)), the pressure exerted by the gas is inversely proportional to its volume: \[ P \propto \frac{1}{V} \iff P V = \text{constant} = k_1 \] \[ P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \]
Physical Mechanism: When the volume of a container is halved at constant temperature, the number density of gas molecules (\( N/V \)) doubles. Consequently, the frequency of molecular collisions per unit wall area doubles, leading to twice the macroscopic pressure.

Charles' Law (Isobaric Processes)

Formulated by Jacques Charles (1787) and Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac (1802):

Charles' Law: For a fixed mass (\( n = \text{constant} \)) of an ideal gas maintained at constant pressure (\( P = \text{constant} \)), the volume occupied by the gas is directly proportional to its absolute thermodynamic temperature: \[ V \propto T \iff \frac{V}{T} = \text{constant} = k_2 \] \[ \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2} \]

In terms of Celsius scale:
Starting from volume \( V_0 \) at \( 0^\circ\text{C} \), the volume at temperature \( t^\circ\text{C} \) is:

\[ V_t = V_0 \left( 1 + \alpha_v t \right) = V_0 \left( 1 + \frac{t}{273.15} \right) \]

where \( \alpha_v = \frac{1}{273.15} \, ^\circ\text{C}^{-1} \) is the volume expansion coefficient of all ideal gases.

Gay-Lussac's Law (Isochoric Processes)

Also known as Amontons' Law of Pressure-Temperature:

Gay-Lussac's Law: For a fixed mass (\( n = \text{constant} \)) of an ideal gas maintained in a rigid container of constant volume (\( V = \text{constant} \)), the pressure of the gas is directly proportional to its absolute thermodynamic temperature: \[ P \propto T \iff \frac{P}{T} = \text{constant} = k_3 \] \[ \frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2} \]

In terms of Celsius scale:

\[ P_t = P_0 \left( 1 + \beta_p t \right) = P_0 \left( 1 + \frac{t}{273.15} \right) \]

where \( \beta_p = \frac{1}{273.15} \, ^\circ\text{C}^{-1} \) is the pressure coefficient of an ideal gas.

Avogadro's Hypothesis and Molar Volume

Amedeo Avogadro (1811) proposed:

Avogadro's Law: Equal volumes of all gases under identical conditions of temperature and pressure contain an equal number of molecules, regardless of their chemical identity: \[ V \propto n \quad (\text{at constant } P, T) \]

Standard Molar Volumes:

  • Traditional STP (\( T = 273.15\text{ K} = 0^\circ\text{C}, P = 1\text{ atm} = 101.325\text{ kPa} \)): \[ V_m = \frac{RT}{P} = \frac{0.0821 \times 273.15}{1} \approx 22.414\text{ L/mol} \approx 22.4\text{ L} \]
  • IUPAC Standard STP (\( T = 273.15\text{ K}, P = 1\text{ bar} = 100\text{ kPa} \)): \[ V_m = \frac{8.314 \times 273.15}{10^5} \approx 22.711\text{ L/mol} \approx 22.7\text{ L} \]
  • NTP (Normal Temperature and Pressure: \( 20^\circ\text{C} = 293.15\text{ K}, 1\text{ atm} \)): \( V_m \approx 24.05\text{ L/mol} \)
