Propagation of Errors

How uncertainties combine in complex calculations.

1. Addition and Subtraction

When quantities are added or subtracted, the absolute error of the final result is the sum of the absolute errors of the individual quantities.

If \( Z = A \pm B \), then \[ \Delta Z = \Delta A + \Delta B \]

2. Multiplication and Division

When quantities are multiplied or divided, the relative error of the result is the sum of the relative errors of the individual quantities.

If \( Z = AB \) or \( Z = A/B \), then \[ \frac{\Delta Z}{Z} = \frac{\Delta A}{A} + \frac{\Delta B}{B} \]

3. General Rule for Powers

If a quantity is raised to a power, its relative error is multiplied by the index of that power.

If \( Z = A^p B^q C^r \), then \[ \frac{\Delta Z}{Z} = p\frac{\Delta A}{A} + q\frac{\Delta B}{B} + r\frac{\Delta C}{C} \]
This is the most general and useful formula for competitive exams. Remember that absolute error of the result is rarely used directly; we always calculate relative/percentage error first.
