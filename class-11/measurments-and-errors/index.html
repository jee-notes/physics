Measurements and Errors

Managing the inevitable uncertainties of the physical world.

Conceptual Overview

Physics relies on measurements to validate theories. However, no measurement is ever "perfect". Every instrument has a limit, and every process has flaws. Understanding these limits is just as important as the measurement itself.

This chapter deals with how we quantify these uncertainties and how they propagate through our calculations.

Precision vs. Truth

A measurement can be very consistent (Precise) without being correct (Accurate). Scientific rigor requires us to distinguish between being reliably wrong and being close to the truth.

The Philosophy of Uncertainty

In physics, "Error" doesn't mean a mistake. It refers to the uncertainty that is inherent in every experimental outcome. A result reported without an error margin is scientifically incomplete.

Precision is about the instrument's resolution; Accuracy is about the method's reliability.
