Systematic and Random Errors

Categorizing the source and nature of measurement uncertainty.

1. Systematic Errors

These are errors that tend to be in one direction (either positive or negative). They follow a specific pattern and can often be eliminated if the cause is identified.

  • Instrumental Errors: Due to faulty design or calibration (e.g., zero error).
  • Imperfection in Method: Due to external conditions or flawed experimental setup.
  • Personal Errors: Due to individual habits or parallax.
Systematic errors affect the Accuracy of the result.

2. Random Errors

These are errors that occur irregularly and are random in magnitude and sign. They are caused by unpredictable fluctuations in experimental conditions (e.g., vibration, temperature changes).

Random errors affect the Precision of the result. They can be reduced by taking a large number of readings and calculating their arithmetic mean.
