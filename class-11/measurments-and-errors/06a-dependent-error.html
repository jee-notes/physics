Dependent Errors

We have so far considered mathematical relations in which the quantities involved in the final calculation can be treated as independent inputs.

However, sometimes the quantities appearing in a mathematical relation are themselves dependent on a common measured quantity.

This creates a situation of what we call dependent errors.

Consider \[ Z=\frac{a}{b} \] Here \(a\) and \(b\) are independently measured quantities. Therefore, their errors \[ \Delta a \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Delta b \] come from separate measurements.

We can therefore apply the usual quotient rule: \[ \boxed{\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}=\frac{\Delta a}{a}+\frac{\Delta b}{b}} \]

We directly add the individual error contributions to obtain the maximum error in \(Z\).

Now consider a different relation: \[ Z=\frac{a}{a+b} \]

The numerator and denominator are now \[ (a) \qquad \text{and} \qquad (a+b) \] These are not independent quantities, because both contain the same measured quantity \(a\).

Therefore, the error in \(a\) appears in both the numerator and the denominator. We cannot simply treat the errors in \(a\) and \(a+b\) as two independent error contributions.

In such cases, we cannot treat \[ a+b=u \] as a separate measured quantity and then apply the usual quotient rule directly.

To determine the maximum error in \(Z\), we need to resolve the differential \(dZ\) in terms of the errors in the originally measured independent quantities \(da\) and \(db\).

For this purpose, we use the same logarithmic and differential method, but we do not add the error contributions directly at first.

Before determining the maximum error, we resolve the differential relation by collecting all terms involving the same independent error sources \(da\) and \(db\).

Examples of Dependent Errors

Let us now apply the method to mathematical relations in which the quantities appearing in the final expression are not independent of one another.

Example 1: Consider \[ Z=\frac{a}{a+b} \]

Taking natural logarithm, \[ \ln Z=\ln a-\ln(a+b) \]

Differentiating, \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=\frac{da}{a}-\frac{da+db}{a+b} \]

Before determining the maximum error, we first collect the terms involving the same independent error sources \(da\) and \(db\): \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{a+b}\right)da-\frac{db}{a+b} \]

Therefore, \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=\frac{b}{a(a+b)}\,da-\frac{1}{a+b}\,db \]

For maximum error, the magnitudes of the independent error contributions are added: \[ \boxed{\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}=\frac{b}{a(a+b)}\,\Delta a+\frac{1}{a+b}\,\Delta b} \]

Notice that we did not treat \(a\) and \(a+b\) as independent quantities. The common error in \(a\) was first resolved in terms of the original independent errors \(da\) and \(db\).

Example 2: Consider \[ Z=\frac{ab}{a+b} \]

Taking natural logarithm, \[ \ln Z=\ln a+\ln b-\ln(a+b) \]

Differentiating, \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=\frac{da}{a}+\frac{db}{b}-\frac{da+db}{a+b} \]

Collecting the terms involving the same independent error sources, \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{a+b}\right)da+\left(\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{a+b}\right)db \]

Therefore, \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=\frac{b}{a(a+b)}\,da+\frac{a}{b(a+b)}\,db \]

For maximum error, \[ \boxed{\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}=\frac{b}{a(a+b)}\,\Delta a+\frac{a}{b(a+b)}\,\Delta b} \]

Again, the important step is to first resolve the differential relation in terms of the original independent errors \(da\) and \(db\), and only then add their maximum contributions.

Careful Addition

The main difference in dependent errors is that we should not directly add up the error contributions before considering their dependence.

Consider \[ Z=\frac{A}{B} \] Even though it is a division, we directly add the fractional errors to determine the maximum error in \(Z\):

\[ \boxed{\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}=\frac{\Delta A}{A}+\frac{\Delta B}{B}} \]

Now let us consider the simplest case of dependency: \[ Z=\frac{A}{A} \] Whatever the error in \(A\), it does not propagate to \(Z\), because \[ Z=1 \] for every value of \(A\).

Therefore, if we mechanically apply the previous rule without considering the dependence, we would obtain

\[ \frac{\Delta Z}{Z}=2\frac{\Delta A}{A} \]

which is clearly wrong.

So, before converting all error contributions into positive quantities for maximum error, we must first collect all terms belonging to the same underlying error source.

First take logarithm and implicitly differentiate:

\[ \ln Z=\ln A-\ln A \] \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=\frac{dA}{A}-\frac{dA}{A} \]

Now, before replacing the individual terms by their magnitudes, collect all the terms containing \(dA\):

\[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=dA\left(\frac{1}{A}-\frac{1}{A}\right) \]

Hence, \[ \frac{dZ}{Z}=0 \]

Only after resolving and simplifying the contributions of each independent error source do we convert them into positive contributions to determine the maximum error.

The order of operations is therefore important: \[ \boxed{\text{Differentiate} \longrightarrow \text{collect common error sources} \longrightarrow \text{simplify} \longrightarrow \text{take magnitudes} } \]
