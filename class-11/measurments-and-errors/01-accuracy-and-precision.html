Accuracy and Precision

Distinguishing between closeness to truth and repeatability.

1. Accuracy

Accuracy refers to how close a measured value is to the "True Value" of a quantity. It is a measure of correctness.

High accuracy means the systematic errors are minimized.

2. Precision

Precision refers to the closeness of two or more measurements to each other. It describes the repeatability or resolution of the measurement, regardless of whether it is correct.

High precision means the random errors are small and the instrument has a high resolution (small least count).

3. The Bullseye Analogy

Imagine throwing darts at a target:

  • Accurate & Precise: All darts hit the center (the true value) and are close to each other.
  • Precise but Inaccurate: All darts are clustered together, but far from the center (e.g., due to a shifted sight).
  • Accurate but Imprecise: Darts are scattered around the center on average, but not close to each other.
