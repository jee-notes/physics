Differentiation: Instantaneous Change

Finding the slope of a curve at a single point.

1. The Concept of Derivative

Differentiation allows us to find the "rate of change" of a function at any given instant. In physics, this is how we transition from "average" quantities to "instantaneous" ones.

\[ \text{Rate of change} = \frac{dy}{dx} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} \]

2. Frequently Used Rules

\[ \frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1} \] \[ \frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x \] \[ \frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x \] \[ \frac{d}{dx}(C) = 0 \quad (\text{where C is a constant}) \]

3. Physical Meanings

The derivative is the mathematical tool for kinematics:

\[ v = \frac{ds}{dt} \quad \text{(Velocity is the derivative of Position)} \] \[ a = \frac{dv}{dt} \quad \text{(Acceleration is the derivative of Velocity)} \]
If you know the equation for position, differentiation tells you the velocity at any moment in time.
