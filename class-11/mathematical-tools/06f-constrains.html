Vector Constraints: Bound Motion

Using vector components to define the limits of physical systems.

The Principle of Rigid Connection

Constraint relations arise when the motion of one body is restricted by another, such as two blocks connected by a string or a rod. The fundamental vector rule is: The components of velocities of two points along the line joining them must be equal if the distance between them is constant.

Component Analysis in Constraints

If a rod of length \( L \) is sliding against a wall, the velocity of its ends (\( \vec{v}_A \) and \( \vec{v}_B \)) are constrained. We resolve these velocities along the length of the rod:

\[ v_A \cos \theta_1 = v_B \cos \theta_2 \]

This ensures the rod neither stretches nor compresses, maintaining its physical integrity through the vector dot product of velocity and the unit vector along the rod.

Constraints allow us to reduce the number of independent variables in a system. If you know the vector direction of the constraint (like a string), you can solve for unknown motion in other directions.
