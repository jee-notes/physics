Vectors: Directional Quantities

Managing entities with both magnitude and orientation.

1. Scalars vs. Vectors

Most quantities in physics (Force, Velocity, Field) require more than just a number to be fully described.

  • Scalars: Numerical value only (e.g., Mass, Time, Temperature).
  • Vectors: Numerical value AND direction (e.g., Displacement, Acceleration, Momentum).

2. Vector Addition and Resolution

Vectors cannot be added like simple numbers. They follow geometric laws.

Resolution of Vectors:

Breaking a vector \(\vec{A}\) into rectangular components along the x and y axes:

\[ A_x = A \cos \theta, \quad A_y = A \sin \theta \] \[ |\vec{A}| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2} \]

3. Vector Products

How we multiply "directions" defines different physical outcomes.

  • Dot Product (Scalar): Used for Work and Flux.
    \( \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = AB \cos \theta \)
  • Cross Product (Vector): Used for Torque and Magnetic Force.
    \( \vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (AB \sin \theta) \hat{n} \)
The Dot Product tells you how much one vector "projects" onto another; the Cross Product tells you how much they are "perpendicular" to each other.
