Integration: Summing Up Elements

Finding the total accumulation or area under a curve.

1. Integration as Inverse Derivative

If differentiation breaks a function down into rates, integration builds it back up. It is the mathematical equivalent of summing up an infinite number of tiny pieces.

\[ \int x^n \, dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C \quad (n \neq -1) \]

2. Definite Integration

In physics, we usually integrate between limits (initial and final states). This gives us a numerical value representing a change.

\[ \int_a^b f(x) \, dx = [F(x)]_a^b = F(b) - F(a) \]

3. Physical Meanings

Integration is the primary tool for finding Work, Energy, and Flux:

\[ \Delta s = \int v \, dt \quad \text{(Displacement is the integral of Velocity)} \] \[ W = \int F \, ds \quad \text{(Work is the integral of Force over displacement)} \]
Whenever a force is variable (changes with position or time), you cannot multiply simple values—you must integrate.
