ASTC

Determining the sign of trigonometric functions using quadrants.

Angle and Quadrants

The independent variable of a trigonometric function is the angle \(\theta\), which may take any real value from \((-\infty,\,+\infty)\).

Every finite angle has a unique terminal side and therefore lies in exactly one of the four quadrants of the Cartesian plane (except when it lies on an axis).

The sign of a trigonometric function depends only on the quadrant in which the terminal side of the angle lies. Hence, sign determination is a geometric problem rather than an algebraic one.

To remember the signs in different quadrants, we use a simple mnemonic called ASTC.

ASTC Rule

The letters A–S–T–C correspond to the four quadrants in order.

  • Quadrant I (A) — All trigonometric functions are positive.
  • Quadrant II (S) — Only Sine and its reciprocal (Cosecant) are positive.
  • Quadrant III (T) — Only Tangent and its reciprocal (Cotangent) are positive.
  • Quadrant IV (C) — Only Cosine and its reciprocal (Secant) are positive.

Common mnemonics include Add Sugar To Coffee and After School To College.

Since \(\csc\theta\), \(\sec\theta\), and \(\cot\theta\) are reciprocals of \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\), and \(\tan\theta\), they always have the same sign as their corresponding primary trigonometric functions. Therefore, only the signs of sin, cos, and tan need to be remembered.

Application of ASTC

The evaluation of a trigonometric function consists of two independent steps:

  1. Determine its magnitude.
  2. Determine its sign.

To determine the magnitude, reduce the given angle to its corresponding reference angle using trigonometric identities.

  • If the angle differs by an even multiple of \(90^\circ\), the trigonometric function remains the same.
  • If the angle differs by an odd multiple of \(90^\circ\), the trigonometric function changes according to \[ \sin \leftrightarrow \cos,\qquad \tan \leftrightarrow \cot,\qquad \sec \leftrightarrow \csc. \]

While determining the magnitude, the sign is ignored. Therefore,

\[ |\sin150^\circ| = |\sin(180^\circ-30^\circ)| = |\sin30^\circ| = \frac12 \] \[ |\sin210^\circ| = |\sin(180^\circ+30^\circ)| = |\sin30^\circ| = \frac12 \]
\[ |\sin60^\circ| = |\sin(90^\circ-30^\circ)| = |\cos30^\circ| = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \] \[ |\sin120^\circ| = |\sin(90^\circ+30^\circ)| = |\cos30^\circ| = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \]

After the magnitude has been obtained, determine the sign independently using the ASTC rule. Since \(150^\circ\) lies in the second quadrant, where sine is positive,

\[ \boxed{\sin150^\circ=\frac12.} \]

Similarly, \(210^\circ\) lies in the third quadrant, where sine is negative. Hence,

\[ |\sin210^\circ| = \frac12 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\sin210^\circ=-\frac12.} \]
Reference-angle identities determine only the magnitude of a trigonometric function. The sign is determined independently by the quadrant using the ASTC rule. Treat these as two separate calculations.
