Geometry and Mensuration

Standard models for physical objects and systems.

1. Surface Area and Volume

Physics often involves modeling charges, masses, or fluids as symmetric bodies. Knowing their geometric properties is essential for applying laws like Gauss's Law or calculating Resistance.

Three-Dimensional Shapes:

  • Sphere:
    Surface Area (\(A\)) = \(4\pi r^2\)
    Volume (\(V\)) = \(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\)
  • Right Circular Cylinder:
    Curved Surface Area = \(2\pi rh\)
    Total Surface Area = \(2\pi r(r + h)\)
    Volume (\(V\)) = \(\pi r^2 h\)
  • Right Circular Cone:
    Curved Surface Area = \(\pi rl\) (where \(l\) is slant height)
    Volume (\(V\)) = \(\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h\)

2. Trigonometric Essentials

Trigonometry is used for resolving forces and analyzing waves.

\[ \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1 \] \[ \sin(2\theta) = 2 \sin \theta \cos \theta \] \[ \cos(2\theta) = \cos^2 \theta - \sin^2 \theta = 2 \cos^2 \theta - 1 = 1 - 2 \sin^2 \theta \]
For very small angles (\(\theta \to 0\)), we use the identities:
\(\sin \theta \approx \theta\)
\(\tan \theta \approx \theta\)
\(\cos \theta \approx 1 - \frac{\theta^2}{2} \approx 1\)
