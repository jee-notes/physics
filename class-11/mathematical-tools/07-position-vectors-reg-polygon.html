Position Vectors of Regular Polygon (PVRP)

A powerful symmetry result connecting geometry, vectors, and complex numbers.

Statement of the Result

A regular polygon is a figure that has symmetric properties like a circle as well as we can use polygon tools like relating sides and angles commonly used in triangles. Earlier in ancient times, regular polygons were used to derive values of \( \pi \). This tool is used in many geometric problems related to rotational symmetry. Consider a regular polygon of n sides inscribed in a circle of radius \(R\), with its center at the origin. This is true as all vertices of a regular polygon are con-cyclic. If \( \vec r_1, \vec r_2, \dots, \vec r_n \) are the position vectors of its vertices, then:

\[ \vec r_1 + \vec r_2 + \dots + \vec r_n = 0 \]

That is, the vector sum of position vectors of all vertices of a regular polygon is zero. This result is independent of how the polygon is oriented in the plane.

Geometric Symmetry Proof

Each vertex lies at equally spaced angular positions:

\[ \theta_k = \frac{2\pi k}{n}, \quad k = 0,1,2,\dots,n-1 \]

The entire configuration is invariant under rotation by \( \frac{2\pi}{n} \). Suppose the sum of all vectors equals some vector \( \vec S \). After rotating the entire polygon by \( \frac{2\pi}{n} \), the vector sum must also rotate.

But due to symmetry, the physical configuration is unchanged. Therefore:

\[ \vec S = \text{Rotation}(\vec S) \]

The only vector unchanged by a non-trivial rotation is:

\[ \vec S = 0 \]
Rotational symmetry forces equilibrium. The symmetry alone guarantees cancellation.

Algebraic Proof (Using Complex Numbers)

As polygon is a 2D figure, we can use complex number algebra of argand plane to prove the result. Represent each vertex as a complex number:

\[ z_k = R e^{i 2\pi k/n} \]

Then the sum becomes:

\[ S = R \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} e^{i2\pi k/n} \]

This is a geometric series:

\[ S = R \{ e^{\frac{i2\pi}{n}0} + e^{\frac{i2\pi}{n}1} + e^{\frac{i2\pi}{n}2} + \dots + e^{\frac{i2\pi}{n}(n-1)}\} \] \[ a = 1, \,\,\, r = e^{\frac{i2\pi}{n}}, \,\,\, n \,\,\, \text{terms} \]
\[ S = R \frac{1 - e^{i2\pi}}{1 - e^{i2\pi/n}} \]

Since:

\[ e^{i2\pi} = 1 \]

The numerator becomes zero, giving:

\[ S = 0 \]

Thus the vector sum of all vertices of a regular polygon is zero.

Deeper Mathematical Insight

This result is equivalent to the fundamental identity:

\[ 1 + \omega + \omega^2 + \dots + \omega^{n-1} = 0 \]

where \( \omega = e^{2\pi i/n} \) is an nth root of unity.

Regular polygons encode rotational symmetry. The zero sum reflects perfect angular balance. This idea also appears in graduate level topics. From exam point of view, this symmetry is asked in many problems indirectly and it is purely a mathematical concept. We can call it as Position Vector sum of Regular Polygon or PVRP.
