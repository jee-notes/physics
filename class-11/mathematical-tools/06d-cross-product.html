Cross Product: Generating Rotation

Finding a mutually perpendicular vector to describe rotational effects.

The Geometry of Twist

Unlike the dot product, the cross product (or vector product) results in a new vector. This new vector is unique because it is perpendicular to both original vectors, representing an axis of rotation or a "twist" in space.

The direction is determined by the Right-Hand Thumb Rule: curl your fingers from the first vector to the second, and your thumb points in the direction of the cross product.

Mathematical Definition

The magnitude of the cross product depends on the sine of the angle \( \theta \) between the vectors:

\[ \vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (AB \sin \theta) \hat{n} \]

Here, \( \hat{n} \) is the unit vector perpendicular to the plane containing \( \vec{A} \) and \( \vec{B} \). In component form, we use a determinant:

\[ \vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{vmatrix} \]

Physical Significance

The cross product is essential for describing Torque \( (\vec{\tau}) \) and Angular Momentum \( (\vec{L}) \). It captures how a force applied at a distance from an axis causes an object to rotate.

\[ \vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} \]
The cross product is not commutative (\( \vec{A} \times \vec{B} = -\vec{B} \times \vec{A} \)). Reversing the order of vectors flips the direction of the resulting vector by 180 degrees.
