Dot Product: The Scalar Projection

Measuring how much of one vector lies in the direction of another.

Concept of Projection

The dot product (or scalar product) is an operation that takes two vectors and returns a single scalar number. Physically, it represents the product of the magnitude of one vector and the projection of the second vector onto the first.

If two vectors are perpendicular, the projection is zero. If they are parallel, the projection is at its maximum. This operation effectively filters out the parts of vectors that don't "align."

Mathematical Definition

For two vectors \( \vec{A} \) and \( \vec{B} \) with an angle \( \theta \) between them, the dot product is defined as:

\[ \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = AB \cos \theta \]

In Cartesian component form, where \( \vec{A} = A_x \hat{i} + A_y \hat{j} + A_z \hat{k} \), the calculation becomes straightforward multiplication of corresponding parts:

\[ \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z \]

Physical Significance

The dot product is the primary tool for calculating Work Done. Work is defined as the product of the force and the displacement in the direction of the force.

\[ W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = Fs \cos \theta \]
The dot product is commutative (\( \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A} \)). It is most useful for finding the angle between two vectors or checking for orthogonality: if \( \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = 0 \), then the vectors are perpendicular.
