Wedge–Block–Rope System

Finding the mass ratio $M/m$ for a stationary wedge $m_0$.

In the system shown, all surfaces are smooth and the string and pulleys are light. The angle of the wedge is \(\theta = \sin^{-1}(3/5)\). When the system is released from rest, the wedge of mass \(m_0\) does not move. Our goal is to find the ratio \(M/m\).

Geometric Constraints

Since the wedge \(m_0\) is stationary, the string constraint is simple: if block m moves down the incline with acceleration a, block M must move upward with the same acceleration magnitude a.

For \(\theta = \sin^{-1}(3/5)\):

\[ \sin\theta = 0.6, \quad \cos\theta = 0.8 \]

Method 1 — Newton's Laws (FBD Approach)

Analyze horizontal forces on the wedge \(m_0\). For \(m_0\) to remain at rest, \(\sum F_x = 0\).

\[ N \sin\theta = T + T \cos\theta \implies N \sin\theta = T(1 + \cos\theta) \quad \dots(1) \]

For block $m$, perpendicular to the incline:

\[ N = mg \cos\theta \quad \dots(2) \]

Substituting (2) into (1):

\[ T = \frac{mg \sin\theta \cos\theta}{1 + \cos\theta} \quad \dots(3) \]

Equations of motion for $m$ (down incline) and $M$ (upward):

\[ mg \sin\theta - T = ma \quad \text{and} \quad T - Mg = Ma \]

Eliminating $a$ gives $M(g+a) = T$, which leads to the ratio:

\[ \frac{M}{m} = \frac{\sin\theta \cos\theta}{1 + \sin\theta + \cos\theta} = \frac{0.48}{2.4} = 0.2 \]

Method 2 — System Momentum Approach

Consider the system: Wedge + Block \(m\) + Rope segment left of point P (shown in green).

The only external horizontal force on this system is the tension $T$ at point P pulling to the right. Since the wedge is stationary, the horizontal acceleration of the center of mass is purely due to block \(m\).

\[ T = m a_x = m (a \cos\theta) \quad \dots(4) \]

Combining this with the equation for block \(M\) (\(T = M(g+a)\)):

\[ M(g+a) = ma \cos\theta \implies \frac{M}{m} = \frac{a \cos\theta}{g+a} \]

From the equation of motion for block \(m\) ($mg \sin\theta - T = ma$), substituting \(T\) from (4):

\[ mg \sin\theta - ma \cos\theta = ma \implies a = \frac{g \sin\theta}{1 + \cos\theta} \]

Substituting $a$ back into the ratio gives the same result: $\mathbf{M/m = 0.2}$.

Method 3 — Work-Energy Approach

By Work-Energy theorem, $\Delta K + \Delta U = 0$ for the whole system over displacement $s$:

\[ \frac{1}{2}(M + m)v^2 + Mgs - mgs \sin\theta = 0 \]

Using $v^2 = 2as$:

\[ a = g \left( \frac{m \sin\theta - M}{M + m} \right) \]

Combining this potential-kinetic trade-off with the stationary wedge constraint ($T = ma \cos\theta$) yields the identical mass ratio. This confirms that the internal tension and normal forces do no net work on the system.
