Vertical Circular Motion

Circular motion in a vertical plane where gravity and the normal (or tension) force together provide centripetal acceleration — analysed elegantly through energy conservation.

Why Energy is the Right Tool

In vertical circular motion, both the speed of the object and the direction of the centripetal force change continuously. Solving Newton's second law at every point would require knowing the speed at that point — which itself requires integration. Energy conservation provides the speed at any height directly, making it the natural starting point.

Newton's second law in the radial direction then gives the tension or normal force at that point, completing the analysis.

Object on a String (Complete Loop)

Consider a ball of mass \(m\) tied to a string of length \(R\) and whirled in a vertical circle. The critical point is the top of the loop, where the string is most likely to go slack.

Condition at the Top

At the top, both the tension \(T\) and gravity act downward (toward the centre), providing centripetal force:

\[T + mg = \frac{mv_{\text{top}}^2}{R}\]

For the string to remain taut, \(T \geq 0\). The minimum speed at the top corresponds to \(T = 0\):

\[mg = \frac{mv_{\min, \text{top}}^2}{R} \implies v_{\min, \text{top}} = \sqrt{gR}\]
\(v_{\min, \text{top}} = \sqrt{gR}\) is the threshold speed at the top for maintaining a taut string. Below this, the string goes slack before reaching the top and the ball leaves circular motion.

Minimum Speed at the Bottom

Using energy conservation from bottom to top (height gain \(= 2R\)):

\[\frac{1}{2}mv_{\text{bottom}}^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_{\text{top}}^2 + mg(2R)\]

Substituting \(v_{\text{top}} = \sqrt{gR}\):

\[\frac{1}{2}mv_{\text{bottom}}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(gR) + 2mgR = \frac{5mgR}{2}\] \[v_{\min, \text{bottom}} = \sqrt{5gR}\]

Object on a Track (Inner Surface of a Loop)

For a ball rolling on the inner surface of a circular loop (like a loop-the-loop track), the normal force \(N\) replaces tension. At the top, \(N\) acts downward:

\[N + mg = \frac{mv_{\text{top}}^2}{R}\]

The condition \(N \geq 0\) gives the same threshold:

\[v_{\min, \text{top}} = \sqrt{gR} \qquad v_{\min, \text{bottom}} = \sqrt{5gR}\]

The physics is identical; only the origin of the centripetal force (string tension vs. normal force) differs.

Object on the Outer Surface (Ball on Top of a Sphere)

A ball placed on top of a smooth sphere of radius \(R\) and given a slight nudge slides off. At angle \(\theta\) from the top, the normal force acts outward (away from centre) and gravity acts downward. The radial equation is:

\[mg\cos\theta - N = \frac{mv^2}{R}\]

Energy conservation from top (speed \(\approx 0\)) to angle \(\theta\) (height drop \(= R - R\cos\theta\)):

\[\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgR(1 - \cos\theta)\]

Substituting \(v^2 = 2gR(1-\cos\theta)\):

\[N = mg\cos\theta - \frac{2mg(1-\cos\theta)}{1} = mg(3\cos\theta - 2)\]

The ball leaves the sphere when \(N = 0\):

\[3\cos\theta = 2 \implies \cos\theta = \frac{2}{3} \implies \theta \approx 48.2°\]
The ball always leaves the sphere at \(\cos\theta = 2/3\), independent of the radius or the mass. The height at which it leaves is \(h = R\cos\theta = \frac{2R}{3}\) from the centre.

Tension and Normal Force as a Function of Position

For the string-in-a-loop problem, using energy conservation from the bottom (speed \(v_0\)) to angle \(\theta\) measured from the bottom (height \(= R - R\cos\theta = R(1-\cos\theta)\)):

\[\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 - mgR(1-\cos\theta)\]

At angle \(\theta\) from the bottom, the radial Newton equation gives:

\[T - mg\cos\alpha = \frac{mv^2}{R}\]

where \(\alpha\) is the angle from the vertical. Substituting the expression for \(v^2\) yields tension as a function of position, allowing analysis of the most and least stressed points of the string.
