Elastic Collisions

Interactions where both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.

Head-On Elastic Collision: Result Formulae

For a 1D elastic collision between mass \(m_1\) (initial velocity \(u_1\)) and \(m_2\) (initial velocity \(u_2\)), solving the two conservation equations simultaneously gives the final velocities \(v_1\) and \(v_2\):

\[v_1 = \frac{(m_1 - m_2)u_1 + 2m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}\] \[v_2 = \frac{(m_2 - m_1)u_2 + 2m_1 u_1}{m_1 + m_2}\]

Special Cases in 1D

  • Equal masses (\(m_1 = m_2\)): The velocities exchange — \(v_1 = u_2,\; v_2 = u_1\). A striking billiard ball stops and the struck one moves off at the original speed.
  • Very heavy projectile (\(m_1 \gg m_2\)) striking a stationary target (\(u_2 = 0\)): \(v_1 \approx u_1\) (barely slows down), \(v_2 \approx 2u_1\) (target shoots forward at twice the projectile speed).
  • Very light projectile (\(m_1 \ll m_2\)) striking a stationary target: \(v_1 \approx -u_1\) (bounces back), \(v_2 \approx 0\) (target barely moves). Example: a light ball bouncing off a massive stationary wall.

Mathematical Derivation of 1D Formulae

The two fundamental equations for perfectly elastic 1D collisions are:

  1. Conservation of Momentum: \(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2\)
  2. Conservation of Kinetic Energy: \(\frac{1}{2}m_1 u_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 u_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_2^2\)

By rearranging and factoring the energy equation into a difference of squares, we can divide it by the rearranged momentum equation to obtain the simple relative velocity relation:

\[u_1 - u_2 = v_2 - v_1\]

This shows that in a perfectly elastic collision, the relative speed of approach equals the relative speed of separation. Substituting this linear relation back into the momentum equation yields the final velocities.

Elastic Collisions in 2D (Glancing Collisions)

If the collision is not head-on (i.e. the centers of mass do not align along the initial velocity vector), the objects will scatter at angles in a 2D plane. Momentum conservation must be applied in two orthogonal directions:

x-direction:

\[m_1 u_1 = m_1 v_{1x} + m_2 v_{2x}\]

y-direction:

\[0 = m_1 v_{1y} + m_2 v_{2y}\]

Kinetic Energy conservation yields a single scalar equation. Since there are four unknown velocity components and only three equations, one scattering angle must usually be measured or given to solve the system completely.

Important Property for Equal Masses in 2D: If two equal-mass objects undergo an elastic glancing collision and one is initially at rest, they will scatter at exactly a 90° angle to each other.
