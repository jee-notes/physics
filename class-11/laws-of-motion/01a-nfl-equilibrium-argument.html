Equilibrium Argument

If no effect, then no cause.

Force causes acceleration

According to Newton's second law, force and acceleration are related through a causal relationship. When a net force acts on a body, it produces acceleration.

In other words, force is the cause and acceleration is the effect.

The equilibrium argument makes use of the reverse logical reasoning. If a body shows no acceleration, then the net force acting on the body must be zero.

Thus the absence of acceleration implies the absence of a net external force.

Illustration

Consider a book resting on a table. The forces acting on the book are:

  • Weight acting downward: \( mg \)
  • Normal force exerted by the table acting upward: \( N \)

The exact magnitude of the normal force is not known beforehand. We only know that the normal force is a constraint force exerted by the surface to prevent the book from penetrating the solid surface of the table.

To determine the magnitude of this force, we use information about the motion of the book.

The book is observed to remain at rest on the table. Since it is at rest and continues to remain at rest, its acceleration must be zero.

From Newton's first law, if the acceleration of the body is zero, the net force acting on it must also be zero.

For the book:

\[ \vec{a} = 0 \] \[ \Rightarrow \Sigma \vec{F} = 0 \] \[ m\vec{g} + \vec{N} = 0 \] \[ N = mg \]

Thus the normal force exerted by the table exactly balances the weight of the book. The forces cancel each other, resulting in zero net force and therefore no acceleration.
