Inertial Frame of Reference

Reference frames in which Newton's laws of motion hold true.

Need for a Reference Frame

In order to describe the motion of a body, we must measure its position and velocity relative to something else. This "something else" is called a reference frame.

A reference frame may consist of a coordinate system together with a clock used to measure position and time. Observers situated in different frames of reference may describe the same motion differently because the measurements are made relative to different backgrounds.

For example, consider a passenger sitting inside a train moving with constant velocity. To the passenger, the seat appears stationary. However, to an observer standing on the ground outside the train, the seat is moving along with the train.

Thus the description of motion always depends on the chosen reference frame.

Definition of an Inertial Frame

Newton's first law provides a natural way to identify a special class of reference frames known as inertial frames of reference.

An inertial frame is a reference frame in which a body on which no net external force acts either remains at rest or continues to move with constant velocity in a straight line.

In such frames the principle expressed by Newton's first law is valid.

\[ \Sigma \vec{F} = 0 \Rightarrow \vec{a} = 0 \]

Therefore, in an inertial frame, a body free from external forces does not accelerate.

Newton's laws of motion are valid only in inertial frames of reference.

Can an Absolutely Inertial Frame be Identified?

An inertial frame is defined as a frame in which a body free from external forces moves with constant velocity.

However, an absolutely inertial frame cannot be determined. Acceleration cannot be defined in an absolute sense because it is always measured relative to a chosen reference frame.

If a frame is changed to another frame that accelerates relative to it, the acceleration of every body changes accordingly. Thus acceleration is not an invariant quantity; its value depends on the reference frame used to measure it.

Since inertiality is defined through the condition of zero acceleration for a force-free body, and acceleration itself depends on the chosen frame, no frame can be identified as absolutely inertial.

Therefore, a frame is to be chosen and assumed to be inertial for operational purposes. For problems studied on Earth, the frame of reference attached to the ground is taken as inertial frame of reference.

Ground Frame is Not Perfectly Inertial

To a good approximation, frames attached to the surface of the Earth are treated as inertial frames for most everyday mechanical phenomena.

For example, when analysing the motion of a falling object, a moving vehicle, or a projectile, we normally assume ground frame to be an inertial frame.

Strictly speaking, the Earth is not perfectly inertial because it rotates and revolves around the Sun. However, the accelerations associated with these motions are small enough that they can usually be neglected in elementary mechanics.

Similarly, a train or a car moving with constant velocity with respect to ground also behaves as an inertial frame for observers inside it.

Non-Inertial Frames

A reference frame that is accelerating with respect to an inertial frame is called a non-inertial frame of reference.

A reference frame that is rotating with respect to an inertial frame is also a non-inertial frame of reference.

In such frames Newton's first law does not hold in its usual form.

For example, consider a passenger standing inside a bus that suddenly accelerates forward. The passenger feels a backward jerk. From the perspective of the bus, it appears as if a force is pushing the passenger backward.

However, an observer standing on the ground sees the situation differently. The passenger tends to remain in his original state of motion due to inertia while the bus accelerates forward.

Thus the apparent backward force arises because the bus is an accelerating (non-inertial) frame.

Importance in Mechanics

The concept of inertial frames is fundamental in classical mechanics because Newton's laws are formulated specifically for such frames.

When analysing mechanical problems, we generally choose a frame that can be treated as inertial. This allows us to apply Newton's laws directly to determine forces and motion.

If the chosen frame is non-inertial, additional correction terms called pseudo forces must be used to correctly describe the motion.

Choosing an inertial frame greatly simplifies the analysis of mechanical systems because Newton's laws can be applied directly.
