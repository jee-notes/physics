Force, Momentum & Impulse

Relation between Force, Momentum & Impulse.

Impulse

Force is an instantaneous physical quantity. At every instant of time, it specifies the instantaneous rate at which the momentum of a body changes.

In many situations, we are interested in the accumulated change in momentum produced over a finite time interval rather than the instantaneous rate of change.

This motivates the definition of another physical quantity called impulse.

Impulse measures the cumulative effect of a force acting over a time interval. It is equal to the change in momentum produced by the force.

\[ \vec{J}=\int \vec{F}\,dt=\Delta\vec{p} \]

Its main use is, when the variation of force with time is not known, but the total change in momentum is known or can be measured.

In such cases, we use the concept of average force, defined by

\[ \vec{F}_{\text{avg}}=\frac{\Delta\vec{p}}{\Delta t} \]
As Force is instantaneous, we can write \(\vec{F}\) as \(\vec{F}(t)\) .
But impulse is accumulated and so we cannot define anything like instantaneous impulse or write \(\vec{J}\) as \(\vec{J}(t)\).
The difference is analogous to how velocity \(\vec{v}\) is instantaneous but displacement \(\delta s = \vec{v}\, dt\) is accumulated.

Illustration

When a disc of mass \(M\) is balanced by is kept floating horizontally by throwing at it \(n\) marbles \(s^{-1}\) from below. Mass of each marble is \(m\) and calculate the velocity \(v\) with which marbles strike the disc. Assume marbles strike along normal and rebound with same speed.

Solution

In this example, we do not know the force profile when marble strikes the disc. So, we do not have information on the cause \(\vec{F}\) , but we have information on the effect \(\Delta\vec{p}\) and using this, we'll find average force exerted by the marbles on the disc \(\vec{F}_{\text{avg}}\) .

Step 1: Calculate the change in momentum of one marble.

Take the upward direction as positive. A marble approaches the disc with velocity \(+v\) and rebounds with velocity \(-v\).

\[ p_i=+mv,\qquad p_f=-mv \]

Therefore, the change in momentum of the marble is

\[ \Delta p_{\text{marble}}=p_f-p_i \] \[ \Delta p_{\text{marble}}=-mv-mv=-2mv \]

Step 2: Calculate the impulse delivered to the disc.

By Newton's third law, the impulse delivered by a marble to the disc is equal and opposite to the impulse delivered by the disc to the marble.

Hence, the upward impulse delivered to the disc by each marble is

\[ J_{\text{disc}}=2mv \]

Step 3: Relate the impulse delivered by each marble to the average force.

From the previous step, we know that each marble delivers an upward impulse of magnitude \(2mv\) to the disc.

But the impulse delivered by a single marble tells us only how much momentum is transferred in one collision. To calculate force, we need to know how much momentum is transferred per unit time.

Suppose \(N\) marbles strike the disc during a time interval \(T\). Since each marble delivers an impulse of \(2mv\), the total upward impulse delivered during this interval is

\[ J_{\text{total}}=N(2mv) \]

Now, recall the relation between impulse and force. Impulse gives the accumulated change in momentum, while force gives the rate of change of momentum. Therefore, the average upward force over the time interval \(T\) is the total upward impulse delivered during that interval divided by the elapsed time:

\[ F_{\text{avg}}=\frac{J_{\text{total}}}{T} \]

Substituting the expression for total impulse,

\[ F_{\text{avg}}=\frac{N(2mv)}{T} \]

Rearranging,

\[ F_{\text{avg}}=\frac{N}{T}(2mv) \]

Here is the key step. The quantity \(N/T\) is the number of marbles striking the disc per unit time. The question states that \(n\) marbles strike the disc every second. Hence,

\[ \frac{N}{T}=n \]

Therefore,

\[ F_{\text{avg}}=n(2mv) \] \[ \boxed{F_{\text{avg}}=2mnv} \]

We can now interpret this result directly:

  • \(2mv\) is the upward impulse delivered by one marble.
  • \(n\) is the number of marbles striking the disc per second.
  • \(2mnv\) is the upward impulse delivered per second, which gives the average upward force.

Notice that the force during an individual collision may vary with time and may be zero between collisions. We do not calculate these instantaneous forces. Instead, we add the impulses delivered by all the marbles over a finite time interval and divide by that interval's duration. This gives the time-averaged force.

Step 4: Apply the condition of equilibrium.

Since the disc remains floating horizontally at rest, the average upward force due to the marbles balances the downward gravitational force on the disc.

\[ F_{\text{avg}}=Mg \] \[ 2mnv=Mg \] \[ \boxed{v=\frac{Mg}{2mn}} \]
We do not need to know the instantaneous force during a collision.
The change in momentum gives the impulse delivered by each marble, and the number of collisions per second gives the average force.
This is precisely where the concept of impulse becomes useful.
