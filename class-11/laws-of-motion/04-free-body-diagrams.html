Free Body Diagrams and Equilibrium

The visual tool for solving dynamics problems.

1. What is an FBD?

A Free Body Diagram (FBD) consists of a diagrammatic representation of a single body or a subsystem of bodies isolated from its surroundings, showing all the forces acting on it.

Crucially, an FBD shows forces acting on the body, not forces exerted by the body.

2. Steps to draw an FBD

  1. Identify the body or system to be analyzed.
  2. Draw the body as a point or a simple shape.
  3. Identify and draw all external forces:
    • Field Forces: Weight (\(mg\)) acting vertically downwards.
    • Contact Forces: Normal force (\(N\)), Tension (\(T\)), Friction (\(f\)), etc.
  4. Choose a coordinate system (usually along the direction of motion and perpendicular to it).
  5. Resolve forces into components along the chosen axes.

3. Translational Equilibrium

A body is in translational equilibrium if the net force acting on it is zero.

\[ \Sigma \vec{F} = 0 \implies \Sigma F_x = 0 \text{ and } \Sigma F_y = 0 \]
