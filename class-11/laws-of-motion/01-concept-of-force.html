Concept of Force and Newton's First Law

The definition of force and the principle of inertia.

1. What is Force?

A force is an interaction that, when unopposed, will change the motion of an object. A force can cause an object with mass to change its velocity (which includes beginning to move from a state of rest), i.e., to accelerate.

Force can also be described intuitively as a push or a pull. It is a vector quantity.

2. Inertia

Inertia is the inherent property of all bodies by virtue of which they resist any change in their state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line.

  • Inertia of Rest: Inability of a body to change its state of rest by itself.
  • Inertia of Motion: Inability of a body to change its state of uniform motion by itself.
  • Inertia of Direction: Inability of a body to change its direction of motion by itself.
Mass is the quantitative measure of inertia. The larger the mass, the larger the inertia.

3. Newton's First Law

Newton's first law states that every object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled to change its state by the action of an external force.

If \(\Sigma \vec{F} = 0 \implies \vec{a} = 0 \implies \vec{v} = \text{constant}\)

This law is also called the Law of Inertia. It defines the condition for equilibrium and introduces the concept of force as the cause of acceleration.
