Momentum and Newton's Second Law

The quantitative measurement of force.

1. Linear Momentum

The linear momentum (\(\vec{p}\)) of a body is defined as the product of its mass (\(m\)) and its velocity (\(\vec{v}\)).

\[ \vec{p} = m\vec{v} \]

It is a vector quantity and its SI unit is \(kg \cdot m/s\).

2. Newton's Second Law

Newton's second law states that the rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts.

\[ \vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \]

3. Force and Acceleration

For a system with constant mass:

\[ \vec{F} = \frac{d(m\vec{v})}{dt} = m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = m\vec{a} \]
\(\vec{F} = m\vec{a}\) is the most famous equation in dynamics, but it only applies when mass is constant. If mass is changing (like in a rocket), the full derivative of momentum must be used.

4. Impulse

Impulse is the effect of a large force acting for a very short time. It is equal to the change in momentum.

\[ \vec{J} = \int \vec{F} dt = \Delta \vec{p} \]
