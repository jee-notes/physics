Introduction to Friction

The resistive force opposing relative motion.

1. Nature of Friction

Friction is a contact force that arises between two surfaces because of their roughness and molecular interactions. It always acts parallel to the surfaces in contact and opposes relative motion.

2. Types of Friction

A. Static Friction (\(f_s\))

Operates when there is no relative motion. It is a self-adjusting force that exactly balances the external force trying to initiate motion.

\[ f_s \leq \mu_s N \]

Where \(\mu_s\) is the coefficient of static friction and \(N\) is the normal force.

B. Kinetic Friction (\(f_k\))

Operates once relative motion has begun. Its magnitude is generally less than the maximum static friction.

\[ f_k = \mu_k N \]

3. Laws of Friction

  • Friction is independent of the apparent area of contact.
  • Kinetic friction is independent of velocity (for moderate speeds).
  • The direction of friction is always opposite to the relative velocity (or tendency of motion).
