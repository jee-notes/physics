Newton's Third Law and Interactions

The reciprocal nature of forces.

Statement

Newton's third law states that whenever two bodies interact, they exert forces on each other that are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

\[ \vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA} \]

Here \( \vec{F}_{AB} \) is the force exerted by body A on body B, and \( \vec{F}_{BA} \) is the force exerted by body B on body A.

Characteristics of Action-Reaction Pairs

  • They act on different bodies. Therefore, they never cancel each other to produce equilibrium for a single body.
  • They are of the same nature (for example, both gravitational, both normal, etc.).
  • They occur simultaneously. There is no time lag between action and reaction.
A common misconception is that action and reaction cancel each other. This is only true if the two interacting bodies are treated as a single system. For an individual body, the reaction force acts on the other body.

Interaction

It is the third Law that defines the characteristic of force as an interaction.

If earth is pulling you by gravity, you are pulling earth by gravity - exactly same magnitude and exactly opposite direction.

However, it is not so normal because force does not only have motion changing effect. Contact force also deforms. We may experience force in different ways.

Conservation of Momentum

Newton's third law provides the basis for the conservation of linear momentum. In an isolated system where no external force acts, all internal forces occur in equal and opposite pairs.

As a result, the total momentum of the system remains constant.
