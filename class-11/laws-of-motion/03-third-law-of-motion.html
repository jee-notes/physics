Newton's Third Law and Interactions

The reciprocal nature of forces.

1. Statement

To every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction. Alternatively, the forces of two bodies on each other are always equal and are directed in opposite directions.

\[ \vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA} \]

2. Characteristics of Action-Reaction Pairs

  • They act on different bodies. Therefore, they never cancel each other out to produce equilibrium for a single body.
  • They are of the same nature (e.g., both gravitational, both normal, etc.).
  • They occur simultaneously. There is no time lag between action and reaction.
A common misconception is that action and reaction cancel out. This is only possible if you consider the two bodies as a single system. For individual bodies, the reaction force acts on the other body.

3. Conservation of Momentum

The third law is the foundation for the principle of conservation of linear momentum. In an isolated system (where no external force acts), the total momentum remains constant because all internal forces occur in equal and opposite pairs.
