Fundamental vs Derived Quantities

The building blocks and the structures of physical measurement.

1. Fundamental Quantities

These are the quantities that are considered basic and independent of any other quantity. They cannot be defined in terms of other physical quantities.

In the SI system, there are seven fundamental quantities: Mass, Length, Time, Electric Current, Temperature, Luminous Intensity, and Amount of Substance.

2. Derived Quantities

The physical quantities that are expressed in terms of fundamental quantities are known as Derived Quantities.

Examples include:

  • Area: Length \(\times\) Length = \([L^2]\)
  • Velocity: Displacement / Time = \([LT^{-1}]\)
  • Force: Mass \(\times\) Acceleration = \([MLT^{-2}]\)
  • Work: Force \(\times\) Displacement = \([ML^2T^{-2}]\)

3. The Process of Derivation

To find the unit of a derived quantity, we substitute the units of the fundamental quantities into the mathematical formula for that quantity.

\[ \text{Unit of Pressure} = \frac{\text{Unit of Force}}{\text{Unit of Area}} = \frac{\text{kg m/s}^2}{\text{m}^2} = \text{kg m}^{-1}\text{s}^{-2} \text{ (Pascal)} \]
