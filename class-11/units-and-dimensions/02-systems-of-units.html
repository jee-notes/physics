Units and the SI System

The universal standards for scientific measurement.

1. Requirements of a Good Unit

For a unit to be scientifically valid, it must be:

  • Invariable: It should not change with time or environmental conditions.
  • Universal: It should be easily reproducible and accessible everywhere.
  • Convenient Size: It should be appropriate for the quantity being measured.

2. The SI System (Système International)

Adopted in 1960, the SI system is the modern form of the metric system. it is based on seven fundamental base units from which all other units are derived.

Quantity Unit Symbol
Length meter m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Electric Current ampere A
Thermodynamic Temperature kelvin K
Amount of Substance mole mol
Luminous Intensity candela cd

3. Supplementary Units

The SI system also includes two dimensionless units for geometric measurements:

  • Plane Angle (\(\theta\)): Measured in Radian (rad). Defined as arc length divided by radius.
  • Solid Angle (\(\Omega\)): Measured in Steradian (sr). Defined as intercepted area divided by radius squared.
