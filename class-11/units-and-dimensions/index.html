Units and Dimensions

The formal structure that makes physical equations meaningful.

Conceptual Overview

Physics is inherently a quantitative science. Every physical law is expressed as an equation relating measurable properties of the universe. For these equations to mean anything, we must agree on a standard way to measure these properties.

Units tell us what "one" of something looks like, while Dimensions tell us the fundamental physical nature of a quantity, regardless of the unit used to measure it.

Why Formalize?

Without a formal system of units (like the SI system), communication between scientists would be impossible. Formalization ensures that a "meter" in Tokyo is exactly the same as a "meter" in London, and that the laws of physics remain universal.

The Essence of Measurement

Every measurement consists of two parts: a number and a unit. Mathematically, the measured value \(Q = nu\). If we change our unit, the number changes inversely (\(n_1u_1 = n_2u_2\)), but the physical quantity itself remains constant.

Units are human conventions, but dimensions are properties of nature. You can measure length in meters or feet, but its dimension is always $[L]$.
