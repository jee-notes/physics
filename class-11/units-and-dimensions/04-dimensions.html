Dimensions and Dimensional Formulae

The qualitative nature of physical quantities.

1. What are Dimensions?

The Dimensions of a physical quantity are the powers to which the fundamental quantities are raised to represent that quantity.

We use square brackets to denote dimensions:

\[ [Q] = [M^a L^b T^c A^d K^e \text{mol}^f \text{cd}^g] \]

2. Common Dimensional Formulae

Quantity Dimensional Formula
Area \([L^2]\)
Volume \([L^3]\)
Velocity \([LT^{-1}]\)
Acceleration \([LT^{-2}]\)
Force \([MLT^{-2}]\)
Work / Energy \([ML^2T^{-2}]\)
Power \([ML^2T^{-3}]\)
Pressure \([ML^{-1}T^{-2}]\)

3. Dimensionless Quantities

Quantities that are the ratio of two similar physical quantities are dimensionless.

  • Angle, Strain, Relative Density, refractive index, etc.
  • Their dimensions are \([M^0L^0T^0]\).
Remember: A dimensionless quantity can still have a unit (e.g., Radian for Angle), but a unitless quantity is always dimensionless.
