Principle of Dimensional Homogeneity

The rule of consistency in physical equations.

The Fundamental Rule

The Principle of Dimensional Homogeneity states that only physical quantities of the same dimensions can be added, subtracted, or equated.

You can add 5 meters to 10 meters, but you cannot add 5 meters to 10 kilograms.

Why Homogeneity?

Dimensions classify physical quantities according to their nature.

Since addition and subtraction are meaningful only between quantities of the same kind, every valid physical equation must preserve dimensional consistency.

Dimensions are the grammar of physics. A physically meaningful equation cannot violate dimensional consistency.

Dimensional homogeneity is not a separate law of nature. It is a direct consequence of the meaning of dimensions.

Mathematical Expression

The Principle of Dimensional Homogeneity can be expressed mathematically.

Whenever physical quantities are connected through addition, subtraction or equality, they must all possess the same dimensions.

For an equation of the form:

\[ A+B=C \]

the dimensions of all terms must be identical:

\[ [A]=[B]=[C] \]

It is important to note that dimensional homogeneity does not require every quantity appearing in an equation to have the same dimensions.

The requirement applies only to quantities that are being added, subtracted or directly compared.

Consider the equation:

\[ s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2 \]

The dimensions of each term are:

\[ [s]=[L] \] \[ [ut]=[LT^{-1}][T]=[L] \] \[ [at^2]=[LT^{-2}][T^2]=[L] \]

Since every term has the same dimensions, the equation is dimensionally homogeneous.

Dimensional homogeneity does not guarantee that an equation is correct.
However, if an equation violates dimensional homogeneity, it must be incorrect.

Transcendental Functions

Dimensional homogeneity places an additional restriction on certain mathematical functions.

The arguments of transcendental functions such as \(\sin x\), \(\cos x\), \(\tan x\), \(e^x\), and \(\log x\) must always be dimensionless.

This is because these functions are defined through infinite series expansions. Terms of different dimensions cannot be added together, so the argument itself must be dimensionless.

For example,

\[ \sin x = x-\frac{x^3}{3!} +\frac{x^5}{5!} -\cdots \]

All terms in the series are added together. Therefore,

\[ [x]=[x^3]=[x^5]=\cdots \]

This is possible only when:

\[ [x]=[M^0L^0T^0] \]

The same requirement applies to all transcendental functions.

Consider the travelling wave equation:

\[ y=A\sin(kx-\omega t) \]

The argument of the sine function must be dimensionless. Therefore,

\[ [kx]=[M^0L^0T^0] \] \[ [\omega t]=[M^0L^0T^0] \]

Since both terms are dimensionless, the quantity \((kx-\omega t)\) is also dimensionless, making the equation physically meaningful.

Whenever a quantity appears inside a trigonometric, exponential or logarithmic function, its dimensions must be zero.
Transcendental functions can only accept dimensionless arguments.
