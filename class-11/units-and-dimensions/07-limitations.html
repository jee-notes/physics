Limitations of Dimensional Analysis

When and why dimensional methods fail.

1. The Numerical Constant Problem

Dimensional analysis cannot provide the value of the proportionality constant (\(K\)). For example, it can show that \(s \propto at^2\), but it cannot tell you that the constant is \(1/2\).

2. Dependency Limits

We cannot derive a relation if a physical quantity depends on more than three fundamental quantities, as we only have three primary equations (for M, L, and T).

3. Functional Blindness

Dimensional analysis fails for relations involving:

  • Trigonometric functions (\(\sin, \cos, \tan\))
  • Logarithmic functions (\(\log, \ln\))
  • Exponential functions (\(e^x\))
  • Addition/Subtraction of multiple terms in a derivation
Even if an equation is dimensionally consistent, we need experimental verification or deeper theoretical derivation to prove its physical validity.
