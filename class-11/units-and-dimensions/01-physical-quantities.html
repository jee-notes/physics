What is a Physical Quantity?

Defining the measurable properties of the physical world.

1. Definition

A Physical Quantity is any property of a material or system that can be quantified by measurement. In other words, if you can measure it and give it a numerical value, it is a physical quantity.

  • Examples: Length, Mass, Time, Temperature, Force, Electric Current.
  • Counter-examples: Love, Hate, Beauty, Happiness (these cannot be measured rigorously).

2. Mathematical Representation

Every measurement of a physical quantity consists of two essential parts:

\[ Q = n \times u \]

Where:

  • \(Q\): The physical quantity itself.
  • \(n\): The numerical value (how many times the unit is contained).
  • \(u\): The unit (the standard reference).

3. The Unit-Magnitude Relationship

The physical quantity remains the same regardless of the unit chosen. Therefore, if you choose a larger unit, the numerical value will be smaller, and vice versa.

\[ n_1u_1 = n_2u_2 \implies n \propto \frac{1}{u} \]
Example: 1 meter (\(u_1\)) = 100 centimeters (\(u_2\)). As the unit becomes 100 times smaller, the number becomes 100 times larger.
