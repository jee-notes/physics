Approximations & Cooling Methods

Average temperature approximation methods, comparative analysis, and experimental calorimetric specific heat determination.

Average Temperature Approximation Method

While the exact differential equation yields an exponential decay function, solving transcendental exponential equations during numerical calculations can be cumbersome. For small temperature intervals where a body cools from \( T_1 \) to \( T_2 \) over time \( t \), Newton's Law is approximated using the Arithmetic Mean Temperature:

\[ \frac{T_1 - T_2}{t} = K \left( \frac{T_1 + T_2}{2} - T_0 \right) \]

where \( \frac{T_1 - T_2}{t} \) is the average rate of fall of temperature, \( \frac{T_1 + T_2}{2} \) is the mean temperature of the body during time interval \( t \), and \( T_0 \) is the surrounding ambient temperature.

This linear average approximation is exceptionally accurate whenever \( (T_1 - T_2) \) is relatively small (typically \( \le 10-15^\circ\text{C} \)) and represents the standard method used in JEE Main and Advanced problem solving.

Comparison of Exact vs Approximate Methods

Comparative Benchmark Problem

A body cools from \( 80^\circ\text{C} \) to \( 60^\circ\text{C} \) in 5 minutes in a room maintained at \( 20^\circ\text{C} \). How long will it take to cool from \( 60^\circ\text{C} \) to \( 40^\circ\text{C} \) under identical surroundings?

Method 1: Approximate (Average) Method:

Case 1 (\( 80^\circ\text{C} \to 60^\circ\text{C} \) in \( t_1 = 5\text{ min} \)):

\[ \frac{80 - 60}{5} = K \left( \frac{80 + 60}{2} - 20 \right) \implies \frac{20}{5} = K(70 - 20) \implies 4 = 50K \implies K = \frac{4}{50} = \frac{2}{25}\text{ min}^{-1} \]

Case 2 (\( 60^\circ\text{C} \to 40^\circ\text{C} \) in time \( t_2 \)):

\[ \frac{60 - 40}{t_2} = K \left( \frac{60 + 40}{2} - 20 \right) \implies \frac{20}{t_2} = K(50 - 20) = 30K \] \[ \frac{20}{t_2} = 30 \left( \frac{4}{50} \right) = \frac{12}{5} \implies t_2 = \frac{20 \times 5}{12} = \frac{100}{12} \approx 8.33\text{ minutes} = 8\text{ min } 20\text{ sec} \]

Method 2: Exact (Logarithmic) Method:

Using \( \ln\left(\frac{T - T_0}{T_i - T_0}\right) = -Kt \):

Case 1:

\[ \ln\left( \frac{60 - 20}{80 - 20} \right) = -K(5) \implies \ln\left(\frac{40}{60}\right) = -5K \implies \ln\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) = -5K \implies K = \frac{\ln(1.5)}{5} \approx \frac{0.4055}{5} \approx 0.0811\text{ min}^{-1} \]

Case 2:

\[ \ln\left( \frac{40 - 20}{60 - 20} \right) = -K(t_2) \implies \ln\left(\frac{20}{40}\right) = -K t_2 \implies \ln(0.5) = -K t_2 \] \[ t_2 = \frac{\ln 2}{K} = \frac{0.6931}{0.0811} \approx 8.55\text{ minutes} \]
The difference between the two results is merely \( \approx 2.5\% \). In examinations, unless explicit natural logarithms are provided, the average method is preferred.

Cooling of Two Liquids (Calorimetric Applications)

Consider two distinct liquids of masses \( m_1, m_2 \) and specific heat capacities \( c_1, c_2 \) filled successively to the same volume in identical calorimeter vessels of water equivalent \( W = m_{\text{cal}} c_{\text{cal}} \).

Because the calorimeter vessels have identical surface area \( A \), surface finish (emissivity \( e \)), and are placed in the same ambient surroundings at temperature \( T_0 \), their rate of heat loss \( \frac{dQ}{dt} \) at any temperature \( T \) is identical:

\[ \frac{dQ}{dt} = \text{constant for both liquids at same } T \]

The total heat lost by each liquid-calorimeter system in cooling across the same temperature range from \( T_1 \) to \( T_2 \) is:

\[ \Delta Q_1 = (m_1 c_1 + W)(T_1 - T_2) \implies \frac{dQ_1}{dt} \approx \frac{(m_1 c_1 + W)(T_1 - T_2)}{t_1} \] \[ \Delta Q_2 = (m_2 c_2 + W)(T_1 - T_2) \implies \frac{dQ_2}{dt} \approx \frac{(m_2 c_2 + W)(T_1 - T_2)}{t_2} \]

Equating the rates of heat loss:

\[ \frac{m_1 c_1 + W}{t_1} = \frac{m_2 c_2 + W}{t_2} \]

Experimental Determination of Specific Heat

The relation derived above forms the basis of the standard laboratory method for finding the specific heat capacity \( c_2 \) of an unknown liquid by comparison with a reference liquid (usually water, where \( c_1 = 4184\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K} \)):

  1. Fill a polished copper calorimeter with a known mass \( m_1 \) of water, heat it to \( \approx 80^\circ\text{C} \), and record the cooling time \( t_1 \) taken to cool from \( T_1 \) to \( T_2 \).
  2. Empty and dry the calorimeter. Fill it with an equal volume of the experimental liquid of mass \( m_2 \).
  3. Heat to the same initial temperature \( T_1 \) and record the cooling time \( t_2 \) across the identical temperature range \( T_1 \to T_2 \).
  4. Calculate \( c_2 \) using:
    \[ c_2 = \frac{1}{m_2} \left[ \frac{t_2}{t_1}(m_1 c_1 + W) - W \right] \]
