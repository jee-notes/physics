Newton's Law of Cooling

Linearized thermal radiation loss, differential cooling equations, and exponential decay dynamics.

Statement and Validity Conditions

Newton's Law of Cooling states that the rate of loss of heat (or rate of cooling) of a body is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surrounding environment, provided the temperature difference is small.

\[ \frac{dQ}{dt} \propto (T - T_0) \implies -\frac{dT}{dt} = K(T - T_0) \]

where \( T \) is the instantaneous temperature of the body, \( T_0 \) is the constant ambient temperature of the surroundings, and \( K \) is the cooling constant.

Strict Validity Conditions:
  1. The temperature difference between the body and surroundings must be small compared to the absolute temperature (\( \Delta T = T - T_0 \ll T_0 \), practically \( \Delta T \lesssim 30-40^\circ\text{C} \)).
  2. Heat loss occurs predominantly by natural convection and radiation in still air.
  3. The surrounding temperature \( T_0 \) remains strictly constant throughout the cooling process.
  4. The temperature within the body is spatially uniform at any instant (negligible internal conductive resistance, Biot number \( Bi \ll 0.1 \)).

Derivation of Newton's Law of Cooling from Stefan's Law

According to the Stefan-Boltzmann Law, the net rate of radiative heat loss from a body of area \( A \), emissivity \( e \), mass \( m \), and specific heat capacity \( c \) at absolute temperature \( T \) in surroundings at \( T_0 \) is:

\[ P_{\text{net}} = -m c \frac{dT}{dt} = e \sigma A (T^4 - T_0^4) \]

Let \( T = T_0 + \Delta T \), where \( \Delta T \ll T_0 \). We express \( T^4 \) as:

\[ T^4 = (T_0 + \Delta T)^4 = T_0^4 \left( 1 + \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \right)^4 \]

Expanding binomially because \( \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \ll 1 \) and neglecting second- and higher-order terms:

\[ \left( 1 + \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \right)^4 \approx 1 + 4\frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \]

Substituting back into the fourth-power temperature difference:

\[ T^4 - T_0^4 = T_0^4 \left( 1 + 4\frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \right) - T_0^4 = 4 T_0^3 \Delta T = 4 T_0^3 (T - T_0) \]

Substituting this linear approximation into the heat loss equation:

\[ -m c \frac{dT}{dt} = e \sigma A \left[ 4 T_0^3 (T - T_0) \right] = (4 e \sigma A T_0^3)(T - T_0) \]
\[ -\frac{dT}{dt} = K (T - T_0), \qquad \text{where } K = \frac{4 e \sigma A T_0^3}{m c} \]
The cooling constant \( K \) depends on:
  1. Surface area \( A \) and emissivity \( e \).
  2. Ambient temperature cubed (\( T_0^3 \)). Higher surrounding temperatures increase \( K \).
  3. Inversely on thermal capacity (\( m c = \rho V c \)).
SI Unit of \( K \): \(\text{s}^{-1}\)  |  Dimensional Formula: \([T^{-1}]\)

Differential Equation and Exponential Decay Form

To determine the explicit temporal temperature profile \( T(t) \), we solve the first-order differential equation:

\[ \frac{dT}{T - T_0} = -K \, dt \]

Integrating from initial time \( t = 0 \) (where \( T = T_i \)) to time \( t \) (where temperature is \( T \)):

\[ \int_{T_i}^T \frac{dT'}{T' - T_0} = -K \int_0^t dt' \] \[ \ln\left( \frac{T - T_0}{T_i - T_0} \right) = -K t \]

Exponentiating both sides:

\[ T(t) = T_0 + (T_i - T_0) e^{-K t} \]
The temperature of the cooling body decays exponentially toward the surrounding temperature \( T_0 \). Asymptotically, as \( t \to \infty \), \( T \to T_0 \).

Cooling Curves and Physical Graphs

  • \( T \) versus \( t \) Curve: Starts at \( T_i \) with a steep negative slope (\( -K(T_i - T_0) \)) and flattens out exponentially, asymptotically approaching \( T_0 \).
  • \( \ln(T - T_0) \) versus \( t \) Graph: Yields a perfectly straight line with intercept \( \ln(T_i - T_0) \) and constant negative slope equal to \( -K \).
  • Rate of Cooling \( \left(-\frac{dT}{dt}\right) \) versus Temperature Difference \( (T - T_0) \): A straight line passing through the origin with slope \( K \).
