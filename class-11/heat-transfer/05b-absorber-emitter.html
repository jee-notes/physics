Absorber-Emitter

Good Absorber is Good Emitter.

Good Absorbers are Good Emitters Principle

Because \( E_{bb,\lambda} \) is a constant for a given temperature:

\[ E_\lambda = a_\lambda \cdot E_{bb,\lambda} \implies E_\lambda \propto a_\lambda \]
Fundamental Takeaway: A body that absorbs strongly at a particular wavelength \( \lambda \) will also emit strongly at that exact same wavelength at the same temperature. Conversely, poor absorbers are necessarily poor emitters.

Classic Demonstrations:

  • Ritchie's Experiment: A hollow metal box has one face coated with lampblack (high absorptance \( a \approx 1 \)) and the opposite face polished silver (high reflectance \( r \approx 1 \)). When hot water is poured inside, the black face radiates far more intensely than the polished face. When cold water is placed inside and radiation shines on both faces, the black face absorbs far more energy.
  • Black Spots on White Porcelain: If a piece of white porcelain with dark spots painted on it is heated in a dark furnace to \( 1000^\circ\text{C} \), the dark spots appear much brighter than the surrounding white ceramic because the dark spots absorbed more radiation at room temperature and must emit more intensely at high temperature.
