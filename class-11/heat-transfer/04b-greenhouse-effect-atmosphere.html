Atmospheric Radiation & Greenhouse Effect

Wavelength-selective transmission in atmospheric optics and planetary radiative equilibrium.

Greenhouse Effect Mechanism

The Greenhouse Effect is the natural warming process by which an atmosphere traps thermal infrared radiation emitted by a planet's surface, maintaining the surface temperature higher than it would be in radiative equilibrium with space alone.

The physical principle rests entirely on the wavelength-dependent transmission properties of glass (or atmospheric gases):

  1. The Sun's surface temperature is extremely high (\( T_{\odot} \approx 5800\text{ K} \)). According to Wien's Displacement Law (\( \lambda_m \propto 1/T \)), solar radiation peaks in the short-wavelength visible and near-infrared band (\( \lambda \approx 0.5\ \mu\text{m} \)).
  2. Glass and Earth's atmosphere are transparent (diathermanous) to these short-wavelength solar rays, allowing sunlight to pass through almost unattenuated to warm the ground.
  3. The warmed Earth's surface re-radiates this absorbed energy at a much lower temperature (\( T_E \approx 288\text{ K} \)). The re-radiated terrestrial spectrum peaks in the long-wavelength thermal infrared band (\( \lambda \approx 10\ \mu\text{m} \)).
  4. Atmospheric greenhouse gases and glass are opaque (athermanous) to long-wavelength infrared radiation. They absorb this terrestrial radiation and re-emit a significant fraction back toward the Earth's surface.

Selective Absorption by Atmospheric Gases

Atmospheric gases exhibit quantized rotational and vibrational molecular absorption bands that interact strongly with infrared photon energies:

  • Water Vapor (\( \text{H}_2\text{O} \)): Responsible for the largest component of natural greenhouse warming (\( \approx 60\% \)), with strong absorption bands around \( 2.7\ \mu\text{m}, 6.3\ \mu\text{m} \), and above \( 12\ \mu\text{m} \).
  • Carbon Dioxide (\( \text{CO}_2 \)): Strong vibrational bending mode absorption centered at \( 15\ \mu\text{m} \), right in the peak emission band of terrestrial radiation.
  • Methane (\( \text{CH}_4 \)) & Nitrous Oxide (\( \text{N}_2\text{O} \)): Extremely potent greenhouse gases per molecule, absorbing in the \( 7-8\ \mu\text{m} \) atmospheric window.

Planetary Heat Balance

For a planet of radius \( R_p \) at distance \( d \) from the Sun to maintain a steady mean surface temperature \( T_p \), the total solar radiative power absorbed must equal the total infrared power radiated by the planet:

\[ P_{\text{absorbed}} = (1 - \mathcal{A}) \cdot S \cdot (\pi R_p^2) \] \[ P_{\text{emitted}} = e \sigma (4\pi R_p^2) T_p^4 \]

where \( \mathcal{A} \) is the planet's albedo (fraction of solar radiation reflected directly into space, \( \approx 0.30 \) for Earth) and \( S \) is the solar constant at the planet's orbit.

Equating \( P_{\text{absorbed}} = P_{\text{emitted}} \) (assuming blackbody emission \( e = 1 \)):

\[ T_{\text{effective}} = \left[ \frac{(1 - \mathcal{A}) S}{4 \sigma} \right]^{1/4} \]
Without the natural greenhouse effect, Earth's radiative equilibrium temperature would be \( T_{\text{effective}} \approx 255\text{ K} \) (\( -18^\circ\text{C} \)), causing the entire globe to freeze over. The atmospheric greenhouse blanket raises Earth's average surface temperature to a hospitable \( 288\text{ K} \) (\( +15^\circ\text{C} \)).
