Piston-spring interactions, multi-compartment partitions, linear P-V curves, and extremal temperature points.

Thermodynamics of Piston-Cylinder Systems

A major portion of advanced JEE problems involves composite thermodynamic-mechanical systems where the gas pressure is dynamically constrained by gravity, external atmospheric forces, and movable pistons.

For a vertical cylinder sealed by a frictionless piston of mass \( M \) and cross-sectional area \( A \) in mechanical equilibrium:

\[ P_{\text{gas}} \cdot A = P_0 \cdot A + M g \implies P_{\text{gas}} = P_0 + \frac{M g}{A} \] where \( P_0 \) is the external ambient atmospheric pressure.

Systems with External Atmospheric Pressure and Springs

When the piston is coupled to a linear mechanical spring of spring constant \( k \), the gas pressure becomes a linear function of piston displacement \( x \), and therefore of gas volume \( V \).

Force Balance & Equation of Path:

Let \( x \) be the displacement of the piston from the relaxed state of the spring. The volume is \( V = V_0 + A x \implies x = \frac{V - V_0}{A} \).
Force balance on the piston: \[ P(V) \cdot A = P_0 \cdot A + k x = P_0 A + k \left(\frac{V - V_0}{A}\right) \]

\[ P(V) = P_0 + \frac{k}{A^2}(V - V_0) \] This represents a straight line on the \( P \)-\( V \) diagram with positive slope \( \frac{dP}{dV} = \frac{k}{A^2} \).

Total Work Done in Spring-Piston Expansion:

\[ W_{\text{gas}} = \int P(V) \, dV = P_0 (V_f - V_i) + \frac{1}{2} k (x_f^2 - x_i^2) = W_{\text{atm}} + \Delta U_{\text{spring}} \]
Work done by the expanding gas is partitioned into pushing back the atmosphere (\( P_0 \Delta V \)) and storing elastic potential energy in the compressed spring (\( \frac{1}{2} k x^2 \)).

Compartments Separated by Fixed or Movable Partitions

Consider an insulated cylinder divided into two compartments \( 1 \) and \( 2 \) by a partition:

  • Rigid & Insulating Partition: Both compartments are completely decoupled; \( \Delta V_1 = 0, \Delta V_2 = 0 \), and \( Q_{1\to 2} = 0 \).
  • Movable & Insulating Partition: The partition moves until mechanical equilibrium is reached: \[ P_{1,\text{final}} = P_{2,\text{final}} \] Total volume is conserved: \( V_1 + V_2 = V_{\text{total}} \). Each compartment undergoes an independent adiabatic process: \( P_1 V_1^\gamma = \text{const} \).
  • Movable & Conducting Partition: Reaches both mechanical equilibrium and thermal equilibrium simultaneously: \[ P_{1,\text{final}} = P_{2,\text{final}} \quad \text{and} \quad T_{1,\text{final}} = T_{2,\text{final}} \]

Conducting vs Insulating Separators and Thermal Equilibrium

When two gas samples of moles \( n_1, n_2 \) with molar heat capacities \( C_{v1}, C_{v2} \) at initial temperatures \( T_1, T_2 \) are brought into thermal contact via a rigid diathermic partition:

\[ Q_1 + Q_2 = 0 \implies n_1 C_{v1} (T_f - T_1) + n_2 C_{v2} (T_f - T_2) = 0 \]
\[ T_{\text{equilibrium}} = \frac{n_1 C_{v1} T_1 + n_2 C_{v2} T_2}{n_1 C_{v1} + n_2 C_{v2}} \]

Linear P-V Processes and Triangular or Elliptical Paths

In JEE physics problems, an ideal gas often undergoes a non-standard process represented by a linear relationship:

\[ P = -\alpha V + \beta \quad (\alpha, \beta > 0) \]

For an expansion from \( (P_1, V_1) \) to \( (P_2, V_2) \):

  • Work Done: Trapezoidal area: \( W = \frac{1}{2}(P_1 + P_2)(V_2 - V_1) \).
  • Internal Energy Change: \( \Delta U = \frac{P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1}{\gamma - 1} \).
  • Heat Exchanged: \( Q = \Delta U + W \).

Maximum and Minimum Temperature Determination along Process Paths

A classic JEE problem involves determining the maximum temperature achieved by a gas during a linear \( P \)-\( V \) process \( P = -\alpha V + \beta \).

Mathematical Method:

1. From the ideal gas equation: \( T = \frac{PV}{nR} = \frac{(-\alpha V + \beta)V}{nR} = \frac{-\alpha V^2 + \beta V}{nR} \).
2. To find the extremum, set \( \frac{dT}{dV} = 0 \): \[ \frac{d}{dV} \left( -\alpha V^2 + \beta V \right) = -2\alpha V + \beta = 0 \implies V_m = \frac{\beta}{2\alpha} = \frac{V_0}{2} \] 3. The corresponding pressure at maximum temperature: \[ P_m = -\alpha \left(\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}\right) + \beta = \frac{\beta}{2} = \frac{P_0}{2} \] 4. Maximum Temperature:

\[ T_{\max} = \frac{P_m V_m}{nR} = \frac{\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}\right)}{nR} = \frac{\beta^2}{4\alpha n R} = \frac{P_0 V_0}{4 n R} \]

Solved Example: Maximum Temperature on a Line

One mole of an ideal gas undergoes a process given by \( P = P_0 \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{V}{2V_0}\right)^2 \right] \) from \( V = 0 \) to \( V = 2V_0 \). Find the maximum temperature reached.

Solution:

\[ T(V) = \frac{PV}{R} = \frac{P_0}{R} \left[ V - \frac{V^3}{4V_0^2} \right] \] Setting \( \frac{dT}{dV} = 0 \implies 1 - \frac{3V^2}{4V_0^2} = 0 \implies V_m = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}V_0 \).
Substituting \( V_m \) back into \( T(V) \): \[ T_{\max} = \frac{P_0}{R} \left[ \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}V_0 - \frac{\frac{8}{3\sqrt{3}}V_0^3}{4V_0^2} \right] = \frac{P_0 V_0}{R} \left[ \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}} \right] = \frac{4 P_0 V_0}{3\sqrt{3} R} \]
