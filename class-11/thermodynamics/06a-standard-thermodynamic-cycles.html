Standard Thermodynamic Cycles

Theoretical models of internal combustion and regenerative gas cycles: Otto, Diesel, Stirling, and Dual cycles.

Otto Cycle (Air Standard Constant Volume Cycle)

The Otto Cycle is the idealized thermodynamic cycle that models the operation of spark-ignition (four-stroke petrol) internal combustion engines.

Four Sequential Stages:

  1. \( 1 \to 2 \): Reversible Adiabatic (Isentropic) Compression: Volume compresses from \( V_1 \) to \( V_2 \). Temperature rises from \( T_1 \) to \( T_2 = T_1 r^{\gamma - 1} \), where \( r = V_1 / V_2 \) is the compression ratio.
  2. \( 2 \to 3 \): Constant Volume (Isochoric) Heat Addition: Spark ignition causes rapid combustion: \( Q_{\text{in}} = n C_v (T_3 - T_2) \).
  3. \( 3 \to 4 \): Reversible Adiabatic Expansion (Power Stroke): High-pressure gas expands from \( V_2 \) back to \( V_1 \). Temperature drops from \( T_3 \) to \( T_4 = T_3 / r^{\gamma - 1} \).
  4. \( 4 \to 1 \): Constant Volume Heat Rejection (Exhaust): \( Q_{\text{out}} = n C_v (T_4 - T_1) \).
Thermal Efficiency of the Otto Cycle: \[ \eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \frac{Q_{\text{out}}}{Q_{\text{in}}} = 1 - \frac{n C_v (T_4 - T_1)}{n C_v (T_3 - T_2)} = 1 - \frac{T_1(T_4/T_1 - 1)}{T_2(T_3/T_2 - 1)} \] Since \( T_4/T_1 = T_3/T_2 \): \[ \eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \frac{T_1}{T_2} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma - 1}} \] where \( r = \frac{V_{\max}}{V_{\min}} \) is the compression ratio and \( \gamma = C_p / C_v \).
The efficiency of an Otto cycle depends exclusively on the compression ratio \( r \) and the adiabatic index \( \gamma \). Higher compression ratios yield higher thermodynamic efficiencies.

Diesel Cycle (Air Standard Constant Pressure Cycle)

The Diesel Cycle models compression-ignition engines (diesel engines) where fuel is injected directly into highly compressed, hot air and ignites spontaneously without a spark plug.

Four Stages of Diesel Cycle:

  1. \( 1 \to 2 \): Adiabatic Compression: Compression ratio \( r = V_1 / V_2 \).
  2. \( 2 \to 3 \): Constant Pressure (Isobaric) Heat Addition: Fuel injection during expansion: \( Q_{\text{in}} = n C_p (T_3 - T_2) \). The cut-off ratio is \( r_c = V_3 / V_2 \).
  3. \( 3 \to 4 \): Adiabatic Expansion: Volume expands from \( V_3 \) to \( V_1 \).
  4. \( 4 \to 1 \): Constant Volume Heat Rejection: \( Q_{\text{out}} = n C_v (T_4 - T_1) \).
Thermal Efficiency of the Diesel Cycle: \[ \eta_{\text{Diesel}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma - 1}} \left[ \frac{r_c^\gamma - 1}{\gamma (r_c - 1)} \right] \] where \( r = V_1 / V_2 \) and \( r_c = V_3 / V_2 \) (\( r_c > 1 \)).

Because \( \frac{r_c^\gamma - 1}{\gamma (r_c - 1)} > 1 \), for the same compression ratio \( r \), \( \eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}} \). However, real diesel engines can operate at much higher compression ratios (\( r \approx 16 - 22 \)) than petrol engines (\( r \approx 8 - 11 \)) without premature detonation (knocking), yielding superior overall fuel economy.

Stirling and Ericsson Cycles

The Stirling and Ericsson cycles are external combustion closed-loop cycles incorporating thermal regenerators to achieve theoretical efficiencies equal to the Carnot efficiency.

1. Stirling Cycle (Isotherms + Isochores):

  • \( 1 \to 2 \): Isothermal expansion at \( T_H \) with heat addition \( Q_{\text{in}} = nRT_H \ln(V_2 / V_1) \).
  • \( 2 \to 3 \): Isochoric cooling at \( V_2 \) to \( T_C \) (heat stored in internal regenerator).
  • \( 3 \to 4 \): Isothermal compression at \( T_C \) with heat rejection \( Q_{\text{out}} = nRT_C \ln(V_2 / V_1) \).
  • \( 4 \to 1 \): Isochoric heating at \( V_1 \) from \( T_C \) to \( T_H \) (regenerator restores heat to gas).
With an ideal regenerator (\( Q_{\text{regen, in}} = Q_{\text{regen, out}} \)): \[ \eta_{\text{Stirling}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} = \eta_{\text{Carnot}} \]

2. Ericsson Cycle (Isotherms + Isobars):

Replaces the two constant-volume regenerative processes of the Stirling cycle with two constant-pressure regenerative legs, also achieving \( \eta = 1 - T_C/T_H \).

Dual Combustion Cycle Overview

The Dual Combustion (Limited Pressure or Sabathe) Cycle is a hybrid cycle that combines features of both the Otto and Diesel cycles. Heat is added in two consecutive stages: partly at constant volume (simulating rapid initial deflagration) and partly at constant pressure (simulating ongoing controlled combustion).

This provides the most accurate mathematical approximation for modern high-speed automotive diesel and gasoline direct-injection (GDI) engines.
