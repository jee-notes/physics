Entropy in Phase Changes, Mixing, & Equilibrium

Thermodynamic entropy changes during phase transformations, irreversible thermal mixing, and cosmological implications.

Entropy Change During Isothermal Phase Change

A phase change (melting, vaporization, sublimation) occurs at a strictly constant temperature and pressure. The heat absorbed or released is the latent heat:

\[ Q = m L \] where \( m \) is the mass and \( L \) is the specific latent heat of the transformation.

Because temperature remains invariant throughout the phase transition (\( T = T_{\text{phase}} \)), the entropy change is calculated directly:

\[ \Delta S_{\text{phase}} = \frac{Q_{\text{rev}}}{T_{\text{phase}}} = \pm \frac{m L}{T_{\text{phase}}} \] where \( T_{\text{phase}} \) must be expressed in Kelvin (\( \text{K} \)).

Key Phase Transition Values:

  • Melting of Ice at \( 0^\circ\text{C} \) (\( 273.15 \, \text{K} \)): \[ \Delta S_{\text{fusion}} = +\frac{m L_f}{273.15 \, \text{K}} > 0 \quad (\text{Order of crystal lattice breaks down into liquid disorder}) \]
  • Freezing of Water at \( 0^\circ\text{C} \): \[ \Delta S_{\text{freezing}} = -\frac{m L_f}{273.15 \, \text{K}} < 0 \]
  • Vaporization of Water at \( 100^\circ\text{C} \) (\( 373.15 \, \text{K} \)): \[ \Delta S_{\text{vap}} = +\frac{m L_v}{373.15 \, \text{K}} > 0 \quad (\text{Massive volume expansion into gaseous state}) \]

Entropy of Mixing of Ideal Gases

Consider two distinct, non-reacting ideal gases \( 1 \) (moles \( n_1 \)) and \( 2 \) (moles \( n_2 \)) initially held in separate compartments of volumes \( V_1 \) and \( V_2 \) at the same temperature \( T \) and pressure \( P \).

When the partition is removed, both gases expand into the combined total volume \( V = V_1 + V_2 \).

Entropy of Mixing: \[ \Delta S_{\text{mix}} = \Delta S_1 + \Delta S_2 = n_1 R \ln\left(\frac{V_1 + V_2}{V_1}\right) + n_2 R \ln\left(\frac{V_1 + V_2}{V_2}\right) \] In terms of mole fractions \( x_1 = \frac{n_1}{n_1 + n_2} \) and \( x_2 = \frac{n_2}{n_1 + n_2} \): \[ \Delta S_{\text{mix}} = -(n_1 + n_2) R \left[ x_1 \ln x_1 + x_2 \ln x_2 \right] \] Since \( x_1, x_2 < 1 \), \( \ln x_i < 0 \), ensuring that \( \Delta S_{\text{mix}} > 0 \) always.
Gibbs Paradox Note: If the two gases are identical (e.g., Helium mixing with Helium at identical \( T \) and \( P \)), removing the partition causes zero physical change in macroscopic state, so \( \Delta S_{\text{mix, identical}} = 0 \). Entropy increases only when distinct, distinguishable chemical species mix.

Entropy Changes in Thermal Equilibrium of Composite Bodies

Consider two bodies \( A \) and \( B \) of heat capacities \( C_A \) and \( C_B \) at initial temperatures \( T_A > T_B \) placed inside a thermally insulated container and allowed to reach a common final equilibrium temperature \( T_f \).

From thermal energy conservation: \[ C_A (T_A - T_f) = C_B (T_f - T_B) \implies T_f = \frac{C_A T_A + C_B T_B}{C_A + C_B} \]

Entropy Change of Individual Bodies:

  • Body \( A \) (Hotter): \( \Delta S_A = \int_{T_A}^{T_f} \frac{C_A \, dT}{T} = C_A \ln\left(\frac{T_f}{T_A}\right) < 0 \) (Entropy decreases)
  • Body \( B \) (Colder): \( \Delta S_B = \int_{T_B}^{T_f} \frac{C_B \, dT}{T} = C_B \ln\left(\frac{T_f}{T_B}\right) > 0 \) (Entropy increases)

Total Entropy Change of the Isolated System (Universe):

\[ \Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_A + \Delta S_B = C_A \ln\left(\frac{T_f}{T_A}\right) + C_B \ln\left(\frac{T_f}{T_B}\right) \] For two identical bodies with equal heat capacity \( C_A = C_B = C \): \[ T_f = \frac{T_A + T_B}{2} \quad (\text{Arithmetic Mean}) \] \[ \Delta S_{\text{total}} = C \ln\left( \frac{T_f^2}{T_A T_B} \right) = 2 C \ln\left( \frac{\frac{T_A + T_B}{2}}{\sqrt{T_A T_B}} \right) = 2 C \ln\left( \frac{\text{Arithmetic Mean}}{\text{Geometric Mean}} \right) \] Since \( \text{Arithmetic Mean} > \text{Geometric Mean} \) for any two unequal positive numbers (\( T_A \neq T_B \)), \( \frac{\text{AM}}{\text{GM}} > 1 \implies \ln\left(\frac{\text{AM}}{\text{GM}}\right) > 0 \).
Therefore: \[ \Delta S_{\text{total}} > 0 \quad \text{strictly} \]

Heat Death of the Universe Concept

The cosmological consequence of the Second Law of Thermodynamics, first proposed by Lord Kelvin and Hermann von Helmholtz, is known as the Heat Death of the Universe (or the Big Freeze).

  • Because the entire universe is an isolated system, all irreversible thermal, mechanical, chemical, and nuclear processes continually generate entropy: \( \Delta S_{\text{universe}} > 0 \).
  • Over astronomical timescales, all temperature gradients across stars, galaxies, and interstellar gas clouds will eventually equilibrate.
  • Once the universe reaches its maximum possible entropy state, all matter will be at a uniform, microscopic thermal equilibrium temperature.
  • In this state of maximum entropy, zero thermodynamic work can ever be extracted, no heat engines can operate, and all physical, chemical, and biological activity will cease permanently.
