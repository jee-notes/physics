Adiabatic Process (Zero Heat Exchange)

Thermodynamics of thermally insulated systems: Poisson's relations, adiabatic work, and curve comparisons.

Definition and Condition (Zero Heat Exchange)

An Adiabatic Process is a thermodynamic transformation in which absolutely no heat enters or leaves the system across its boundary:

\[ dQ = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad Q = 0 \]

Physical Realization Requirements:

  1. The container walls and piston must be made of a perfectly insulating material (adiabatic boundary).
  2. Alternatively, the process must occur extremely rapidly (sudden expansion or compression) so that negligible time is available for heat to conduct across the boundaries (e.g., propagation of sound waves in air, sudden bursting of an inflated tyre, or compression strokes in an internal combustion engine).

Applying the First Law of Thermodynamics (\( dQ = dU + dW \)):

\[ 0 = dU + dW \implies dW = -dU = -n \, C_v \, dT \] \[ W = -\Delta U = n \, C_v \, (T_i - T_f) \]
  • Adiabatic Expansion (\( W > 0 \)): Work is done by the gas solely at the expense of its own internal energy. Therefore, \( \Delta U < 0 \implies \) the gas cools down (\( T_f < T_i \)).
  • Adiabatic Compression (\( W < 0 \)): Work is done on the gas, increasing its internal energy. Therefore, \( \Delta U > 0 \implies \) the gas heats up (\( T_f > T_i \)).

Adiabatic Relations Between P, V, and T

For a quasi-static reversible adiabatic process of an ideal gas, the state variables satisfy Poisson's Relations:

1. Derivation of \( P V^\gamma = \text{constant} \):

From the differential First Law: \[ dQ = n \, C_v \, dT + P \, dV = 0 \implies n \, C_v \, dT = -P \, dV \] From the ideal gas equation (\( PV = nRT \)), differentiating yields \( P\,dV + V\,dP = nR\,dT \). Substituting \( n\,dT = \frac{P\,dV + V\,dP}{R} \): \[ C_v \left( \frac{P\,dV + V\,dP}{R} \right) = -P \, dV \] Using Mayer's relation \( R = C_p - C_v \): \[ C_v (P\,dV + V\,dP) = -(C_p - C_v) P \, dV \] \[ C_v V \, dP + C_p P \, dV = 0 \] Dividing throughout by \( C_v P V \) and setting \( \gamma = C_p / C_v \): \[ \frac{dP}{P} + \gamma \frac{dV}{V} = 0 \] Integrating both sides:

\[ \ln P + \gamma \ln V = \text{constant} \implies P V^\gamma = \text{constant} \]

2. Equivalent State Formulations:

  • Temperature - Volume Relation: Using \( P = \frac{nRT}{V} \):
    \[ T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant} \implies T_1 V_1^{\gamma - 1} = T_2 V_2^{\gamma - 1} \]
  • Temperature - Pressure Relation: Using \( V = \frac{nRT}{P} \):
    \[ T^\gamma P^{1-\gamma} = \text{constant} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{T^\gamma}{P^{\gamma - 1}} = \text{constant} \implies \frac{T_1^\gamma}{P_1^{\gamma - 1}} = \frac{T_2^\gamma}{P_2^{\gamma - 1}} \]

Work Done in Adiabatic Expansion and Compression

To calculate the boundary work done as volume changes from \( V_i \) to \( V_f \) along \( P V^\gamma = K \):

\[ W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P \, dV = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} K V^{-\gamma} \, dV = K \left[ \frac{V^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma} \right]_{V_i}^{V_f} = \frac{K V_f^{1-\gamma} - K V_i^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma} \] Since \( K = P_f V_f^\gamma = P_i V_i^\gamma \):
\[ W = \frac{P_i V_i - P_f V_f}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{n R (T_i - T_f)}{\gamma - 1} \] where:
  • \( W \): Work done by the gas (SI Unit: Joule (\( \text{J} \)))
  • \( \gamma \): Ratio of specific heats (\( C_p / C_v \))
  • \( P_i, V_i, T_i \): Initial state parameters
  • \( P_f, V_f, T_f \): Final state parameters

Adiabatic Slope vs Isothermal Slope Comparison

Differentiating the adiabatic equation \( P V^\gamma = \text{constant} \):

\[ \frac{dP}{P} + \gamma \frac{dV}{V} = 0 \implies \left(\frac{dP}{dV}\right)_{\text{adiabatic}} = -\gamma \frac{P}{V} \]

Recalling that the isothermal slope is \( \left(\frac{dP}{dV}\right)_{\text{isothermal}} = -\frac{P}{V} \):

\[ \text{Slope}_{\text{adiabatic}} = \gamma \times \text{Slope}_{\text{isothermal}} \] Since \( \gamma > 1 \), the adiabatic curve on a \( P \)-\( V \) plane is always \( \gamma \) times steeper than the isothermal curve at any given intersection point \( (P_0, V_0) \).

Adiabatic Bulk Modulus of an Ideal Gas

The Adiabatic Bulk Modulus (\( B_S \)) represents the elastic stiffness of a gas under rapid, adiabatic pressure variations:

\[ B_S = -V \left(\frac{dP}{dV}\right)_{\text{adiabatic}} = -V \left(-\gamma \frac{P}{V}\right) = \gamma P \] where \( P \) is the absolute pressure.

Ratio of Elastic Moduli:

\[ \frac{B_S}{B_T} = \frac{\gamma P}{P} = \gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v} \]
This fundamental relationship explains Laplace's correction to Newton's formula for the speed of sound in gases: \( v = \sqrt{\frac{B_S}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} \).

Comparison of Isothermal and Adiabatic Curves

Starting from a single common initial state \( A \, (P_0, V_0, T_0) \):

Comprehensive Ranking Rules from Common Initial State \( (P_0, V_0) \):
  1. During Expansion to the same final volume \( V_f > V_0 \):
    • \( P_{\text{iso}} > P_{\text{adia}} \)
    • \( T_{\text{iso}} > T_{\text{adia}} \) (Adiabatic cools down)
    • \( W_{\text{iso}} > W_{\text{adia}} \) (Area under isothermal is strictly larger)
  2. During Compression to the same final volume \( V_f < V_0 \):
    • \( P_{\text{adia}} > P_{\text{iso}} \)
    • \( T_{\text{adia}} > T_{\text{iso}} \) (Adiabatic heats up)
    • \( |W_{\text{adia}}| > |W_{\text{iso}}| \) (More external work is required to compress adiabatically)

Solved Example: Sudden tyre burst

An ideal diatomic gas (\( \gamma = 1.4 \)) at \( 27^\circ\text{C} \) (\( 300 \, \text{K} \)) and \( 32 \, \text{atm} \) pressure expands adiabatically to atmospheric pressure (\( 1 \, \text{atm} \)). Find the final temperature of the gas.

Solution:

Using the temperature-pressure relation \( \frac{T_2^\gamma}{P_2^{\gamma-1}} = \frac{T_1^\gamma}{P_1^{\gamma-1}} \):

\[ T_2 = T_1 \left( \frac{P_2}{P_1} \right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} = 300 \left( \frac{1}{32} \right)^{\frac{1.4 - 1}{1.4}} = 300 \left( \frac{1}{2^5} \right)^{\frac{0.4}{1.4}} = 300 \left( 2^{-5} \right)^{2/7} = 300 \times 2^{-10/7} \approx 300 \times 0.3715 \approx 111.4 \, \text{K} \]

In Celsius: \( T_2 = 111.4 - 273.15 \approx -161.7^\circ\text{C} \). The rapid expansion results in severe cooling.
