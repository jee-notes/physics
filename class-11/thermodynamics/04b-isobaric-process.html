Isobaric Process (Constant Pressure)

Thermodynamics under constant load: boundary work, isobaric heating, and work-heat fractioning.

Definition and Condition (Constant Pressure)

An Isobaric Process is a thermodynamic transformation in which the pressure of the system is maintained strictly constant throughout:

\[ P = \text{constant} \implies dP = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta P = 0 \]

This is physically realized when a gas expands or contracts in a vertical cylinder fitted with a frictionless piston of mass \( M \) subject to constant atmospheric pressure \( P_0 \): \[ P_{\text{gas}} = P_0 + \frac{Mg}{A} = \text{constant} \]

From the ideal gas equation (\( PV = nRT \)), with \( P \) constant:

\[ \frac{V}{T} = \frac{nR}{P} = \text{constant} \implies \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2} \quad \text{(Charles's Law)} \]

Work Done in Isobaric Process

Since pressure \( P_0 \) is invariant, it can be factored directly out of the work integral:

\[ W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P_0 \, dV = P_0 \int_{V_i}^{V_f} dV = P_0 (V_f - V_i) = P_0 \Delta V \] Using the ideal gas equation (\( P\Delta V = nR\Delta T \)): \[ W = n \, R \, (T_f - T_i) = n \, R \, \Delta T \]

Heat Supplied and Internal Energy Change

When heat is added at constant pressure, it splits simultaneously into increasing internal energy and doing mechanical boundary work:

\[ Q = n \, C_p \, \Delta T = n \left(\frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}\right) \Delta T \] \[ \Delta U = n \, C_v \, \Delta T = n \left(\frac{R}{\gamma - 1}\right) \Delta T = \frac{P_0 \Delta V}{\gamma - 1} \] \[ W = n \, R \, \Delta T = P_0 \Delta V \]

Verifying energy balance via the First Law: \[ \Delta U + W = n C_v \Delta T + n R \Delta T = n (C_v + R) \Delta T = n C_p \Delta T = Q \]

Fraction of Heat Converted into Work

A cornerstone concept in competitive examinations is the partitioning ratio of heat supplied during an isobaric expansion of an ideal gas:

1. Fraction of Heat Converted to Mechanical Work (\( f_W \)):

\[ f_W = \frac{W}{Q} = \frac{n R \Delta T}{n C_p \Delta T} = \frac{R}{C_p} = \frac{C_p - C_v}{C_p} = 1 - \frac{1}{\gamma} = \frac{2}{f + 2} \]

2. Fraction of Heat Stored as Internal Energy (\( f_U \)):

\[ f_U = \frac{\Delta U}{Q} = \frac{n C_v \Delta T}{n C_p \Delta T} = \frac{C_v}{C_p} = \frac{1}{\gamma} = \frac{f}{f + 2} \] Notice that \( f_W + f_U = \left(1 - \frac{1}{\gamma}\right) + \frac{1}{\gamma} = 1 \).

Graphical Analysis on P-V and V-T Planes

The mathematical slope of an isobaric curve on the \( P \)-\( V \) diagram is strictly horizontal:

\[ \left( \frac{dP}{dV} \right)_{\text{isobaric}} = 0 \]

Diagrammatic Shapes:

  • \( P \)-\( V \) Diagram: A horizontal straight line at constant ordinate \( P = P_0 \). The area under the line from \( V_1 \) to \( V_2 \) is a simple rectangle of area \( P_0(V_2 - V_1) \).
  • \( V \)-\( T \) Diagram: A straight line passing through the origin (\( V = \frac{nR}{P_0} T \)). The slope is inversely proportional to pressure: \( \text{Slope} = \frac{nR}{P_0} \).
  • \( P \)-\( T \) Diagram: A horizontal straight line at constant height \( P = P_0 \).

Solved Example: Isobaric Expansion

\( 1 \, \text{mole} \) of an ideal monoatomic gas (\( \gamma = 5/3 \)) at \( 300 \, \text{K} \) expands isobarically until its volume doubles. Find \( W \), \( \Delta U \), and \( Q \) in terms of \( R \).

Solution:

Since \( V_2 = 2V_1 \) at constant pressure, by Charles's Law: \( T_2 = 2T_1 = 600 \, \text{K} \implies \Delta T = 300 \, \text{K} \).

\[ W = n R \Delta T = (1)(R)(300) = 300 \, R \] \[ \Delta U = n C_v \Delta T = (1)\left(\frac{3}{2}R\right)(300) = 450 \, R \] \[ Q = n C_p \Delta T = (1)\left(\frac{5}{2}R\right)(300) = 750 \, R \]

Check: \( Q = \Delta U + W = 450R + 300R = 750R \) (Exact conservation).
