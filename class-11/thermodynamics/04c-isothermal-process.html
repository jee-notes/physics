Isothermal Process (Constant Temperature)

Thermodynamics of thermal reservoirs: invariant internal energy, logarithmic work, and isothermal elasticity.

Definition and Condition (Constant Temperature)

An Isothermal Process is a thermodynamic transformation in which the absolute temperature of the system remains strictly constant throughout:

\[ T = \text{constant} \implies dT = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta T = 0 \]

Physical Realization Requirements:

  1. The container walls must be made of a perfectly conducting material (diathermic boundary) placed in intimate contact with a large constant-temperature thermal reservoir.
  2. The process must be carried out infinitely slowly (quasi-statically) so that any heat generated or absorbed is instantly equilibrated with the reservoir, maintaining \( T_{\text{system}} = T_{\text{reservoir}} \) at every instant.

For an ideal gas, since \( T \) is fixed, the equation of state reduces to Boyle's Law:

\[ P V = n R T_0 = \text{constant} \implies P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \]

Work Done in Isothermal Process

As the gas expands from volume \( V_i \) to \( V_f \), its pressure varies as \( P = \frac{nRT_0}{V} \). Substituting this into the work integral:

\[ W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P \, dV = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{n R T_0}{V} \, dV = n R T_0 \int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{dV}{V} \] \[ W = n R T_0 \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = 2.303 \, n R T_0 \log_{10}\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) \] Since \( P_i V_i = P_f V_f \implies \frac{V_f}{V_i} = \frac{P_i}{P_f} \): \[ W = n R T_0 \ln\left(\frac{P_i}{P_f}\right) \] where:
  • \( W \): Work done by the gas (SI Unit: Joule (\( \text{J} \)))
  • \( T_0 \): Constant absolute temperature in Kelvin
  • \( V_i, V_f \): Initial and final volumes
  • \( P_i, P_f \): Initial and final pressures

Heat Transfer in Isothermal Process

According to Joule's Law, the internal energy of an ideal gas depends exclusively on absolute temperature. Because \( \Delta T = 0 \):

\[ \Delta U = n \, C_v \, \Delta T = 0 \quad \text{(Strictly for an Ideal Gas)} \]

Applying the First Law of Thermodynamics (\( Q = \Delta U + W \)):

\[ Q = W = n R T_0 \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = n R T_0 \ln\left(\frac{P_i}{P_f}\right) \]

Physical Interpretation:

  • Isothermal Expansion (\( V_f > V_i \implies W > 0 \)): The gas absorbs heat from the thermal reservoir (\( Q > 0 \)) and converts \( 100\% \) of this absorbed heat into mechanical boundary work without changing its internal energy.
  • Isothermal Compression (\( V_f < V_i \implies W < 0 \)): External work done on the gas is completely extracted and dumped into the reservoir as heat (\( Q < 0 \)).
  • Molar Heat Capacity: Since \( dQ \neq 0 \) while \( dT = 0 \): \[ C_{\text{isothermal}} = \frac{dQ}{n \, dT} = \frac{dQ}{0} = \pm \infty \]

Slope of Isothermal Curve on P-V Diagram

On a \( P \)-\( V \) diagram, the locus of an isothermal process is a rectangular hyperbola called an isotherm.

Differentiating \( P V = \text{constant} \):

\[ P \, dV + V \, dP = 0 \implies \left(\frac{dP}{dV}\right)_{\text{isothermal}} = -\frac{P}{V} \]
The slope is negative everywhere, indicating that pressure decreases monotonically as volume increases. For two different isotherms, the isotherm corresponding to a higher temperature \( T_2 > T_1 \) lies strictly above and to the right of the lower-temperature isotherm.

Isothermal Bulk Modulus of an Ideal Gas

The Bulk Modulus of Elasticity (\( B \)) measures the resistance of a substance to uniform volumetric compression:

\[ B = -\frac{\Delta P}{\frac{\Delta V}{V}} = -V \frac{dP}{dV} \]

Substituting the isothermal slope \( \left(\frac{dP}{dV}\right)_T = -\frac{P}{V} \):

\[ B_T = -V \left(-\frac{P}{V}\right) = P \] where:
  • \( B_T \): Isothermal Bulk Modulus of an ideal gas (SI Unit: Pascal (\( \text{Pa} \)))
  • \( P \): Instantaneous absolute pressure of the gas

Isothermal Compressibility (\( \kappa_T \)): The reciprocal of the bulk modulus: \[ \kappa_T = \frac{1}{B_T} = \frac{1}{P} \]

Solved Example: Isothermal Compression Work

Two moles of an ideal gas at \( 300 \, \text{K} \) are compressed isothermally from an initial pressure of \( 1 \, \text{atm} \) to a final pressure of \( 4 \, \text{atm} \). Calculate the work done and the heat exchanged with the reservoir. (\( R = 8.314 \, \text{J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K}) \), \( \ln 4 \approx 1.386 \)).

Solution:

\[ W = n R T \ln\left(\frac{P_i}{P_f}\right) = (2)(8.314)(300) \ln\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) = (4988.4)(-\ln 4) = (4988.4)(-1.386) \approx -6914 \, \text{J} \]

Since \( \Delta U = 0 \), the heat exchanged is:

\[ Q = W = -6914 \, \text{J} \]

Interpretation: \( 6914 \, \text{J} \) of mechanical work was done on the gas, and an equal amount (\( 6.914 \, \text{kJ} \)) of heat was released to the thermal reservoir.
