Isochoric Process (Constant Volume)

Thermodynamics of rigid containers: zero boundary work and direct thermal-to-internal conversion.

Definition and Condition (Constant Volume)

An Isochoric Process (also referred to as an isometric or isovolumetric process) is a thermodynamic transformation in which the volume of the system remains strictly constant throughout:

\[ V = \text{constant} \implies dV = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta V = 0 \]

This condition is physically achieved whenever a gas is confined inside a rigid, immovable container (such as a closed metallic autoclave or a sealed bomb calorimeter).

From the ideal gas equation of state (\( PV = nRT \)), with \( V \) and \( n \) fixed:

\[ \frac{P}{T} = \frac{nR}{V} = \text{constant} \implies \frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2} \quad \text{(Gay-Lussac's Law)} \]

Work Done in Isochoric Process

Because the boundaries of the system do not undergo any physical displacement, the boundary mechanical work performed is identically zero:

\[ W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P \, dV = \int_{V_0}^{V_0} P \, (0) = 0 \]
No matter how intensely a gas is heated or pressurized inside a rigid container, if the boundary does not move (\( dV = 0 \)), the mechanical boundary work done by the gas is strictly zero.

Heat Added and Internal Energy Change

Applying the First Law of Thermodynamics (\( dQ = dU + dW \)) with \( dW = 0 \):

\[ dQ = dU = n \, C_v \, dT \] \[ Q = \Delta U = n \, C_v \, (T_2 - T_1) = \frac{V_0 \, (P_2 - P_1)}{\gamma - 1} \] where:
  • \( Q \): Heat added to the system (SI Unit: Joule (\( \text{J} \)))
  • \( \Delta U \): Change in internal energy (SI Unit: Joule (\( \text{J} \)))
  • \( C_v = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{f}{2}R \): Molar heat capacity at constant volume
  • \( \Delta T = T_2 - T_1 = \frac{V_0(P_2 - P_1)}{nR} \): Temperature increment

Key Energy Partitioning:

  • Heating (\( Q > 0 \)): \( \Delta U > 0 \implies T \) increases and \( P \) increases proportionally. Exactly \( 100\% \) of added heat is stored as microscopic internal kinetic energy.
  • Cooling (\( Q < 0 \)): \( \Delta U < 0 \implies T \) drops and \( P \) decreases proportionally. Heat is released purely from internal energy depletion.

Slope and Representation on State Diagrams

The mathematical slope on the standard \( P \)-\( V \) plane is vertical:

\[ \left( \frac{dP}{dV} \right)_{\text{isochoric}} = \lim_{dV \to 0} \frac{\Delta P}{dV} = \pm \infty \]

Diagrammatic Profiles:

  • \( P \)-\( V \) Diagram: A vertical straight line parallel to the pressure axis at fixed abscissa \( V = V_0 \). (Area under vertical line = 0, confirming \( W = 0 \)).
  • \( P \)-\( T \) Diagram: A straight line passing through the origin (\( P = \frac{nR}{V_0} T \)). The slope is inversely proportional to volume: \( \text{Slope} = \frac{nR}{V_0} \).
  • \( V \)-\( T \) Diagram: A horizontal straight line at constant ordinate \( V = V_0 \).

Solved Example: Heating Rigidly Enclosed Gas

A rigid container of volume \( 0.05 \, \text{m}^3 \) contains \( 2 \, \text{moles} \) of an ideal monoatomic gas (\( C_v = 1.5R \)) at an initial pressure of \( 1.0 \times 10^5 \, \text{Pa} \). Heat is supplied until the pressure triples to \( 3.0 \times 10^5 \, \text{Pa} \).
Calculate: (a) Work done \( W \), (b) Change in internal energy \( \Delta U \), (c) Heat supplied \( Q \).

Solution:

(a) Since the container is rigid, \( dV = 0 \implies W = 0 \).

(b) Using the state-variable formula for \( \Delta U \):

\[ \Delta U = \frac{\Delta(PV)}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{V_0(P_f - P_i)}{\frac{5}{3} - 1} = \frac{0.05 \times (3.0 \times 10^5 - 1.0 \times 10^5)}{2/3} = \frac{0.05 \times 2.0 \times 10^5}{2/3} = 15,000 \, \text{J} = 15 \, \text{kJ} \]

(c) By the First Law: \( Q = \Delta U + W = 15 \, \text{kJ} + 0 = 15 \, \text{kJ} \).
