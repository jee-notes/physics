Polytropic Process (General Index)

The master generalized process: polytropic heat capacity, general work integrals, and negative specific heats.

General Polytropic Equation: PV^x = Constant

A Polytropic Process is any reversible thermodynamic transformation undergone by an ideal gas that satisfies the general empirical relation:

\[ P V^x = \text{constant} \] where \( x \) is the Polytropic Index (\( -\infty < x < \infty \)).

Equivalent state relations in terms of temperature \( T \) and volume \( V \) or pressure \( P \):

  • Temperature - Volume: \( T V^{x - 1} = \text{constant} \implies T_1 V_1^{x - 1} = T_2 V_2^{x - 1} \)
  • Temperature - Pressure: \( T^x P^{1 - x} = \text{constant} \implies \frac{T_1^x}{P_1^{x - 1}} = \frac{T_2^x}{P_2^{x - 1}} \)

Molar Heat Capacity for Polytropic Process

The molar heat capacity \( C \) for a general polytropic process \( P V^x = C \) is derived directly from the First Law of Thermodynamics.

Rigorous Derivation:

1. From the First Law for 1 mole of an ideal gas: \[ dQ = dU + dW = C_v \, dT + P \, dV \] 2. Differentiating \( P V^x = K \): \[ P (x V^{x-1} \, dV) + V^x \, dP = 0 \implies P x \, dV + V \, dP = 0 \] 3. From the ideal gas equation (\( PV = RT \)), differentiating gives \( P\,dV + V\,dP = R\,dT \). Substituting \( V\,dP = -x P\,dV \): \[ P \, dV - x P \, dV = R \, dT \implies P (1 - x) \, dV = R \, dT \implies P \, dV = \frac{R \, dT}{1 - x} \] 4. Substituting \( P\,dV \) back into the First Law: \[ dQ = C_v \, dT + \frac{R \, dT}{1 - x} = \left( C_v + \frac{R}{1 - x} \right) dT \] By definition of molar heat capacity \( dQ = C \, dT \):

\[ C = C_v + \frac{R}{1 - x} = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} + \frac{R}{1 - x} \] where:
  • \( C \): Molar heat capacity for the polytropic process (SI Unit: \( \text{J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K}) \))
  • \( C_v = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} \): Molar heat capacity at constant volume
  • \( x \): Polytropic index

Work Done in Polytropic Process

The boundary work done during a polytropic expansion from \( (P_i, V_i) \) to \( (P_f, V_f) \) (for \( x \neq 1 \)):

\[ W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P \, dV = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} K V^{-x} \, dV = \frac{P_i V_i - P_f V_f}{x - 1} = \frac{n R (T_i - T_f)}{x - 1} \]

For the special case \( x = 1 \) (Isothermal), the integral yields the logarithmic form: \( W = n R T \ln(V_f / V_i) \).

Special Cases for Various Values of Index x

The polytropic framework elegantly unifies all classical thermodynamic processes under a single equation:

Polytropic Index (\( x \)) Governing Equation Process Identity Molar Heat Capacity (\( C \))
\( x = 0 \) \( P V^0 = P = \text{const} \) Isobaric \( C = C_v + R = C_p \)
\( x = 1 \) \( P V^1 = \text{const} \) Isothermal \( C = C_v + \frac{R}{0} = \pm \infty \)
\( x = \gamma \) \( P V^\gamma = \text{const} \) Adiabatic \( C = C_v + \frac{R}{1 - \gamma} = C_v - C_v = \mathbf{0} \)
\( x \to \pm \infty \) \( P^{1/x} V = V = \text{const} \) Isochoric \( C = C_v + \frac{R}{\infty} = C_v \)

Condition for Negative Heat Capacity and Physical Interpretation

A frequently tested phenomenon in advanced physics is the occurrence of a negative molar heat capacity (\( C < 0 \)).

Mathematical Criterion:

Setting \( C < 0 \): \[ C_v + \frac{R}{1 - x} < 0 \implies \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} < \frac{R}{x - 1} \] Since \( x > 1 \) and \( \gamma > 1 \):

\[ 1 < x < \gamma \iff C < 0 \]

Physical Meaning of Negative Heat Capacity:

When \( 1 < x < \gamma \), heat is supplied to the gas (\( dQ > 0 \)), yet its temperature decreases (\( dT < 0 \)).

Why does temperature drop when heat is added?
In this intermediate regime, the gas expands so aggressively that the external mechanical work it performs (\( dW \)) is strictly greater than the heat energy supplied (\( dQ \)). To make up the energy deficit, the gas must extract energy from its own internal store (\( dU = dQ - dW < 0 \)), causing its temperature to fall despite absorbing heat!

Solved Example: Process with Negative Heat Capacity

An ideal monoatomic gas (\( \gamma = 5/3 \)) undergoes a process following \( P V^2 = \text{constant} \).
Find: (a) Molar heat capacity \( C \), (b) Work done when \( 1 \, \text{mole} \) heats up from \( 300 \, \text{K} \) to \( 400 \, \text{K} \).

Solution:

(a) Here \( x = 2 \). Since \( \gamma = 5/3 \approx 1.67 \), \( x > \gamma \), so \( C \) will be positive:

\[ C = C_v + \frac{R}{1 - x} = \frac{3}{2}R + \frac{R}{1 - 2} = \frac{3}{2}R - R = \frac{1}{2}R \]

(b) Work done for \( \Delta T = 400 - 300 = 100 \, \text{K} \):

\[ W = \frac{nR(T_i - T_f)}{x - 1} = \frac{(1)(R)(300 - 400)}{2 - 1} = -100 \, R \]

Heat supplied: \( Q = n C \Delta T = (1)\left(\frac{1}{2}R\right)(100) = +50 \, R \).
