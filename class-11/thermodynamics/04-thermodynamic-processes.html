Thermodynamic Processes on Ideal Gases

Comparative mechanics, governing constraints, slopes, and energy balances of standard gas processes.

Classification and Overview of Thermodynamic Processes

A thermodynamic process describes the path through which a system transitions from an initial equilibrium state \( (P_i, V_i, T_i) \) to a final equilibrium state \( (P_f, V_f, T_f) \). In JEE Physics, processes on ideal gases are categorized according to which state parameter or physical constraint remains constant during the transition:

  • Isochoric (Isometric) Process: Volume remains constant (\( V = \text{const} \)). No boundary work is done (\( W = 0 \)).
  • Isobaric Process: Pressure remains constant (\( P = \text{const} \)). The gas does mechanical work against constant external load.
  • Isothermal Process: Absolute temperature remains constant (\( T = \text{const} \)). Internal energy remains invariant for ideal gases (\( \Delta U = 0 \)).
  • Adiabatic Process: System is thermally isolated from surroundings (\( Q = 0 \)). Work is done entirely at the expense of internal energy.
  • Polytropic Process: General process following the path equation \( P V^x = \text{constant} \), unifying all classical processes.
  • Free Expansion: Sudden, non-quasistatic expansion of gas into a vacuum without external work or heat transfer.

Governing Equations and Constraints

Each fundamental process satisfies a characteristic constraint equation linking the macroscopic state variables \( P, V, T \):

Process Constraint Condition P-V Relation T-V Relation P-T Relation
Isochoric \( V = \text{const} \) \( V = C \) \( T \propto P \) \( \frac{P}{T} = \text{const} \)
Isobaric \( P = \text{const} \) \( P = C \) \( \frac{V}{T} = \text{const} \) \( T \propto V \)
Isothermal \( T = \text{const} \) \( PV = \text{const} \) \( T = C \) \( T = C \)
Adiabatic \( dQ = 0 \) \( P V^\gamma = \text{const} \) \( T V^{\gamma-1} = \text{const} \) \( T^\gamma P^{1-\gamma} = \text{const} \)
Polytropic \( PV^x = \text{const} \) \( P V^x = \text{const} \) \( T V^{x-1} = \text{const} \) \( T^x P^{1-x} = \text{const} \)

Comparative Slopes on P-V Diagrams

The slope of a process curve at any point on the \( P \)-\( V \) diagram represents the rate of change of pressure with respect to volume, \( \left( \frac{dP}{dV} \right) \).

1. Isothermal Slope:

Differentiating \( PV = \text{constant} \) with respect to \( V \): \[ P + V \frac{dP}{dV} = 0 \implies \left( \frac{dP}{dV} \right)_{\text{iso}} = -\frac{P}{V} \]

2. Adiabatic Slope:

Differentiating \( PV^\gamma = \text{constant} \) with respect to \( V \): \[ \gamma P V^{\gamma-1} + V^\gamma \frac{dP}{dV} = 0 \implies \left( \frac{dP}{dV} \right)_{\text{adia}} = -\gamma \frac{P}{V} \]

\[ \left( \frac{dP}{dV} \right)_{\text{adiabatic}} = \gamma \times \left( \frac{dP}{dV} \right)_{\text{isothermal}} \] Since \( \gamma > 1 \), the adiabatic curve on a \( P \)-\( V \) diagram is strictly steeper (has a greater negative slope) than the isothermal curve passing through the same point.
Expansion vs Compression Comparisons from a Common Initial State \( (P_0, V_0) \):
  • During Expansion (\( V > V_0 \)): \[ P_{\text{isobaric}} > P_{\text{isothermal}} > P_{\text{adiabatic}} \] \[ W_{\text{isobaric}} > W_{\text{isothermal}} > W_{\text{adiabatic}} \]
  • During Compression (\( V < V_0 \)): \[ P_{\text{adiabatic}} > P_{\text{isothermal}} > P_{\text{isobaric}} \] \[ |W_{\text{adiabatic}}| > |W_{\text{isothermal}}| > |W_{\text{isobaric}}| \]

Summary Table of Work, Heat, and Internal Energy Changes

The energy balance for 1 mole (or \( n \) moles) of an ideal gas across all standard processes:

Process Work Done (\( W \)) Internal Energy (\( \Delta U \)) Heat Supplied (\( Q \)) Molar Heat Capacity (\( C \))
Isochoric \( 0 \) \( n C_v \Delta T \) \( n C_v \Delta T \) \( C_v = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} \)
Isobaric \( P\Delta V = nR\Delta T \) \( n C_v \Delta T \) \( n C_p \Delta T \) \( C_p = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1} \)
Isothermal \( nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) \) \( 0 \) \( nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) \) \( \infty \)
Adiabatic \( \frac{P_i V_i - P_f V_f}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{nR\Delta T}{1-\gamma} \) \( n C_v \Delta T \) \( 0 \) \( 0 \)
Polytropic (\( PV^x=C \)) \( \frac{P_i V_i - P_f V_f}{x - 1} = \frac{nR\Delta T}{1-x} \) \( n C_v \Delta T \) \( n C \Delta T \) \( C = C_v + \frac{R}{1-x} \)
