Indicator Diagrams & Work Along Paths

Geometric representation of state transitions, area interpretations, and diagram transformations.

Indicator Diagrams and State Representations

An Indicator Diagram is a graphical representation of the successive equilibrium states through which a thermodynamic system passes during a quasi-static process.

Because any two independent state variables completely specify the equilibrium state of a fixed mass of an ideal gas (via the equation of state \( PV = nRT \)), a process can be mapped onto any two-dimensional coordinate plane:

  • \( P \)-\( V \) Diagram (Clapeyron Diagram): The standard mechanical representation where work is directly visible as an area.
  • \( P \)-\( T \) Diagram: Pressure vs Absolute Temperature, useful for identifying phase lines and constant volume isochores (straight lines passing through origin: \( P \propto T \)).
  • \( V \)-\( T \) Diagram: Volume vs Absolute Temperature, where isobaric processes are straight lines through the origin (\( V \propto T \)).
  • \( T \)-\( S \) Diagram: Temperature vs Entropy, where the area under the curve represents the reversible heat exchanged: \( Q_{\text{rev}} = \int T\,dS \).

Geometric Interpretation of Work as Area Under P-V Curve

The mathematical definition of mechanical work done during an expansion from \( V_i \) to \( V_f \) is:

\[ W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P \, dV = \text{Area under the } P\text{-}V \text{ curve bounded by } V = V_i, V = V_f, \text{ and the } V\text{-axis} \]
Sign Rules for Area:
  • If moving from Left to Right (\( V \) increases, expansion): \( W = +\text{Area} > 0 \).
  • If moving from Right to Left (\( V \) decreases, compression): \( W = -\text{Area} < 0 \).
  • For a Closed Loop (Cyclic Process):
    • Clockwise Cycle: \( W_{\text{net}} = +\text{Enclosed Area of Loop} > 0 \) (Heat Engine).
    • Counter-Clockwise Cycle: \( W_{\text{net}} = -\text{Enclosed Area of Loop} < 0 \) (Refrigerator).

Path Dependence of Work along Linear and Non-Linear Paths

Because work is an inexact differential, the area under the curve depends on the trajectory taken between the same initial state \( (P_1, V_1) \) and final state \( (P_2, V_2) \).

Linear P-V Process Calculation

A gas expands from state \( (P_1, V_1) \) to state \( (P_2, V_2) \) along a straight line path on the \( P \)-\( V \) diagram. Calculate the work done.

Method: Area of Trapezoid

The area under a straight line on the \( P \)-\( V \) plane forms a right trapezoid bounded by parallel vertical heights \( P_1 \) and \( P_2 \), and base \( (V_2 - V_1) \):

\[ W = \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} (P_1 + P_2) (V_2 - V_1) \]

This simple formula avoids explicit integration and solves a large family of standard JEE problems.

Even though \( \Delta U \) is identical for all paths connecting state 1 and state 2, \( W_{\text{path A}} \neq W_{\text{path B}} \). Therefore, by the First Law (\( Q = \Delta U + W \)), the heat absorbed \( Q \) is also strictly path-dependent.

Conversion Between Different Thermodynamic State Diagrams

A critical skill in JEE Advanced thermodynamics is mapping a process described on one diagram (e.g., \( P \)-\( T \) or \( V \)-\( T \)) to another (e.g., \( P \)-\( V \)).

Transformation Strategy:

  1. Identify the Process Equation: Use the Ideal Gas Equation \( PV = nRT \) to find how the variables relate along each leg.
    • Isochoric (\( V = \text{const} \)): \( P \propto T \) (straight line through origin on \( P \)-\( T \); vertical line on \( P \)-\( V \)).
    • Isobaric (\( P = \text{const} \)): \( V \propto T \) (straight line through origin on \( V \)-\( T \); horizontal line on \( P \)-\( V \)).
    • Isothermal (\( T = \text{const} \)): \( PV = \text{const} \) (hyperbola on \( P \)-\( V \); vertical line on \( P \)-\( T \); horizontal on \( V \)-\( T \)).
  2. Map the Coordinates of Key Vertices: Determine \( (P, V, T) \) at every state vertex \( A, B, C, \dots \).
  3. Trace the Sense of Rotation: A clockwise cycle on \( P \)-\( V \) corresponds to positive net work; ensure the mapped cycle maintains the proper thermodynamic sequence of expansion and compression.

Diagram Conversion Example

Consider a cycle \( A \to B \to C \to A \) on a \( P \)-\( T \) diagram:
\( A \to B \): Isochoric heating from \( (P_0, T_0) \) to \( (2P_0, 2T_0) \) with volume \( V_0 \).
\( B \to C \): Isothermal expansion at \( 2T_0 \) from \( 2P_0 \) to \( P_0 \) (volume expands to \( 2V_0 \)).
\( C \to A \): Isobaric cooling at \( P_0 \) from \( 2T_0 \) back to \( T_0 \) (volume compresses from \( 2V_0 \) to \( V_0 \)).

Mapping onto P-V Diagram:

  • \( A \to B \): Vertical straight line upward at \( V = V_0 \) from \( P_0 \) to \( 2P_0 \).
  • \( B \to C \): Rectangular hyperbola curving downward from \( (2P_0, V_0) \) to \( (P_0, 2V_0) \).
  • \( C \to A \): Horizontal straight line to the left at \( P = P_0 \) from \( 2V_0 \) to \( V_0 \).

The resulting closed loop on the \( P \)-\( V \) plane runs clockwise, demonstrating that this system operates as a net work-producing heat engine.
