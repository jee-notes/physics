Determining the Direction of Friction

The most critical skill in solving multi-body dynamics.

1. The Fundamental Principle

Friction always acts in a direction that opposes the relative motion (or the tendency of relative motion) between two surfaces.

To find the direction of friction on a body, ask yourself: "If the surface were frictionless, in which direction would this body slide relative to the other surface?" Friction will point exactly opposite to that sliding direction.

2. Examples

A. Walking

When you walk, you push the ground backward with your foot. Without friction, your foot would slide backward. Therefore, friction on your foot acts forward, providing the force needed for your acceleration.

B. Block on a Moving Truck

If a truck accelerates forward, a block on its bed tends to slide backward relative to the truck. Friction on the block acts forward to minimize this relative sliding.

3. Friction on Wheels

Determining the direction of friction on a rolling wheel depends on whether the wheel is driving (like a car's rear wheels) or being towed (like a bicycle's front wheel).

  • Driving Wheel: Tends to slip backward at the contact point \(\implies\) Friction acts forward.
  • Towed Wheel: Ground pushes the wheel point backward \(\implies\) Friction acts backward to initiate rotation.
