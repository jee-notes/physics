Kinetic Friction: Active Resistance

Resisting motion once sliding has begun.

1. Definition

Once the applied force exceeds the limiting static friction, the body starts sliding. At this point, the resistive force is called Kinetic Friction (\(f_k\)).

2. Magnitude

Kinetic friction is proportional to the normal force and is generally constant for a given pair of surfaces, regardless of the relative velocity (at moderate speeds).

\[ f_k = \mu_k N \]
Empirical observation shows that \(\mu_k < \mu_s\). It is always harder to start motion than to maintain it. This is why a car's braking system (ABS) tries to keep tires in a state of static rolling rather than kinetic sliding.

3. Direction

Kinetic friction always acts in the direction exactly opposite to the relative velocity of the sliding surface.
