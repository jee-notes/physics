The Physical World

From phenomenological appearance to structural description.

What is Physical World?

The physical world is the objective reality that exists independently of an individual's thoughts, beliefs, or imagination.

When the Sun rises at a particular location on Earth, it rises for everyone present there. When it stands overhead at noon, it is overhead for all observers at that location. When it sets, it sets for all. It is the same Sun for everyone. In this sense, we share the same reality.

Therefore, the position of the Sun in the sky at a given place and time is an objective fact, irrespective of how any individual feels or what they believe about it.

Even if no one believes in it, the physical world remains what it is. Some people may believe in ghosts while others may not. But if the Earth were to stop rotating, the consequences would affect every person on the planet, regardless of their beliefs.

The existence of the physical world does not depend on our opinions, beliefs, or imagination.

Contrast with the Imaginary

Human imagination is powerful. One may daydream of winning heroic battles or of saving damsels in distress.

But these experiences do not occur in shared space and time. They leave no physical trace. They cannot be independently verified.

The physical world is shared by all observers. Imaginary worlds are private experiences. This distinction forms the basis of the difference between what is objective and what is subjective.

Curiosity and Desire to Control

The physical world is a shared reality, and events in the physical world influence both living and non-living things.

There was a time when dinosaurs walked the Earth and dominated it. Today they are extinct. Scientists study fossils found in rocks to understand why and how this extinction occurred.

Many events occur in Physical World and are called as "natural phenomenon". All living things are ultimately consuming energy from the sun and matter from the planet (mother earth).

Humans are naturally curious. We ask questions such as Why do these phenomena occur? and How do they occur? Understanding them also allows us to predict and sometimes control their outcomes, enabling us to manage our interaction with nature more effectively.

Understanding is a mental process

Although the physical world exists independently of our minds, understanding it requires us to construct mental models.

Understanding itself is a mental process. To reason about the physical world, we must use imagination to form conceptual pictures and theoretical descriptions of how nature works.

The creative use of imagination is a defining trait of scientists and physicists.

There are famous anecdotes about August Kekulé, who reportedly visualized the ring structure of the benzene molecule after imagining a snake biting its own tail.

Science therefore does not eliminate imagination. Instead, it relies on the disciplined and correct use of imagination to construct models that explain the physical world.

Without such imaginative modeling, we would merely observe phenomena without truly understanding them.

Core ideas

This core ideas of this topic are:

  • The physical world is objective and shared by all observers.
  • Events in the physical world influence everyone.
  • Humans study the physical world out of curiosity and the desire to understand and control natural phenomena.
  • Understanding requires the creation of mental models.
  • Scientific knowledge evolves as these models improve over time.
