Change of Level

Problems linking Displaced Liquid and Buoyancy.

The Core Principle

There are certain type of problems which ask to comment about change of water level in a tank when some buoyancy scenario changes. The key concept in such problems is to link it with liquid displaced.

This is because if \(M\) is floating in water, then:

\[ V_{\text{displaced}} \cdot \rho_{l} \cdot g = M \cdot g \] \[ V_{\text{displaced}} = \frac{M}{\rho_{l}} \]

This will be true irrespective of whether \(M\) is in direct contact or not.

Simplest example

Question

A tank of water contains a block of ice of mass \(M\) floating on it. The ice melts. Comment on the change in level of water.

Solution

The trick is to find change in displaced liquid. Imagine ice block taken out of the water and Volume of empty space in water = Volume of displaced liquid. After melting, if displaced liquid decreased, level has decreased. If displaced liquid increased, level has increased. If displaced liquid same, level is same.

Step 1: Initial Volume Displaced

While the ice is floating, it displaces a volume of water \(V_1\) such that its weight is supported by buoyancy:

\[ V_1 = \frac{M}{\rho_{w}} \]

The Volume of the ice block is: \(V_{ice} = \frac{M}{\rho_{ice}}\)

Step 2: Volume after Melting

When the ice of mass \(M\) melts, it turns into liquid water. The mass remains \(M\), but its state changes. The volume of water formed, \(V_2\), is given by:

\[ V_2 = \frac{M}{\rho_{w}} \]

The Volume of the ice contracted: \(V_{water} = V_{ice} \cdot \frac{\rho_{ice}}{\rho_{water}}\) to exactly fit into the empty void.

Conclusion:

Since \(V_1 = V_2\), the space previously occupied by the submerged part of the ice is exactly filled by the water formed from the melted ice.

Result: The water level remains unchanged.

Level Change in Different Liquids

Question

A piece of ice of mass \(M\) is floating in a liquid of density \(\rho_L\). What happens to the level of the liquid when the ice melts?

Solution

Step 1: Volume of liquid displaced (while floating)

\[ Mg = \rho_L V_D g \quad \Rightarrow \quad V_D = \frac{M}{\rho_L} \]

Step 2: Volume of water formed after melting

\[ V_F = \frac{M}{\rho_w} \]

The change in liquid level depends on comparison between displaced volume \(V_D\) and final volume \(V_F\).

\[ V_D = \frac{M}{\rho_L}, \quad V_F = \frac{M}{\rho_w} \]

Case 1: Liquid is water (\(\rho_L = \rho_w\))

\[ V_D = V_F \]
No change in level.

Case 2: Liquid denser than water (\(\rho_L > \rho_w\))

\[ \frac{M}{\rho_L} \lt \frac{M}{\rho_w} \quad \Rightarrow \quad V_D \lt V_F \]
Final volume is greater than displaced volume → level rises (in case of mercury).

Case 3: Liquid lighter than water (\(\rho_L \lt \rho_w\))

\[ \frac{M}{\rho_L} > \frac{M}{\rho_w} \quad \Rightarrow \quad V_D > V_F \]
Final volume is less than displaced volume → level falls.

Case Study: Ice with Air Bubble

Question

An ice block of mass \(M\) containing an air bubble of negligible mass is floating in a tank of water. What happens to the water level when the ice melts?

Solution

Step 1: Initial Volume Displaced (\(V_{D1}\))

Since air is massless, the total floating mass is effectively just \(M\). To support this weight:

\[ V_{D1} = \frac{M}{\rho_{w}} \]

Step 2: Final Volume after Melting (\(V_{F}\))

When the ice melts, it turns into liquid water of mass \(M\). The air bubble simply escapes and does not displace any liquid. The volume of new water formed is:

\[ V_{F} = \frac{M}{\rho_{w}} \]

Result: Since \(V_{D1} = V_{F}\), the water level remains unchanged.

Case Study: Ice with Lead Shot

Question

An ice block of mass \(M\) contains a lead shot of mass \(m\) (\(\rho_{Pb} > \rho_{w}\)). The system is floating. What happens to the level when the ice melts and the lead shot sinks?

Solution

Step 1: Initial Displacement (Floating System)

The total weight \((M + m)g\) is balanced by the buoyancy of displaced water:

\[ V_{D1} = \frac{M}{\rho_{w}} + \frac{m}{\rho_{w}} \]

Step 2: Final Displacement (Ice Melted, Lead Sunk)

The ice becomes water of volume \(M/\rho_{w}\). However, the lead shot is now resting at the bottom. Because it has sunk, it displaces only its own physical volume:

\[ V_{D2} = \frac{M}{\rho_{w}} + \frac{m}{\rho_{Pb}} \]

Step 3: Comparison

Since lead is denser than water (\(\rho_{Pb} > \rho_{w}\)):

\[ \frac{m}{\rho_{Pb}} \lt \frac{m}{\rho_{w}} \implies V_{D2} \lt V_{D1} \]

Result: The total volume of displaced liquid decreases, so the water level falls.
