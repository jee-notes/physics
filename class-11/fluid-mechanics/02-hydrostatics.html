Hydrostatics

Continuum of Scalar Fields

What Hydrostatic Means?

Fluid mechanics is broadly divided into two regimes based on whether the fluid is in motion or not:

  • Fluid Statics: Study of fluids at rest
  • Fluid Dynamics: Study of fluids in motion

The term hydrostatic specifically refers to fluids that are at rest.

"Rest" here means that there is no relative motion between different parts of the fluid. The entire fluid may be moving as a whole (like water in a moving container), but as long as there is no internal flow, it is treated as static.

Hydrostatics is not about absolute rest — it is about absence of internal motion.

In this condition, the fluid settles into a state of equilibrium where all forces balance exactly.

What Changes in Hydrostatics?

When a fluid is at rest, a major simplification occurs:

  • No relative motion between layers
  • No velocity field
  • No shear stresses

Because there is no motion, the fluid cannot sustain tangential forces. As a result, the only internal force that exists is pressure, which always acts perpendicular to surfaces.

In hydrostatics, all internal interactions in the fluid reduce to a single scalar field: pressure.

This is what makes hydrostatics significantly simpler than fluid dynamics — we do not need to track motion or acceleration, only how pressure varies in space.

Forces in a Static Fluid

Even though the fluid is at rest, forces are still present.

Consider a small element of fluid inside a larger body:

  • It experiences pressure forces from surrounding fluid
  • It experiences body forces such as gravity

For the fluid to remain at rest, these forces must balance exactly.

This leads to the central idea of hydrostatics:

\[ \text{Net force on every fluid element} = 0 \]

This condition of equilibrium determines how pressure varies inside the fluid.

Pressure variation in a fluid is not arbitrary — it is dictated by force balance.

Why Study Hydrostatics Separately?

Hydrostatics is studied separately because the absence of motion removes many complexities.

In fluid dynamics, we must consider:

  • Velocity fields
  • Acceleration of fluid elements
  • Energy transfer
  • Viscous effects

In hydrostatics, all of this disappears. The problem reduces to:

  • Understanding pressure distribution
  • Balancing forces under gravity

This simplification allows us to derive powerful results such as:

  • Pressure variation with depth
  • Pascal’s law
  • Buoyant force (Archimedes’ principle)
Hydrostatics is the foundation — once pressure distribution is understood, many real-world phenomena follow naturally.

Role of Motion and Kinetic Energy

Hydrostatics does not require the fluid to be absolutely at rest in every frame. It only requires that there is no relative motion between different parts of the fluid.

From mechanics, the kinetic energy of a system can be written as:

\[ K_{\text{total}} = K_{\text{COM}} + K_{\text{internal}} \]

Here:

  • Bulk kinetic energy (\(K_{\text{COM}}\)) → motion of the fluid as a whole
  • Internal kinetic energy (\(K_{\text{internal}}\)) → relative motion between different parts of the fluid

In hydrostatics:

  • No relative motion between fluid elements → \(K_{\text{internal}} = 0\)
  • The fluid may still have bulk motion → \(K_{\text{COM}}\) can be non-zero

For example, a liquid inside an accelerating container is in hydrostatic equilibrium in the container frame, even though it has kinetic energy in the ground frame.

Hydrostatics is defined by absence of internal motion, not by absence of kinetic energy.

In this chapter, we focus only on the internal equilibrium of the fluid. Any overall motion of the system is treated separately using rigid-body mechanics.
