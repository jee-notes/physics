Reynolds Number

How do we know mathematically whether a flowing fluid will remain in a smooth streamline state or burst into chaotic turbulence? The answer is a dimensionless ratio.

Reynolds Number (\( R_e \))

\[ R_e = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta} \]
Where \( \rho \) is density, \( v \) is average velocity, \( d \) is the pipe diameter, and \( \eta \) is viscosity.

Physically, Reynolds Number represents the ratio of inertial forces (mass and speed) to viscous forces (internal friction).

  • \( R_e < 1000 \): Viscous forces violently dominate. The flow is strictly streamline (laminar).
  • \( 1000 < R_e < 2000 \): Transition state; the flow is highly unstable.
  • \( R_e > 2000 \): Inertial forces dominate. The flow becomes fully turbulent.
