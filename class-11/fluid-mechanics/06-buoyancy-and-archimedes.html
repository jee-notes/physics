Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle

Understanding exactly why massive ships float but dense coins sink.

Buoyant Force

When a body is partially or fully immersed in a fluid, the surrounding fluid exerts pressure forces on its surface.

At every point on the surface, the force due to the fluid is perpendicular to the surface and equal to the local pressure multiplied by the area.

Since pressure increases with depth, the force acting on the bottom surface of the object is greater than the force acting on the top surface.

The horizontal components of pressure forces cancel out due to symmetry. However, the vertical components do not cancel.

This imbalance of pressure forces results in a net upward force on the object.

The resultant of all these pressure forces is called the buoyant force (\( F_B \)).

Buoyancy is not a separate force — it is the resultant of pressure forces acting over the surface of the object.
  • A body immersed in water appears lighter
  • Objects less dense than the fluid float
  • A helium-filled balloon rises in air

Archimedes' Principle

The magnitude of the buoyant force can be obtained by summing (integrating) the pressure forces over the surface of the object.

Archimedes' Principle
When a body is partially or completely immersed in a fluid at rest, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

\[ \vec{F}_B = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V_{\text{submerged}} g \]
  • \( \rho_{\text{fluid}} \): density of the fluid
  • \( V_{\text{submerged}} \): volume of fluid displaced
  • \( g \): acceleration due to gravity
The buoyant force depends only on the fluid and the volume of fluid displaced. It is independent of the material, shape, or mass of the object.
Archimedes’ principle is a direct consequence of pressure variation with depth. The net pressure force over the surface equals the weight of the displaced fluid.

Laws of Floatation

The motion of an object in a fluid is determined by the balance between its weight and the buoyant force.

  • Sinking (\( W > F_B \)): Object is denser than fluid
  • Neutral Suspension (\( W = F_B \)): Densities are equal
  • Floating (\( W = F_B \), partial immersion): Object is less dense than fluid

For a floating object:

\[ \rho_{\text{obj}} V_{\text{total}} g = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V_{\text{submerged}} g \] \[ \frac{V_{\text{submerged}}}{V_{\text{total}}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{obj}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}} \]

Apparent Weight

When an object is immersed in a fluid, the buoyant force reduces the net force experienced by the object.

\[ W_{\text{app}} = W_{\text{true}} - F_B \] \[ W_{\text{app}} = (\rho_{\text{obj}} - \rho_{\text{fluid}})\, V g \]
Apparent weight is the result of partial cancellation of gravity by buoyancy.

For a floating object, the buoyant force exactly balances the weight, and the apparent weight becomes zero.
