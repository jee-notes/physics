Fluid Pressure

Understanding how fluids exert force macroscopic surfaces and how pressure varies with altitude and depth.

Pressure in a Fluid

When a fluid is at rest in a container, it exerts a force that is everywhere strictly perpendicular to the walls of the container. If there were a component parallel to the surface, the fluid would start flowing. The normal force exerted by a fluid per unit area is called pressure.

Pressure \( (P) \)
Pressure is defined as the magnitude of the normal force \( F_{\perp} \) exerted per unit area \( A \).

\[ P = \frac{F_{\perp}}{A} \]
Where:
  • \( F_{\perp} \) is the normal force exerted by the fluid.
  • \( A \) is the area over which the force acts.
SI Unit: \text{Pascal} (Pa) or \text{N m}^{-2}
Dimensional Formula: \( [\text{M}^1 \text{L}^{-1} \text{T}^{-2}] \)
Despite being defined by the ratio of force (a vector) to area (also treated as a vector in some contexts), pressure itself is a highly isotropic scalar quantity. At any specific point within a static fluid, the pressure is identical in all directions.

Variation of Pressure with Depth

Pressure within a fluid under gravity changes depending on depth. Consider a cylindrical element of fluid of cross-sectional area \( A \) and height \( h \), situated within a larger volume of fluid of density \( \rho \). Let the top surface be at pressure \( P_1 \) and the bottom be at pressure \( P_2 \).

Since the fluid element is in equilibrium, the net force in the vertical direction must be zero:

\[ P_2 A - P_1 A - m g = 0 \]

The mass of the element is \( m = \rho (\text{volume}) = \rho A h \). Substituting this:

\[ (P_2 - P_1) A = \rho A h g \implies P_2 - P_1 = \rho g h \]
Hydrostatic Pressure Equation
The absolute pressure \( P \) at a depth \( h \) below the surface of a fluid is:

\[ P = P_0 + \rho g h \]
Where:
  • \( P_0 \) is the pressure at the reference surface (often atmospheric pressure \( P_a \)).
  • \( \rho \) is the fluid density.
  • \( g \) is the acceleration due to gravity.
  • \( h \) is the depth below the surface.

This explains why hydrostatic pressure depends only on depth and completely ignores the container's shape—a phenomenon famously known as the hydrostatic paradox.

Pascal's Principle and Hydraulic Lift

Because fluids apply pressure uniformly in all directions at a given depth, applying an external pressure forces the entire fluid to adjust its internal pressure profile simultaneously.

Pascal's Principle
A change in pressure applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished to every point of the fluid and to the walls of the container.

One of the most consequential applications of Pascal's Principle is the Hydraulic Lift. Two pistons of varying cross-sectional areas \( A_1 \) and \( A_2 \) are connected by a fluid-filled tube. A small force \( F_1 \) is applied to the small area \( A_1 \).

\[ P_1 = \frac{F_1}{A_1} \]

According to Pascal's Principle, this pressure change is transmitted to piston 2 exactly:

\[ P_2 = P_1 \implies \frac{F_2}{A_2} = \frac{F_1}{A_1} \implies F_2 = F_1 \frac{A_2}{A_1} \]
Because the area \( A_2 \) is much larger than \( A_1 \), the output force \( F_2 \) is magnificently amplified. The hydraulic lift allows us to effortlessly elevate a massive car by applying only a tiny force downward, mimicking the principle of a mechanical lever for continuous media.

Atmospheric and Gauge Pressure

Living on Earth, we are constantly buried at the bottom of a fluid ocean—our atmosphere. The column of air above us exerts a significant Atmospheric Pressure (\( P_a \)). At sea level, its standard value is:

\[ 1 \, \text{atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5 \, \text{Pa} \]
Gauge Pressure (\( P_g \))
Gauge pressure is the difference between the absolute pressure \( P \) and the atmospheric pressure \( P_a \). It effectively zeroes out the background atmospheric pressure because atmospheric pressure pushes on all sides equivalently.

\[ P_g = P - P_a = \rho g h \]

Most mechanical pressure gauges (like tire pressure gauges) read gauge pressure. A tire pressure of "30 psi" means the pressure inside is 30 psi higher than the atmospheric ambient pressure outside.
