Alloy Purity

Archimedes' principle literal use (The Golden Crown Problem).

Concept: Determining Composition

When two metals are melted together to form an alloy, we can determine the volume or mass of each constituent (\(V_1, V_2\) or \(M_1, M_2\)) by weighing the alloy in air and then in water.

Key Logic: The "loss in weight" when submerged is exactly equal to the weight of the water displaced by the total volume of the alloy.

Case 1: Combined Mass \(M\) is known

If we know the mass of the alloy in air is \(M\), then:

\[ M = M_1 + M_2 = V_1 \rho_1 + V_2 \rho_2 \]

When weighed in water, the apparent mass \(M'\) is recorded:

\[ M' = M - (V_1 + V_2)\rho_w \] \[ M - M' = (V_1 + V_2)\rho_w \]

By substituting \(V_1 = \frac{M_1}{\rho_1}\) and \(V_2 = \frac{M_2}{\rho_2}\), we can solve for \(M_1\) and \(M_2\).

Case 2: Combined Volume \(V\) is known

If the total volume \(V\) is given:

\[ V = V_1 + V_2 \]

The apparent weight equation gives us the combined density \(\rho_c\):

\[ M'g = V\rho_c g - V\rho_w g \] \[ \rho_c = \frac{M'}{V} + \rho_w \]

Once \(\rho_c\) is found, we use the mixture formula:

\[ \rho_c = \frac{V_1 \rho_1 + V_2 \rho_2}{V_1 + V_2} \]

Apparent Weight vs. Loss of Weight

In buoyancy problems, we distinguish between three distinct "weights." Understanding the difference is crucial for solving alloy and purity problems.

1. Actual Weight (\(W_{air}\))

The true gravitational pull on the object in a vacuum (or effectively, in air).

\[ W_{air} = M \cdot g = V \cdot \rho_{body} \cdot g \]

2. Apparent Weight (\(W_{app}\))

The reading on a spring balance when the object is submerged in a fluid. It is the net force acting downward.

\[ W_{app} = W_{air} - F_B \]

Where \(F_B\) is the Buoyant Force. Substituting the values:

\[ W_{app} = V \rho_{body} g - V \rho_{L} g = Vg(\rho_{body} - \rho_{L}) \]

3. Loss of Weight (\(\Delta W\))

The difference between the weight in air and the weight in liquid. According to Archimedes' Principle, this is exactly equal to the weight of the displaced liquid.

\[ \text{Loss of Weight} = W_{air} - W_{app} = F_B \] \[ \Delta W = V \cdot \rho_{L} \cdot g \]

Note for Calculations:

The Relative Density (R.D.) of a solid can be expressed as:

\[ \text{R.D.} = \frac{W_{air}}{W_{air} - W_{water}} = \frac{\text{Weight in Air}}{\text{Loss of Weight in Water}} \]
