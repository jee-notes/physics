Temperature and Heat

Understanding the macroscopic fundamental quantities that govern thermal physics.

Concept of Temperature

Temperature is a macroscopic physical property of matter that quantitatively expresses the common notions of hot and cold. Microscopically, temperature is directly proportional to the average kinetic energy of the random, chaotic translational motion of the particles (atoms or molecules) constituting the system.

Definition of Temperature: Temperature is a scalar physical quantity that determines the direction of heat flow when two bodies are placed in thermal contact. Heat always flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature.
  • SI Unit: Kelvin (\(K\))
  • Dimensional Formula: \( [\Theta] \) or \( [K] \)

It is crucial to understand that temperature is an intensive property; it does not depend on the amount of substance present. A cup of boiling water and a large pot of boiling water both have the same temperature (\(100^\circ\text{C}\)), even though the pot contains significantly more total thermal energy.

Concept of Heat

While temperature is a state variable (describing the state of a system), heat is a process variable. It describes the transfer of energy between systems.

Definition of Heat: Heat (\(Q\)) is the form of energy that is transferred continuously across a system boundary due strictly to a temperature difference between the system and its surroundings.
  • SI Unit: Joule (\(J\))
  • Common Unit: Calorie (\(\text{cal}\)), where \( 1\text{ cal} = 4.184\text{ J} \)
  • Dimensional Formula: \( [ML^2T^{-2}] \)
A system does not "contain" heat. It contains internal energy. Heat is only defined during the process of energy transfer. Once the energy has entered the system, it ceases to be "heat" and becomes part of the system's internal energy.

Thermal Equilibrium and Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics

When two macroscopic bodies at different temperatures are placed in thermal contact, heat flows from the hotter body to the colder body. As this happens, their temperatures approach each other. Eventually, the net heat flow ceases, and the bodies are said to be in thermal equilibrium.

This empirical observation is the foundation of the Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics.

The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics: If body \(A\) is in thermal equilibrium with body \(B\), and body \(B\) is separately in thermal equilibrium with body \(C\), then body \(A\) and body \(C\) must be in thermal equilibrium with each other.

While this law may seem trivial, it is of profound importance because it effectively defines the concept of temperature mathematically. It implies that there exists a universal scalar property—temperature—that determines whether two systems will be in thermal equilibrium.

By letting body \(B\) be a thermometer, the Zeroth Law ensures that if body \(A\) and body \(C\) both give the same reading on the thermometer, they will not exchange heat if placed in contact.
