Internal Energy & Specific Heats from KTG

Theoretical derivations of molar heat capacities, adiabatic exponents, and thermodynamic properties of gas mixtures.

Total Internal Energy of an Ideal Gas

In the kinetic theory of gases, molecules of an ideal gas exert no intermolecular attractive forces. Therefore, the intermolecular potential energy is identically zero (\( U_{\text{pot}} = 0 \)), and the total macroscopic internal energy \( U \) consists entirely of the kinetic energy of its constituent molecules.

For \( n \) moles of an ideal gas possessing \( f \) active degrees of freedom at absolute temperature \( T \):

\[ U = n \cdot N_A \left( \frac{f}{2} k_B T \right) = n \left( \frac{f}{2} R T \right) \] where:
  • \( U \): Internal energy of the gas (SI Unit: Joule (\( \text{J} \)), Dimension: \( [M L^2 T^{-2}] \))
  • \( n \): Number of moles of gas
  • \( f \): Active degrees of freedom
  • \( R \approx 8.314 \, \text{J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K}) \): Universal gas constant
  • \( T \): Absolute temperature in Kelvin
Internal energy depends solely on temperature \( T \) and degrees of freedom \( f \). For any process involving temperature change \( \Delta T \): \[ \Delta U = n \left( \frac{f}{2} R \right) \Delta T = \frac{\Delta(PV)}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{P_f V_f - P_i V_i}{\gamma - 1} \]

Theoretical Derivation of Cv from Equipartition Theorem

The Molar Heat Capacity at Constant Volume (\( C_v \)) is the rate of change of molar internal energy with respect to temperature:

\[ C_v = \frac{1}{n} \left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_V = \frac{1}{n} \frac{d}{dT}\left[ n \left(\frac{f}{2} R T\right) \right] \]
\[ C_v = \frac{f}{2} R \] where \( C_v \) is expressed in \( \text{J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K}) \).

Theoretical Derivation of Cp and Mayer's Relation

Under constant pressure heating, the heat supplied increases the internal energy and performs boundary work (\( dW = P\,dV = nR\,dT \)):

\[ dQ = dU + dW = n C_v dT + n R dT = n (C_v + R) dT \]

By definition of molar heat capacity at constant pressure (\( dQ = n C_p dT \)):

\[ C_p = C_v + R = \frac{f}{2} R + R = \left( \frac{f}{2} + 1 \right) R = \left( \frac{f + 2}{2} \right) R \] \[ C_p - C_v = R \quad \text{(Mayer's Relation)} \]

Specific Heat Ratio for Various Gas Atomicity

The Adiabatic Index (Poisson's Ratio, \( \gamma \)) is the ratio of molar heat capacities:

\[ \gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v} = \frac{\left(\frac{f}{2}+1\right)R}{\frac{f}{2}R} = 1 + \frac{2}{f} \]

Molar Heat Capacity and Internal Energy of Gas Mixtures

Consider a non-reacting mixture of \( k \) gases containing \( n_1, n_2, \dots, n_k \) moles with degrees of freedom \( f_1, f_2, \dots, f_k \) at temperature \( T \).

1. Total Internal Energy of Mixture:

\[ U_{\text{mix}} = U_1 + U_2 + \dots = \left( n_1 \frac{f_1}{2} + n_2 \frac{f_2}{2} + \dots \right) R T \]

2. Equivalent \( C_{v,\text{mix}} \) and \( C_{p,\text{mix}} \):

\[ C_{v,\text{mix}} = \frac{n_1 C_{v1} + n_2 C_{v2} + \dots + n_k C_{vk}}{n_1 + n_2 + \dots + n_k} = \frac{\sum n_i C_{vi}}{\sum n_i} \] \[ C_{p,\text{mix}} = C_{v,\text{mix}} + R = \frac{\sum n_i C_{pi}}{\sum n_i} \]

3. Equivalent Mixture Ratio \( \gamma_{\text{mix}} \):

\[ \gamma_{\text{mix}} = \frac{C_{p,\text{mix}}}{C_{v,\text{mix}}} = \frac{\sum n_i C_{pi}}{\sum n_i C_{vi}} = \frac{\sum n_i \left(\frac{\gamma_i}{\gamma_i - 1}\right)}{\sum n_i \left(\frac{1}{\gamma_i - 1}\right)} \]

Solved Example: Mixture Thermodynamics

One mole of Helium (\( \text{He} \), monoatomic, \( f_1 = 3 \)) is mixed with two moles of Nitrogen (\( \text{N}_2 \), rigid diatomic, \( f_2 = 5 \)).
Calculate: (a) Total internal energy at \( 300 \, \text{K} \), (b) \( C_{v,\text{mix}} \), (c) \( \gamma_{\text{mix}} \).

Solution:

(a) Total Internal Energy:

\[ U = n_1 C_{v1} T + n_2 C_{v2} T = (1)\left(\frac{3}{2}R\right)(300) + (2)\left(\frac{5}{2}R\right)(300) = 450R + 1500R = 1950R = 1950 \times 8.314 \approx \mathbf{16.21 \, \text{kJ}} \]

(b) \( C_{v,\text{mix}} \):

\[ C_{v,\text{mix}} = \frac{1(1.5R) + 2(2.5R)}{1 + 2} = \frac{1.5R + 5R}{3} = \frac{6.5R}{3} = \frac{13}{6}R \approx \mathbf{2.167 \, R} \]

(c) \( \gamma_{\text{mix}} \):

\[ C_{p,\text{mix}} = C_{v,\text{mix}} + R = \frac{13}{6}R + R = \frac{19}{6}R \] \[ \gamma_{\text{mix}} = \frac{C_{p,\text{mix}}}{C_{v,\text{mix}}} = \frac{19/6}{13/6} = \frac{19}{13} \approx \mathbf{1.462} \]
