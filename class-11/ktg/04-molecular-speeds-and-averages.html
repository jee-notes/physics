Molecular Speeds & Averages

Mathematical formulations of RMS, average, and most probable speeds, their ratios, and sonic analogies.

Concept of Molecular Velocity Distribution

Due to billions of random intermolecular collisions occurring every second, the velocity and kinetic energy of any individual gas molecule fluctuate incessantly. Some molecules are temporarily slowed to near-rest after head-on collisions, while others are accelerated to extremely high speeds after glancing collisions.

Consequently, a gas in thermal equilibrium does not possess a single uniform speed. Instead, molecular speeds are distributed across a continuous statistical spectrum characterized by three fundamental statistical averages:

  1. Root Mean Square Speed (\( v_{\text{rms}} \))
  2. Average / Mean Speed (\( v_{\text{avg}} \) or \( \bar{v} \))
  3. Most Probable Speed (\( v_{\text{mp}} \) or \( v_p \))

Root Mean Square Speed and Formulae

The Root Mean Square Speed (\( v_{\text{rms}} \)) is defined as the square root of the arithmetic mean of the squares of the speeds of all molecules:

\[ v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\overline{v^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + \dots + v_N^2}{N}} \]

Because translational kinetic energy is directly proportional to \( \overline{v^2} \) (\( \overline{E_k} = \frac{1}{2}m\overline{v^2} = \frac{3}{2}k_BT \)), \( v_{\text{rms}} \) directly gauges the thermal energy content of the gas.

Master Formulas for \( v_{\text{rms}} \): \[ v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 k_B T}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 R T}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 P}{\rho}} \] where:
  • \( v_{\text{rms}} \): Root mean square speed (SI Unit: \( \text{m/s} \), Dimension: \( [L T^{-1}] \))
  • \( k_B \approx 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \, \text{J/K} \): Boltzmann constant
  • \( R \approx 8.314 \, \text{J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K}) \): Universal gas constant
  • \( T \): Absolute temperature in Kelvin
  • \( m \): Molecular mass of single molecule (\( \text{kg} \))
  • \( M = m N_A \): Molar mass (in \( \text{kg/mol} \); e.g., \( 0.032 \, \text{kg/mol} \) for \( \text{O}_2 \))
  • \( P \): Absolute pressure (\( \text{Pa} \))
  • \( \rho \): Mass density (\( \text{kg/m}^3 \))
Crucial Calculation Warning: When evaluating \( \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} \), molar mass \( M \) MUST be inserted in \( \text{kg/mol} \) (SI Units). For Oxygen (\( \text{O}_2 \)), use \( M = 32 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{kg/mol} \), NOT \( 32 \, \text{g/mol} \)!

Average or Mean Speed

The Average Speed (\( v_{\text{avg}} \) or \( \bar{v} \)) is the simple arithmetic mean of the speeds of all the molecules in the gas:

\[ v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{v_1 + v_2 + \dots + v_N}{N} = \int_0^\infty v \, f(v) \, dv \]

Evaluating the integral using the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution function yields:

\[ v_{\text{avg}} = \sqrt{\frac{8 k_B T}{\pi m}} = \sqrt{\frac{8 R T}{\pi M}} = \sqrt{\frac{8 P}{\pi \rho}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2.546 R T}{M}} \]

Average speed directly dictates collision rates and transport phenomena such as diffusion and effusion.

Most Probable Speed

The Most Probable Speed (\( v_{\text{mp}} \) or \( v_p \)) is the speed possessed by the maximum fraction of molecules in the gas sample at temperature \( T \).

It corresponds mathematically to the peak (maximum) of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve (\( \frac{df(v)}{dv} = 0 \)):

\[ v_{\text{mp}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 k_B T}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 R T}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 P}{\rho}} \]

Comparison, Ratios, and Ordering of Molecular Speeds

Comparing the numerical pre-factors under the radical for the three speeds:

\[ v_{\text{mp}} = \sqrt{2 \left(\frac{RT}{M}\right)} \approx 1.414 \sqrt{\frac{RT}{M}} \] \[ v_{\text{avg}} = \sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi} \left(\frac{RT}{M}\right)} \approx \sqrt{2.546 \left(\frac{RT}{M}\right)} \approx 1.596 \sqrt{\frac{RT}{M}} \] \[ v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{3 \left(\frac{RT}{M}\right)} \approx 1.732 \sqrt{\frac{RT}{M}} \]
The Fundamental Inequality & Ratio: \[ \mathbf{v_{\text{rms}} > v_{\text{avg}} > v_{\text{mp}}} \] \[ v_{\text{mp}} : v_{\text{avg}} : v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{2} : \sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}} : \sqrt{3} \approx 1 : 1.128 : 1.225 \] \[ v_{\text{rms}} : v_{\text{avg}} : v_{\text{mp}} = \sqrt{3} : \sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}} : \sqrt{2} \approx 1.732 : 1.596 : 1.414 \]
Memory Mnemonic ("RAM Rule"):
In descending magnitude: \( \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{M} \) (\( v_{\text{rms}} > v_{\text{avg}} > v_{\text{mp}} \)).

Relationship Between RMS Speed and Speed of Sound in Gases

According to Laplace's formula, the speed of longitudinal sound waves in an ideal gas of adiabatic index \( \gamma = C_p / C_v \) is:

\[ v_{\text{sound}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} \]

Comparing \( v_{\text{sound}} \) with the RMS molecular speed \( v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} \):

\[ \frac{v_{\text{rms}}}{v_{\text{sound}}} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{\gamma}} \implies v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{\gamma}} \, v_{\text{sound}} \]
Since \( \gamma < 3 \) for all gases (maximum \( \gamma = 5/3 = 1.67 \) for monoatomic gases), \( \sqrt{\frac{3}{\gamma}} > 1 \).
Therefore, \( v_{\text{rms}} > v_{\text{sound}} \) always. Sound is carried by mechanical molecular collisions; information cannot travel through the gas faster than the microscopic particles carrying it!

Solved Example: Speed Calculation for Oxygen

Calculate \( v_{\text{mp}} \), \( v_{\text{avg}} \), and \( v_{\text{rms}} \) for Oxygen gas (\( M = 32 \, \text{g/mol} = 0.032 \, \text{kg/mol} \)) at \( 27^\circ\text{C} \) (\( 300 \, \text{K} \)).

Solution:

\[ \frac{RT}{M} = \frac{8.314 \times 300}{0.032} = \frac{2494.2}{0.032} = 77943.75 \, \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2 \]

1. Most Probable Speed:

\[ v_{\text{mp}} = \sqrt{2 \times 77943.75} = \sqrt{155887.5} \approx \mathbf{394.8 \, \text{m/s}} \]

2. Average Speed:

\[ v_{\text{avg}} = \sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi} \times 77943.75} = \sqrt{2.5465 \times 77943.75} = \sqrt{198483} \approx \mathbf{445.5 \, \text{m/s}} \]

3. RMS Speed:

\[ v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{3 \times 77943.75} = \sqrt{233831.25} \approx \mathbf{483.6 \, \text{m/s}} \]
