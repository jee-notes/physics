Kinetic Derivation of Gas Pressure

Derivation of pressure equation from Newtonian momentum conservation.

Momentum Transfer During Wall Collisions

Consider an ideal gas containing \( N \) identical molecules, each of mass \( m \), enclosed within a cubical container of side length \( L \) and volume \( V = L^3 \). Let the edges of the cube lie along the Cartesian axes \( x, y, z \).

Let the velocity of the \( i \)-th molecule be \( \vec{v}_i = (v_{xi}, v_{yi}, v_{zi}) \). Its speed squared is given by:

\[ v_i^2 = v_{xi}^2 + v_{yi}^2 + v_{zi}^2 \]

When this molecule collides elastically with the right-hand wall \( A_1 \) (perpendicular to the \( x \)-axis at \( x = L \)):

  • Initial \( x \)-momentum: \( p_{xi} = +m v_{xi} \)
  • Final \( x \)-momentum after rebound: \( p_{xi}' = -m v_{xi} \)
  • Impulse imparted to the molecule during collision: \[ J_{xi} = \Delta p_{xi} = p_{xi}' - p_{xi} = (-m v_{xi}) - (m v_{xi}) = -2 m v_{xi} \]
  • Impulse imparted to the wall per collision: \[ J_{\text{wall}} = -J_{xi} = 2 m v_{xi} \]

Force on Wall

After rebounding from wall \( A_1 \), the molecule travels across the length of the container to the opposite wall at \( x = 0 \), rebounds, and returns to strike wall \( A_1 \) again.

The distance covered between two successive collisions with wall \( A_1 \) is \( 2L \). Therefore, the time interval between successive collisions on wall \( A_1 \) is:

\[ \Delta t_i = \frac{2L}{v_{xi}} \]

According to Newton's Second Law, the time-averaged force \( F_{xi} \) exerted by the \( i \)-th molecule on wall \( A_1 \) is:

\[ F_{xi} = \frac{J_{\text{wall}}}{\Delta t_i} = \frac{2 m v_{xi}}{\frac{2L}{v_{xi}}} = \frac{m v_{xi}^2}{L} \]

Summing over all \( N \) molecules in the container, the total normal force \( F_x \) acting on wall \( A_1 \) is: \[ F_x = \sum_{i=1}^N F_{xi} = \frac{m}{L} \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2 \]

Root Mean Square Speed

We are trying to relate the pressure of gas with microscopic parameters using collision.

We got the value of force on the wall due to single molecule \(F_{xi} = \frac{m v_{xi}^2}{L}\). It depends on molecular mass, directional speed and container dimensions.

However, we are considering thermodynamic equilibrium in which the gas is present in container and pressure and temperature are well-defined and uniform.

The situation is like a steady state dynamic equilibrium and we can say that the pressure is due to all the \( N \) molecules in the container colliding with the wall as the momentum is distributed. The summed value obtained is:

\[ F_x = \sum_{i=1}^N F_{xi} = \frac{m}{L} \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2 \]

The pressure \( P \) exerted on wall \( A_1 \) of area \( A = L^2 \) is the total normal force per unit area:

\[ P = \frac{F_x}{A} = \frac{F_x}{L^2} = \frac{m}{L^3} \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2 = \frac{m}{V} \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2 \]

We need a way to mathematically resolve \( \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2 \) and this is done using statiscal math concept of root mean square.

Root Mean Square Speed

The speed of ith molecule is \(v_{xi}\) and there are \(N\) such molecules.

We define a quantity called root mean square speed:

\[ \overline{v_x} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2} \implies N \overline{v_x^2} = \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2 \]

We use rms speed in pressure equation:

\[ P = \frac{m}{V} \sum_{i=1}^N v_{xi}^2 = \frac{mN}{V} \overline{v_x^2} = \frac{m_{\text{gas}}}{V} \overline{v_x^2} = \rho \overline{v_x^2} \]

Applying Isotropic Symmetry

Because molecular motion is completely random and chaotic with no preferred direction of velocity, the mean square velocity components along all three Cartesian axes must be identically equal:

\[ \overline{v_x^2} = \overline{v_y^2} = \overline{v_z^2} \]

The total mean square speed \( \overline{v^2} \) is:

\[ \overline{v^2} = \overline{v_x^2} + \overline{v_y^2} + \overline{v_z^2} = 3 \overline{v_x^2} \implies \overline{v_x^2} = \frac{1}{3} \overline{v^2} \]

Substituting \( \overline{v_x^2} = \frac{1}{3} \overline{v^2} \) into the pressure formula yields the Fundamental Kinetic Pressure Equation:

\[ P = \frac{1}{3} \frac{m N}{V} \overline{v^2} = \frac{1}{3} \rho \, \overline{v^2} = \frac{1}{3} \rho \, v_{\text{rms}}^2 \]
  • \( P \): Absolute pressure of the gas (SI Unit: Pascal (\( \text{Pa} = \text{N/m}^2 \)), Dimension: \( [M L^{-1} T^{-2}] \))
  • \( m \): Mass of an individual molecule (SI Unit: \( \text{kg} \))
  • \( N \): Total number of molecules in the vessel
  • \( V \): Total volume of the container (SI Unit: \( \text{m}^3 \))
  • \( \rho = \frac{m N}{V} \): Mass density of the gas (SI Unit: \( \text{kg/m}^3 \))
  • \( \overline{v^2} = v_{\text{rms}}^2 \): Mean square speed of the molecules (SI Unit: \( \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2 \))

Translational Kinetic Energy

The total translational kinetic energy \( E_{\text{trans}} \) of all \( N \) molecules in the container is:

\[ E_{\text{trans}} = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{1}{2} m v_i^2 = \frac{1}{2} m N \overline{v^2} \]

The translational kinetic energy per unit volume (Energy Density, \( u \)) is:

\[ u = \frac{E_{\text{trans}}}{V} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{m N}{V}\right) \overline{v^2} = \frac{1}{2} \rho \, \overline{v^2} \]

Comparing with the pressure equation \( P = \frac{1}{3} \rho \overline{v^2} \):

\[ P = \frac{2}{3} \left( \frac{1}{2} \rho \overline{v^2} \right) = \frac{2}{3} u = \frac{2}{3} \left( \frac{E_{\text{trans}}}{V} \right) \] \[ E_{\text{trans}} = \frac{3}{2} P V = \frac{3}{2} nRT \]
The pressure of an ideal gas is strictly equal to two-thirds of its translational kinetic energy density, regardless of whether the gas is monoatomic, diatomic, or polyatomic!
Translational KE is a function of temperature, regardless of whether the gas is monoatomic, diatomic, or polyatomic!

Elastic Collisions and Momentum Exchange at Walls

Consider a single gas molecule of mass \( m \) moving with velocity vector \( \vec{v} = (v_x, v_y, v_z) \) approaching a rigid wall perpendicular to the \( x \)-axis.

When the molecule strikes the smooth, stationary wall elastically:

  • The parallel velocity components remain unchanged: \( v_y' = v_y \), \( v_z' = v_z \).
  • The normal velocity component is reversed in direction without change in magnitude: \( v_x' = -v_x \).

Change in Momentum of the Molecule: \[ \Delta p_{x, \text{molecule}} = p_{\text{final}} - p_{\text{initial}} = (-m v_x) - (+m v_x) = -2 m v_x \] Momentum Transferred to the Wall: By Newton's Third Law, the momentum delivered to the container wall per collision is: \[ \Delta p_{x, \text{wall}} = +2 m v_x \]

The continuous, steady macroscopic pressure registered by a pressure sensor is the time-averaged cumulative force produced by trillions of these individual momentum-transfer impacts per second across each square meter of the wall.

Pressure Invariance in Non-Cubical and Arbitrary Geometry Vessels

Although derived using a cubical box for Cartesian simplicity, the result \( P = \frac{1}{3}\rho\overline{v^2} \) is universally valid for any container of arbitrary geometry (spherical, cylindrical, or irregular):

  • For any surface element \( d\vec{A} \) on an arbitrary boundary wall, the momentum exchanged depends solely on the normal velocity component \( v_n = \vec{v} \cdot \hat{n} \).
  • Because the velocity distribution is perfectly isotropic in space, the directional average of \( (\vec{v}\cdot\hat{n})^2 \) over a sphere of unit normals is always \( \frac{1}{3}\overline{v^2} \).
  • Thus, pressure is completely uniform and isotropic across all walls of any container in thermal equilibrium.

Solved Example: Gas Pressure from RMS Speed

Nitrogen gas (\( M = 28 \, \text{g/mol} = 0.028 \, \text{kg/mol} \)) is enclosed in a vessel at a density of \( \rho = 1.25 \, \text{kg/m}^3 \). If the RMS speed of the molecules is \( 500 \, \text{m/s} \), calculate:
(a) The gas pressure \( P \).
(b) The translational kinetic energy per unit volume.

Solution:

(a) Using the kinetic pressure equation:

\[ P = \frac{1}{3} \rho \, v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{1}{3} (1.25 \, \text{kg/m}^3) (500 \, \text{m/s})^2 = \frac{1}{3} (1.25) (250000) \approx 1.042 \times 10^5 \, \text{Pa} \approx 1.03 \, \text{atm} \]

(b) Translational kinetic energy density:

\[ u = \frac{1}{2} \rho \, v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{1}{2} (1.25) (250000) = 1.5625 \times 10^5 \, \text{J/m}^3 \]

Check: \( P = \frac{2}{3} u = \frac{2}{3}(156250) \approx 1.042 \times 10^5 \, \text{Pa} \).
