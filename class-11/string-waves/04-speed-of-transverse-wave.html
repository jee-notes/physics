Speed of Transverse Waves on a String

Dynamic derivation of wave speed from Newton's laws, medium parameters, and environmental dependencies.

Derivation of Wave Speed on a Stretched String: v = sqrt(T/mu)

The speed at which a transverse mechanical wave propagates along a flexible stretched string is determined entirely by the balance between the restoring tension (elastic property) and the linear mass density (inertial property).

Dynamic Derivation (Circular Arc Reference Frame)

Consider a symmetric transverse pulse traveling to the right at speed \( v \) along a string held under tension \( T \). Let us transform into a reference frame moving along with the pulse at speed \( v \). In this moving frame, the pulse shape is stationary, and the string appears to slide through the pulse to the left at speed \( v \).

Consider a small differential element of the string of length \( \Delta l = R \Delta\theta \) at the peak of the pulse, where \( R \) is the local radius of curvature and \( \Delta\theta \) is the subtended angle:

  • Mass of the small element: \( \Delta m = \mu \Delta l = \mu R \Delta\theta \), where \( \mu \) is the linear mass density.
  • The tension \( T \) acts tangentially at both ends of the arc. The horizontal components of tension cancel by symmetry, while the downward radial components add up to provide the centripetal force: \[ F_{\text{net}} = 2 T \sin\left(\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}\right) \]

For an infinitesimal angle, using the small-angle approximation \( \sin(\Delta\theta / 2) \approx \Delta\theta / 2 \):

\[ F_{\text{net}} \approx 2 T \left( \frac{\Delta\theta}{2} \right) = T \Delta\theta \]

Equating this net radial restoring force to the required centripetal force \( \Delta m \frac{v^2}{R} \):

\[ T \Delta\theta = (\mu R \Delta\theta) \frac{v^2}{R} \]

Canceling \( R \Delta\theta \) on both sides yields the classical wave speed equation:

Transverse Wave Speed on a Taut String: \[ v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} \]

Dependence on Elastic and Inertial Properties

The wave speed formula embodies the universal general principle of mechanical wave propagation:

\[ v = \sqrt{\frac{\text{Elastic Property (Restoring Factor)}}{\text{Inertial Property (Mass Factor)}}} \]
Physical Parameter Symbol & Unit Dimensional Formula Impact on Wave Speed \( v \)
Tension in String (\( T \)) \( \text{N} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2 \) \( [M^1 L^1 T^{-2}] \) \( v \propto \sqrt{T} \) (Increasing tension increases speed).
Linear Mass Density (\( \mu = m/L \)) \( \text{kg/m} \) \( [M^1 L^{-1} T^0] \) \( v \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}} \) (Heavier strings transmit waves slower).
Wave speed does not depend on the frequency \( f \), amplitude \( A \), or wave profile \( y(x,t) \) of the source. It is exclusively determined by the intrinsic physical properties of the medium.

Effect of Temperature, Strain, and Material Density

In realistic physical configurations and JEE Advanced problems, linear mass density and tension can be expressed in terms of microscopic material constants:

1. In Terms of Volumetric Density and Cross-Sectional Area

If a wire has volumetric density \( \rho \), radius \( r \), and cross-sectional area \( S = \pi r^2 \):

\[ \mu = \frac{m}{L} = \frac{\rho \cdot (S \cdot L)}{L} = \rho S \] \[ v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\rho S}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{\rho}} \]

where \( \sigma = \frac{T}{S} \) is the tensile stress in the wire.

2. In Terms of Young's Modulus and Elastic Strain

From Hooke's Law of Elasticity, stress is related to fractional elongation (strain) by \( \sigma = Y \frac{\Delta L}{L} \), where \( Y \) is Young's Modulus:

\[ v = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho} \left(\frac{\Delta L}{L}\right)} \]

3. Effect of Temperature Change

If a wire is clamped between two rigid, fixed supports at temperature \( \theta \) and cooled by \( \Delta\theta \), thermal contraction creates tension:

\[ \frac{\Delta L}{L} = \alpha \Delta\theta \implies \sigma = Y \alpha \Delta\theta \] \[ v = \sqrt{\frac{Y \alpha \Delta\theta}{\rho}} \]

where \( \alpha \) is the coefficient of linear thermal expansion of the wire material.
