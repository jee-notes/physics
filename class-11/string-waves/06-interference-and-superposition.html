Superposition Principle and Interference

Linear superposition of waves, resultant amplitude phasor derivation, and interference conditions.

Principle of Linear Superposition

The Principle of Superposition states that when two or more wave disturbances travel simultaneously through the same elastic medium, the net displacement of any medium particle at any instant is equal to the algebraic (vector) sum of the displacements that would have been produced by each individual wave acting alone.

Superposition Principle: \[ y_{\text{net}}(x, t) = y_1(x, t) + y_2(x, t) + \dots + y_n(x, t) = \sum_{i=1}^n y_i(x, t) \]
The physical validity of the superposition principle arises directly from the linearity of the differential wave equation \( \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial x^2} = \frac{1}{v^2}\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial t^2} \). It holds strictly for small-amplitude linear elastic waves where Hooke's law is obeyed.

Superposition of Two Harmonic Waves of Same Frequency

Consider two harmonic waves having identical frequencies (\( \omega \)), wavelengths (\( k \)), and propagating in the positive \( x \)-direction along the same string, with amplitudes \( A_1 \) and \( A_2 \), and a mutual phase difference \( \phi \):

\[ y_1(x, t) = A_1 \sin(kx - \omega t) \] \[ y_2(x, t) = A_2 \sin(kx - \omega t + \phi) \]

According to the principle of superposition, the resultant displacement is:

\[ y_{\text{net}}(x, t) = y_1 + y_2 = A_1 \sin(kx - \omega t) + A_2 \sin(kx - \omega t + \phi) \]

Expanding using trigonometric angle addition identities:

\[ y_{\text{net}} = A_1 \sin(kx - \omega t) + A_2 \left[ \sin(kx - \omega t)\cos\phi + \cos(kx - \omega t)\sin\phi \right] \] \[ y_{\text{net}} = \left( A_1 + A_2 \cos\phi \right) \sin(kx - \omega t) + \left( A_2 \sin\phi \right) \cos(kx - \omega t) \]

Defining substitution variables:

\[ A_1 + A_2 \cos\phi = A_{\text{net}} \cos\theta \] \[ A_2 \sin\phi = A_{\text{net}} \sin\theta \]

Substituting these yields the resultant wave equation:

Resultant Wave Equation: \[ y_{\text{net}}(x, t) = A_{\text{net}} \sin(kx - \omega t + \theta) \]

The resultant wave is also a simple harmonic traveling wave of the exact same frequency \( \omega \) and wave number \( k \).

Resultant Amplitude and Phase Angle

Squaring and adding the two substitution equations:

\[ A_{\text{net}}^2 \left(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta\right) = (A_1 + A_2 \cos\phi)^2 + (A_2 \sin\phi)^2 \] \[ A_{\text{net}}^2 = A_1^2 + A_2^2 \cos^2\phi + 2A_1 A_2 \cos\phi + A_2^2 \sin^2\phi \]
Resultant Amplitude Formula: \[ A_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1 A_2 \cos\phi} \]

The initial phase angle \( \theta \) of the resultant wave is given by:

\[ \tan\theta = \frac{A_2 \sin\phi}{A_1 + A_2 \cos\phi} \]

Special Case: Equal Amplitudes (\( A_1 = A_2 = A \))

When both superposing waves have identical amplitudes:

\[ A_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{2A^2(1 + \cos\phi)} = \sqrt{4A^2 \cos^2\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)} = 2A \left|\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)\right| \]

Constructive and Destructive Interference Conditions

The redistribution of wave energy resulting from the superposition of two or more coherent waves is known as Interference.

1. Constructive Interference (Maximum Amplitude & Intensity)

Occurs when the two waves arrive at the point in phase (\( \cos\phi = +1 \)):

  • Phase Difference: \( \phi = 2n\pi \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots) \)
  • Path Difference: \( \Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi} \phi = n\lambda \)
  • Maximum Resultant Amplitude: \( A_{\text{max}} = A_1 + A_2 \) (If \( A_1 = A_2 = A \implies A_{\text{max}} = 2A \))
  • Maximum Resultant Intensity: \( I_{\text{max}} = \left(\sqrt{I_1} + \sqrt{I_2}\right)^2 \) (If \( I_1 = I_2 = I_0 \implies I_{\text{max}} = 4I_0 \))

2. Destructive Interference (Minimum Amplitude & Intensity)

Occurs when the two waves arrive completely out of phase (\( \cos\phi = -1 \)):

  • Phase Difference: \( \phi = (2n + 1)\pi \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots) \)
  • Path Difference: \( \Delta x = (2n + 1)\frac{\lambda}{2} \)
  • Minimum Resultant Amplitude: \( A_{\text{min}} = |A_1 - A_2| \) (If \( A_1 = A_2 \implies A_{\text{min}} = 0 \))
  • Minimum Resultant Intensity: \( I_{\text{min}} = \left(\sqrt{I_1} - \sqrt{I_2}\right)^2 \) (If \( I_1 = I_2 \implies I_{\text{min}} = 0 \))
Intensity Contrast Ratio: \[ \frac{I_{\text{max}}}{I_{\text{min}}} = \left( \frac{A_1 + A_2}{A_1 - A_2} \right)^2 = \left( \frac{\sqrt{I_1} + \sqrt{I_2}}{\sqrt{I_1} - \sqrt{I_2}} \right)^2 \]

Coherence and Path Difference Relationships

For a stable, observable interference pattern to be sustained over time, the two wave sources must be coherent:

Conditions for Coherence:
  1. The two sources must have identical frequencies (\( \omega_1 = \omega_2 \)).
  2. The phase difference \( \phi \) between the sources must remain constant in time (\( \frac{d\phi}{dt} = 0 \)).

Solved JEE Problem: Interference Contrast

Problem: Two coherent waves on a string are given by \( y_1 = 3 \sin(kx - \omega t) \) and \( y_2 = 4 \sin(kx - \omega t + \phi) \) (in cm).
Calculate: (a) Maximum and minimum possible amplitudes, (b) The ratio \( I_{\text{max}} / I_{\text{min}} \), (c) The resultant amplitude if the phase difference is \( \phi = \pi/2 \).

Solution:

  • (a) Extreme Amplitudes: \[ A_{\text{max}} = A_1 + A_2 = 3 + 4 = 7 \text{ cm} \] \[ A_{\text{min}} = |A_1 - A_2| = |3 - 4| = 1 \text{ cm} \]
  • (b) Intensity Ratio: \[ \frac{I_{\text{max}}}{I_{\text{min}}} = \left(\frac{A_{\text{max}}}{A_{\text{min}}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{7}{1}\right)^2 = 49 \]
  • (c) At \( \phi = \pi/2 \) (\( 90^\circ \)): \[ A_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + 2(3)(4)\cos(\pi/2)} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 0} = 5 \text{ cm} \]
