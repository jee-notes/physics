Non-Uniform Strings & Hanging Heavy Ropes

Kinematics and transit time calculations for transverse wave pulses propagating through variable-tension systems.

Hanging Heavy Rope under Gravity

When a uniform rope of mass \( M \), length \( L \), and uniform linear mass density \( \mu = M/L \) is suspended vertically from a rigid ceiling, the tension in the rope is not constant. Instead, the tension at any point is created solely by supporting the weight of the rope segment hanging below that point.

Let \( x \) denote the vertical coordinate measured upward from the free bottom end of the rope (\( x = 0 \)) toward the fixed ceiling (\( x = L \)).

The weight of the rope segment of length \( x \) below point \( P \) is:

\[ W(x) = (\mu x) g \]

For static equilibrium of this segment, the tension at height \( x \) from the bottom must be:

Tension Profile: \[ T(x) = \mu g x = \left(\frac{M}{L}\right) g x \]

Variable Wave Velocity v(x) = sqrt(gx)

Since the local tension varies continuously with height \( x \), the local speed of a transverse wave pulse at position \( x \) is also position-dependent:

\[ v(x) = \sqrt{\frac{T(x)}{\mu}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu g x}{\mu}} \]
Local Wave Velocity: \[ v(x) = \sqrt{g x} \]
Remarkable Properties:
  • The speed of the wave at any height \( x \) is completely independent of the mass \( M \), length \( L \), or thickness of the rope! It depends only on the local acceleration due to gravity \( g \) and height \( x \).
  • At the bottom end (\( x = 0 \)), the wave speed is zero (\( v = 0 \)).
  • At the top support (\( x = L \)), the wave speed is maximum: \( v_{\text{top}} = \sqrt{gL} \).

Calculation of Transit Time for Wave Pulses

Let us find the total time \( t \) required for a wave pulse generated at the bottom free end (\( x = 0 \)) to reach the top support (\( x = L \)).

From the definition of instantaneous velocity:

\[ v(x) = \frac{dx}{dt} = \sqrt{gx} \implies dt = \frac{dx}{\sqrt{gx}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}} x^{-1/2} dx \]

Integrating from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = L \):

\[ t = \int_0^L \frac{dx}{\sqrt{gx}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}} \left[ \frac{x^{1/2}}{1/2} \right]_0^L = 2 \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \]
Transit Time (Bottom to Top): \[ t = 2 \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \]

Acceleration of the Wave Pulse

Differentiating the wave speed with respect to time:

\[ a_{\text{pulse}} = v \frac{dv}{dx} = (\sqrt{gx}) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt{gx}) = \sqrt{gx} \cdot \left(\frac{g}{2\sqrt{gx}}\right) = \frac{g}{2} = \text{constant} \]
The wave pulse travels upward with a constant upward acceleration of \( a = \frac{g}{2} \). Applying standard kinematic equations with \( u = 0 \) and \( s = L \): \[ s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \implies L = 0 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{g}{2}\right)t^2 \implies t = \sqrt{\frac{4L}{g}} = 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \]

Accelerating Systems and Vertically Suspended Mass-String Systems

1. Hanging Rope with an Attached Load Block

Suppose a heavy block of mass \( M_0 \) is suspended from the bottom of the rope of mass \( M \) and length \( L \):

The tension at distance \( x \) from the bottom end is now:

\[ T(x) = (M_0 + \mu x) g \]

The local wave speed becomes:

\[ v(x) = \sqrt{\frac{(M_0 + \mu x)g}{\mu}} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{M_0}{\mu} + x\right) g} \]

The total transit time from bottom to top is obtained by integration:

\[ t = \int_0^L \frac{dx}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{M_0}{\mu} + x\right)g}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{g}} \left[ \sqrt{\frac{M_0}{\mu} + L} - \sqrt{\frac{M_0}{\mu}} \right] \]

Special Case Comparison

  • If \( M_0 = 0 \) (No bottom load): \( t = \frac{2}{\sqrt{g}}[\sqrt{L} - 0] = 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \).
  • If \( M_0 \gg M \) (Light rope / Heavy block): The tension is approximately uniform throughout (\( T \approx M_0 g \)), so \( v \approx \sqrt{\frac{M_0 g}{\mu}} \), giving transit time \( t \approx \frac{L}{v} = L\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{M_0 g}} \).

2. Rope in an Accelerating Elevator

If the entire system is placed inside an elevator accelerating vertically with acceleration \( a_0 \), the effective gravity is modified:

\[ g_{\text{eff}} = \begin{cases} g + a_0 & \text{(Elevator accelerating upward)} \\ g - a_0 & \text{(Elevator accelerating downward, } a_0 < g\text{)} \end{cases} \]

The resulting transit time is:

\[ t = 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{g_{\text{eff}}}} \]
