Energy Transport and Power in String Waves

Kinetic and potential energy densities, instantaneous power flow, and average rate of energy transfer.

Kinetic Energy Density of a Wave Element

Consider a sinusoidal transverse wave traveling along a taut string in the \( +x \)-direction:

\[ y(x, t) = A \sin(kx - \omega t) \]

Consider an infinitesimal element of the string located between \( x \) and \( x + dx \). Its mass is \( dm = \mu \, dx \), and its instantaneous transverse particle velocity is \( v_p = \frac{\partial y}{\partial t} = -\omega A \cos(kx - \omega t) \).

The kinetic energy \( dK \) stored in this differential mass element is:

\[ dK = \frac{1}{2} (dm) v_p^2 = \frac{1}{2} (\mu \, dx) \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial t} \right)^2 \]

The kinetic energy density (kinetic energy per unit length of the string, \( u_k \)) is:

Kinetic Energy Density: \[ u_k = \frac{dK}{dx} = \frac{1}{2} \mu \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial t} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \mu \omega^2 A^2 \cos^2(kx - \omega t) \]

Potential Energy Density (Elastic Strain Energy)

As the transverse wave passes, the string element is stretched from its undisturbed equilibrium length \( dx \) to an extended length \( dl \).

From Pythagoras' theorem, the stretched length \( dl \) is:

\[ dl = \sqrt{dx^2 + dy^2} = dx \sqrt{1 + \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right)^2} \]

Using the binomial approximation for small slopes (\( \left|\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\right| \ll 1 \)):

\[ dl \approx dx \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right)^2 \right] \implies \Delta l = dl - dx = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right)^2 dx \]

The work done by the tension \( T \) in stretching this element is stored as elastic potential energy \( dU \):

\[ dU = T \Delta l = \frac{1}{2} T \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right)^2 dx \]

The potential energy density \( u_p = \frac{dU}{dx} \) is:

Potential Energy Density: \[ u_p = \frac{1}{2} T \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} T \left[ k A \cos(kx - \omega t) \right]^2 = \frac{1}{2} T k^2 A^2 \cos^2(kx - \omega t) \]

Since \( T k^2 = (\mu v^2) \left(\frac{\omega}{v}\right)^2 = \mu \omega^2 \), we obtain the profound equality:

\[ u_p = \frac{1}{2} \mu \omega^2 A^2 \cos^2(kx - \omega t) = u_k \]
Critical Conceptual Comparison (Traveling Wave vs Isolated Harmonic Oscillator):
  • In a simple pendulum or mass-spring oscillator, kinetic energy and potential energy are \( 90^\circ \) out of phase: when \( K \) is maximum (mean position), \( U \) is zero, and vice-versa.
  • In a traveling wave, kinetic energy density and potential energy density are exactly in phase and equal at every point and instant (\( u_k = u_p \)). Both are simultaneously maximal at the mean position (\( y = 0 \)), where slope and particle speed are greatest, and both are simultaneously zero at the crests and troughs (\( y = \pm A \)).

Total Energy Density and Energy in One Wavelength

The total mechanical energy density \( u(x,t) \) (total energy per unit length) at any point on the string is:

\[ u(x, t) = u_k + u_p = \mu \omega^2 A^2 \cos^2(kx - \omega t) \]

Spatial / Time Average Energy Density

Since the average value of \( \cos^2 \theta \) over a complete cycle is \( \frac{1}{2} \):

Average Energy Density (\( \langle u \rangle \)): \[ \langle u \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \mu \omega^2 A^2 = 2\pi^2 \mu f^2 A^2 \quad [\text{SI Unit: J/m}] \]

Total Energy Contained in One Full Wavelength (\( \lambda \))

\[ E_\lambda = \langle u \rangle \cdot \lambda = \frac{1}{2} \mu \omega^2 A^2 \lambda = 2\pi^2 \mu f^2 A^2 \lambda \]

Instantaneous Power Transmitted Along the String

Power is the rate at which mechanical work is done by the left part of the string on the right part across any given cross-section.

The transverse force exerted by the string on the adjacent element across the cross-section is \( F_y = -T \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \).

The instantaneous power \( P(x,t) \) transmitted across that point is the rate of work done by this transverse force:

\[ P(x, t) = F_y \cdot v_p = \left( -T \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right) \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial t} \right) \]

Substituting \( \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} = k A \cos(kx - \omega t) \) and \( \frac{\partial y}{\partial t} = -\omega A \cos(kx - \omega t) \):

\[ P(x, t) = (-T)(k A \cos(kx - \omega t))(-\omega A \cos(kx - \omega t)) = T k \omega A^2 \cos^2(kx - \omega t) \]

Since \( T k = (\mu v^2)\left(\frac{\omega}{v}\right) = \mu v \omega \):

Instantaneous Power Transmitted: \[ P(x, t) = \mu v \omega^2 A^2 \cos^2(kx - \omega t) = u(x,t) \cdot v \]
Instantaneous power equals the total instantaneous energy density multiplied by the wave propagation speed (\( P = u \cdot v \)). Power is always non-negative (\( P \ge 0 \)), meaning energy flows strictly in the direction of wave travel.

Average Power Transmitted and Wave Intensity

Averaging the instantaneous power over one complete period (\( \langle \cos^2(kx - \omega t) \rangle = 1/2 \)):

Average Power (\( P_{\text{avg}} \)): \[ P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{2} \mu v \omega^2 A^2 = 2\pi^2 \mu v f^2 A^2 \quad [\text{SI Unit: Watts (W)}] \]

Wave Intensity (\( I \))

Intensity is defined as the average power transmitted per unit cross-sectional area \( S \) of the string:

\[ I = \frac{P_{\text{avg}}}{S} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} (\rho S) v \omega^2 A^2}{S} = \frac{1}{2} \rho v \omega^2 A^2 = 2\pi^2 \rho v f^2 A^2 \quad [\text{SI Unit: W/m}^2] \]

Solved JEE Problem: Power Transmission

Problem: A taut string of linear mass density \( \mu = 5 \times 10^{-2} \text{ kg/m} \) is under a tension of \( T = 80 \text{ N} \). A sinusoidal wave of frequency \( f = 60 \text{ Hz} \) and amplitude \( A = 2 \text{ cm} = 0.02 \text{ m} \) propagates along the string.
Calculate: (a) Wave speed, (b) Average energy density, (c) Average power transmitted along the string.

Solution:

  • (a) Wave Speed: \[ v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} = \sqrt{\frac{80}{0.05}} = \sqrt{1600} = 40 \text{ m/s} \]
  • (b) Angular frequency: \[ \omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi (60) = 120\pi \text{ rad/s} \] Average Energy Density: \[ \langle u \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \mu \omega^2 A^2 = \frac{1}{2} (0.05) (120\pi)^2 (0.02)^2 = 0.025 \times 14400\pi^2 \times 0.0004 \approx 1.42 \text{ J/m} \]
  • (c) Average Power: \[ P_{\text{avg}} = \langle u \rangle \cdot v = 1.42 \times 40 \approx 56.85 \text{ W} \]
