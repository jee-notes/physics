Composite Strings & Medium Boundary Transitions

Wave propagation across spliced string junctions, frequency invariance, and wavelength transformations.

Joint of Strings with Different Linear Densities

A composite string consists of two or more distinct strings of different materials, thicknesses, or linear mass densities (\( \mu_1 \) and \( \mu_2 \)) knotted or spliced together at a seamless junction point, held under a uniform longitudinal tension \( T \).

Because both string segments are in static longitudinal equilibrium under the same applied pulling force, the tension \( T \) is identical throughout both segments.

The wave propagation speed in each medium is given by:

\[ v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu_1}}, \quad v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu_2}} \]

Taking the ratio of speeds:

Ratio of Wave Speeds in a Composite String: \[ \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}} \]

Waves travel faster in the rarer (lighter) medium and slower in the denser (heavier) medium.

Frequency Invariance Across Media Boundaries

When a continuous periodic wave arrives at the junction connecting String 1 and String 2, the physical continuity of the string requires that the single physical point forming the knot oscillates harmonically up and down.

The knot serves as the periodic physical driver for String 2. Since the knot can oscillate only at the frequency \( f \) with which it is driven by the incident wave from String 1, the oscillatory frequency remains strictly invariant across the boundary:

Law of Frequency Invariance: \[ f_1 = f_2 = f_{\text{source}} = \text{Constant} \] \[ \omega_1 = \omega_2 = \omega_{\text{source}} = \text{Constant} \]
Frequency is determined solely by the source that originates the oscillation. When a wave passes from one medium to another, its speed and wavelength change, but its frequency never changes.

Wavelength Variation in Composite Strings

Because the wave speed changes across the boundary while the frequency remains invariant, the spatial wavelength \( \lambda \) must adjust proportionally in accordance with the fundamental wave relation \( v = f \lambda \):

\[ \lambda_1 = \frac{v_1}{f}, \quad \lambda_2 = \frac{v_2}{f} \]

Dividing the two relations yields:

Ratio of Wavelengths: \[ \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}} \]
  • When moving from a lighter string to a denser string (\( \mu_1 < \mu_2 \)): Wave speed decreases (\( v_2 < v_1 \)) and wavelength contracts (\( \lambda_2 < \lambda_1 \)).
  • When moving from a denser string to a lighter string (\( \mu_1 > \mu_2 \)): Wave speed increases (\( v_2 > v_1 \)) and wavelength expands (\( \lambda_2 > \lambda_1 \)).

Solved JEE Problem: Transit Time in Composite Wire

Problem: A composite string is formed by joining a steel wire of length \( L_1 = 1.0 \text{ m} \) and linear mass density \( \mu_1 = 4 \times 10^{-3} \text{ kg/m} \) with an aluminum wire of length \( L_2 = 1.5 \text{ m} \) and linear mass density \( \mu_2 = 9 \times 10^{-3} \text{ kg/m} \). The combination is kept under a tension of \( T = 360 \text{ N} \).
Calculate: (a) The wave speed in each wire, (b) The total time taken for a pulse to travel from one end of the composite wire to the other.

Solution:

  • (a) Wave speeds: \[ v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{360}{4 \times 10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{90000} = 300 \text{ m/s} \] \[ v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{360}{9 \times 10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{40000} = 200 \text{ m/s} \]
  • (b) Total Transit Time: \[ t_{\text{total}} = t_1 + t_2 = \frac{L_1}{v_1} + \frac{L_2}{v_2} \] \[ t_{\text{total}} = \frac{1.0}{300} + \frac{1.5}{200} = \frac{1}{300} + \frac{3}{400} = \frac{4 + 9}{1200} = \frac{13}{1200} \approx 0.0108 \text{ s} = 10.83 \text{ ms} \]
