Harmonic / Sinusoidal Traveling Waves

Mathematical formulation of harmonic waves, wave parameters, phase dynamics, and particle SHM kinematics.

Standard Equation of a Harmonic Traveling Wave

When the source driving a string executes Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) along the transverse direction, it generates a harmonic (sinusoidal) wave. Every particle of the string subsequently undergoes SHM with identical frequency and amplitude, but with a progressive phase delay proportional to its distance from the source.

The standard displacement equation for a sinusoidal wave propagating along the \( +x \)-axis with amplitude \( A \), angular frequency \( \omega \), wave number \( k \), and initial phase angle \( \phi \) is given by:

Standard Form of a 1D Harmonic Wave: \[ y(x, t) = A \sin(kx - \omega t + \phi) \quad \text{(Traveling along } +x\text{)} \] \[ y(x, t) = A \sin(kx + \omega t + \phi) \quad \text{(Traveling along } -x\text{)} \]

Equivalent alternative forms frequently used in physics include:

\[ y(x,t) = A \sin\left[ 2\pi \left( \frac{x}{\lambda} - \frac{t}{T} \right) + \phi \right] = A \sin\left[ k(x - vt) + \phi \right] \]

Wave Parameters: Amplitude, Wavelength, Frequency, and Wave Number

The characteristics of a harmonic wave are defined by the following fundamental physical quantities:

Quantity & Symbol Definition & Governing Formula SI Unit Dimensional Formula
Amplitude (\( A \)) Maximum transverse displacement of a string particle from its equilibrium position. \( \text{m} \) \( [M^0 L^1 T^0] \)
Wavelength (\( \lambda \)) Minimum spatial distance between two consecutive particles vibrating in the same phase. \( \text{m} \) \( [M^0 L^1 T^0] \)
Time Period (\( T \)) Time taken by any single particle to complete one full oscillation. \( \text{s} \) \( [M^0 L^0 T^1] \)
Frequency (\( f \) or \( \nu \)) Number of complete oscillations executed per unit time: \( f = 1/T \). \( \text{Hz } (\text{s}^{-1}) \) \( [M^0 L^0 T^{-1}] \)
Angular Frequency (\( \omega \)) Rate of change of phase angle with respect to time: \( \omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T} \). \( \text{rad/s} \) \( [M^0 L^0 T^{-1}] \)
Angular Wave Number (\( k \)) Spatial rate of change of phase: \( k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \). \( \text{rad/m} \) \( [M^0 L^{-1} T^0] \)
Wave Speed (\( v \)) Propagation velocity of the phase / wave profile: \( v = f\lambda = \frac{\omega}{k} \). \( \text{m/s} \) \( [M^0 L^1 T^{-1}] \)

Phase, Initial Phase Constant, and Path Difference

The argument of the sinusoidal function represents the total phase \( \Phi(x,t) \) of the particle at position \( x \) at time \( t \):

\[ \Phi(x, t) = kx - \omega t + \phi \]

The phase completely determines both the instantaneous displacement and the direction of velocity of the oscillating particle at that instant.

1. Phase Difference between Two Points at the Same Instant (\( \Delta t = 0 \))

For two particles separated by a spatial distance (path difference) \( \Delta x \):

\[ \Delta \Phi = k \Delta x = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x \]

2. Phase Difference for the Same Particle over a Time Interval (\( \Delta x = 0 \))

For a single particle observed over a time duration \( \Delta t \):

\[ \Delta \Phi = \omega \Delta t = \frac{2\pi}{T} \Delta t \]
General Phase Difference Formula: When both spatial position and time change simultaneously: \[ \Delta \Phi = \left| k \Delta x - \omega \Delta t \right| = 2\pi \left| \frac{\Delta x}{\lambda} - \frac{\Delta t}{T} \right| \]

Particle Velocity and Acceleration in SHM Wave

Every individual particle on the string undergoes transverse SHM along the \( y \)-axis. Differentiating the displacement equation with respect to time:

1. Particle Velocity (\( v_p \))

\[ v_p = \frac{\partial y}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[ A\sin(kx - \omega t + \phi) \right] = -\omega A \cos(kx - \omega t + \phi) \]

In terms of instantaneous displacement \( y \):

\[ v_p = \pm \omega \sqrt{A^2 - y^2} \]

The maximum particle velocity occurs as the particle crosses its mean position (\( y = 0 \)):

\[ v_{p,\text{max}} = \omega A = 2\pi f A \]

2. Particle Acceleration (\( a_p \))

\[ a_p = \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial t^2} = -\omega^2 A \sin(kx - \omega t + \phi) = -\omega^2 y \]

The maximum particle acceleration occurs at the extreme displacements (\( y = \pm A \)):

\[ a_{p,\text{max}} = \omega^2 A = 4\pi^2 f^2 A \]
Ratio of Maximum Particle Speed to Wave Speed: \[ \frac{v_{p,\text{max}}}{v} = \frac{\omega A}{\omega / k} = k A = \frac{2\pi A}{\lambda} \] For small amplitude linear wave theory to hold strictly, the wave amplitude must be much smaller than the wavelength (\( A \ll \lambda \)), which guarantees that \( v_{p,\text{max}} \ll v \) and the slope \( \left|\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\right| \ll 1 \).

Solved JEE Problem: Wave Characteristics Extraction

Problem: The transverse displacement of a string wave is described by: \[ y(x,t) = 0.05 \sin(4\pi x - 20\pi t + \pi/6) \quad (\text{SI units}) \] Find: (a) Amplitude, (b) Wavelength, (c) Frequency, (d) Wave speed & direction, (e) Maximum particle speed.

Solution:

  • (a) Amplitude: \( A = 0.05 \text{ m} = 5 \text{ cm} \).
  • (b) Wavelength: Comparing \( k = 4\pi \text{ rad/m} \implies \lambda = \frac{2\pi}{k} = \frac{2\pi}{4\pi} = 0.5 \text{ m} \).
  • (c) Frequency: Comparing \( \omega = 20\pi \text{ rad/s} \implies f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{20\pi}{2\pi} = 10 \text{ Hz} \).
  • (d) Wave Speed: \( v = \frac{\omega}{k} = \frac{20\pi}{4\pi} = 5 \text{ m/s} \). Since the sign between \( x \) and \( t \) is negative, the wave travels along the \( +x \)-direction.
  • (e) Maximum Particle Speed: \( v_{p,\text{max}} = \omega A = (20\pi)(0.05) = \pi \approx 3.14 \text{ m/s} \).
