Sonometer, Melde's Experiment & Resonance

Experimental acoustics, transverse vibration laws, Melde's electrical setup, and acoustic beat phenomena.

Sonometer Construction and Principle

A sonometer is a classic experimental apparatus designed to study the transverse vibrations of stretched strings, verify the laws of vibration, and determine the unknown frequency of an acoustic source (such as a tuning fork).

Key Components:

  • Hollow Wooden Resonant Box: Communicates vibrations from the wire to the enclosed air column, amplifying the sound volume through resonance.
  • Movable Knife-Edge Bridges (A & B): Clamps the wire at defined nodal points, allowing precise adjustment of the vibrating length \( L \).
  • Frictionless Pulley and Hanger: Applies a known longitudinal tension \( T = M g \).

Laws of Transverse Vibrations of a Stretched String

The fundamental resonant frequency of a stretched string is given by:

\[ f = \frac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} \]

From this fundamental formula, three experimental laws of transverse vibration are derived:

1. Law of Length

The fundamental frequency of vibration is inversely proportional to the resonating length \( L \), provided the tension \( T \) and linear mass density \( \mu \) are kept constant:

\[ f \propto \frac{1}{L} \implies f \cdot L = \text{Constant} \]

2. Law of Tension

The fundamental frequency is directly proportional to the square root of the tension \( T \), provided the vibrating length \( L \) and linear mass density \( \mu \) are constant:

\[ f \propto \sqrt{T} \implies \frac{f}{\sqrt{T}} = \text{Constant} \]

3. Law of Mass

The fundamental frequency is inversely proportional to the square root of the linear mass density \( \mu \), provided the length \( L \) and tension \( T \) are constant:

\[ f \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}} = \frac{1}{r\sqrt{\pi\rho}} \implies f \cdot \sqrt{\mu} = \text{Constant} \]

Determination of Frequency of Tuning Fork

To determine the unknown frequency \( f \) of a tuning fork using a sonometer:

  1. A small V-shaped paper strip (called a paper rider) is placed at the exact midpoint of the string between the two bridges.
  2. The tuning fork is struck against a rubber pad and its vibrating stem is pressed firmly against the top of the wooden sonometer box.
  3. The distance \( L \) between the bridges is gradually adjusted until the paper rider violently flutters and is thrown off the wire.
Resonance Condition: When the natural fundamental frequency of the vibrating string segment exactly matches the frequency of the tuning fork, acoustic resonance occurs. An antinode with large vibrational amplitude forms at the center, throwing off the paper rider. The tuning fork frequency is then calculated as: \[ f = \frac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} \]

Melde's Experiment (Transverse and Longitudinal Arrangements)

In Melde's experiment, an electrically maintained tuning fork is used to generate standing waves in a long thread attached to one of its prongs.

1. Transverse Arrangement

The prong of the tuning fork vibrates perpendicular to the length of the thread.

  • During one complete oscillation of the fork prong, the string is pulled up and down once.
  • Frequency of string vibration equals the frequency of the fork: \( f_{\text{string}} = f_{\text{fork}} \).
  • If the thread of length \( L \) vibrates in \( p \) loops: \[ f_{\text{fork}} = \frac{p}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} \]
  • Melde's Law: \( p^2 T = \text{Constant} \).

2. Longitudinal Arrangement

The prong of the tuning fork vibrates parallel to the length of the thread.

  • When the prong moves forward, the thread slackens (forming a loop); when it moves backward, the thread tightens. Thus, the string completes one full transverse vibration for every two complete vibrations of the fork: \[ f_{\text{string}} = \frac{1}{2} f_{\text{fork}} \]
  • If the thread of length \( L \) vibrates in \( p \) loops: \[ f_{\text{fork}} = \frac{p}{L}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} \]
  • Melde's Law: \( p^2 T = \text{Constant} \).
For the same string, length, and tension, the number of loops formed in the transverse arrangement is exactly twice the number of loops formed in the longitudinal arrangement (\( p_{\text{transverse}} = 2 \, p_{\text{longitudinal}} \)).

Beats Produced by Vibrating Strings

When two musical strings of nearly equal frequencies \( f_1 \) and \( f_2 \) vibrate simultaneously, their superposition produces periodic waxing and waning of acoustic intensity known as beats.

Beat Frequency: \[ f_b = |f_1 - f_2| = \left| \frac{1}{2L_1}\sqrt{\frac{T_1}{\mu_1}} - \frac{1}{2L_2}\sqrt{\frac{T_2}{\mu_2}} \right| \quad [\text{beats/second}] \]

Standard JEE Reasoning: Loading and Filing

Action Performed Effect on Parameter Effect on Frequency \( f \)
Loading tuning fork / string with wax Increases mass / inertia (\( \mu \uparrow \)) Frequency decreases (\( f \downarrow \))
Filing tuning fork / thinning string Decreases mass / inertia (\( \mu \downarrow \)) Frequency increases (\( f \uparrow \))
Tightening string (increasing peg tension) Increases tension (\( T \uparrow \)) Frequency increases (\( f \uparrow \))
Increasing bridge separation \( L \) Increases vibrating length (\( L \uparrow \)) Frequency decreases (\( f \downarrow \))
