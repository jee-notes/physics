Standing Waves and Energy Trapping

Superposition of counter-propagating waves, nodal structures, spatial phase coherence, and energy localization.

Superposition of Two Oppositely Directed Traveling Waves

When two identical harmonic waves having the same amplitude, frequency, and wavelength travel in opposite directions along the same stretched string, their linear superposition produces a wave pattern that does not advance along the medium. This stationary disturbance is called a Standing Wave (or Stationary Wave).

Consider two such waves:

\[ y_1(x, t) = A \sin(kx - \omega t) \quad \text{(Wave traveling along } +x\text{)} \] \[ y_2(x, t) = A \sin(kx + \omega t) \quad \text{(Wave traveling along } -x\text{)} \]

Mathematical Equation of Standing Waves: y(x,t) = 2A sin(kx) cos(wt)

By the principle of superposition:

\[ y(x, t) = y_1 + y_2 = A \left[ \sin(kx - \omega t) + \sin(kx + \omega t) \right] \]

Using the standard trigonometric sum-to-product identity \( \sin(C) + \sin(D) = 2\sin\left(\frac{C+D}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{C-D}{2}\right) \):

Standing Wave Equation: \[ y(x, t) = \left[ 2A \sin(kx) \right] \cos(\omega t) = A(x) \cos(\omega t) \]

where \( A(x) = \left| 2A \sin(kx) \right| \) is the position-dependent amplitude of the particle at position \( x \).

In a traveling wave \( y = A\sin(kx - \omega t) \), all particles oscillate with the same amplitude \( A \), but reach their peaks at different times (different phase). In a standing wave \( y = [2A\sin(kx)]\cos(\omega t) \), all particles oscillate with different amplitudes depending on their position \( x \), but they all pass through their extremes and zero displacements simultaneously in time.

Nodes and Antinodes: Positions and Spatial Separation

1. Nodes (Points of Zero Amplitude)

Points where the oscillatory amplitude is permanently zero (\( A(x) = 0 \)):

\[ \sin(kx) = 0 \implies kx = n\pi \implies \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} x = n\pi \]
Positions of Nodes: \[ x_N = \frac{n \lambda}{2} = 0, \frac{\lambda}{2}, \lambda, \frac{3\lambda}{2}, \dots \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, \dots) \]
  • Permanent displacement = \( 0 \), particle velocity = \( 0 \).
  • Strain and pressure variation are maximum at nodes (maximum string slope).

2. Antinodes (Points of Maximum Amplitude)

Points where the oscillatory amplitude reaches its maximum value \( 2A \) (\( |\sin(kx)| = 1 \)):

\[ kx = (2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{2} \implies \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} x = (2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{2} \]
Positions of Antinodes: \[ x_A = (2n + 1)\frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{\lambda}{4}, \frac{3\lambda}{4}, \frac{5\lambda}{4}, \dots \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, \dots) \]

Key Spatial Distances

  • Distance between any two successive nodes: \( \Delta x_{NN} = \frac{\lambda}{2} \)
  • Distance between any two successive antinodes: \( \Delta x_{AA} = \frac{\lambda}{2} \)
  • Distance between adjacent node and antinode: \( \Delta x_{NA} = \frac{\lambda}{4} \)

Phase Relationships Between Oscillating Particles

From the equation \( y(x,t) = [2A\sin(kx)]\cos(\omega t) \):

  • Particles within the same loop (between two adjacent nodes): The term \( \sin(kx) \) has the same algebraic sign for all points in the loop. Therefore, all particles in that loop oscillate in exact phase (\( \Delta\Phi = 0 \)), reaching their maximum displacements and crossing zero simultaneously.
  • Particles in adjacent loops: Crossing a node changes the sign of \( \sin(kx) \). Therefore, particles situated in adjacent loops oscillate in exact opposite phase (\( \Delta\Phi = \pi \text{ radians} = 180^\circ \)).

Energy Trapping and Energy Exchange in Standing Waves

In a standing wave, because the nodes are permanently motionless at all times (\( v_p = 0 \)), the instantaneous power flow across any node is identically zero:

\[ P_{\text{node}} = \left( -T \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right) \cdot v_p = \left( -T \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \right) \cdot (0) = 0 \]

Consequently, no energy can cross a node. The mechanical energy remains permanently trapped inside each individual loop between successive nodes.

Dynamic Energy Exchange in Standing Waves:
  • When the string is completely flat (\( t = \frac{T}{4}, \frac{3T}{4} \dots \)): Displacement \( y = 0 \) everywhere, potential energy \( U = 0 \). All trapped energy is in the form of pure Kinetic Energy, with particles moving at their maximum speeds.
  • When the string has maximum deformation (\( t = 0, \frac{T}{2} \dots \)): Particles are momentarily at rest (\( v_p = 0 \)), kinetic energy \( K = 0 \). All trapped energy is stored as pure Elastic Potential Energy in the stretched string.

Comparison Between Traveling and Standing Waves

Feature Progressive / Traveling Waves Stationary / Standing Waves
Wave Equation \( y = A\sin(kx \mp \omega t) \) \( y = [2A\sin(kx)]\cos(\omega t) \)
Energy Transport Transports energy and momentum continuously through the medium at speed \( v \). Zero net energy transport; energy is trapped between nodes.
Particle Amplitudes All particles vibrate with the same amplitude \( A \). Different particles have different amplitudes from \( 0 \) (nodes) to \( 2A \) (antinodes).
Phase Relations Phase varies continuously from point to point (\( \Delta\Phi = k\Delta x \)). All particles in a loop are in phase; adjacent loops are in opposite phase (\( \pi \)).
Passage through Equilibrium Different particles pass through equilibrium at different times. All particles across the entire medium pass through equilibrium simultaneously twice per period.
Particle Velocity vs Slope Max velocity occurs at max slope (\( v_p = -v \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \)). Nodes have zero velocity with maximum slope; Antinodes have maximum velocity with zero slope.
