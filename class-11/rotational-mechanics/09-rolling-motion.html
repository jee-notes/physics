Rolling Motion

The combination of translation and rotation with the constraint \(v_{\text{CM}} = \omega R\).

What is Rolling Without Slipping?

Rolling without slipping (pure rolling) means the point of the body in contact with the surface is instantaneously at rest. The body both translates (COM moves) and rotates (spins about an axis through the COM), with a strict kinematic link:

\[v_{\text{CM}} = \omega R\]

where \(R\) is the radius. Differentiating:

\[a_{\text{CM}} = \alpha R\]

This condition eliminates one variable, making rolling problems one degree of freedom simpler than general combined motion.

The rolling condition \(v = \omega R\) relates the translational and rotational speeds. If \(v > \omega R\), there is forward slipping (skidding). If \(v < \omega R\), there is backward slipping (spinning in place). Pure rolling lies exactly at the boundary.

Velocity Distribution in Rolling

For a disc rolling to the right with COM velocity \(v\):

  • Bottom point (contact): \(v_{\text{trans}} - v_{\text{rot}} = v - \omega R = 0\). Instantaneously at rest.
  • Centre (COM): \(v_{\text{trans}} = v\). Only translational velocity.
  • Top point: \(v_{\text{trans}} + v_{\text{rot}} = v + \omega R = 2v\). Fastest point on the body.

In general, the velocity of any point at angle \(\phi\) from the bottom is:

\[v_{\text{point}} = 2v\sin\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)\]

Instantaneous Axis of Rotation

Rolling can be viewed as pure rotation about the contact point (the instantaneous axis of rotation). The moment of inertia about this axis is:

\[I_{\text{contact}} = I_{\text{CM}} + MR^2 \qquad \text{(parallel axis theorem)}\]

The total kinetic energy can then be written as:

\[K = \frac{1}{2}I_{\text{contact}}\omega^2 = \frac{1}{2}(I_{\text{CM}} + MR^2)\omega^2\]

This equals \(\frac{1}{2}Mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{\text{CM}}\omega^2\) — confirming that both viewpoints give the same result.

Kinetic Energy of Rolling

The total KE of a body rolling without slipping:

\[K = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{\text{CM}}\omega^2 = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2\left(1 + \frac{k^2}{R^2}\right)\]

where \(k\) is the radius of gyration (\(I_{\text{CM}} = Mk^2\)). The fraction of total KE that is rotational:

\[\frac{K_{\text{rot}}}{K_{\text{total}}} = \frac{k^2/R^2}{1 + k^2/R^2}\]
  • Solid sphere (\(k^2/R^2 = 2/5\)): 2/7 of KE is rotational
  • Solid cylinder (\(k^2/R^2 = 1/2\)): 1/3 of KE is rotational
  • Hollow sphere (\(k^2/R^2 = 2/3\)): 2/5 of KE is rotational
  • Thin ring (\(k^2/R^2 = 1\)): 1/2 of KE is rotational

The Role of Friction in Rolling

Static friction is essential for rolling without slipping — it provides the torque needed to maintain the constraint \(v = \omega R\). However:

  • The contact point has zero velocity, so static friction does no work.
  • Mechanical energy is therefore conserved in pure rolling (if no other dissipative forces act).
  • If external forces would cause the body to slip, friction acts to prevent slipping. If the required friction exceeds \(\mu_s N\), the body begins to slip.
This is a crucial subtlety: friction is present and essential, yet does no work. It redirects energy between translational and rotational forms without dissipating any.
