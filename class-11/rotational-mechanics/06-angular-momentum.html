Angular Momentum

The rotational analogue of linear momentum — the quantity that relates torque to rotational state.

Angular Momentum of a Particle

The angular momentum of a particle of mass \(m\), with position vector \(\vec{r}\) and linear momentum \(\vec{p} = m\vec{v}\), about a point \(O\) is:

\[\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p} = \vec{r} \times (m\vec{v})\]

The magnitude is:

\[L = mvr\sin\theta = mv \cdot r_\perp = mr \cdot v_\perp\]

where \(r_\perp\) is the perpendicular distance from \(O\) to the line of motion, and \(v_\perp\) is the component of velocity perpendicular to \(\vec{r}\).

A particle moving in a straight line has angular momentum about any point not on the line. Angular momentum is not exclusive to circular or rotational motion — it is a general property of any motion viewed from a reference point.

Angular Momentum of a Rigid Body

For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis with angular velocity \(\omega\), the angular momentum about that axis is:

\[L = I\omega\]

where \(I\) is the moment of inertia about the axis. This is the rotational analogue of \(p = mv\).

The direction of \(\vec{L}\) is along the axis, given by the right-hand rule: it points in the direction of \(\vec{\omega}\).

Torque and Angular Momentum

Newton's second law in its most general rotational form states:

\[\vec{\tau}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt}\]

This is the rotational analogue of \(\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}\). For a rigid body about a fixed axis (where \(I\) is constant):

\[\tau = \frac{dL}{dt} = I\frac{d\omega}{dt} = I\alpha\]

which recovers the familiar \(\tau = I\alpha\). But the general form \(\vec{\tau} = \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt}\) is more powerful — it applies even when \(I\) changes (as in the spinning skater problem).

Angular Momentum of a Particle in Circular Motion

A particle of mass \(m\) in a circle of radius \(r\) with speed \(v\):

\[L = mvr = mr^2\omega\]

This matches \(L = I\omega\) with \(I = mr^2\) — the moment of inertia of a point mass.

Angular Momentum About the COM

For a system of particles (or a rigid body), the total angular momentum about a fixed point can be decomposed as:

\[\vec{L}_{\text{total}} = \vec{L}_{\text{orbital}} + \vec{L}_{\text{spin}}\]
  • \(\vec{L}_{\text{orbital}} = M\vec{R}_{\text{CM}} \times \vec{V}_{\text{CM}}\) — angular momentum of the COM (as if all mass were concentrated there).
  • \(\vec{L}_{\text{spin}} = I_{\text{CM}}\vec{\omega}\) — angular momentum about the COM (intrinsic rotation).

For a rolling object, both terms contribute. For a spinning body at rest, only the spin term survives.
