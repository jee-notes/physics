Torque

The rotational analogue of force — the cause of angular acceleration.

Definition

The torque of a force \(\vec{F}\) about a point \(O\) is defined as the cross product of the position vector \(\vec{r}\) (from \(O\) to the point of application) and the force:

\[\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}\]

The magnitude is:

\[\tau = rF\sin\theta = F \cdot d\]

where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\vec{r}\) and \(\vec{F}\), and \(d = r\sin\theta\) is the perpendicular distance from \(O\) to the line of action — also called the lever arm or moment arm.

Torque is zero when the force passes through the pivot (lever arm = 0), or when the force is parallel to the position vector (\(\sin\theta = 0\)). A large force does not guarantee a large torque — the lever arm matters equally.

Direction of Torque

By the right-hand rule for cross products, the torque vector is perpendicular to the plane containing \(\vec{r}\) and \(\vec{F}\). For rotation in a plane:

  • Counterclockwise (CCW) torque → positive (out of the page)
  • Clockwise (CW) torque → negative (into the page)

The sign convention is chosen to match the axis direction using the right-hand rule.

Couple

A couple consists of two equal and opposite forces whose lines of action do not coincide. The net force is zero, but the net torque is non-zero:

\[\tau_{\text{couple}} = F \cdot d\]

where \(d\) is the perpendicular distance between the two lines of action. The remarkable property of a couple is:

  • The torque is the same about every point. (It does not depend on the choice of pivot.)
  • A couple produces pure rotation without any translation.

Torque About an Axis

In fixed-axis rotation problems, we need the component of torque along the rotation axis. If the axis is along \(\hat{n}\):

\[\tau_{\text{axis}} = \hat{n} \cdot (\vec{r} \times \vec{F})\]

Only forces (or components of forces) that tend to rotate the body about the given axis contribute. Forces parallel to the axis or forces whose line of action intersects the axis produce zero torque about that axis.

Conditions for Equilibrium

A rigid body is in static equilibrium (no translation and no rotation) when:

\[\sum \vec{F} = 0 \qquad \text{(translational equilibrium)}\] \[\sum \vec{\tau} = 0 \qquad \text{(rotational equilibrium)}\]

The torque equilibrium condition must hold about any point (if the net force is zero, the net torque is the same about all points). In practice, choose a pivot that eliminates unknown forces from the torque equation.

Smart choice of pivot can dramatically simplify equilibrium problems. Choose a point where the most unknown forces act — their torques vanish, leaving fewer unknowns.
