Vector Addition — Parallelogram Law

The fundamental operation for combining vectors.

Why Vector Addition?

The vector addition laws are not physical laws like Ohm's law. They are mathematical rules for combining vectors, just as ordinary addition is a mathematical rule for combining numbers.

Historically, these rules were called laws when the mathematical treatment of vectors had not yet been fully developed. But the physical quantities of position and displacement are vectors because they mathematically represent properties of physical space itself.

Physical space has both extent and direction. Position and displacement provide a mathematical representation of these properties and therefore have the vector character.

Velocity and acceleration are also vectors because they are obtained from displacement and velocity by taking their time derivatives:

\[ \vec v=\frac{d\vec r}{dt} \qquad \vec a=\frac{d\vec v}{dt} \]

Since time is a scalar quantity, taking the time derivative preserves the vector character of the quantity.

According to Newton's second law,

\[ \vec F=m\vec a \]

and since mass is a scalar, force is also a vector.

The vector character of these physical quantities can be traced through their relations to position and displacement.
Vector addition is therefore a mathematical rule used to combine such vector quantities; it is not a physical law discovered experimentally.
