Equation of Trajectory

The geometric path of a particle in a plane.

1. Definition

The equation of trajectory is a mathematical relation between the coordinates $(x, y)$ of a moving particle, describing its path in space without explicit reference to time $t$.

2. Derivation for a Projectile

For a projectile fired at velocity $u$ and angle $\theta$:

X-coordinate:
\(x = (u\cos\theta)t \implies t = \frac{x}{u\cos\theta}\)
Y-coordinate:
\(y = (u\sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2\)

Substituting $t$ into the $y$ equation:

\[ y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta} \]

3. Parabolic Nature

The resulting equation is of the form \(y = ax + bx^2\), which is the standard equation of a parabola. This proves that neglecting air resistance, the path of any projectile is a parabolic arc.

If the projectile is fired from a height, or if the target is at a different elevation, the trajectory remains parabolic but the origin shift must be accounted for.
