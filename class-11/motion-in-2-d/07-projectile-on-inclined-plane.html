Projectile on an Inclined Plane

Adjusting the coordinate system to simplify complex unlevel trajectories.

Non-Vertical Resolution

We know that it is advantageous to resolve vectors orthogonally. When acceleration is constant, we resolve initial velocity because only the component of initial velocity along the acceleration changes with time.

We used this concept in vertical projectiles in G2G and T2G cases.

But, in case of inclined plane projectiles (up the incline and down the incline), we want to utilise the information that net displacement perpendicular to the plane is 0 .

This information is more convenient depending on what information is provided in the question and what we need to find.

In this topic, we build a mathematical framework for such cases.

Projection Up the Incline

Consider an inclined plane making an angle \(\alpha\) with the horizontal. A projectile is launched from the bottom of this incline with an initial velocity \(u\) at an angle \(\theta\) with respect to the incline. We need to solve this situation in terms of knowing T,H,R.

The most efficient way to solve this is to rotate our axis system.

Let the x-axis lie parallel up the incline, and the y-axis lie perpendicular to the incline above level positive. We determine \(\vec u\) and \(\vec a\) along the new axes:

  • \(u_x = u\cos\theta\)
  • \(u_y = u\sin\theta\)
  • \(a_x = -g\sin\alpha\) (Gravity now critically has a component opposing the horizontal motion)
  • \(a_y = -g\cos\alpha\)

Time of Flight (\(T\)):

The projectile hits the incline when its perpendicular displacement \(y = 0\). \[ y = u_y T + \frac{1}{2}a_y T^2 \qquad 0 = u\sin\theta \, T - \frac{1}{2}(g\cos\alpha) \, T^2 \] \[ T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g\cos\alpha} \qquad T = \frac{2u_y}{a_y} \]

Maximum Height (\(H\)):

The maximum height is attained \(v_y = 0\) . Using: \[ v_y^2 = u_y^2 + 2(a_y)H \qquad 0 = u^2\sin^2\theta + 2 (-g\cos\alpha) \, H \] \[ H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g\cos\alpha} \qquad H = \frac{u_y^2}{2a_y} \]

The expressions for \(T\) and \(H\) are same for both cases: up the incine as well as down the incine.
This is because time of flight and maximum height depend only on the Y component as we define the Y component itself in this way.

Range Up the Incline (\(R\)):

Unlike ground-to-ground, we must account for \(a_x\):

\[ R = u_x T + \frac{1}{2}a_x T^2 = u\cos\theta \, T + \frac{1}{2}(-g\sin\alpha)\, T^2 \]

We need to substitute the value for \(T\) in the above equation to get \(R\) .

Projection Down the Incline

Now consider a particle projected from the top of the incline down the incline with an initial speed \(u\) at an angle \(\theta\) with respect to the incline. We again want to determine the time of flight \(T\), maximum height \(H\), and range \(R\).

We use the same rotated coordinate system, but take the positive \(x\)-axis down the incline. The positive \(y\)-axis is perpendicular to the incline, away from the plane.

The initial velocity and acceleration are then resolved along these axes:

  • \(u_x=u\cos\theta\)
  • \(u_y=u\sin\theta\)
  • \(a_x=+g\sin\alpha\)
  • \(a_y=-g\cos\alpha\)

Gravity now has a component along the positive \(x\)-direction, so it assists the motion down the incline.

Time of Flight (\(T\)):

As in the projection up the incline, the projectile hits the incline when its perpendicular displacement returns to zero:

\[ \Delta y=0 \]

Using the 1D kinematic equation in the perpendicular direction,

\[ \Delta y=u_yT+\frac12a_yT^2 \]

we get

\[ 0=u\sin\theta\,T-\frac12(g\cos\alpha)T^2 \]

The non-zero solution gives the time of flight:

\[ \boxed{T=\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g\cos\alpha}=\frac{2u_y}{g\cos\alpha}} \]

Maximum Height (\(H\)):

The maximum perpendicular height is attained when the perpendicular component of velocity becomes zero:

\[ v_y=0 \]

Using the velocity-displacement equation in the perpendicular direction,

\[ v_y^2=u_y^2+2a_yH \]

we get

\[ 0=u^2\sin^2\theta-2g\cos\alpha\,H \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{H=\frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g\cos\alpha}=\frac{u_y^2}{2g\cos\alpha}} \]
The expressions for \(T\) and \(H\) are the same as for projection up the incline.
This is because both quantities are determined entirely by the motion in the direction perpendicular to the incline.
In both cases, the particle starts from the plane and returns to the same plane, so the net perpendicular displacement is zero.

Range Down the Incline (\(R\)):

Unlike the perpendicular motion, the motion along the incline is affected by gravity:

\[ a_x=+g\sin\alpha \]

Since gravity assists the motion down the incline, the range is obtained from

\[ R=u_xT+\frac12a_xT^2 \]

Substituting the resolved quantities,

\[ R=u\cos\theta\,T+\frac12g\sin\alpha\,T^2 \]

Using

\[ T=\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g\cos\alpha} \]

we obtain

\[ R=\frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}\left[\sin(2\theta-\alpha)+\sin\alpha\right] \] \[ \boxed{R=\frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha}\left[\sin(2\theta-\alpha)+\sin\alpha\right]} \]

The \(+\sin\alpha\) term appears because gravity has a component in the same direction as the projectile's motion along the incline.
