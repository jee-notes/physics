Projectile on an Inclined Plane

Adjusting the coordinate system to simplify complex unlevel trajectories.

Projection Up the Incline

Consider an inclined plane making an angle \(\alpha\) with the horizontal. A projectile is launched from the bottom of this incline with an initial velocity \(u\) at an angle \(\theta\) with respect to the horizontal.

The most efficient way to solve this is to rotate our axis system. Let the x-axis lie parallel up the incline, and the y-axis lie perpendicular to the incline. The angle of projection relative to the incline itself becomes \((\theta - \alpha)\).

Adjusted Kinematics:
  • \(u_x = u\cos(\theta - \alpha)\)
  • \(u_y = u\sin(\theta - \alpha)\)
  • \(a_x = -g\sin\alpha\) (Gravity now critically has a component opposing the horizontal motion)
  • \(a_y = -g\cos\alpha\)

Time of Flight (\(T\)): The projectile hits the incline when its perpendicular displacement \(y = 0\). \[ y = u_y T + \frac{1}{2}a_y T^2 \implies 0 = u\sin(\theta - \alpha)T - \frac{1}{2}(g\cos\alpha)T^2 \] \[ T = \frac{2u\sin(\theta - \alpha)}{g\cos\alpha} \]

Range Up the Incline (\(R\)): Unlike ground-to-ground, we must account for \(a_x\): \[ R = u_x T + \frac{1}{2}a_x T^2 = u\cos(\theta - \alpha)T - \frac{1}{2}g\sin\alpha T^2 \] Through trigonometric simplification, the range simplifies nicely to: \[ R = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha} \left[ \sin(2\theta - \alpha) - \sin\alpha \right] \] Maximum range up the incline occurs when \(\sin(2\theta - \alpha) = 1\), or \(2\theta - \alpha = \pi/2\).

Projection Down the Incline

If the projectile is launched from the top of the incline, pointing downwards, we adjust our rotated axis so the positive x-axis points down the incline. The projection angle relative to the incline surface is now \((\theta + \alpha)\).

Adjusted Downward Kinematics:
  • \(u_x = u\cos(\theta + \alpha)\)
  • \(u_y = u\sin(\theta + \alpha)\)
  • \(a_x = +g\sin\alpha\) (Gravity now assists the horizontal motion down the plane)
  • \(a_y = -g\cos\alpha\)

Time of Flight (\(T\)): \[ T = \frac{2u\sin(\theta + \alpha)}{g\cos\alpha} \]

Range Down the Incline (\(R\)): Since gravity assists, the formula adds the sine terms rather than subtracting: \[ R = \frac{u^2}{g\cos^2\alpha} \left[ \sin(2\theta + \alpha) + \sin\alpha \right] \]
