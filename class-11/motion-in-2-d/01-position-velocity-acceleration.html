Position, Velocity, and Acceleration in 2D

The kinematic foundation for motion in a plane.

1. Position Vector

In 2D motion, the position of a particle at any time \(t\) is represented by a position vector \(\vec{r}\) relative to an origin \(O\).

\[ \vec{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j} \]

The magnitude of the position vector is the distance from the origin: \(r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\).

2. Displacement

Displacement \(\Delta\vec{r}\) is the change in the position vector from time \(t_1\) to \(t_2\).

\[ \Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1 = (x_2 - x_1)\hat{i} + (y_2 - y_1)\hat{j} \]

3. Velocity

Average Velocity: The ratio of displacement to the time interval.

\[ \vec{v}_{avg} = \frac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}\hat{i} + \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t}\hat{j} \]

Instantaneous Velocity: The limit of average velocity as \(\Delta t \to 0\). This vector is always tangent to the particle's path.

\[ \vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = v_x\hat{i} + v_y\hat{j} \]

4. Acceleration

Acceleration \(\vec{a}\) is the rate of change of velocity. Like velocity, it can be resolved into two components.

\[ \vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = a_x\hat{i} + a_y\hat{j} \]
Remember, even if speed is constant, a change in direction results in non-zero acceleration.
