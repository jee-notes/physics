River-Boat Problems

Navigating cross-currents using vector velocity addition.

Setup

Consider a patch of still water of width \(L\) and a motorboat capable of speed \(v_b\) attempts to cross the patch. It will head straight towards the opposite side and reach the other saide in time \(L/v_b\) .

Now the same motorboat is in a river of uniform width \(L\) flowing horizontally with a constant velocity \(\vec{v}_r\). The motorboat attempts to cross it by heading straight towards the opposite side.

In this case, the motorboat is headed towards the opposite side. Velocity of boat relative to river \(=\vec{v}_{br} = v_{\text{br}}\hat j\) . This is also the headed velocity of the boat.

However, the boat will not reach the opposite side at the point exactly opposite to the starting point. This is because the river is dragging the boat horizontally.

If we want to determine the location of boat on ground, we need velocity of boat in the ground frame \(=\vec{v}_{b}\) .

Following our fundamental relative velocity equation \(\vec{v}_{br} = \vec{v}_b - \vec{v}_r\), resolving for the actual ground velocity yields a vector addition: \[ \vec{v}_b = \vec{v}_{br} + \vec{v}_r \]

The boat structurally possesses entirely autonomous physical control over its orientation relative to the water (\(\vec{v}_{br}\)), but it has absolutely zero control over the unyielding downstream flow of the river (\(\vec{v}_r\)). The resultant path is their geometric sum.

For building a generic formula structure, we take velocity of river along X axis \(=\vec{v}_{r} = v_{\text{r}}\hat i\) . And we take the width of the river \(=\vec{s}_{y} = L\hat j\) .

Time and Drift

We need to determine the time taken by the boat to cross the river \(T\). This is same as saying that the displacement along Y axis in the ground frame is given as \(L\) - the width of the river.

Thus, we need the Y component of the velocity of boat in ground frame.

Let:

\[ \vec{v}_{br} = v_{br/x}\hat i + v_{br/y}\hat j \]

Going by

\[ \vec{v}_b = \vec{v}_{br} + \vec{v}_r = (v_{br/x} + v_r)\hat i + v_{br/y}\hat j \]

We get,

\[ v_{b/x} = (v_{br/x} + v_r) \qquad v_{b/y} = v_{br/y} \]

For time to cross the river \(T\) , we evaluate motion in Y axis:

\[ T = \frac{s_y}{v_y} = \frac{L}{v_{b/y}} = \frac{L}{v_{br/y}} \]

Drift \(D\) is give by the horizontal distance from the starting point.

\[ D = v_{b/x}T = v_{b/x}\frac{L}{v_{b/y}} = (v_{br/x} + v_r)\frac{L}{v_{br/y}} \]

Crossing the River in Shortest Time

The boat mathematically needs to traverse a vertical displacement distance \(d\). As established, horizontal flow cannot physically influence the vertical state.

Therefore, to cross in the absolute shortest time \(T_{min}\), the boat must maximize its vertical velocity component. This strictly means pointing the nose of the boat exactly structurally perpendicular straight across the river flow.

Shortest Time Condition:
  • Set the heading angle perpendicular: \(\theta = 90^\circ\) relative to current.
  • The vertical velocity is fully effectively \(v_b\).
  • \[ T_{min} = \frac{d}{v_b} \]

However, because it did not actively counteract the river, upon reaching the opposite bank, the boat will have systematically drifted horizontally permanently downstream by a distance \(X\): \[ X = v_r \times T_{min} = \frac{v_r d}{v_b} \]

Crossing the River along Shortest Path (Zero Drift)

If the boat intends to firmly land exactly linearly directly opposite its specific starting point (zero macroscopic drift, \(X=0\)), its resultant ground velocity vector \(\vec{v}_b\) must physically point structurally exclusively perpendicularly straight across without any horizontal component.

To ruthlessly cancel the river's \(v_r\) pushing it right, the boat must intelligently point its nose upstream at some optimal deflection structural angle \(\theta\).

Zero Drift Derivation:

The horizontal component of the boat's relative velocity must exactly equal and stubbornly structurally dynamically cancel the river velocity: \[ v_b\sin\theta = v_r \implies \sin\theta = \frac{v_r}{v_b} \] From this, we construct the angle \(\theta\).

The remaining active vertical velocity efficiently driving the boat relentlessly smoothly smoothly effectively cleanly strongly directly across the exact width \(d\) is exclusively mechanically logically structurally \(v_b\cos\theta = \sqrt{v_b^2 - v_r^2}\).

The total crossing time becomes: \[ T = \frac{d}{\sqrt{v_b^2 - v_r^2}} \]

Note: This is strictly physically geometrically physically possible only if the boat is strictly faster mathematically powerfully decisively structurally vigorously firmly faster cleanly dependably expertly robustly structurally specifically explicitly reliably efficiently than the river (\(v_b > v_r\)).
