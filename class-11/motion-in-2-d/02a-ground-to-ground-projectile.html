Ground-to-Ground Projectile

Analyzing the standard symmetric trajectory over a level plane.

Time of Flight and Maximum Height

Consider a projectile launched from the level ground \((y=0)\) with an initial velocity \(u\) at an angle \(\theta\) above the horizontal. It traces a parabolic path and eventually impacts the ground again.

Time of Flight (\(T\)):

The total time the projectile remains airborne can be found by setting the final vertical displacement \(y = 0\) in the fundamental kinematic equation: \[ y = u_y t + \frac{1}{2}a_y t^2 \] \[ 0 = (u\sin\theta)T - \frac{1}{2}g T^2 \] Assuming \(T \neq 0\) (which represents the launch moment), we solve for the impact time: \[ T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g} \]

Maximum Height (\(H\)):

At the absolute peak of its trajectory, the vertical component of the projectile's velocity momentarily becomes zero (\(v_y = 0\)). Using the velocity-displacement kinematic equation: \[ v_y^2 = u_y^2 + 2a_y H \] \[ 0 = (u\sin\theta)^2 - 2g H \] \[ H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g} \]

Horizontal Range and Maximum Range Conditions

The Horizontal Range (\(R\)) is the total horizontal distance covered by the projectile during its complete time of flight \(T\).

Range Formula (\(R\)):

Since there is zero acceleration horizontally (\(a_x = 0\)), the horizontal distance is simply velocity multiplied by time: \[ R = u_x \times T = (u\cos\theta) \times \left(\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\right) \] Using the trigonometric identity \(2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \sin(2\theta)\), we elegantly obtain: \[ R = \frac{u^2\sin(2\theta)}{g} \]

Maximum Range Condition: For a fixed launch speed \(u\), the range \(R\) strictly depends on the angle \(\theta\). The range is mathematically maximized when \(\sin(2\theta)\) attains its maximum value of \(1\).

This explicitly occurs when \(2\theta = 90^\circ\), or unequivocally when exactly: \[ \theta = 45^\circ \] At this optimal launch angle, the absolute maximum horizontal range evaluates to: \[ R_{max} = \frac{u^2}{g} \]
Complementary Launch Angles: Note that \(\sin(2\theta) = \sin(180^\circ - 2\theta) = \sin(2(90^\circ - \theta))\). This mathematical symmetry proves that two distinct complementary launch angles, \(\theta\) and \((90^\circ - \theta)\), will fundamentally produce the identically exact same horizontal range \(R\), despite tracing dramatically different height trajectories.
