Projectile Motion: Oblique Projection

Motion under the constant influence of gravity.

1. Assumptions

  • No air resistance.
  • Acceleration due to gravity \(g\) is constant in magnitude and direction.
  • The Earth's curvature and rotation are neglected.

2. Component Analysis

For a projectile fired with initial velocity \(u\) at an angle \(\theta\):

  • Horizontal: \(u_x = u\cos\theta, \quad a_x = 0\) (Constant velocity)
  • Vertical: \(u_y = u\sin\theta, \quad a_y = -g\) (Uniform acceleration)

3. Key Parameters

Time of Flight (\(T\)): Total time the projectile remains in the air.

\[ T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g} \]

Maximum Height (\(H\)): The highest vertical displacement reached.

\[ H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g} \]

Horizontal Range (\(R\)): Total horizontal displacement.

\[ R = \frac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g} \]
Max range is achieved at \(\theta = 45^\circ\). For a given speed, two angles \(\theta\) and \((90^\circ - \theta)\) yield the same range.
