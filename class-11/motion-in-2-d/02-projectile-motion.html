Projectile Motion

The most celebrated classical example of independent two-dimensional kinematics under constant acceleration.

Principle of Physical Independence of Motions

When an object is thrown into the air, its subsequent unpowered flight under the sole influence of gravity (ignoring air resistance) is called Projectile Motion. The object mathematically traces a distinct, beautifully symmetric curve traversing both the horizontal and vertical spatial dimensions.

Galileo was the first to mathematically codify the secret to solving projectile motion: Physical Independence. The horizontal progression of the projectile is absolutely completely mechanically blind to its vertical vertical rise and fall.

A projectile is merely two independent 1D motions happening simultaneously on the exact same stopwatch:
1. A simple, perfectly uniform horizontal motion (constant velocity).
2. A standard, 1D vertical free-fall motion (constant acceleration \(g\)).

Because gravity (\(\vec{g}\)) points strictly vertically downward, it definitively possesses absolutely zero horizontal geometric component (\(g\cos 90^\circ = 0\)). Therefore, throughout the entirety of the flight, the horizontal mathematical acceleration \(a_x\) is rigidly exactly zero.

Equation of Trajectory

To visualize the exact physical path the projectile traces in the actual \((x,y)\) sky, we must derive its Equation of Trajectory—a direct mathematical relationship seamlessly connecting the \(y\) coordinate to the \(x\) coordinate, effectively eliminating the temporal parameter \(t\).

Resolution of Launch Velocity:

  • Vertical: \(u_y = u\sin\theta\) (subject to \(a_y = -g\))

  1. Horizontal constraint: \( x(t) = (u\cos\theta)t \)
