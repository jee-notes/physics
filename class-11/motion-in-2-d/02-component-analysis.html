Component Resolution and Independence

Simplifying 2D motion into two independent 1D motions.

1. Resolving Vectors

Any vector \(\vec{A}\) in a plane can be broken down into two perpendicular components. If the vector makes an angle \(\theta\) with the positive X-axis:

\[ A_x = A \cos\theta, \quad A_y = A \sin\theta \]

2. Principle of Physical Independence

The most critical concept in 2D kinematics is that motion along the X-axis and motion along the Y-axis are mutually independent. A force or acceleration in the Y-direction does not change the velocity component in the X-direction.

This is why we can use two sets of 1D kinematic equations simultaneously to solve one 2D problem.

3. Kinematic Equations in Component Form

For motion with constant acceleration \(\vec{a} = a_x\hat{i} + a_y\hat{j}\):

X-axis:
\(v_x = u_x + a_x t\)
\(x = u_x t + \frac{1}{2}a_x t^2\)
\(v_x^2 = u_x^2 + 2a_x x\)
Y-axis:
\(v_y = u_y + a_y t\)
\(y = u_y t + \frac{1}{2}a_y t^2\)
\(v_y^2 = u_y^2 + 2a_y y\)
