Angle between Vectors

Theory and problems on the angle between vectors.

Angle between Vectors

The angle between two vectors is a measure of the difference in their directions.

When a vector is represented geometrically as an arrow or a ray, its magnitude is represented by its length.

Changing the length of a vector while keeping its orientation unchanged changes only its magnitude; its direction remains the same.

On the other hand, changing the direction of a vector involves an angular rotation.

Therefore, the angle between two vectors measures their angular separation and hence their directional difference.

Change in direction is measured by angular rotation.
Just as in navigation, we may say that a ship turned \(20^\circ\) eastward to reach the nearest harbour.

Which Angle?

Two non-collinear vectors are always co-planar. In their common plane, their two directions divide a complete \(360^\circ\) rotation into two angles:

\[ \theta \qquad\text{and}\qquad 360^\circ-\theta \]

For the angle between vectors, we do not distinguish between clockwise and anti-clockwise rotation.

By convention, we take the smaller of the two angles as the angle between the vectors.

Thus, if \(\theta\) is the angle between two vectors \(\vec A\) and \(\vec B\), then

\[ \boxed{0^\circ\le\theta\le180^\circ} \]

What does Angle Represent?

If \(\theta\) is the angle between two vectors \(\vec A\) and \(\vec B\), then

\[ 0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ \]

Thus, \(\theta\) represents the minimum angular rotation required to align \(\vec A\) with \(\vec B\), or \(\vec B\) with \(\vec A\).

Therefore, when representing the angle between two vectors in a diagram, the vectors should be translated so that either their tails coincide or their heads coincide. The smaller angle at their common point is then the angle between the vectors.
