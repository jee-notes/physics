Kinematics of 2D Motion

Establishing the fundamental vector formulations for spatial position, velocity, and acceleration.

Position, Displacement, and Velocity Vectors

Unlike 1D motion where position is given merely by an \(x\)-coordinate, the exact location of a particle in a 2D plane perfectly requires two coordinates \((x, y)\).

The Position Vector (\(\vec{r}\)):

A vector drawn directly from the mathematically chosen origin \((0,0)\) to the particle's present location \(P(x,y)\). It rigorously structurally pinpoints where the particle is inside the plane.

\[ \vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} \] The absolute distance from the origin is the magnitude: \(|\vec{r}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\).

When the particle dynamically physically moves from point \(P_1(x_1, y_1)\) to \(P_2(x_2, y_2)\), its geometric shift is described precisely by the Displacement Vector (\(\Delta\vec{r}\)):

\[ \Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1 = (x_2 - x_1)\hat{i} + (y_2 - y_1)\hat{j} \] \[ \Delta\vec{r} = \Delta x\hat{i} + \Delta y\hat{j} \]

By structurally evaluating the rate at which this displacement vector fundamentally changes over time, we define the Instantaneous Velocity Vector (\(\vec{v}\)):

\[ \vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}(x\hat{i} + y\hat{j}) = \frac{dx}{dt}\hat{i} + \frac{dy}{dt}\hat{j} \] \[ \vec{v} = v_x\hat{i} + v_y\hat{j} \]
Crucially, the instantaneous velocity vector is always perfectly geometrically tangent to the particle's actual curved path (trajectory) at any given exact specific instant.

Average and Instantaneous Acceleration in a Plane

If the velocity specifically visibly physically continuously alters either its raw scalar magnitude (speed) AND/OR its geometric vectorial spatial direction, the particle structurally unmistakably undergoes Acceleration.

The Average Acceleration \(\vec{a}_{avg}\) over a time interval \(\Delta t\) is essentially simply:

\[ \vec{a}_{avg} = \frac{\Delta\vec{v}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{v}_2 - \vec{v}_1}{t_2 - t_1} \]

By rigorously analytically aggressively shrinking the time interval \(\Delta t \rightarrow 0\), we logically meticulously precisely elegantly fundamentally isolate the exact Instantaneous Acceleration (\(\vec{a}\)):

\[ \vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \frac{dv_x}{dt}\hat{i} + \frac{dv_y}{dt}\hat{j} \] \[ \vec{a} = a_x\hat{i} + a_y\hat{j} \]

Where \(a_x = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}\) and \(a_y = \frac{d^2y}{dt^2}\).

Unlike velocity, the acceleration vector is famously not strictly tangent to the curved trajectory. It fundamentally dynamically explicitly typically usually distinctly points stubbornly generally rigidly decidedly inwardly directly toward the concave side of the curve, representing the physical force violently visibly structurally yanking the particle off its straight-line inertia path.
