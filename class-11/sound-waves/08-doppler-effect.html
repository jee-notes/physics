Doppler Effect in Sound Waves

Apparent frequency shifts, wavefront compression geometry, and master relative motion formulations.

Physical Principle of the Doppler Effect in Sound

The Doppler Effect in acoustics is the phenomenon wherein the apparent frequency of sound detected by an observer differs from the actual natural frequency emitted by the source whenever there is relative motion between the source, the observer, or the propagating medium.

  • When the distance between the source and observer is decreasing (approaching each other), the observed frequency is higher than the source frequency (\( f' > f_0 \)), producing a higher acoustic pitch.
  • When the distance between the source and observer is increasing (receding from each other), the observed frequency is lower than the source frequency (\( f' < f_0 \)), producing a lower acoustic pitch.

Wavefront Geometry and Apparent Wavelength Shift

The fundamental physical distinction between a moving source and a moving observer is critical in classical wave mechanics:

1. Moving Source: Alters the Actual Wavelength in the Medium

When the source moves forward at speed \( v_s \), each successive spherical wavefront is emitted from a new forward position.

  • Ahead of the source: Wavefronts are compressed together: \[ \lambda' = \frac{v - v_s}{f_0} \]
  • Behind the source: Wavefronts are stretched farther apart: \[ \lambda'' = \frac{v + v_s}{f_0} \]

2. Moving Observer: Alters the Rate of Intercepting Wavefronts

When the observer moves with velocity \( v_o \) through a stationary medium, the wavelength \( \lambda \) in the air remains completely unchanged. However, the relative speed at which the observer intercepts the oncoming wavefronts changes to \( v_{\text{rel}} = v \pm v_o \).

Master Doppler Formula with Medium Drift

Combining the independent effects of source velocity \( v_s \), observer velocity \( v_o \), and medium wind velocity \( w \) along the line of sight yields the Master Doppler Formula:

Master Doppler Formula in Acoustics: \[ f' = f_0 \left( \frac{v \pm v_o \pm w}{v \mp v_s \pm w} \right) \]
Golden Sign Convention Rule:
  1. Draw a straight vector pointing directly from the Source to the Observer (\( S \to O \)).
  2. Treat the direction from Source to Observer as POSITIVE for sound propagation speed \( v \).
  3. Any velocity vector (\( v_s \), \( v_o \), or \( w \)) pointing in the direction of \( S \to O \) is taken with a positive sign; pointing opposite to \( S \to O \) is taken with a negative sign.
