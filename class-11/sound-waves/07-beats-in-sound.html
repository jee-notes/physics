Beats in Sound Waves & Tuning Fork Calibration

Acoustic interference in the time domain, envelope modulation derivation, and tuning fork calibration techniques.

Physical Mechanism of Acoustic Beats

When two sound waves having nearly equal frequencies (\( f_1 \approx f_2 \)) and traveling in the same direction superpose at a point, they produce a periodic rise and fall in the resultant acoustic intensity. This phenomenon is known as Acoustic Beats.

  • Waxing: Moment when the two waves are temporarily in phase, producing constructive interference and a peak in loudness.
  • Waning: Moment when the two waves become \( 180^\circ \) out of phase, producing destructive interference and silence.
  • Beat Period (\( T_b \)): The time elapsed between two successive maxima (or two successive minima).
  • Beat Frequency (\( f_b \)): The number of intensity peaks heard per second: \( f_b = 1/T_b \).

Mathematical Derivation of Beat Frequency fb = |f1 - f2|

Consider a point in space (take \( x = 0 \)) where two harmonic sound waves of equal amplitude \( A \) and slightly different angular frequencies \( \omega_1 = 2\pi f_1 \) and \( \omega_2 = 2\pi f_2 \) arrive:

\[ s_1(t) = A \sin(\omega_1 t) \] \[ s_2(t) = A \sin(\omega_2 t) \]

By the principle of linear superposition:

\[ s_{\text{net}}(t) = s_1 + s_2 = A \left[ \sin(\omega_1 t) + \sin(\omega_2 t) \right] \]

Applying the trigonometric identity \( \sin C + \sin D = 2\cos\left(\frac{C-D}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{C+D}{2}\right) \):

Resultant Beat Wave Equation: \[ s_{\text{net}}(t) = \left[ 2A \cos\left( \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{2} t \right) \right] \sin\left( \frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2} t \right) = A_{\text{mod}}(t) \sin(\omega_{\text{avg}} t) \]

where:

  • \( A_{\text{mod}}(t) = \left| 2A \cos\left( \pi (f_1 - f_2) t \right) \right| \): Slowly varying Amplitude Envelope.
  • \( \omega_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2} \): Rapid carrier oscillation frequency.

Time Envelope of Amplitude and Intensity Waxing/Waning

Since intensity is proportional to the square of the amplitude (\( I \propto A_{\text{mod}}^2 \)):

1. Condition for Intensity Maxima (Waxing)

\[ \cos\left( \pi (f_1 - f_2) t \right) = \pm 1 \implies \pi (f_1 - f_2) t = n\pi \implies t_n = \frac{n}{|f_1 - f_2|} \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, \dots) \]

The time interval between consecutive maxima is:

\[ T_b = t_{n+1} - t_n = \frac{1}{|f_1 - f_2|} \]

2. Condition for Intensity Minima (Waning)

\[ \cos\left( \pi (f_1 - f_2) t \right) = 0 \implies \pi (f_1 - f_2) t = (2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{2} \implies t_n' = \frac{2n + 1}{2|f_1 - f_2|} \]
Beat Frequency Formula: \[ f_b = \frac{1}{T_b} = |f_1 - f_2| \quad [\text{beats per second}] \]
Persistence of Hearing Limit: The human auditory system has an acoustic persistence time of approximately \( 0.1 \text{ seconds} \). For distinct beats to be counted by the human ear, the beat frequency must satisfy: \[ f_b \le 10 \text{ beats/second} \implies |f_1 - f_2| \le 10 \text{ Hz} \] If \( f_b > 10 \text{ Hz} \), the ear perceives a continuous harsh buzzing tone rather than discrete beats.

Determination of Unknown Tuning Fork Frequency (Waxing and Filing)

Suppose an unknown tuning fork \( X \) of frequency \( f_x \) produces \( f_b \) beats per second when sounded with a standard calibrated fork \( A \) of known frequency \( f_A \).

The unknown frequency must be:

\[ f_x = f_A + f_b \quad \text{or} \quad f_x = f_A - f_b \]

To resolve this ambiguity, we modify the mass of fork \( X \) (or fork \( A \)):

Action Performed Effect on Inertia / Mass Effect on Frequency
Loading a fork with Wax Increases mass (\( m \uparrow \)) Frequency Decreases (\( f \downarrow \))
Filing the prongs of a fork Decreases mass (\( m \downarrow \)) Frequency Increases (\( f \uparrow \))

Solved JEE Problem: Finding Unknown Frequency

Problem: A tuning fork \( A \) of frequency \( 256 \text{ Hz} \) produces \( 4 \text{ beats/second} \) with an unknown tuning fork \( B \). When the prongs of \( B \) are loaded with a little wax, the beat frequency increases to \( 6 \text{ beats/second} \). What was the original frequency of tuning fork \( B \)?

Solution:

  1. Possible initial values for \( f_B \): \[ f_B = 256 + 4 = 260 \text{ Hz} \quad \text{or} \quad f_B = 256 - 4 = 252 \text{ Hz} \]
  2. Loading \( B \) with wax causes its frequency to decrease (\( f_B' < f_B \)).
  3. Let us test both possibilities:
    • If \( f_B = 260 \text{ Hz} \): When \( f_B \) decreases (e.g., to \( 258 \text{ Hz} \)), the beat frequency \( |f_B' - 256| = |258 - 256| = 2 \text{ Hz} \) → Beat frequency decreases. (Contradicts problem statement).
    • If \( f_B = 252 \text{ Hz} \): When \( f_B \) decreases (e.g., to \( 250 \text{ Hz} \)), the beat frequency \( |f_B' - 256| = |250 - 256| = 6 \text{ Hz} \) → Beat frequency increases! (Matches problem statement).
  4. Conclusion: The original frequency of fork \( B \) was \( \mathbf{252 \text{ Hz}} \).
