Collinear Doppler Motion: Source & Observer

Systematic mathematical analysis of standard one-dimensional relative motion configurations.

Moving Source and Stationary Observer

Consider a source emitting sound of frequency \( f_0 \) and wavelength \( \lambda_0 = v / f_0 \), observed by a stationary observer (\( v_o = 0 \)).

1. Source Moving Toward Stationary Observer (Approaching)

In time \( t = T_0 = 1/f_0 \), the source moves forward by a distance \( v_s T_0 \). The wavelength in the direction of motion contracts:

\[ \lambda' = \lambda_0 - v_s T_0 = \frac{v - v_s}{f_0} \]

The apparent frequency detected by the observer is:

\[ f' = \frac{v}{\lambda'} = f_0 \left( \frac{v}{v - v_s} \right) \quad (f' > f_0) \]

2. Source Moving Away from Stationary Observer (Receding)

The wavelength expands: \( \lambda'' = \frac{v + v_s}{f_0} \).

\[ f'' = \frac{v}{\lambda''} = f_0 \left( \frac{v}{v + v_s} \right) \quad (f'' < f_0) \]

Stationary Source and Moving Observer

When the source is at rest (\( v_s = 0 \)), the wavelength in the medium remains completely unchanged at \( \lambda_0 = v / f_0 \).

1. Observer Moving Toward Stationary Source (Approaching)

The relative speed with which the observer encounters the oncoming wavefronts is \( v_{\text{rel}} = v + v_o \):

\[ f' = \frac{v_{\text{rel}}}{\lambda_0} = \frac{v + v_o}{v / f_0} = f_0 \left( \frac{v + v_o}{v} \right) \quad (f' > f_0) \]

2. Observer Moving Away from Stationary Source (Receding)

The relative speed is \( v_{\text{rel}} = v - v_o \):

\[ f'' = \frac{v_{\text{rel}}}{\lambda_0} = f_0 \left( \frac{v - v_o}{v} \right) \quad (f'' < f_0) \]
Asymmetry of Classical Doppler Effect: Notice that moving the source toward the observer produces \( f' = f_0 \left(\frac{v}{v - u}\right) \), whereas moving the observer toward the source with the same speed \( u \) produces \( f'' = f_0 \left(\frac{v + u}{v}\right) \).
Since \( \frac{v}{v - u} > \frac{v + u}{v} \), the frequency shift is greater when the source moves than when the observer moves with the identical speed relative to the medium!

Simultaneous Collinear Motion of Source, Observer, and Medium

Physical Configuration Relative Motion Apparent Frequency Formula
Both Source & Observer approaching each other Distance decreasing rapidly \[ f' = f_0 \left( \frac{v + v_o}{v - v_s} \right) \]
Both Source & Observer receding from each other Distance increasing rapidly \[ f' = f_0 \left( \frac{v - v_o}{v + v_s} \right) \]
Source chasing Observer (\( v_s \) and \( v_o \) in same direction) Source moves toward Observer, Observer moves away \[ f' = f_0 \left( \frac{v - v_o}{v - v_s} \right) \]
Observer chasing Source Observer moves toward Source, Source moves away \[ f' = f_0 \left( \frac{v + v_o}{v + v_s} \right) \]

Low-Speed Approximation (\( v_s, v_o \ll v \))

When the relative speed \( v_{\text{rel}} \) between source and observer is much smaller than the speed of sound:

Fractional Frequency Shift: \[ \frac{\Delta f}{f_0} = \frac{f' - f_0}{f_0} \approx \pm \frac{v_{\text{rel}}}{v} \]

Solved JEE Problem: Crossing Whistle Train

Problem: A train moving at \( v_s = 36 \text{ km/h} = 10 \text{ m/s} \) blows a whistle of frequency \( f_0 = 660 \text{ Hz} \) as it passes a stationary observer on a platform. If the speed of sound in air is \( v = 340 \text{ m/s} \), calculate:
(a) The frequency heard as the train approaches,
(b) The frequency heard as the train recedes,
(c) The percentage change in frequency heard by the observer.

Solution:

  • (a) Approaching: \[ f_1 = f_0 \left( \frac{v}{v - v_s} \right) = 660 \left( \frac{340}{340 - 10} \right) = 660 \left( \frac{340}{330} \right) = 680 \text{ Hz} \]
  • (b) Receding: \[ f_2 = f_0 \left( \frac{v}{v + v_s} \right) = 660 \left( \frac{340}{340 + 10} \right) = 660 \left( \frac{340}{350} \right) \approx 641.14 \text{ Hz} \]
  • (c) Percentage Drop across Crossing: \[ \Delta f = f_1 - f_2 = 680 - 641.14 = 38.86 \text{ Hz} \] \[ \% \text{ change} = \frac{38.86}{680} \times 100\% \approx 5.71\% \]
