Displacement & Pressure Wave Formulations

Mathematical description of longitudinal displacement, bulk modulus derivation of excess pressure, and phase relations.

Displacement Wave Function s(x,t)

Consider a one-dimensional harmonic sound wave propagating along the \( +x \)-direction through an elastic medium. Let \( s(x,t) \) represent the longitudinal displacement of a medium particle from its undisturbed equilibrium position \( x \) at time \( t \):

Harmonic Displacement Wave Equation: \[ s(x, t) = s_0 \sin(kx - \omega t + \phi) \]

where:

  • \( s_0 \): Displacement amplitude (maximum longitudinal displacement of particles) \([\text{m}]\), Dimension: \([M^0 L^1 T^0]\).
  • \( k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \): Angular wave number \([\text{rad/m}]\).
  • \( \omega = 2\pi f \): Angular frequency \([\text{rad/s}]\).
  • \( \phi \): Initial phase constant \([\text{rad}]\).

Derivation of Excess Pressure Wave Delta P(x,t)

To derive how local fluid pressure varies with particle displacement, consider a small cylindrical element of fluid of cross-sectional area \( A \), bounded between equilibrium coordinates \( x \) and \( x + \Delta x \).

The equilibrium volume of this fluid slice is:

\[ V = A \Delta x \]

When the wave passes, the left face at \( x \) is displaced by \( s(x, t) \), and the right face at \( x + \Delta x \) is displaced by \( s(x + \Delta x, t) \approx s(x, t) + \frac{\partial s}{\partial x}\Delta x \).

The change in volume \( \Delta V \) of this fluid element is:

\[ \Delta V = A \left[ (x + \Delta x + s(x + \Delta x)) - (x + s(x)) \right] - A \Delta x = A \left( \frac{\partial s}{\partial x} \right) \Delta x \]

The fractional volume change (volume strain) is:

\[ \frac{\Delta V}{V} = \frac{A \left( \frac{\partial s}{\partial x} \right) \Delta x}{A \Delta x} = \frac{\partial s}{\partial x} \]

From the definition of the Bulk Modulus (\( B \)) of the fluid:

\[ B = -\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V / V} \implies \Delta P(x,t) = -B \left( \frac{\Delta V}{V} \right) = -B \left( \frac{\partial s}{\partial x} \right) \]

Differentiating \( s(x,t) = s_0 \sin(kx - \omega t) \) with respect to \( x \):

\[ \frac{\partial s}{\partial x} = k s_0 \cos(kx - \omega t) \]

Substituting back into the excess pressure equation:

Excess Pressure Wave Equation: \[ \Delta P(x, t) = -B k s_0 \cos(kx - \omega t) = \Delta P_0 \sin\left( kx - \omega t + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) \]

Pressure Amplitude Delta P0 = B k s0 = rho v omega s0

The maximum fluctuation in pressure above or below atmospheric equilibrium is called the Pressure Amplitude (\( \Delta P_0 \)):

\[ \Delta P_0 = B k s_0 \]

Since the speed of sound in the fluid is \( v = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}} \implies B = \rho v^2 \), and wave number \( k = \frac{\omega}{v} \):

\[ \Delta P_0 = (\rho v^2) \left( \frac{\omega}{v} \right) s_0 = \rho v \omega s_0 = 2\pi \rho v f s_0 \]
Quantity Formula SI Unit Dimensional Formula
Displacement Amplitude (\( s_0 \)) \( s_0 = \frac{\Delta P_0}{\rho v \omega} \) \( \text{m} \) \( [M^0 L^1 T^0] \)
Pressure Amplitude (\( \Delta P_0 \)) \( \Delta P_0 = \rho v \omega s_0 = B k s_0 \) \( \text{N/m}^2 = \text{Pa} \) \( [M^1 L^{-1} T^{-2}] \)
Bulk Modulus (\( B \)) \( B = \rho v^2 \) \( \text{Pa} \) \( [M^1 L^{-1} T^{-2}] \)
Medium Density (\( \rho \)) \( \rho = \text{mass / volume} \) \( \text{kg/m}^3 \) \( [M^1 L^{-3} T^0] \)

Phase Relationship: Displacement Wave vs Pressure Wave

Comparing the displacement wave and the excess pressure wave:

\[ s(x, t) = s_0 \sin(kx - \omega t) \] \[ \Delta P(x, t) = \Delta P_0 \cos(kx - \omega t) = \Delta P_0 \sin\left( kx - \omega t + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) \]
Critical Takeaway: The pressure wave and displacement wave are out of phase by \( 90^\circ \) (\( \pi / 2 \) radians):
  • When particle displacement is zero (\( s = 0 \)), the pressure variation is maximum (\( \Delta P = \pm \Delta P_0 \)) → Compressions and Rarefactions!
  • When particle displacement is maximum (\( s = \pm s_0 \)), the pressure variation is zero (\( \Delta P = 0 \), ambient atmospheric pressure).

Density Fluctuation Wave Equation

Since mass is conserved in any differential fluid element (\( m = \rho V = \text{constant} \)):

\[ d(\rho V) = 0 \implies \rho \Delta V + V \Delta \rho = 0 \implies \frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho_0} = -\frac{\Delta V}{V} = -\frac{\partial s}{\partial x} \]

Multiplying by equilibrium density \( \rho_0 \):

Density Fluctuation Wave: \[ \Delta \rho(x, t) = -\rho_0 \left( \frac{\partial s}{\partial x} \right) = \Delta \rho_0 \cos(kx - \omega t) \] \[ \Delta \rho_0 = \rho_0 k s_0 = \frac{\Delta P_0}{v^2} \]

The density variation is in phase with the pressure wave: regions of maximum compression correspond directly to regions of maximum density.
