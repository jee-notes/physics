Environmental Factors Affecting Speed of Sound

Impact of pressure, absolute temperature, relative humidity, wind drift, and molecular composition.

Effect of Pressure at Constant Temperature

From Laplace's formula, the speed of sound is given by \( v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} \).

According to Boyle's Law, at a constant temperature \( T \), the pressure of an ideal gas is directly proportional to its density:

\[ \frac{P}{\rho} = \frac{R T}{M} = \text{Constant} \]

If atmospheric pressure is doubled or halved while the temperature remains fixed, the gas density \( \rho \) adjusts proportionally by the exact same factor, leaving the ratio \( P/\rho \) unchanged.

Effect of Pressure: At constant temperature, change in pressure has NO effect on the speed of sound in a gas.

Effect of Temperature and Temperature Coefficient

For a given gas of fixed molar mass \( M \) and adiabatic index \( \gamma \), the speed of sound is directly proportional to the square root of the absolute temperature (\( T \)) in Kelvin:

\[ v \propto \sqrt{T} \implies \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\frac{T_1}{T_2}} \]

Temperature Coefficient of Sound Speed

Let \( v_0 \) be the speed of sound at \( 0^\circ\text{C} \) (\( 273.15 \text{ K} \)), and \( v_t \) be the speed at temperature \( t^\circ\text{C} \) (\( 273.15 + t \text{ K} \)):

\[ \frac{v_t}{v_0} = \sqrt{\frac{273.15 + t}{273.15}} = \left( 1 + \frac{t}{273.15} \right)^{1/2} \]

Using the binomial expansion for \( t \ll 273.15 \):

\[ v_t \approx v_0 \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2}\frac{t}{273.15} \right) = v_0 + \left( \frac{v_0}{546.3} \right) t \]

Substituting \( v_0 \approx 332 \text{ m/s} \):

Linear Temperature Approximation: \[ v_t \approx v_0 + 0.61 \, t \quad (\text{where } t \text{ is in } ^\circ\text{C}) \]
The speed of sound in air increases by approximately \( 0.61 \text{ m/s} \) for every \( 1^\circ\text{C} \) rise in temperature.

Effect of Humidity (Moist Air vs Dry Air)

Water vapor has a molar mass of \( M_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \approx 18 \text{ g/mol} \), whereas dry air (predominantly \( \text{N}_2 \) and \( \text{O}_2 \)) has an average effective molar mass of \( M_{\text{dry}} \approx 28.8 \text{ g/mol} \).

When humidity increases, lighter water vapor molecules displace heavier nitrogen and oxygen molecules, reducing the effective average density of the air mixture:

\[ \rho_{\text{moist}} < \rho_{\text{dry}} \]

Since \( v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} \):

\[ v_{\text{moist}} > v_{\text{dry}} \]
Sound travels faster in humid (moist) air than in dry air at the same temperature and pressure. That is why sounds are heard more clearly and travel faster on humid days or over water surfaces.

Effect of Wind Velocity and Medium Drift

Because sound waves propagate through the medium, any bulk translational motion of the medium (wind) carries the sound wavefronts with it.

If wind blows with speed \( w \) at an angle \( \theta \) relative to the direction of sound propagation:

Effective Speed of Sound: \[ v_{\text{eff}} = v + w \cos\theta \]
  • Sound in the direction of wind (\( \theta = 0^\circ \)): \( v_{\text{eff}} = v + w \) (Speed increases).
  • Sound against the wind (\( \theta = 180^\circ \)): \( v_{\text{eff}} = v - w \) (Speed decreases).
  • Sound perpendicular to wind (\( \theta = 90^\circ \)): \( v_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{v^2 - w^2} \).

Effect of Gas Composition and Molecular Weight

From \( v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}} \), at identical temperatures:

\[ v \propto \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{M}} \]

Solved JEE Problem: Comparison of Sound Speeds

Problem: Compare the speed of sound in Hydrogen gas (\( \text{H}_2 \), \( M = 2 \text{ g/mol} \), \( \gamma = 7/5 \)) and Helium gas (\( \text{He} \), \( M = 4 \text{ g/mol} \), \( \gamma = 5/3 \)) at the same temperature.

Solution:

\[ \frac{v_{\text{H}_2}}{v_{\text{He}}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{\text{H}_2} / M_{\text{H}_2}}{\gamma_{\text{He}} / M_{\text{He}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{(7/5) / 2}{(5/3) / 4}} = \sqrt{\frac{7/10}{5/12}} = \sqrt{\frac{7 \times 12}{10 \times 5}} = \sqrt{\frac{84}{50}} = \sqrt{\frac{42}{25}} = \frac{\sqrt{42}}{5} \approx 1.296 \]

Sound travels approximately \( 30\% \) faster in Hydrogen than in Helium at the same temperature due to Hydrogen's lower molecular weight.
