Resonance Column Apparatus & Kundt's Tube

Laboratory acoustic resonance methods, exact speed of sound determination, and Kundt's dust tube.

Resonance Tube Apparatus and Experimental Principle

The Resonance Column Apparatus is a classic laboratory instrument used to determine the speed of sound in air and measure the end correction of a pipe.

Experimental Setup: A long vertical glass tube is connected to a water reservoir. The water surface acts as a perfectly rigid closed end (Displacement Node), while the open top rim acts as an open boundary (Displacement Antinode with end correction \( e \)).

A vibrating tuning fork of known frequency \( f \) is held above the open end. By lowering the water level, the length of the air column is increased until a sharp booming sound (acoustic resonance) is detected.

Determination of Speed of Sound in Air: v = 2 f (l2 - l1)

Let \( l_1 \) be the length of the air column for the first resonance (fundamental mode), and \( l_2 \) be the length for the second resonance (first overtone):

\[ l_1 + e = \frac{\lambda}{4} \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)} \] \[ l_2 + e = \frac{3\lambda}{4} \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)} \]

Subtracting Equation (1) from Equation (2):

\[ (l_2 + e) - (l_1 + e) = \frac{3\lambda}{4} - \frac{\lambda}{4} \implies l_2 - l_1 = \frac{\lambda}{2} \] \[ \lambda = 2(l_2 - l_1) \]

Since \( v = f \lambda \), the speed of sound is given by:

Speed of Sound from Resonance Column: \[ v = 2 f (l_2 - l_1) \]
Because the end correction \( e \) cancels out completely when subtracting the two lengths, this method provides an exact, error-free measurement of the speed of sound in air without requiring prior knowledge of the tube radius.

Experimental Calculation of End Correction e = (l2 - 3 l1) / 2

Multiplying Equation (1) by 3:

\[ 3 l_1 + 3e = \frac{3\lambda}{4} \]

Equating this with Equation (2):

\[ 3 l_1 + 3e = l_2 + e \implies 2e = l_2 - 3 l_1 \]
Experimental End Correction: \[ e = \frac{l_2 - 3 l_1}{2} \]

In an ideal tube with zero end correction (\( e = 0 \)), the second resonance occurs at exactly \( l_2 = 3 l_1 \). In real physical tubes, \( l_2 > 3 l_1 \).

Kundt's Tube Experiment for Rods and Gases

Kundt's Tube is an apparatus designed to measure the speed of sound in various gases as well as solid metal rods.

Working Principle:

  1. A metal rod of length \( L \) clamped at its center is stroked with a rosined cloth, setting up longitudinal standing waves with a Node at the clamp and Antinodes at both ends (\( \lambda_{\text{rod}} = 2L \)).
  2. The vibrating disc at the end of the rod excites standing waves in the gas enclosed in the glass tube. Fine lycopodium powder settles into heaps at the Displacement Nodes (where air is motionless) and is blown away from Displacement Antinodes.
  3. The distance between two consecutive powder heaps equals half the wavelength in the gas (\( \Delta l = \frac{\lambda_{\text{gas}}}{2} \)).
Ratio of Sound Speeds: \[ \frac{v_{\text{rod}}}{v_{\text{gas}}} = \frac{f \lambda_{\text{rod}}}{f \lambda_{\text{gas}}} = \frac{2L}{2 \Delta l} = \frac{L}{\Delta l} \]
