Calorimetry

Heat transfer analyzed through macroscopic energy conservation.

Conceptual Overview

The study of thermodynamics is fundamentally concerned with energy transfer between macroscopic systems. Within this broad domain, Calorimetry focuses specifically on the measurement and calculation of heat exchange.

When two systems at different temperatures are placed in thermal contact, they interact. Energy naturally and spontaneously flows from the system at a higher temperature to the system at a lower temperature until thermal equilibrium is established. This energy in transit is what physicist refer to as heat.

Calorimetry is built upon a single, powerful pillar: the Conservation of Energy. In an isolated system where no heat leaks to the surroundings, the total heat lost by hot bodies must be exactly equal to the total heat gained by cold bodies. This macroscopic balance equation allows us to predict final equilibrium temperatures, identify unknown materials by calculating their specific heat capacities, and quantify the unseen energy involved in phase transitions.
