Heat

The macroscopic manifestation of microscopic energy transfer.

What is Heat?

The most important question of this chapter to understand anything further. What is heat? So far we have learned that heat is a form of energy. Just like light, sound, kinetic energy etc. And we know that energy is capacity to do work. This just means that Heat is some kind of capacity to do work - in the sense that - we can extract work from heat. Work is very specifically defined as \( \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{s} \).

  • SI Unit: Joule (\( \text{J} \))
  • Common Unit: Calorie (\( \text{cal} \)), where \( 1 \text{ cal} = 4.184 \text{ J} \)
  • Dimensional Formula: \( [Q] = \text{M}^1 \text{L}^2 \text{T}^{-2} \)

Heat as Energy in Transit

Definition: Heat (\(Q\)) is the form of energy that is transferred between two systems (or a system and its surroundings) by virtue of a temperature difference between them.

In the language of physics, the word "heat" is used very precisely. It is not an intrinsic property of a body. When you touch a hot coffee cup, the kinetic energy of the rapidly vibrating molecules in the cup is transferred to the slower-moving molecules in your hand via collisions. The energy that is flowing during this exact moment of interaction is heat.

  • SI Unit: Joule (\( \text{J} \))
  • Common Unit: Calorie (\( \text{cal} \)), where \( 1 \text{ cal} = 4.184 \text{ J} \)
  • Dimensional Formula: \( [Q] = \text{M}^1 \text{L}^2 \text{T}^{-2} \)

Spontaneous Flow

Without the intervention of external work (like a refrigerator or a heat pump), heat will only flow in one specified direction.

Heat naturally and spontaneously flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature.

This unidirectional flow continues until both bodies reach the same temperature. Once thermal equilibrium is achieved, the net transfer of heat between them drops strictly to zero.

Why Heat is Not a Property

A pervasive misconception in thermodynamics is treating heat like a fluid stored inside an object, a leftover idea from the obsolete "caloric theory."

An object contains internal energy (the total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of its molecules), but it does not contain "heat." Heat only exists as a transaction. Just as "work" is energy transferred by macroscopic forces and is not "possessed" by an object, "heat" is energy transferred by microscopic temperature gradients.

You cannot say an object "has a lot of heat." You can only say that an object "transferred a lot of heat" or that it "possesses a high internal energy." Heat is the process, not the possession.
