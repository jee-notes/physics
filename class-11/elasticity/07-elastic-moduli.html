Elastic Moduli

Categorizing material stiffness based on the mode of deformation.

Young's Modulus of Elasticity (\(Y\))

Young's Modulus measures the material's resistance to longitudinal (lengthwise) deformation. It is defined as the ratio of longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain within the elastic limit.

\[ Y = \frac{\text{Longitudinal Stress}}{\text{Longitudinal Strain}} = \frac{F/A}{\Delta L/L} = \frac{FL}{A\Delta L} \]
Young's modulus is relevant only for **solids**. Fluids (liquids and gases) do not have a fixed length and cannot be stretched like a wire.

For a wire of radius \(r\) and length \(L\), if a mass \(M\) is suspended to produce an extension \(\Delta L\): \[ Y = \frac{MgL}{\pi r^2 \Delta L} \]

Bulk Modulus (\(B\)) and Compressibility (\(K\))

Bulk Modulus measures a material's resistance to change in volume without change in shape. It is the ratio of volumetric stress (pressure change) to volumetric strain.

\[ B = -\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V / V} = -\frac{V \Delta P}{\Delta V} \]

The negative sign indicates that as pressure increases (\(\Delta P > 0\)), volume decreases (\(\Delta V < 0\)), making \(B\) a positive quantity.

Compressibility (\(K\)): The reciprocal of Bulk Modulus is called Compressibility. \[ K = \frac{1}{B} = -\frac{1}{V} \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta P} \]
Unlike Young's Modulus, Bulk Modulus is defined for all states of matter: solids, liquids, and gases. Gases are much more compressible (low \(B\)) compared to solids and liquids.

Shear Modulus or Modulus of Rigidity (\(G\) or \(\eta\))

Shear Modulus measures a material's resistance to change in shape. It is defined as the ratio of shearing stress to shearing strain.

\[ G (\text{or } \eta) = \frac{\text{Shearing Stress}}{\text{Shearing Strain}} = \frac{F/A}{\theta} = \frac{F}{A\theta} \]
Shear modulus is primarily defined for **solids**. Fixed fluids cannot sustain shear stress; they flow instead.

In most materials, Young's Modulus is generally larger than the Shear Modulus (\(Y \approx 3G\)). This means it is usually easier to twist or bend a material than to stretch it.
