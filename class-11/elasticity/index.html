Elasticity

How materials respond to applied forces.

Conceptual Overview

When external forces are applied to a body, its shape or size may change. This process is called deformation. However, many materials possess an inherent ability to resist these changes and return to their original state once the forces are removed. This property of regaining original shape and size is known as elasticity.

In the study of elasticity, we do not treat bodies as perfectly rigid. Instead, we investigate how real materials respond to varying types of stresses—such as stretching, compressing, or shearing. Hooke’s Law forms the cornerstone of this study, establishing a fundamental relationship between the applied stress and the resulting strain within the elastic limit.

Understanding elastic behavior is critical not only for fundamental physics but also for engineering applications, as it dictates the strength and durability of materials under everyday mechanical loads.
