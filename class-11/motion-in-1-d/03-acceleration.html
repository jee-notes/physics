Acceleration

The rate at which an object's velocity changes over time.

Average Acceleration

Just as velocity measures the rate of change of position, acceleration measures the rate of change of velocity. If a particle's velocity changes, it is said to be accelerating.

Definition: Average Acceleration
Average acceleration over a time interval \( \Delta t \) is defined as the change in velocity \( \Delta \vec{v} \) divided by the time interval. \[ \vec{a}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{v}_f - \vec{v}_i}{t_f - t_i} \] Where:
  • \( \vec{v}_f \) = Final velocity
  • \( \vec{v}_i \) = Initial velocity
  • \( \Delta t \) = Time interval
  • Nature: Vector quantity.
  • SI Unit: \( \text{m/s}^2 \) (meters per second squared).
  • Dimensional Formula: \( [M^0 L^1 T^{-2}] \)

In one-dimensional motion, average acceleration is simply \( a_{\text{avg}} = \frac{v_f - v_i}{\Delta t} \).

Instantaneous Acceleration

Average acceleration gives the overall rate of change over an interval, but to find the acceleration at a precise moment, we use instantaneous acceleration.

Definition: Instantaneous Acceleration
Instantaneous acceleration is the limit of the average acceleration as the time interval \( \Delta t \) approaches zero. \[ \vec{a} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} \]

Since velocity is the derivative of position (\( v = \frac{dx}{dt} \)), acceleration is the second derivative of position with respect to time: \[ a = \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{dx}{dt} \right) = \frac{d^2 x}{dt^2} \]

Another extremely useful form for acceleration in 1D, derived using the chain rule, is: \[ a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt} = v \frac{dv}{dx} \] This formula is invaluable when velocity is given as a function of position, \( v(x) \), rather than a function of time.

Acceleration vs. Retardation (Deceleration)

  • Speeding up: If the velocity and acceleration vectors point in the same direction (i.e., they have the same sign), the speed of the particle increases.
  • Slowing down (Retardation): If the velocity and acceleration vectors point in opposite directions (i.e., they have opposite signs), the speed of the particle decreases. This is often called deceleration or retardation.

Note: A negative acceleration does NOT always mean the object is slowing down. If an object is moving in the negative direction (\( v < 0 \)) and has negative acceleration (\( a < 0 \)), it is actually speeding up in the negative direction!
