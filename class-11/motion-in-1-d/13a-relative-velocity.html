Equations of Motion in Relative Context.

Average Speed

Example: Kinematic Doppler

Men are running in a line along a road with speed \(9\,\text{km/h}\), one behind another at equal intervals of \(20\,\text{m}\). Cyclists are also moving along the same line in the same direction with speed \(18\,\text{km/h}\), one behind another at equal intervals of \(30\,\text{m}\). Find the speed with which an observer must travel in the opposite direction so that whenever he meets a runner, he also meets a cyclist.

Solution

Let the speed of the observer be \(V\). Since the observer moves opposite to the runners and cyclists, the relative speeds are

\[ V+9 \]

for runners and

\[ V+18 \]

for cyclists.

The time taken by the observer to meet two consecutive runners is therefore

\[ T_R=\frac{20}{V+9}. \]

Similarly, the time taken to meet two consecutive cyclists is

\[ T_C=\frac{30}{V+18}. \]

For every meeting with a runner to coincide with a meeting with a cyclist, these encounter intervals must be equal:

\[ \frac{20}{V+9}=\frac{30}{V+18} \] \[ 20(V+18)=30(V+9) \] \[ 2V+36=3V+27 \] \[ \boxed{V=9\,\text{km/h}} \]
The observer is encountering successive members of two uniformly spaced groups. The meeting rate depends on the relative speed and the spacing of the objects.

For the meetings to remain synchronized, the observer must encounter the two groups at the same rate: \[ \boxed{ \frac{\text{relative speed}}{\text{spacing}} = \text{constant} } \] This is a purely kinematic analogue of the idea behind the Doppler effect.
