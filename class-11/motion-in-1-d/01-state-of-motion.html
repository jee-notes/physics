Motion as a State

Qualitatively, motion is a state assigned to a body by an observer.

State of Being

What do we mean by a state?

A state is simply a description of how something is at a particular instant. For example, a person may be happy or sad, awake or asleep, rich or poor.

Similarly, a physical object may be hot or cold, charged or neutral, at rest or in motion.

But are all states the same? For instance, how is describing an object as "green" different from describing it as "in motion"? Is it because motion implies dynamism? The answer is no. The difference is that there are broadly different kinds of states based on their dependence on the observer:

Type of State Examples Observer Dependent?
Intrinsic Mass, charge, temperature No
Relational Position, rest, motion Yes
Perceptual Green, loud, bright Partially
Internal Happy, asleep, excited Depends on model

Notice that the state of rest or motion is a relational state. A helpful analogy to understand this is the percentile system used in exams.

If you score 150 marks, that score is an intrinsic attribute of your performance. However, achieving the "95th percentile" is a relational quantity—it simply means you performed better relative to 95% of the other candidates. If the group of candidates changes, your percentile can change even though your raw marks remain exactly the same.

Intrinsic Property Relational State
MassPosition
ChargeVelocity
TemperatureRest
ColourMotion
Exam MarksPercentile

Just as a percentile does not describe a student's performance in isolation, the state of motion does not describe a body in isolation. A body cannot simply be said to be "moving" or "at rest" without specifying with respect to whom.

Although physics ultimately seeks quantitative descriptions of natural phenomena, we do not perceive the physical world in terms of numbers and equations.

We first recognize phenomena qualitatively and then develop mathematical tools to describe them precisely.

Thus, before studying motion quantitatively, it is useful to understand it qualitatively as a state describing how a body appears to an observer.

State of Rest

What does it mean when we state that a body, object, or particle is in a state of rest?

Rest is not absolute; it is defined strictly relative to an observer. If an observer continuously monitors a particle and finds that its position relative to them does not change as time passes, the particle is in a state of rest with respect to that observer.

Physical Examples of Rest

  • A book on a desk: To a student sitting quietly in the room, the distance and direction of the book do not change over time. The book is at rest relative to the student.
  • Passengers in a moving vehicle: Consider two passengers sitting next to each other in a cruising airplane. Even though the plane is traveling at 800 km/h relative to the ground, the distance between the two passengers is not changing. Therefore, they are in a state of rest relative to one another.

State of Motion

What does it mean when we state that a body, object, or particle is in a state of motion?

Similarly, motion is defined relative to an observer. If an observer monitors a particle and finds that its position relative to them changes as time passes, the particle is in a state of motion with respect to that observer.

Physical Examples of Motion

  • A falling apple: To a person sitting under a tree, an apple detaching from a branch continuously changes its position as it falls toward the ground. It is in a state of motion relative to the person.
  • The airplane passengers (from outside): Let's revisit the airplane passengers. To an observer standing on the ground looking up at the sky, the airplane and the passengers inside are changing their position continuously. Thus, the exact same passengers who are at rest relative to each other are in a state of motion relative to the ground observer.
