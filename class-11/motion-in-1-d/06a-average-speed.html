Average Speed

Useful results for special cases frequently encountered in numerical problems.

Average Speed

The average speed of a particle is always defined as

\[ v_{\rm avg}=\frac{\text{Total Distance Travelled}}{\text{Total Time Taken}} \]

The formulas discussed below are not independent formulas. They are direct consequences of the above definition and are applicable only under the specified conditions.

Average Speed for Equal Time Intervals

Suppose a particle moves with speed \(v_1\) for a time interval \(t\), and then with speed \(v_2\) for the same time interval \(t\).

Total distance travelled \[ =v_1t+v_2t=(v_1+v_2)t \]

Total time taken \[ =2t \]

Therefore, \[ v_{\rm avg}=\frac{(v_1+v_2)t}{2t}=\boxed{\frac{v_1+v_2}{2}} \]

This is simply the arithmetic mean of the two speeds.

Average Speed for Equal Distances

Suppose a particle covers the same distance \(d\) with speed \(v_1\), and then covers another equal distance \(d\) with speed \(v_2\).

Total distance travelled \[ =2d \]

Total time taken \[ =\frac{d}{v_1}+\frac{d}{v_2} \]

Therefore, \[ v_{\rm avg}=\frac{2d}{\dfrac{d}{v_1}+\dfrac{d}{v_2}}=\boxed{\frac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2}} \]

This is the harmonic mean of the two speeds.

Important Note

These two formulas are valid only under their respective conditions.
  • Use \(\displaystyle \frac{v_1+v_2}{2}\) only when the particle travels with the two speeds for equal time intervals.
  • Use \(\displaystyle \frac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2}\) only when the particle covers equal distances with the two speeds.
In all other situations, always return to the fundamental definition \[ v_{\rm avg} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} \]
