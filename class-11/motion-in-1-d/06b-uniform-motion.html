Uniform Motion

Uniform Motion is simplest state of motion.

State of Motion

A body is said to be in a state of motion if its position changes with time.

Equivalently, a body is in a state of motion if it has a non-zero speed. In this sense, speed measures the amount (magnitude) of motion.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, the terms speed and velocity always refer to their instantaneous values.
Average speed and average velocity are stated explicitly whenever they are intended.

A body with zero speed is therefore not said to be in a state of motion. It is said to be in a state of rest.

Saying that a body is in a state of motion with zero speed is similar to saying that a person is rich with zero money. A person with zero money is not rich — they are poor. Likewise, a body with zero speed possesses no motion and is therefore in a state of rest.

Motion is characterized by instantaneous speed.
If the instantaneous speed is zero, the body is in a state of rest.
If the instantaneous speed is non-zero, the body is in a state of motion.
This is because a state is an instantaneous description of a body.

Uniform Motion

A body is said to be in a state of uniform motion if its instantaneous velocity \(\vec{v}\) remains constant (in time). Mathematically we can write it as:

\[ \vec{v}(t) = \text{Constant} \]

Since velocity is a vector quantity, both its magnitude (speed) and direction should remain unchanged throughout the motion for uniform motion.

Consequently, the velocity is constant not only in time domain but also in space domain. That is, the body has the same velocity at every instant of time and at every point location along its path. Mathematically we can write it as:

\[ \vec{v}(t) = \vec{v}(x) = \text{Constant} \]

Uniform motion is the simplest possible state of motion of a body because its velocity remains completely unchanged throughout the motion.

In uniform circular motion, only the speed is constant.
The direction of the velocity is continuously changing.
As a result, uniform circular motion is not an example of uniform motion.

Characteristics of Uniform Motion

Since the instantaneous velocity \(\vec{v}\) remains constant in time, we can take it out of the integral to find total displacement.

\[ \text{Total Displacement} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \vec{v} \, dt \]

as \(\vec{v}\) is constant,

\[ \text{total displacement} = \vec{v} \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \, dt = \vec{v} \Delta t \]

Average velocity \( \langle \vec{v} \rangle \) is given by (Total Displacement)/(Time Interval).

\[ \langle\vec{v}\rangle = \frac{\vec{v} \Delta t}{\vec{v}} = \vec{v} \]

Thus, the average velocity over any time interval is equal to the instantaneous velocity of any instant in that time interval.

\[ \langle\vec{v}\rangle=\vec{v} \]

Consequently, the average speed over any time interval is equal to the instantaneous speed of any instant in that time interval.

\[ |\langle\vec{v}\rangle|=|\vec{v}| \]

Since the direction of motion also remains unchanged, the distance travelled during any time interval is equal to the magnitude of the corresponding displacement.

\[ \vec{s}=\int\vec{v}\,dt \] \[ d=\int |\vec{v}|\,dt \]

Therefore,

\[ |\vec{s}|=\left|\int\vec{v}\,dt\right|=\int |\vec{v}|\,dt=d \]

Thus, for uniform motion, the distance covered in any time interval is equal to the magnitude of displacement in the interval. |Displacement| = Distance

\[ \boxed{\left|\int\vec{v}\,dt\right|=\int |\vec{v}|\,dt} \]

Uniform Motion vs Unidirectional Motion

In Uniform Motion, both the magnitude as well as the direction of the instantaneous velocity remain constant.

Thus, a body in uniform motion moves along a straight line, in one direction and with constant speed.

In Unidirectional Motion, only the direction of the instantaneous velocity remains constant. There is no constraint on its magnitude (speed).

Thus, a body in unidirectional motion moves along a straight line and in one direction, but its speed may vary from instant to instant.

\[ 0\lt|\vec v(t)|\lt\infty \]

Hence,

  • Uniform Motion \(\implies\) Unidirectional Motion
  • Unidirectional Motion \(\not\Rightarrow\) Uniform Motion
