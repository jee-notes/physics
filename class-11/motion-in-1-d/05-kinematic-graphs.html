Kinematic Graphs

Visualizing motion through position, velocity, and acceleration graphs.

Interpreting Slopes and Areas

Before diving into specific graphs, it is essential to understand the calculus connections that allow us to extract information from kinematic plots.

The Rules of Graphical Kinematics:
  • Slope of a curve: The slope of a tangent line on a graph representing \( y \) vs \( x \) gives the derivative \( \frac{dy}{dx} \).
  • Area under a curve: The area under the curve of a \( y \) vs \( x \) graph gives the integral \( \int y \, dx \).
Applying these rules to kinematics:
  • Slope of Position-Time (x-t) Graph = Velocity (\( v = \frac{dx}{dt} \))
  • Slope of Velocity-Time (v-t) Graph = Acceleration (\( a = \frac{dv}{dt} \))
  • Area under Velocity-Time (v-t) Graph = Displacement (\( \Delta x = \int v \, dt \))
  • Area under Acceleration-Time (a-t) Graph = Change in Velocity (\( \Delta v = \int a \, dt \))

Position-Time (x-t) Graph

The position-time graph shows how a particle's position varies as time progresses. Time is plotted on the horizontal axis and position on the vertical axis.

  • Object at Rest: The position does not change with time. The graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis. The slope is zero (velocity is zero).
  • Uniform Motion (Constant Velocity): The position changes linearly with time. The graph is an inclined straight line. A positive slope indicates positive velocity; a negative slope indicates negative velocity.
  • Accelerated Motion: The position changes quadratically (or otherwise non-linearly) with time. The graph is a curve (e.g., a parabola). If the curve opens upwards (concave up), acceleration is positive. If it opens downwards (concave down), acceleration is negative.

Velocity-Time (v-t) Graph

The velocity-time graph is arguably the most useful kinematic graph because it yields both acceleration (from its slope) and displacement (from its area).

  • Object at Rest: The graph is a straight line lying exactly on the time axis (\( v=0 \)).
  • Uniform Motion (Constant Velocity): The graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis, but shifted up or down depending on the speed. Slope is zero (\( a=0 \)).
  • Uniformly Accelerated Motion: The graph is an inclined straight line. The constant slope equals the constant acceleration.
    • Positive slope = Positive acceleration.
    • Negative slope = Negative acceleration.
  • Non-Uniform Acceleration: The graph is a curve. The instantaneous acceleration at any time is the slope of the tangent to the curve at that point.
Distance vs Displacement from v-t Graph: To find displacement, calculate the signed area under the v-t curve (areas above the t-axis are positive, areas below are negative). To find distance, calculate the total absolute area (treat all areas as positive).

Acceleration-Time (a-t) Graph

The acceleration-time graph shows how a particle's acceleration varies with time.

  • Uniform Acceleration: The graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis.
  • Non-Uniform Acceleration: The graph is an inclined line or a curve.

The area under the a-t graph gives the change in velocity: \( \Delta v = v_f - v_i = \int a \, dt \).
