Introduction: Rest, Motion, and Displacement

The foundational concepts of kinematics and describing the exact location of a particle.

Rest and Motion

In physics, we describe the state of an object as being either at rest or in motion.

  • Rest: An object is said to be at rest if it does not change its position with respect to its surroundings with the passage of time.
  • Motion: An object is said to be in motion if it changes its position with respect to its surroundings with the passage of time.

It is crucial to understand that rest and motion are relative terms. There is no such thing as absolute rest or absolute motion. For example, a passenger sitting in a moving train is at rest with respect to his fellow passengers, but in motion with respect to a person standing on the platform.

Because the Earth itself is constantly moving (rotating on its axis and revolving around the Sun), nothing on Earth is at absolute rest. We must always define the "surroundings" or the "observer" to meaningfully talk about motion.

Frame of Reference

To describe motion unambiguously, we use a Frame of Reference.

Definition: Frame of Reference
A frame of reference is a coordinate system rigidly attached to an observer, along with a clock to measure time. It provides a standard against which the position and motion of objects can be measured.

In 1D motion, our frame of reference is simply a straight line (the x-axis) with a chosen origin (\( x = 0 \)), a defined positive direction, and a clock starting at \( t = 0 \).

Position, Distance, and Displacement

To describe exactly where an object is and how far it has traveled, we define three crucial terms.

Position

The position of a particle is its location with respect to the origin of the chosen frame of reference. In 1D, it is simply the coordinate \( x \). Position can be positive, negative, or zero.

Distance (Path Length)

Definition: Distance
Distance is the total length of the actual path traversed by a moving object in a given time interval.
  • Nature: Scalar quantity (magnitude only).
  • SI Unit: Meter (m).
  • Dimensional Formula: \( [M^0 L^1 T^0] \)

Distance is always positive and never decreases with time for a moving particle.

Displacement

Definition: Displacement
Displacement is the shortest distance from the initial position to the final position of the object. It is a vector pointing from the initial to the final position.

Mathematically, for 1D motion: \[ \Delta x = x_f - x_i \] Where:
  • \( \Delta x \) = Displacement
  • \( x_f \) = Final position
  • \( x_i \) = Initial position
  • Nature: Vector quantity (magnitude and direction).
  • SI Unit: Meter (m).
  • Dimensional Formula: \( [M^0 L^1 T^0] \)
In 1D motion, the direction of displacement is indicated by its sign (+ or -). A positive displacement means the object shifted in the positive x-direction, while a negative displacement means it shifted in the negative x-direction.

Comparison: Distance vs. Displacement

  • The magnitude of displacement is always less than or equal to distance: \( |\Delta x| \le \text{Distance} \).
  • They are equal only when the particle moves in a straight line without reversing its direction.
  • If a particle returns to its starting point, its displacement is zero, but the distance traveled is not zero.
